Workshop registration with limited spots: how to set it up step by step

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

A workshop with twelve spots falls apart over details: someone who signed up by message and never paid, two people who picked the same option that had already run out, the kit you bought for fifteen when nine showed up. Registration is the part that puts all that in order, and it is worth thinking through before you load the event. Here is how to set it up in the ENTRÁ form, field by field, and what to ask (and what not to) the people signing up.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Before opening the form: three decisions

There are three things you should have settled on paper before loading anything:

  • Is it paid or free? Charging in advance creates more commitment: whoever paid shows up. If it is an open class or an outreach talk, free registration works too, with the same list and the same cap.
  • How many spots are there really? Not how many fit in the room, but how many you can attend to properly: chairs, tables, kits, glasses, computers.
  • Are there options that change the price or the cap? With or without materials, beginner or advanced, morning or afternoon session. Each of those will be a ticket type.

The form, field by field

When you create the event in ENTRÁ, this is what you fill in and what to put in each part:

  • Title and description. The description is required and it is what people who are unsure read. Say what you do, who it is for, what level is needed, what the price includes and what each person has to bring. Every question you answer there is one message less.
  • Date and time. Enter the real date. The system lets you leave it as to be confirmed, but in that case the reminder for the day before does not go out.
  • Venue. Full address. If it is a house or a studio with no sign, add in the description how to get there and which bell to ring.
  • Free event. It is a switch. If you turn it on, prices show as "Gratis" (free), people reserve without paying and still receive their entry QR.
  • Ticket types. Each one has a name, price and cap. This is where options are handled: "Spot with materials" and "Spot without materials", each with its limit. When a type sells out, nobody else can choose it.
  • Presale. Each ticket type can be marked as presale, with its own price and cap, to reward the first people who sign up.

If the workshop has several sessions, it is loaded as a multi-day event. Once loaded, the event goes through a review before it is published.

What data registration asks for, and why it is enough

People signing up don't need to create an account. The purchase form asks for:

  • Full name, as it appears on their ID.
  • Email, which is where the QR arrives and later the reminder.
  • ID number (DNI), which is used to find the person at the door if they arrive without the QR.
  • Phone, optional.

For a workshop, that covers the essentials: you know who is coming, how to contact them and how to identify them. If you need anything else, it is added as a custom question (below). More required fields at checkout tend to make people abandon the registration halfway.

What to ask separately (and how)

There is information a workshop does need: the level, apron size, an allergy at a tasting, whether someone brings their own machine. The practical rule is this:

  • If the answer changes the price or the cap, it is a ticket type. "Brings their own laptop" and "Uses one from the workshop", with the cap of machines you have. That way the option is chosen when paying and there is no way to go over.
  • If it changes none of that, it is a custom question for the event. When creating the event you can add questions for people signing up: free text, number or a list of options (for example, sizes S, M and L), and mark which ones are required. They appear at checkout and each answer comes out in its own column of the attendee list you export from the dashboard.
  • Two or three questions at most. Each extra field at checkout is a reason to abandon it halfway. Whatever can wait, ask in a single email to the list a few days before.
  • Only ask what you are going to use. If you are not going to change the menu for a dietary restriction, don't ask about it: it creates an expectation you won't meet.

That same email beforehand is a good moment to send whatever people need to read or install first, so the first part of the class is not spent on that.

The registration list, in the dashboard

In the event's attendees tab you see each person with their ticket type, you can search and filter, and export. The summary shows total capacity adding up what was sold and the complimentary tickets, so you know how many spots are really left. For a workshop, the list is useful for three concrete things: buying materials for the real number, arranging tables or groups and keeping a record of who came so you can tell them about the next date. What else is worth watching in the numbers is in what data a ticketing platform should give you.

The reminder the day before

You don't have to schedule it. ENTRÁ automatically sends an email the day before the event, based on the date in Argentina, with the subject "Es mañana" (it's tomorrow) and the workshop name, date, time and venue. One email per person arrives, even if they bought several spots, and it tells them how many tickets they have. In the dashboard's emails tab you see which emails went out and which failed.

Two things for it to work: the date has to be entered (not "to be confirmed") and the event has to still be active. If sales are paused because you filled the cap, the reminder still goes out.

If registration is free, the risk is a different one: people who reserve and don't come. There is a separate guide for that, free event: how to stop people reserving and not showing up.

Frequently asked questions

What data does ENTRÁ ask from someone signing up for a workshop?

Full name, email and ID number (DNI) are required, and phone is optional. There is no need to create an account. All of it appears in the attendee list you export from the dashboard.

How do I ask about level, size or whether someone brings materials?

If the answer changes the price or the cap, set up one ticket type per option, for example with materials and without materials, each with its limit. If it is information that changes none of that, like a size or an allergy, ask by email to the registration list a few days before.

When does the workshop reminder go out?

The day before, based on the date in Argentina, and one email per person arrives even if they bought several spots. If the event was left with the date to be confirmed, the reminder does not go out, so enter the real date first.

Can I make registration free and still have a cap?

Yes. You turn on the free event option when creating it and people reserve their spot without paying, with the same details and their entry QR. The cap for each ticket type works the same as in a paid workshop.
