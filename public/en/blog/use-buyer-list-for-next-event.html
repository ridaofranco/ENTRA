Your buyer list: how to use it to sell the next date

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

People who already paid once for your event are the easiest to convince for the next one: they know you, they know what it is like and they trusted you enough to buy. That list is the most valuable thing a date leaves you, and also the easiest to burn if you use it badly. This guide goes in order: what the export includes, how to ask for permission, who to tell first and how to set up a presale for them.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

What the export includes

From the event dashboard you download the attendee list as a CSV file that opens in Excel or Google Sheets. It has one row per ticket with these columns:

  • Name, email, ID number (DNI) and phone of the buyer.
  • Ticket type, origin and price. The origin says whether it was a purchase or a complimentary ticket.
  • Purchase date. It shows you who buys early and who buys at the last minute.
  • Check-in. It says "Ingresó" (entered) if the ticket was scanned at the door and "Pendiente" (pending) if not.
  • Transferred, if the ticket passed from one person to another.
  • The extra questions you added to the event, one column per question.

There is also a sales export, per order, with the details of what each person bought, the total and the source and campaign of the sale. What to look at in those numbers is in what data a ticketing platform should give you.

Ask for permission before you write

Someone giving you their email to receive a ticket does not mean they want your news. Argentina's Personal Data Protection Law (Ley 25.326) says that processing data is unlawful when the owner has not given their "free, express and informed consent". For data used for advertising, it adds that the owner may "at any time request the withdrawal or blocking of their name". In practice:

  • Ask at checkout. At ENTRÁ you can add extra questions to the event, including a multiple choice one. Something like "Do you want us to let you know about the next date?" with "Yes" and "No". The answer shows up as a column in the export.
  • Only write to people who said yes. The rest of the list is for the event's accounts, not for sending promotions.
  • Give them an easy way out. Every message has to say how to stop receiving them, and whoever asks comes off the list that same day.

If you have doubts about what applies to your case, check with someone who knows the law: this guide is not legal advice.

Look after the list

It is your audience's personal data, so treat it that way:

  • Only one person should hold it. Do not send it to the organizers' WhatsApp group.
  • Remove the columns you will not use. To announce a new date, name, email or phone, check-in and the permission answer are enough. You do not need the ID number.
  • Do not share it or sell it. Not to another promoter, not to a sponsor.

Don't spam: few messages, each with something concrete

A list burns out fast if it gets a message every week with nothing new. Write when you have something to say:

  • A short thank you after the event, with photos if you have them. Without selling anything.
  • The announcement of the next date, once it is confirmed, with venue, day and price.
  • A reminder before the presale closes. That's it.

Use the channel they gave you permission for. If they said yes to email, do not write to them on WhatsApp.

Tell the people who came first

The check-in column separates those who bought and came from those who bought and never showed up. The ones who came are your safest audience: tell them before anyone else. For those who bought and did not come, a different message ("you couldn't make it last time, here is the next one") works better than the same announcement for everyone. Complimentary tickets are also marked in the export: decide whether you include them in the announcement or not.

A presale just for them

What makes people buy now instead of "later" is having something the rest do not have. Two ways to give it to them:

  • A discount code only for your list. You create it from the event dashboard with a usage limit, and the codes table shows how many times it was used. That way you measure how much your list sold.
  • A few days' head start. Tell them before you announce the date on social media, with the presale price that everyone will get later.

Frequently asked questions

What data does the ENTRÁ attendee export include?

A CSV file with name, email, ID number (DNI), phone, ticket type, whether it was a purchase or complimentary, price, purchase date, whether the person came in and whether the ticket was transferred. If the event had extra questions, each one comes in its own column.

Can I write to everyone who bought to promote the next date?

Argentina's Law 25.326 requires free, express and informed consent to process personal data, and anyone receiving advertising can ask at any time to be removed from the list. The clean way is to ask at checkout whether they want to receive news, write only to those who said yes and remove anyone who asks.

How do I set up a presale only for the people who came?

Create a discount code with a usage limit and send it only to those who checked in at the previous event and agreed to receive news. The dashboard shows how many times the code was used.
