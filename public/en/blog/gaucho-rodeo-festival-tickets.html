Jineteada or gaucho festival: how to sell the tickets

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 6 min read

A jineteada (the traditional Argentine gaucho rodeo, with riders on unbroken horses) or a fiesta criolla (a gaucho country festival) has everything that complicates a door: grounds on the edge of town, signal that comes and goes, whole families arriving by pickup truck, kids of all ages and lots of people who decide to go that same morning. This guide sorts out sales before the day and entry on the day of the event, with what ENTRÁ does and what has to be handled separately.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Before selling: what has to be in order

An event with animals and an audience needs a license from the municipality and, depending on the province, other permits. Ask the municipality what they require before announcing the date. In addition, Argentina's Law 14.346 (in Spanish) applies nationwide: it punishes with prison anyone who mistreats animals or subjects them to acts of cruelty, and among acts of cruelty it includes holding public or private animal fights, bullfights, bullfights with young bulls and parodies in which animals are killed, injured or harassed. If you have doubts about how it applies to your program, check with the municipality or a lawyer before selling.

Advance online sales and the gate

In the countryside a lot of people buy at the gate, and that won't change from one year to the next. What you can do is give them a reason to buy earlier:

  • A cheaper advance ticket, and said everywhere: the flyer, the town radio, the WhatsApp group of the local rural society.
  • A clear cutoff date: "Advance tickets until Friday at 8 pm". When the time comes, you raise the price of that type in the dashboard, or leave it sold out and add a "Gate" type with another price.
  • A simple purchase: on ENTRÁ you buy without creating an account, with name, email and ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID). On paying, the ticket with the QR appears on screen and also arrives by email.

With ENTRÁ you collect 100% of the price you set for each ticket; service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying. If part of your audience isn't comfortable buying online, add someone from the team to help people buy from their phones in the days before, for example at the general store or at the club.

About the gate, so as not to promise what isn't there: ENTRÁ has no box office and doesn't record cash payments. Online sales stay open during the event, until the end time you entered (if you didn't enter one, until three hours after the start), so whoever arrives without a ticket can buy from their phone in the line, if there is signal. If cash at the gate is central to your festival, that money stays outside ENTRÁ and you control it yourself with your own spreadsheet or ticket book. Ticket types: adults, kids and groups

Whole families come to a gaucho festival. Sort out the prices before loading:

  • "General", for adults.
  • "Child 6 to 12", with the age in the name. Decide from what age people pay and write it down: "Under 6 free".
  • "Retiree", if you want a special price. Make clear what they have to show at the door.

Each type has its own name, price and cap, and each person gets their own QR. At the door the scanner shows the type of each ticket: if it says "Child" and an adult walks in, you see it on the spot. If the youngest don't pay but you want to count them, you can issue them a complimentary ticket from the dashboard with name, email and reason; if not, count them by hand, because they still take up space in the stands. If the festival runs two days (jineteada on Saturday, parade and folk music on Sunday), you can load it as a multi-day event and choose whether the QR is valid for every day, once per day, or whether the buyer picks which days they attend.

Parking

At rural grounds almost everyone arrives by car, pickup or van, and the parking area fills up before the stands. If you charge for parking, set it up as a separate ticket type:

  • "Car parking" and, if needed, "Van or trailer parking", each with its own price.
  • A cap equal to the real spots in the parking area. When it fills up, that type closes on its own and nobody else can buy it.
  • One QR per vehicle, scanned at the entrance to the parking area, not at the public gate.

One detail: the total capacity the dashboard shows adds up all ticket types, parking included. To know how many people you expect, subtract the car spots. And write on the page where vehicles come in, what time the parking area opens and whether people can arrive on horseback or by horse cart, which happens at these festivals.

Access control with poor signal

It is the number one problem in the countryside. The ENTRÁ scanner keeps validating offline, on one condition: the event has to be selected on each phone while there is still internet. This is how it works:

  • It validates against what it already downloaded and marks the ticket as used instantly. If the same QR goes through that phone twice, it is rejected.
  • It saves each check-in on the phone and syncs it by itself when the connection returns. A yellow banner at the top warns that it is working offline.
  • The search also uses what was downloaded: anyone who can't find their QR can be looked up by surname, ID number or email.

The limit you need to know: offline, each phone only knows what it scanned itself. With two stations without signal at the same time, a shared screenshot could get through once at each; when the connection returns, the dashboard flags which tickets got through twice and at which station. And a phone without signal doesn't know about anything sold after its last connection, which at a festival with sales in the line is a lot. So:

  • Walk the grounds beforehand and find where there is signal. Sometimes it is fifty meters from the gate.
  • Bring a SIM card from another carrier to share data with the phones on the gate.
  • Export the attendee list as CSV before leaving, with name, ID number, ticket type and code, and keep it on another phone or printed.
  • A spare phone with the scanner open and a power bank per station.

All the detail is in access control without internet.

What to say on the page

  • The program: what time the jineteada starts, the categories, the parade, who plays at the folk gathering and when.
  • How to get there: the kilometer on the highway, the dirt stretch, what happens if it rains and the road gets bad.
  • What there is to eat: asado (Argentine barbecue), a canteen, whether you can bring a cooler and whether you have to pay in cash inside.
  • Prices by age and parking, all together and clear.
  • Rain plan: whether it is called off, moved to another date or goes ahead anyway.

Frequently asked questions

Does the ENTRÁ scanner work on grounds without signal?

Yes, if you selected the event in the scanner while you had a connection. The phone validates against the tickets it already downloaded, marks each one as used and syncs check-ins when the connection returns. That phone doesn't know about anything sold after its last connection, so it is worth having at least one station with mobile data.

Can I sell for cash at the gate with ENTRÁ?

No. ENTRÁ has no box office and doesn't record cash payments. What you can do is leave online sales open during the event so people buy from their phones, as long as there is signal at the entrance. If you take cash, that money stays outside the system and you control it yourself.

How do I charge for kids' tickets at a gaucho festival?

With a "Child" ticket type, with the age in the name, for example "Child 6 to 12", and its price. Decide and write down from what age people pay. Each person gets their QR, and the scanner shows the type when validating.

Can I sell parking together with the ticket?

Yes, as a separate ticket type, for example "Car parking", with its price and a cap equal to the spots in the parking area. Keep in mind that the total capacity the dashboard shows adds up all types, so subtract parking to count people.
