Dinner show: how to sell tickets with dinner and show only

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

An event with food has a limit that a regular show doesn't: the kitchen. You can have room for lots of people and meals for far fewer. That is why a dinner show sells better with two different tickets, one with dinner and one show only, each with its own capacity. This guide goes in order: how to set up the ticket types, how many meals to put on sale, what to do about special menus and how to handle the door.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Two ticket types, two capacities

At ENTRÁ each ticket type has its own price and capacity. For a dinner show, that is enough to separate what you offer:

  • Ticket with dinner. Includes the food and the show. The capacity is the number of meals that can be served well that night, not the hall's capacity.
  • Show only ticket. For people who arrive after eating or prefer to have a drink at the bar. The capacity is the rest of the venue's capacity, minus the dinner places.
  • Names that leave no doubt. "Dinner + show" and "Show only" are clearer than "Stalls" and "General". In the event description, say what dinner includes: courses, drinks or not, what time it is served.

The dashboard shows how many of each type have sold in real time, so you know how many meals to confirm with the kitchen without doing the math by hand.

Meal capacity: count with the kitchen, not the hall

The typical dinner show mistake is putting as many dinners on sale as there are chairs. Before publishing, sit down with whoever is cooking:

  • How many meals can be served on time? If dinner has to finish before the show starts, what counts is how many plates come out in that window.
  • By when do they need the final number? The kitchen buys the groceries in advance. Set a cutoff date for the dinner ticket and, after that date, leave only the show ticket.
  • What about no-shows? A dinner bought and not used is food that was paid for anyway. Make sure the event's numbers account for it.
  • How much does each meal cost? The price difference between the two tickets has to cover the food, not just come close. How to do the math is in how many tickets to sell so you don't lose money.

Special menus: ask at checkout

Vegetarian, gluten free, lactose free: if the kitchen finds out at the table, it is too late. At ENTRÁ you can add extra questions to the event that the buyer fills in when buying. Some ways to use them:

  • A multiple choice question with the menus the kitchen can make: "Regular menu", "Vegetarian", "Gluten free". Only offer what can really be served.
  • A text field for allergies or other restrictions, which the kitchen reviews one by one.
  • The list for the kitchen. The answers come as a column in the attendee export, together with the name and ticket type. Filter the "Dinner + show" ones and send it after the cutoff date.

If someone buys several tickets in one purchase, explain in the description how to indicate each companion's menu, so you don't find out at the table.

Tables and seating: say how it works

ENTRÁ sells ticket types with capacity, without a table map or numbered seats. If the hall has tables, decide before selling how they are filled and write it in the description:

  • First come, first served, with a clear doors time.
  • Shared tables, if people will sit with strangers. Better they know beforehand.
  • Groups who want to sit together: have them buy all their tickets in the same purchase and arrive together.

Complimentary guests eat too

An invitation with dinner is a meal the kitchen serves anyway. Decide beforehand how many complimentary tickets include dinner (artists, press, venue staff) and how many are show only. At ENTRÁ each complimentary ticket is loaded with its ticket type and reaches the person with their QR; they do not take from the sales capacity but they do count toward the total, so add them to the number you give the kitchen. How many to give without giving the night away is in complimentary tickets and invitations: how many to give.

The door

Each ticket has a unique QR that is valid only once and is scanned from a phone, and the scanner shows each QR's ticket type. With that, whoever is at the door knows right away whether the person is going to a dinner table or the show area:

  • Two entry times. If dinner starts earlier, open the door first for dinner tickets and later for the show. Write it in the description and in the email before the event.
  • Someone between the door and the hall to take dinner guests to their place, so the line keeps moving.
  • The special menu list at hand for whoever serves, with each person's name.

Frequently asked questions

How do I sell tickets with dinner and show only for the same event?

With two different ticket types, each with its own price and capacity. The capacity of the dinner ticket is the number of meals the kitchen can serve, and the show only ticket gets the rest of the venue's capacity.

How do I know who needs a vegetarian or gluten free menu?

Add an extra question to the event, for example a multiple choice one, so the buyer marks their menu. The answers come as a column in the attendee export and you pass them to the kitchen before the event.

Does ENTRÁ assign tables or numbered seats?

No. ENTRÁ sells ticket types with capacity, without a table map or numbered seats. If the event has tables, seating is handled at the venue, with a rule worth announcing before you sell.
