Guide for organizers

Livestreamed event: in-person and online tickets

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

A talk, a play or a show in a venue, plus people who want to watch from home or from another province. Selling both adds audience without adding seats, but there is one part you need to get right: how the link reaches whoever paid and how you keep that link from ending up in a WhatsApp group. Here is what ENTRÁ does, what it doesn't do and how to set it up on the side without complications.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

What ENTRÁ has and what it doesn't

So as not to promise what isn't there: ENTRÁ is a ticketing platform with access control. It issues tickets with QR codes that are scanned at the door, each one valid only once, and gives you a dashboard with sales and the buyer spreadsheet. It does not sell online access with a private link: it has no field to load the stream link, does not send it in the ticket email and has no player of its own.

What it does have is enough to set it up on the side:

  • Ticket types with their own name, price and capacity. You can have "Presencial" (in person) and "Online" in the same event, with different prices.
  • The buyer spreadsheet, which you export from the dashboard. It includes name, email, ID number (DNI) and ticket type, among other columns.
  • Your own questions at checkout, if you need any other detail from the buyer.
  • The scanner shows the ticket type, which helps you make sure nobody gets into the venue with an online ticket.

Setting up the two ticket types

In the same event you load two types:

  • In person, with the venue's capacity. It is the usual ticket: QR code, scanner, door.
  • Online (livestream), with that spelled out in the name so nobody buys it by mistake. The capacity can be high, but give it one: it protects you from surprises and lets you see how much it moved.

A few decisions worth making before publishing:

  • The online price. It is usually lower than in person, since there is no seat and no bar. But don't give it away: whoever buys it is paying for the work of the stream (camera, sound, connection), which has its cost.
  • Whether it will be recorded. Say whether the stream is live only or whether the link works for some time afterwards, and for how long.
  • Where it can be watched. If the platform you are going to use requires an account or an app, say so in the description.

One detail: you can buy up to four tickets of each type per purchase. For the online ticket you rarely need more than one per household; if someone buys several, the link still goes to one email, because it goes to the buyer's email.

With ENTRÁ you keep 100% of the price you set for each ticket, the online one included. Service and processing costs are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

What to say on the event page

The description has to make clear, in a few lines, what the online buyer gets. Something like this:

  • How and when the link arrives: "The online ticket has no QR code for the venue. The link to the stream reaches you by email, at the address you entered when buying, on the day, one hour before the start".
  • Where it can be watched and whether an account is needed.
  • Until when it can be watched, if it is recorded.
  • That the online ticket doesn't get you into the venue, stated plainly.

How to write a description that is clear and sells is covered in the event description that sells.

Sending the link from the spreadsheet

This is the central part, and it takes ten minutes:

  • Export the spreadsheet from the event dashboard, close to the time you are going to send the link.
  • Filter by the ticket type column and keep the "Online" rows.
  • Check refunded and transferred tickets. The spreadsheet has a column with each ticket's status and another one that marks whether it was transferred. If you refunded a ticket, remove that email from the list; if one was transferred, confirm the email you have is that of the person who holds it now.
  • Send the email with recipients in BCC, so nobody sees the others' emails. If there are many, a bulk email tool or a mail merge from the spreadsheet does the same with one email per person.
  • A short email: event name, start time, the link, how to watch and who to write to if it doesn't work.

Online sales can stay open until the end time you loaded for the event. That means someone can buy the online ticket after your first send. You have two options: close the "Online" type when you send the link (in the dashboard you lower the total to what has already sold and it shows as sold out), or do a second send a while later, exporting again and sending only to those who weren't on the first list. If you leave sales open during the stream, someone has to keep an eye on the dashboard and send links: decide that beforehand.

Look after the spreadsheet: it is your buyers' personal data. Use it for this event and to let them know about the next one, and don't share it.

How to keep the link from spreading

A link is easy to forward. There is no way to prevent it completely, but there are ways to shrink the problem:

  • Send it late. One or two hours before the start, not the week before. The less time the link lives before the stream, the less it travels.
  • Keep it unlisted. Set up the stream so it doesn't appear on your channel or in search engines and, if the platform allows it, with a password separate from the link.
  • If there is a recording, give it a take-down date and stick to it.
  • Don't post it on social media or in the event description, not even "in a comment".
  • Accept some leakage. Some people will watch two to a couch. If the online price is reasonable, most people would rather pay than go around asking for the link.

The door and the stream, on the same day

  • At the door, the QR code is scanned as usual. If someone arrives with an online ticket, the scanner shows the type and the door person doesn't let them in. If you want to give them the option, they can buy an in-person ticket from their phone on the event page, while there is capacity left.
  • One person for the stream who is not the door person. Besides the camera, they have to answer the "my link doesn't work" emails.
  • Test the venue's connection before the day. The stream needs a stable connection, and the scanner also uses the internet to sync tickets sold at the last minute. What happens if it drops is in access control without internet.
  • Keep the link handy to resend it to anyone who says it didn't arrive. Before sending it, look them up in the spreadsheet and confirm they bought the online ticket.

Frequently asked questions

Does ENTRÁ sell online access with a private link to the stream?

No. ENTRÁ issues tickets with QR codes for access control at the door and does not deliver stream links. You can handle it on the side: an "Online" ticket type and, before the event, you email the link to those who bought it, taking the list from the buyer spreadsheet.

How do I get the list of people who bought the online ticket?

From the event dashboard you export the buyer spreadsheet. It includes name, email, ID number (DNI) and ticket type, among other columns. Filter by the "Online" type and you have the emails to send the link to.

What happens if someone tries to get into the venue with an online ticket?

The online ticket also has a QR code, because ENTRÁ issues all tickets the same way. But the scanner shows the ticket type, so the door person sees it says "Online" and doesn't let them in. Mention it in the event description and in the ticket type name.

When is the best time to send the stream link?

Close to the start, for example one or two hours before, so it spreads as little as possible. Since online sales can stay open until the end time, do a second send for those who bought after the first one.

The venue and the stream, in the same sale

In ENTRÁ you set up an in-person type and an online type with their own price and capacity, export the spreadsheet to send the link, and the scanner shows you which ticket is coming through the door. Creating the account and loading the event costs nothing.
