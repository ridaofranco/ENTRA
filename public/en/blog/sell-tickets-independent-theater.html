Guide for organizers

Selling tickets for an independent theater play: short run, small venue and press

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

An independent play has little room for mistakes: few shows, few seats and almost no budget for promotion. What doesn't sell in the first weeks can't be recovered, because the run ends. This guide doesn't repeat the basics of loading shows and ticket types: it focuses on what happens to an independent company that rents or shares a venue.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

The short run: sell before opening night

In a run of a few weeks, sales follow a familiar shape: they start slow, rise with opening night and word of mouth, and by the time things start working only a few dates are left. That is why it pays to push sales early and not wait for the play to "catch on".

  • Open sales early. The whole cast shares the same link from day one. Family, friends and students from the cast's workshops are the first audience and the quickest to buy.
  • Watch sales show by show. If a date is coming in slow, there is still time to move it with a discount code for a specific group (a drama school, a student union) instead of lowering the price for everyone. In ENTRÁ discount codes are created from the event dashboard.
  • Plan the last week. "Final shows" works as a call to action. Have the promotional pieces ready before it arrives.

The small venue: every seat counts

In a room with few seats, the difference between a good show and a bad one is ten people. Some things worth sorting out before opening sales:

  • Count the real seats, not the ones on the plan. Subtract those with no view, the tech operator's seat if it is in the stalls and any used for the set.
  • Set aside from the start what isn't for sale. Press, programmers, the cast's families: decide how many seats per show go to comps before publishing, so you don't sell spots you later need. In ENTRÁ comps don't come out of the sales capacity but they do count toward the room capacity, so the dashboard shows you the room's real total.
  • If you do two shows on the same day, or need an exact seat cap per show, load each show as a separate event.

The deal with the venue: put it in writing before selling

Many independent companies don't rent the venue at a fixed price but share the takings with it, and each venue has its own way of doing it. There is no single rule, so what matters is that the deal is clear before the first show. Questions to ask:

  • Who sells the tickets? If the venue has its own ticketing platform or box office, you need to agree whether it coexists with the company's sales and how capacity is split between the two.
  • What amount is the venue's share calculated on? On the ticket price, on what the company actually receives, with or without comps. Don't leave it to argue about later.
  • How is each show settled? Define which number both sides look at. With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of each ticket's price (the service fee is paid by the buyer, itemized at checkout) and the dashboard shows sales in real time, so you have a clear number per show to settle accounts with the venue.
  • Who controls the door? If it is venue staff, show them the scanner before opening night: it runs in the phone's browser and there is no need to buy readers.

For the tax side of what the company receives, ask your accountant.

Students and retirees: concession tickets without headaches

A concession ticket is almost standard in independent theater, and it is also where most arguments at the door happen. Three decisions that help:

  • Put a cap on concessions. If it is a ticket type with its own capacity, you decide how many seats go at the reduced price per show and general admission doesn't get emptied.
  • Say on the flyer and in the description what card is required (student card, retiree card). ENTRÁ doesn't verify cards: the scanner shows each QR code's ticket type and that is when the person at the door asks for the card.
  • Agree on what happens if they don't bring it. If the person at the door knows the answer (paying the difference, for example), you avoid an argument with the line waiting.

The press: opening night is decided beforehand

For a company with no advertising budget, a review or an article in an outlet can move more than any post. Press isn't secured on opening night:

  • Build the media list ahead of time: critics, culture sections, theater blogs and accounts, neighborhood radio shows.
  • Send the press release with the invitation included. In ENTRÁ you load each comp with name, email, phone, ID number (DNI), ticket type, quantity and reason (press, programming, cast). It arrives by email with its QR code and you can also send it by WhatsApp.
  • Spread the press across more than one show. Not everyone can make opening night, and a press show in the second week also helps.

Public support for independent theater

In Argentina there is the Instituto Nacional del Teatro (INT, the National Theater Institute), created by Argentina's National Theater Law 24.800 "as the governing body for the promotion and support of theater activity". According to its website, it prioritizes independent, community and amateur theater, and as of October 29, 2026 it publishes open calls all year round, among them "Producción de Obra" (play production) and "Gestión de Espacios Teatrales" (theater venue management). The terms, amounts and requirements of each line are on its calls page: read them there before putting together the play's budget.

Frequently asked questions

How do I sell tickets for an independent play with only a few shows?

Open sales early, share a single link across the whole cast and watch sales show by show. In ENTRÁ you load the play with its show dates and ticket types with price and capacity, and buyers don't need to create an account.

How do I set up concession tickets for students and retirees?

With a separate ticket type, with its own price and its own capacity per event. ENTRÁ doesn't verify cards: the scanner shows the ticket type and the card is checked at the door, so say on the flyer which card is needed.

How do I organize things with the venue if we share the takings?

Put in writing before selling who sells, what amount the venue's share is calculated on and how each show is settled. With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of each ticket's price and the dashboard shows sales in real time to close out each show.

Is there government support for independent theater?

Yes. Argentina's Instituto Nacional del Teatro, created by Law 24.800, publishes calls such as "Producción de Obra" (play production) and "Gestión de Espacios Teatrales" (theater venue management). The terms and requirements of each are on its official website.
