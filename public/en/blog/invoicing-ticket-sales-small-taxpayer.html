How to invoice ticket sales for an event if you are a monotributista in Argentina

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 7 min read

You sold tickets, the money came in and now the question nobody answered at the start shows up: how do I invoice this? Here is what ARCA (Argentina's tax agency) says, with the sources at the bottom, and what you should take to your accountant already sorted out. It is not tax advice: it is the map so you don't go in blind.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). This guide is about Argentine tax rules (ARCA, the national tax agency formerly known as AFIP, and the monotributo, a simplified tax regime for small taxpayers). It is not tax advice: check your own case with your accountant.

First: what ARCA asks of you as a monotributista

The basic rule is written in the official monotributo help pages: monotributistas in every category must issue type "C" electronic invoices. The only exception it mentions is exports, where type "E" applies. There is no type A or B invoice for you, and no paper: it is an electronic type C invoice.

To issue it you need two things that are done only once:

  • A point of sale. It is a 4 or 5 digit code that identifies the method you will use to invoice. It is registered with your tax password (clave fiscal) in the Registro Único Tributario (single tax registry) service, where you also choose the invoicing system. Each method has its own point of sale.
  • A system to issue invoices. ARCA offers several: the Facturador (facturador.afip.gob.ar), which works from a computer or phone and is designed for all monotributistas; Comprobantes en línea (online receipts), the clave fiscal service that issues invoices, credit notes and debit notes; the Facturador móvil, an Android only app; and the fiscal controller, an approved device that makes no sense for a small event.

If you already invoice for another activity, check that your point of sale and your registered activity work for this. If it is your first time charging for an event, do it with time to spare: the day you open the presale is not the day to fight with your tax password.

Which money is yours and which isn't

Before invoicing you need to be clear about how much came in and whose it is. At ENTRÁ the model is simple: the organizer receives 100% of the price they set for each ticket, and the service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible at checkout before paying.

In practice that means that in your dashboard sales are measured on the price of your tickets, not on what each person paid in total. That extra charge the buyer sees belongs to the ticketing service and payment processing, not to your event. How each part is treated in your accounts is something to confirm with your accountant, but the starting number has to be this one: ticket price times quantity sold, excluding complimentary tickets, which were not charged.

If at some point you use another ticketing platform, ask this before anything else: if the fee is deducted from you, what reaches you is less than what you sold and the math changes. We explain it in how to choose a ticketing platform.

How to get organized to invoice without going crazy

The most repeated question is whether you need one invoice for each ticket sold. ARCA doesn't answer it on the monotributo help page, and it depends on how you sell and on your situation. Don't decide it on your own: ask your accountant with this data in hand, which is what they will ask you for:

  • How many tickets you sold, of which type and at what price. It comes from your event dashboard, where you see sales in real time: who bought, when and what.
  • How many were complimentary. The dashboard separates them from purchased tickets, so they don't get mixed in with sales.
  • Whether there were refunds. A refunded ticket is not income, and if you had already invoiced it you may need a credit note (Comprobantes en línea issues them).
  • The exported buyer list. ENTRÁ lets you export it at no extra cost.

With that at hand, the conversation with your accountant takes ten minutes, not an afternoon.

Watch your category: one event can move you

This is the part that surprises first time organizers most. The monotributo is categorized by annual gross income, as well as floor area, energy use and rent. According to the ARCA table in force since 08/01/2026, category A goes up to ARS 12,009,410.45 of annual gross income and K, the highest, up to ARS 126,610,838.75.

An event that sells well can push you up a category in one go. And recategorization is not optional: it is done every year in February and August. If you don't recategorize or do it wrong, ARCA can do it automatically when it detects that your purchases, expenses or bank deposits exceed your category's cap. It notifies you in your electronic tax address (Domicilio Fiscal Electrónico) and you have 15 days to appeal.

In plain terms: if the event money lands in your account, ARCA sees it. Invoice it, add it to your income and check before the next recategorization whether you are still in the same category.

If you organize with someone else

It happens a lot: two friends throw a party and the money goes into one person's account. For ARCA the income belongs to whoever received it. Before opening sales, decide who loads the event, which account the money goes into and who invoices. Fixing it afterwards, with the money already split, is much harder. The same applies if the event is run by a club, an association or a company: then it is no longer your monotributo, and it has to be checked with whoever handles their books.

The checklist before opening the presale

  • Tax password working and point of sale registered.
  • Invoicing system chosen: Facturador, Comprobantes en línea or the app.
  • A rough idea of how much you will invoice with the event and whether that changes your category.
  • Agreed with your accountant how you will invoice tickets and how complimentary tickets and refunds are handled.
  • If there are several of you, written down who collects and who invoices.

Frequently asked questions

What invoice does a monotributista issue for ticket sales?

An electronic type C invoice. ARCA's official help says monotributistas in every category must issue type C electronic invoices, except for export operations, where type E applies. To issue it you need a point of sale registered with your tax password (clave fiscal).

Which system can I use to issue the invoice as a monotributista?

ARCA offers the Facturador (facturador.afip.gob.ar), which works from a computer or phone, the Comprobantes en línea (online receipts) service with your tax password, which also issues credit and debit notes, and the Facturador móvil, an Android only app. Each method has its own point of sale.

Can an event make me change monotributo category?

Yes. The category is defined, among other parameters, by annual gross income, and recategorization is mandatory in February and August. If you don't recategorize, ARCA can do it automatically when your purchases, expenses or bank deposits exceed your category's cap.

If I sell with ENTRÁ, how much of what the buyer pays is mine?

The organizer receives 100% of the price they set for each ticket. The service and processing fees are paid by the buyer separately, with the breakdown visible at checkout. How each part is recorded in your accounts is worth confirming with your accountant.
