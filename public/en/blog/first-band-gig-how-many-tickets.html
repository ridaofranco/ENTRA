Your band's first headline show: how many people you will really bring

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Playing as a support act or on a shared bill is one thing. Doing your first headline show, with your name at the top of the flyer and the venue rental on you, is another: that is when the number of people you bring stops being a feeling and turns into money. This guide is for estimating that number before you commit, choosing a venue that fits it and setting up sales so the presale tells you in time how it is going.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Your real network: the list with first and last names

The most common mistake at a first show is counting followers. Followers, "interested" clicks on an event and "of course I'm going" messages don't pay for tickets until they do. The number that matters comes from a list:

  • Each band member makes their own. Family, friends, coworkers or classmates, students if someone teaches. With first and last names, not "the football group".
  • Mark three columns. "Definitely coming and paying", "might come" and "said yes but never comes". Be tough on the third: everyone has one.
  • Count only the first column as your base. The second is what you can gain by working the sales. The third doesn't count.
  • Remove duplicates. If two members listed the same friend, they count once.

The sum of everyone's first columns is your real base. It is smaller than you expected, and that's fine: it is the number that protects you.

What the other band and the venue add

If you share the night with another band, their network adds up, but only if they commit to moving it. Talk about it before closing the deal: will they share the link? Will they sell with their own code so you know how many they brought? Count what the other band says they will bring the same way you counted yours, with their own list. Some venues have their own crowd who come for the place; ask the manager, but don't add it to your base until you have seen it.

The right size venue

With your real base in hand, only then look for a venue. The idea is for your base to fill a good part of the room and for what you add by working the sales to complete it:

  • A small packed venue sells the next show. The photo with people pressed against the stage does more for the second show than any post. A big venue half empty feels like a failure even if you brought more people.
  • Look at the total cost, not just the rental. Rental or bar minimum, sound, technician, flyers, moving gear. Some venues charge a flat fee, others keep a share of the ticket or ask for a minimum crowd: get it in writing before confirming.
  • Ask for the licensed capacity. The number of people the venue can take is a non negotiable limit, and it is your sales ceiling.

Setting up sales

With the venue booked, sales take a few steps. These are the decisions that weigh most at a first show:

  • A price your network will pay without thinking. At a first show your audience is people who know you. An affordable, easy to remember price helps more than squeezing every ticket.
  • A cheaper presale with a cap. A "preventa" (presale) ticket type with its own price and capacity, then general admission. The price difference is what makes your network buy now and not "next week".
  • One link for everyone. Nobody sells by hand or collects bank transfers to their own account. If you want to know who moved each sale, create a discount code per band member from the event dashboard.
  • Few complimentary tickets. The sound engineer, someone taking photos, the venue's person. Each invitation is a ticket that doesn't add to the math, and in ENTRÁ they count toward capacity even though they don't come out of the sales capacity.

The presale as a thermometer

The presale is for selling cheaper, and also for measuring. Open it early and follow it with your list next to you:

  • Cross check sales against the list. The dashboard shows who bought, when and which ticket type. Mark on your list those who already bought and message the rest of the first column, one by one.
  • Watch the pace, not just the total. If in the first week almost nobody from your "definitely" list bought, the list was optimistic. Better to know early than on the night of the show.
  • Set a review date. Pick a day before the show to look at the number and decide: if it is going well, push general admission; if it is weak, go after the second column, add a friendly band or talk to the venue.

The night and the day after

At the door, each ticket has a unique QR valid only once, scanned from a phone, so someone you trust can handle check in while the band does the soundcheck. The next day, export your buyer base from the dashboard: it is the audience for your second show, made of people who already paid once to see you (take care of it: it is personal data). Compare how many came with what you estimated on the list and you will have a much more accurate number for next time.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know how many people will come to my band's first show?

Make a list with first and last names of people who might come, member by member, and mark only those who would confirm by paying. That number is your real base; followers and "interested" clicks don't count until they buy.

What size venue is best for a first show?

One where your real base fills a good part of the room. A small packed venue sells the next show better than a big half empty one, and leaves you fewer costs to cover.

What is the presale for at a first show?

Measuring. What sells in the presale, added to your list of confirmed people, tells you whether the show is going well or needs a push. In the ENTRÁ dashboard you see sales in real time and by ticket type.

How do we split sales among band members?

With one link for everyone and a different discount code per member if you want to know who moved each sale. That way no cash changes hands and there is a single total.
