Guide for organizers

How many tickets to sell based on the venue's licensed capacity

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

The most expensive mistake at a small party or show is not selling too little: it is selling more than the venue can hold. You end up with paying ticket holders on the sidewalk, arguments at the door and, if there is an inspection, a serious problem for you and the venue. Here is how to go from the licensed capacity to a number of tickets, with what the City of Buenos Aires rules say and what you need to subtract before opening sales.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). The rules quoted here are from the City of Buenos Aires; elsewhere, ask the venue and the local authority.

You don't decide the capacity: the license does

Every licensed venue has a maximum authorized capacity. It does not come from dividing the square meters by a number by eye. In Buenos Aires city rules, the principle is written like this for dance venues operating as a complementary activity (for example, inside a bar or restaurant): the authorized capacity is the lowest figure that results from considering three things.

  • The occupancy factor: how many people the floor area allows under the Building Code.
  • The means of egress: how many people can get out through the venue's doors and corridors.
  • The complementary facilities, like restrooms, which are assessed against the total population.

The idea to take away for any venue is the same: a large hall with a single narrow exit can have a low capacity. That is why the number that counts is the one on the license, not the one you calculate.

Ask for the number before signing

Before closing the rental, ask the venue in writing for:

  • The maximum licensed capacity and what activity it is licensed for (a party hall is not the same as a theater or a dance venue).
  • A copy or the number of the current license.
  • How they count staff: whether the capacity they give you is only for the audience or includes people working.
  • Which areas can be used: if a mezzanine, a patio or a terrace is not licensed for the public, don't count it.

If the event will exceed a thousand attendees, there is a separate procedure on top of this: the mass event permit. We explain it in what permit an event needs in Buenos Aires city.

From capacity to tickets on sale: the math

With the venue's number in hand, the quantity that goes on sale is built by subtracting, not adding:

  • Start with the licensed capacity for the public.
  • Subtract staff if the venue tells you they count within that capacity: bar, security, technical crew, production.
  • Subtract artists and the artists' guests.
  • Subtract the complimentary tickets you plan to give: press, sponsors, friends. To decide how many, see how many complimentary tickets to give at an event.
  • Leave a margin for door sales, if you will sell there.

What remains is the total number of tickets on sale. Then you split it between ticket types (general, advance, VIP) and tiers. The total of all types combined can never exceed that number. If you are unsure how to split into tiers, it is in how to price tickets and set up tiers.

How it is loaded in ENTRÁ

At ENTRÁ capacity is loaded per ticket type when you create the event: each type has its own number available, and the form won't let you publish if any type has no capacity. What is worth knowing:

  • The sum of the capacities is your total. If you load 300 general and 50 VIP, you are selling 350. Do the math from the previous section before splitting.
  • Each purchase takes from that type's capacity. When a type reaches zero, it shows as sold out on the event page and cannot be chosen.
  • When every type reaches zero, the whole event shows as sold out and stops selling. No overselling in a last minute rush.
  • In a single purchase up to 4 tickets of each type can be bought, never more than what is left.
  • In the dashboard you follow sales in real time, so you see how far you are from the limit.

On the day: making the math hold at the door

Capacity protects online sales, but the limit is looked after inside. Some simple things:

  • Check every ticket at the door with the scanner: each QR is valid once, so a shared screenshot doesn't add one more person. More in access control on the day of the event.
  • If you sell at the door, stop when you reach the total. The number you worked out before opening sales is the same for the box office.
  • Don't reopen capacity "because there's room". Looking empty at eleven doesn't change the license.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell as many tickets as the venue's square meters allow?

No. Capacity is set by the venue's license, not by your own square meter calculation. Under Buenos Aires city rules, the authorized capacity is the lowest of what the floor area, the exits and the venue's facilities allow, so square meters alone are not enough.

Where do I find out the licensed capacity of a hall or club?

Ask the owner or whoever rents you the venue in writing, together with the current license. It is the information the venue must give you before you sign, and if the event exceeds a thousand attendees you will also need it for the mass event permit.

Do complimentary tickets and staff count toward capacity?

Everyone inside takes up space, whether they have a paid ticket, an invitation or a work credential. The prudent thing is to subtract guests, artists and crew before deciding how many tickets go on sale, and to confirm with the venue how each group is counted.

What happens in ENTRÁ when the whole capacity sells?

Each ticket type has its own capacity. When a type reaches zero it shows as sold out and can no longer be chosen, and when every type reaches zero the whole event shows as sold out and stops selling.
