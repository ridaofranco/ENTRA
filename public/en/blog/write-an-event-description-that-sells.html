Guide for organizers

What to write on your event page so people buy

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

People land on the event page from a flyer or a post, and within a minute they decide whether to buy or leave. What stops them is almost never the price: it is a question the page doesn't answer. This guide goes over what the page needs to say, in what order, and which ENTRÁ fields already take care of it for you.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

What the page already shows

In ENTRÁ, when you load the event you fill in the title, the event type, the flyer, the date and time (with an optional end time), the venue name, the city or area, the ticket types with their price and capacity, and the description. The page shows all of that and also gives the buyer a button to add the event to their calendar.

That means the description doesn't need to repeat the date and venue as if they were news. It needs to answer everything else. The description keeps line breaks, so use them: short blocks, one per topic.

The title and the flyer

  • The title says what it is. "Jazz night in Palermo" is clear; a made up name on its own is not. If the event has its own name, add what it is.
  • The flyer is read small. Most people will see it on their phone. The name, date and venue should be readable without zooming in.

What to write in the description

Think of it in this order, which is the order in which someone about to buy asks themselves the questions:

  • What it is, in two lines. What is going to happen and why it is worth it. No superlatives: "three local rock bands, closing with a DJ until 4 a.m." says more than "the best night of the year".
  • Who it is for. Whether there is a minimum age, whether it is for beginners or experienced people, whether you can bring kids.
  • Schedule. What time doors open, what time the main part starts, what time it ends. Whoever shows up at 9 p.m. for a show that starts at 11 is going to complain.
  • What the ticket includes. Whether there is a drink, materials, a T shirt, parking. And what it doesn't include, if someone might assume it does.
  • What sets each ticket type apart. If you have "General" and "VIP", explain what each one gets. If you have a presale, say how long it lasts.
  • How to get there. Landmarks, public transport, whether there is parking, which door to use if the venue has more than one.
  • What to bring. The QR on the phone and, if you are going to ask for it, an ID (DNI, the Argentine national ID).

The questions people always ask

If three people message you with the same question, the answer goes in the description. The usual ones:

  • Can I buy at the door? Is it more expensive?
  • Is there a cloakroom? Can I bring food, mate (the Argentine herbal drink) or equipment?
  • Is it accessible for people with reduced mobility?
  • Can I pass my ticket on to someone else? If you have transfers enabled, say so: the official transfer invalidates the old QR and issues a new one for the person who receives it.

If you need a piece of information from each buyer (a size, a company name, a preference), ENTRÁ lets you add extra questions that the buyer fills in when buying. Use them only for what you will actually use: every extra field is one more reason to abandon the purchase.

What happens if it is called off

This is the paragraph almost nobody writes and the one that saves the most complaints. Before selling, decide and write down:

  • If it is rescheduled, the ticket is valid for the new date. What option people who can't make it have.
  • If it is cancelled, what your refund policy is and through which channel it is requested.
  • If it is outdoors, what happens if it rains, with the backup date and the time the call is made.
  • A contact channel you will actually check.

If you need to change something later, the description can be edited from the dashboard. But what was written when people bought is what people read.

Before publishing, read it as a buyer

Open the page on your phone as if you knew nothing about the event, and check whether in a minute you understand what it is, when, where, how much it costs and what happens if something changes. If not, fix it before promoting it. At ENTRÁ, on top of that, every event goes through a review before it is published. Everything else you need to have ready is in the checklist for organizing your first event.

Frequently asked questions

What should an event description say?

What it is in two lines, who it is for, the schedule, what each ticket type includes, how to get there and what to bring. Also the questions people always ask you and what happens if the event is called off or rescheduled.

Do I have to repeat the date and venue in the description?

No need. At ENTRÁ the page already shows the date, time, venue and city you loaded, and it has a button to add the event to the calendar. The description is for everything else, for example what time doors open and what time the show starts.

Can I ask buyers for extra information?

Yes. When loading the event you can add questions the buyer fills in when buying, like a size. Ask only for what you will use, because every extra field makes the purchase longer.

A clear page sells on its own

With ENTRÁ you upload the flyer, ticket types, description and extra buyer questions, and every event is reviewed before it goes live. Creating the account and loading the event costs nothing.
