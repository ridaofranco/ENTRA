Guide for organizers

Board game or role playing night at a bar: places per table, drinks and tournament

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

A board game or role playing night looks easy to set up: a bar, a few boxes, people who want to play. But it has its own snags. Tables have a fixed number of chairs, a role playing session can't take extra people, the bar wants to sell and, if there is a tournament, players pay something different from those who come to play casually. This guide sorts all that out before you publish the link.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Capacity comes from the tables, not the room

At a game night, the number of people is not set by how many fit standing, but by how many can sit down and play. A quick card game works with four to six people per table; a role playing session, with the game master plus four or five players. Before loading the event:

  • Count tables and chairs with the room set up, not the capacity on the license.
  • Ask the bar how many people the venue allows and never go over it, not even with guests.
  • Save places for the people explaining games and for those running sessions. In ENTRÁ complimentary tickets don't come out of the sales allocation, but they do count toward the total capacity shown in the dashboard. If you have forty chairs and will give out four invitations, sell thirty six.

Table by table or general

There are two ways to sell places, and the choice depends on the kind of night:

  • General ticket with a total capacity, for open play: people arrive, pick a game from the library and tables form on the spot.
  • One ticket type per table, when each table is a different session. For example "Mesa de rol: campaña de terror · 5 lugares" (role playing table: horror campaign, 5 places) or "Mesa de estrategia · 4 lugares" (strategy table, 4 places). Each type has its own name, price and capacity, and when one table fills up, the others stay on sale.

ENTRÁ has no table map or numbered seats on a floor plan, so the table is chosen through the ticket type, not a drawing of the room. With eight or ten tables the list still reads well; with more, it is better to group by game style ("Rol", "Estrategia", "Juegos rápidos") and assign on the spot.

One detail: each purchase allows up to four tickets per type. For a group of friends that is enough, but if a group of five wants a whole table, one person buys four and another buys the fifth, or two people buy separately. Mention it in the description so nobody gets stuck.

Ticket with a drink included

At most bars the game night exists because the bar sells. There are two ways to charge for that:

  • Ticket only, plus a minimum spend at the bar, stated on the page. The bar charges it and the bar controls it.
  • Ticket with a drink included, as a separate ticket type: "Juego libre + bebida" (open play plus a drink), "Mesa de rol + pinta" (role playing table plus a pint). The name has to say what is included, because it is what the person sees when buying and what the door sees when scanning.

The ENTRÁ QR is good for one use only and is used at the door: the bar can't scan it again. What the scanner does show is the ticket type, so at the door you hand out a token or a wristband that is later exchanged at the bar. Agree beforehand with the bar which part of each ticket goes to them. The full setup is in ticket with a drink or combo included.

Tournament with separate registration

Many nights combine open play with a tournament: collectible cards, a strategy game or a classic game. Those competing pay a registration fee and those who come to play casually pay something else. To keep them apart:

  • A ticket type "Inscripción torneo" (tournament registration), with its own price and a capacity equal to the number of players in the bracket. If the bracket is sixteen, the capacity is sixteen and sales close automatically.
  • Another type "Juego libre" (open play) with the rest of the room. That way people who don't compete don't take tournament places.
  • A required extra question for the tournament details: username, team or deck. Extra questions are answered once per purchase, not per ticket. If someone buys two registrations for themselves and a friend, ask for both names in the same question.

Put the format, start time and whether there is a prize in the description. If you want to see how a tournament with a bracket and capacity is set up, it is in gaming tournament or video game event.

With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket. The service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

A weekly series, one event per date

Game nights are usually fixed: every Tuesday, or the first Friday of each month. The tidy approach is one event per date, not one event with several days inside it. Each date has its own link, capacity, attendee list and door, and if one week sells out, the next stays on sale.

There is another practical reason: the scanner rejects tickets from another event. If someone arrives on Tuesday with the ticket for the following Tuesday, the screen marks it red and the door doesn't have to guess. ENTRÁ also allows one QR per day within the same event, but that is chosen once when creating the event and is meant for consecutive multi-day events, like a festival. For a weekly series, one date per event is still the clearest.

To grow the series, use your buyer base: the sales export includes each buyer's email, and with that you announce the next date before posting it on social media. The door, on the night

People arrive in waves, but it is still worth keeping the door organized:

  • One person fixed at the door, not the one explaining games. Scanning is done from the phone's browser: green with the name and ticket type, red with the reason.
  • The ticket type tells you which table each person goes to. If you sold by table, the door sends the person straight to their place.
  • If someone can't find the email, they are looked up by name, email or ID number (DNI). If the bar's signal drops, entries are stored on the phone.
  • Whoever arrives without a ticket buys from their phone, without creating an account. ENTRÁ has no box office and does not record cash payments: put up a sign with the QR of the event link, away from the scanner.

The event page sells until the end time you loaded, and if you didn't load one, until three hours after the start.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell places at a specific table?

ENTRÁ has no table map. If there are few tables, create one ticket type per table, for example "Mesa de rol · 5 lugares", with that table's number of chairs as capacity. If there are many, sell a general ticket with a total capacity and form the tables on the spot.

How do I charge the tournament registration separately from the ticket?

Create a ticket type "Inscripción torneo" with its own price and player capacity, separate from the open play ticket. With a required extra question you ask for the team name or username, and the answer is stored in the attendee list.

Should a weekly series be a single event?

It is not a good idea. The tidy approach is one event per date: each Thursday with its own link, capacity and list. That way a date that sells out doesn't hold back the others, and the scanner rejects a ticket from another week, because each QR belongs to its own event.
