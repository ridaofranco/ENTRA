Guide for organizers

How to sell tickets for your event with Mercado Pago

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Almost everyone who buys a ticket in Argentina already has Mercado Pago (the country's most widely used online payment app) on their phone. The problem isn't getting paid: it is what happens between someone paying and that person holding a valid ticket. If you handle that stretch by hand, checking receipts and building lists, that is where it breaks. This guide explains how that stretch works when you sell with ENTRÁ.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First: connect your Mercado Pago account

In the ENTRÁ dashboard there is a button to connect your Mercado Pago. It takes you to Mercado Pago, you authorize the connection with your account and you return to the dashboard with a notice that it is connected. From that moment on, sales from your events go straight into your account.

Two details worth knowing:

  • Nobody can connect an account in your name. The system checks your session before starting the connection and signs that data, so the account is tied to your organizer user.
  • Your credentials are not exposed. The key Mercado Pago provides is stored only on the server side; from the dashboard you only see whether you are connected.

ENTRÁ's service fee is paid by the buyer, visible at checkout, and Mercado Pago splits it off automatically at the moment of payment. You receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket.

What happens when someone buys

The flow, step by step, is this:

  • They choose their tickets on the event page and enter their details.
  • The server recalculates everything. Price, discounts and capacity are checked again against what you loaded. The number sent by the browser is never used, so nobody can pay less by editing the page.
  • The order is created as pending. There is no ticket yet and the capacity hasn't been touched.
  • They pay on Mercado Pago. The buyer goes to Mercado Pago's checkout and pays there. On the card statement, the charge shows as "ENTRA TICKETS".
  • Mercado Pago reports the payment. The system doesn't just take the notice at face value: it asks Mercado Pago for the real status of that payment.
  • If it is approved, the tickets are issued. The amount paid is compared with the order total and, if they match, in a single operation the capacity is deducted, the order is marked as paid and the tickets are issued with their QR codes. The email goes out before the process is considered finished.

The underlying rule is simple: there is no ticket without an approved payment, and no approved payment is left without a ticket.

If the payment is pending

Some payment methods aren't approved instantly. When that happens, the buyer comes back to ENTRÁ and sees the notice "Pago en proceso" (payment in progress), with a message telling them that Mercado Pago is still processing the payment and that, once it is approved, the tickets will arrive by email automatically. They don't have to buy again or let you know.

While the payment is pending, the ticket is not issued and the capacity is not deducted. Keep that in mind if you are close to selling out: the spot is taken when the payment is approved, not when the person taps "pay".

Sometimes Mercado Pago's notice doesn't arrive. For that there is a backup path: an automatic task checks every few minutes for orders that are still pending, asks Mercado Pago whether they were paid and, if they are approved, issues the tickets with the same amount check. Orders that have had no approved payment after three days stop being checked.

If the payment is rejected

When the rejection is final, the buyer gets a single email telling them the payment didn't go through, so they can try again. With a pending payment that email never goes out, because telling someone "you didn't pay" when they did is worse than saying nothing.

When you get the money

When each sale becomes available in your account is not decided by ENTRÁ: it depends on Mercado Pago, on your account and on the method each person paid with. We are not going to promise you a timeframe here. Check it in your Mercado Pago account before committing payments to suppliers with the money from sales, especially if the event is soon.

The alternative: selling by bank transfer

Many people start out taking payment by bank transfer to their alias (the short name Argentine accounts use to receive transfers) and writing names on a spreadsheet. For a small event among people you know, it works. What changes when the event grows:

  • You confirm each payment yourself. You look at receipts one by one, and an edited screenshot looks a lot like a real one.
  • The list is your only control. At the door people are looked up by name, with a line and arguments.
  • You don't know how much you sold until you add it all up by hand.

And whichever way you get paid, what you need to invoice is a separate matter: see how to invoice ticket sales as a small taxpayer in Argentina and ask your accountant.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell tickets for my event with Mercado Pago?

Yes. On ENTRÁ the buyer chooses their tickets, pays at Mercado Pago's checkout and, once the payment is approved, receives their ticket with a QR code by email. If you connected your Mercado Pago account from the dashboard, the sale goes straight into your account.

When does the buyer receive the ticket?

Only when Mercado Pago confirms the payment is approved. At that point the system checks that the amount paid matches the order, deducts the capacity and issues the tickets, which arrive by email and are stored in the buyer's profile.

What happens if the payment is pending?

The ticket isn't issued yet and the buyer sees a notice that Mercado Pago is processing the payment. When it is approved, the ticket goes out by email on its own. On top of that, an automatic task checks again with Mercado Pago for orders that are still pending, in case the payment notice didn't arrive.

Is it better to take payment by bank transfer to an alias?

For a small event among people you know, it may be enough. The problem appears when it grows: you have to check every receipt by hand, build the list and control it at the door. With integrated payment, the payment, the ticket and the check-in stay linked without you touching anything.

Get paid with Mercado Pago, without checking receipts

You connect your account from the dashboard, sales go straight in and every approved payment automatically becomes a ticket with a QR code. Creating the account and loading the event costs nothing.
