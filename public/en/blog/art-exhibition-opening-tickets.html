Art exhibition opening or vernissage: tickets, reservations and guests

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 6 min read

An opening has an odd problem: it is almost always free and it almost always fills up. The gallery is small, people all arrive at the same time, and between artists, collectors, press and friends of friends nobody knows how many will be standing in front of the works at eight in the evening. This guide puts the decisions in order before you open registration: free or paid, cap, time slots, guests and the welcome drink.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Free with reservation or a paid ticket

Most openings are open and free of charge. That doesn't mean you shouldn't ask for anything. There are three possible formats, and it is best to choose one before publishing:

  • Open with no registration. It works if the space is large and you are not worried about it filling up. The problem is you don't know how many people are coming or who came.
  • Free with reservation. People sign up, receive a QR and you know how many to expect. It is the most useful format for a gallery: you control the cap and you keep the list of who came.
  • Paid ticket. It makes sense at an art fair, at an exhibition with an activity (a tour with the artist, a performance, a talk) or when the ticket includes something, like a drink or the catalog.

If you choose a free reservation, in ENTRÁ you turn on the free event option when creating it. Prices are set to zero, the page shows "reservá tu lugar" (reserve your spot) instead of "comprar" (buy), and the reservation costs nothing for the person signing up: there is no service charge and it does not go through Mercado Pago. It asks for name, email and ID number (DNI), phone is optional, and there is no need to create an account.

How many people fit at once

At an exhibition the number that matters is not how many pass through during the whole evening, but how many can be inside at the same time without anyone being unable to see the works. Before setting the cap, look at:

  • The capacity of the space, requested in writing from the gallery or cultural center.
  • What the works take up: sculptures on the floor, installations, pedestals. Each one takes away room to move around.
  • The bar and the drinks area, which is where the bottleneck forms.
  • Whether there is a sidewalk or patio where people stay chatting. That takes pressure off the room.

One clarification: the ENTRÁ scanner records who goes in, not who goes out. It has no real-time counter of people inside. If the room can reach its limit, put someone with a manual counter at the exit or split the evening into time slots.

Cap by time slot

If you expect more people than fit at once, split the evening. ENTRÁ has no time-slot feature with a calendar, but each ticket type has its own name, price and cap, and when one fills up nobody else can choose it. With that you build the slots:

  • "7 to 8 pm", cap 60.
  • "8 to 9 pm", cap 60.
  • "9 to 10 pm", cap 60.

Put the time at the start of the name: it is the first thing people reserving read and the first thing the door sees when scanning. Keep in mind the scanner shows the ticket type but does not reject anyone for arriving in another slot. That is up to whoever is on the door. Write the rule in the description ("Your reservation is valid for the time you chose; if you arrive early, you wait outside") and decide beforehand what you do with latecomers. How to set this up in more detail is in guided tour with a cap per time slot, which works the same way.

With a free reservation, some people sign up and don't come. ENTRÁ automatically sends a reminder email the day before to each person who reserved, as long as the date is entered. If you want to release a few more reservations than the cap to compensate, only do it if there is real room to spare.

The guest list: named complimentary tickets

At an opening there are always people who shouldn't have to reserve: the artist and their family, collectors, curators, press, the people from the space. If you write them on a paper list, at the door you don't know who came, and if someone passed their name to someone else, you never find out.

In ENTRÁ invitations are generated as complimentary tickets from the event dashboard. For each one you enter name and email (required), phone and ID number if you want, the ticket type, the quantity and the reason ("Artist", "Press", "Collector"). Each guest receives their QR by email, and if you entered the phone you can also send it by WhatsApp from the dashboard. Complimentary tickets don't come out of the public reservation cap, but they do count toward the event's total capacity, so you don't go over the room's limit without noticing.

A practical tip: set up a "Guests" ticket type separate from the public and use it for complimentary tickets. At the door the scanner shows that name, and later, in the exported list, each ticket appears with its origin ("Compra", purchase, or "Cortesía", complimentary). That way you know how many guests really came. How many to give and how to hand them out is in complimentary tickets and invitations: how many to give and how to control them.

Ticket with a drink

The welcome drink is part of the custom, and it is where most money goes uncontrolled. If you want to include it, there are two ways:

  • A drink for everyone, at no extra charge. Calculate drinks by the total cap (reservations plus guests), not by how many you think will come.
  • A ticket type with a drink, for example "Ticket + glass of wine", paid, alongside a free or cheaper "General admission". Each type with its own cap, and the drink one with the number of glasses you have.

The scanner shows the type of each QR, so the door can hand over the token or wristband at that moment, to be exchanged later at the bar. More ideas on how to set it up in tickets that include a drink. If the ticket is paid, with ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set; the service and processing charges are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

A multi-day art fair

A fair with many booths and several days is set up differently. You can load it as a multi-day event and choose whether the QR is valid for every day (once per day) or whether the buyer chooses which days they attend. Gallerists and their teams are not the public: give them complimentary tickets with the reason "Exhibitor", so they go through the scanner without mixing with sales. What to say on the page

  • Who is exhibiting and what is on show, with an image of a work and a short curatorial text.
  • Whether it is free with reservation or paid, and what the ticket includes.
  • Opening hours and, if there are slots, the rule for each one.
  • Until when the exhibition runs and what hours it can be visited afterwards, for anyone who doesn't get a spot on opening night.
  • Whether it is accessible: stairs, elevator, restroom.

The door

  • One person dedicated to receiving people, not the one from the space who has to attend to buyers of the works.
  • The QR is scanned from the phone's browser: green with the name and ticket type, or red with the reason. Each QR works only once.
  • Anyone who can't find the email is looked up by last name, ID number or email from the scanner.
  • Anyone who arrives without a reservation can reserve from their phone on the event page, if there are spots left.

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth asking for reservations for a free opening?

Yes, if the room has a limit on people or you want to know how many are coming. In ENTRÁ you mark the event as free, people reserve without paying with name, email and ID number, receive their QR by email and the cap closes on its own when it fills up.

How do I set up time slots for an exhibition?

ENTRÁ has no time slots as a separate feature. It is solved with one ticket type per time, for example "7 to 8 pm" and "8 to 9 pm", each with its cap. The scanner shows the type, but it does not reject someone who arrives in another slot: that is up to the door.

How do I invite artists, collectors and press without them taking up public reservations?

With complimentary tickets from the event dashboard: you enter name, email, ticket type, quantity and reason, and each guest receives their QR. Complimentary tickets don't come out of the reservation cap, but they do count toward the event's total capacity.

Can I sell a ticket that includes a drink?

Yes, as a ticket type with that detail in the name, for example "Ticket + glass of wine". The scanner shows that name when validating the QR, so the door knows who gets the token or wristband for the bar.
