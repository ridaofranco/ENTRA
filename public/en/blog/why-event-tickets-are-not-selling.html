Why your tickets aren't selling: what to check first

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 7 min read

You published the event, shared the link and the counter isn't moving. Before cutting the price or paying for ads, it is worth knowing where the sale breaks down: whether people don't reach the page, reach it and leave, or want to buy and something stops them. Each case is fixed differently. This guide goes in order, from what you can check in two minutes to what takes more work.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First, the numbers: what the dashboard tells you

In the event dashboard you have three things that help with the diagnosis:

  • The breakdown by ticket type: how many of each type you sold out of the total and what percentage that is. If one type sells and another doesn't, you already have a clue.
  • Recent activity: the latest purchases with their dates. Look at when the last one was. If there were sales the first two days and then nothing, promotion died out; if there were none from the start, the problem is something else.
  • Where sales came from: each sale records the source of the link the buyer came in through (Instagram, WhatsApp, Google, direct). You see how many sales and how much money each one brought.

For that last block to tell you something useful, the link you share has to be tagged. If you post in a story and in a pinned Instagram post, both will show up as "instagram". To separate them, add something like this to the link for each piece: ?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=story-friday. With a different name per post you know which one sold and which didn't.

What the dashboard doesn't show is page visits. You get that from the network where you posted: the story's stats tell you how many people tapped the link. The math is simple:

  • Few clicks: people aren't arriving. The problem is promotion, the flyer or the link.
  • Lots of clicks and few sales: people arrive and leave. The problem is on the page, the price or the date.
  • People message you saying they can't buy: something technical or a setting is wrong. Start there, it is the quickest to fix.

Make sure people can buy: the technical side

Open the link from a phone that isn't yours, not signed in, and try to get as far as payment. Check:

  • That the event is published. Every event goes through a review before going on sale. In the dashboard, above the title, it has to say "Publicado" (published).
  • Which types show before and after launching the official sale. At ENTRÁ, ticket types whose name includes "preventa", "anticipada", "promo", "early" or "lote" (Spanish words for presale, advance and batch) are shown while you haven't launched the official sale. When you press Lanzar Venta Oficial (launch official sale), those are hidden and the others appear. If all your types are called "Anticipada" and you launched the sale, the page is left with nothing to buy. Check the names before pressing the button.
  • That there is room left. If you lowered a type's total to close it, it is now sold out. Check in the breakdown that the type you want to sell has spots available.
  • The end time. The page sells until the end time you entered. If you didn't enter one, it sells until three hours after the start and then considers the event over. For most events that is enough, but if you run a six hour party and want to sell to the line at three in the morning, enter the end time. And the other way around: if you entered the start date wrong (a month or a year off), the event may show as past and not sell.

Make sure the link is shared properly

The link is the channel. Three common mistakes:

  • Sharing it before loading the flyer. When you paste the link into WhatsApp or Instagram, the preview shows the event's name, date and flyer. If you shared it without a flyer, some apps keep that old version for quite a while. Load the flyer first and only then promote.
  • Sending the dashboard link instead of the public page. The dashboard is yours; the buyer needs the event page. Test it from another phone before sending it to a group.
  • Using link shorteners or bridge pages. Each hop is a place where people drop off, and if the shortener doesn't keep the utm parameters, the dashboard doesn't know which channel the sale came from. Share the official address, with utm if you want to measure.

Another classic: the link is in the bio but the story flyer doesn't say it's there. If there is no concrete call to action ("tickets at the link in bio"), many people won't look for it.

The event page

If people arrive and leave, it is almost always a question the page doesn't answer. The page already shows date, time, venue, city and prices. The description has to answer the rest:

  • What it is, in two lines. Who is playing or giving the talk, what is going to happen. A catchy name on its own doesn't sell.
  • What time doors open and what time the main part starts.
  • What each ticket type includes. If you have "General" and "VIP" and don't explain the difference, people hesitate and buy neither.
  • What happens if it is called off and whether the ticket can be passed to someone else.

A test that works: send the link to someone who knows nothing about the event and ask them what they understood. Whatever they can't answer is what's missing. The full guide is in what to write on the event page so people buy.

The flyer

The flyer is the first thing people see, and they almost always see it small, in a story or a group. If there is no flyer, the link preview looks poor and so does the page. If there is one, check that the name, date and venue can be read without zooming in. A pretty flyer that doesn't say when the event is won't sell tickets. More in the flyer that sells.

The price

Price is rarely the first problem, but sometimes it is. Some signs:

  • There are clicks, the page is clear and nobody buys. Compare with what similar events in your city charge, not with what you'd like to charge.
  • The advance ticket costs the same as at the door. Then nobody has a reason to buy early. Say in your promotion that it costs more at the door.
  • One price for everything. A cheaper first tier with a short cap gives a reason to buy now.

Prices and caps can be changed from the dashboard while the event is on sale. If you are going to lower the price, think about those who already paid more: it usually works better to add a new type (a tier, a 2 for 1 for groups) than to lower the one you already sold. The date

Sometimes the date works against you and there isn't much to do except know it: a long weekend when people travel, an important match, another similar event the same night, the end of the month. If you still have time, look at the calendar before publishing. If it is already published, focus on the audience that is there: those who already bought from you for other dates.

The opposite also happens: you published well in advance and people don't buy because it's still a long way off. It is normal for a good share of sales to come in the last few days. It is not a sign that things are going badly; it is a sign that you need to show up again close to the date.

What to change first

With all of the above, this is the order we recommend, from cheapest to most expensive:

  • 1. Test the purchase from another phone. Two minutes. If something is broken (no visible types, sold out, the event shows as past), nothing else matters.
  • 2. Fix the link and the flyer. Load the flyer, share the right link with utm per piece, put the call to buy in the story.
  • 3. Complete the description. The three questions people asked you most by message go there.
  • 4. Look at the source of sales again after two or three days. Reinforce the channel that brings sales and stop spending energy on the one that doesn't.
  • 5. Only then, touch the price. With a new tier or a group type, rather than a general discount.

If you have buyers from previous dates, export the attendee list from the dashboard and let them know. They are the people most likely to buy, because they already paid you once.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know where my event's sales come from?

In the event dashboard, in the "De dónde vinieron las ventas" (where sales came from) block, you see how many sales and how much money each source brought, based on the link each buyer came in through. To separate two posts from the same channel, add utm parameters to the link, for example ?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=story-friday.

Does the ENTRÁ dashboard show how many visits the event page had?

No. The dashboard shows sales, ticket types sold and the source of each sale, but not visits. You see clicks in the stats of the network where you posted, for example taps on the link in an Instagram story. If there are lots of clicks and few sales, the problem is on the page or the price; if there are few clicks, it is in the promotion.

Until when does the event page sell?

Until the end time you entered. If you didn't enter an end time, ENTRÁ keeps selling until three hours after the start and then considers the event finished. If your event lasts longer, enter the end time so sales don't stop early.
