Event in a small town in Argentina: how selling works differently

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

Putting on an event in a small city or a town in the Argentine provinces (what Argentines call "el interior") is not a scaled down version of doing it in a big city. There are fewer people, but they know each other better; there are fewer media outlets, but everyone listens to the ones there are; and the decision to go is often made the same week, or the same night. This guide is for selling by those rules: which channels move sales, when online sales make sense, what happens at the door and what to check with the municipality.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Word of mouth rules

In a small town, the question that decides the sale is "who's going?". If people know their friends are going, they buy. To make that work in your favor:

  • Bring in people with their own network. The club, the dance school, the local opening band, the graduating class group. Each one brings their own people and spreads the word.
  • Make the momentum visible. Posting that the first tier is running out, or which groups already bought (with their permission), answers the question of who's going.
  • Avoid dates that clash with something else. In a small place, a big wedding, the town festival or an important match takes your audience. Ask around before choosing the date.

Local radio, shops and posters

Social media helps, but in many small towns the promotion that reaches everyone still goes through other channels:

  • Local radio. A short interview or an ad on the morning show reaches people who don't follow you anywhere. Bring the information ready: date, venue, price and how to buy.
  • Shops. The corner store, the bakery, the bar, the gas station. A poster with a QR that leads to the purchase link works all day.
  • WhatsApp groups for the town, the club or the school, with a short message and the link.

Make everything point to the same sales link. If each channel sends people somewhere different, people get lost and you lose count.

When online sales make sense

In many towns the habit is paying at the door, and that doesn't change overnight. Online sales still add up, for several reasons:

  • You know how many people are coming before the date. With part of it sold in advance you can decide how much to buy for the bar or how many staff to call. The dashboard shows sales in real time.
  • The out of town crowd. Whoever travels from the neighboring town wants their ticket secured before setting out.
  • Less cash at the door. Less money in hand, less change and less risk at the end of the night.
  • A cheaper presale gives people a reason to buy early instead of "seeing how it goes that day". How to stagger prices is in how to price your tickets and set up tiers.

The buyer doesn't need to create an account and the ticket arrives by email with its QR. If some people aren't comfortable buying online, help them with a simple message explaining how, or have someone on the team help them buy from their phone.

The door: where half the night is sold

In a small town, many people decide at the last minute. The door needs as much thought as the presale:

  • Online sales open during the event. ENTRÁ has no box office and does not record cash, but you can leave sales open so people buy from their phone in the line, with the same link.
  • A sign with the link's QR at the entrance, where the line can see it.
  • Scanning from your phone. Each ticket has a unique QR valid only once, and checking is done from the phone's browser without buying scanners.
  • Signal at the venue. Test how the internet works at the door beforehand. What happens with check in if it goes down is in access control without internet.

The municipality: ask before selling

Each municipality has its own rules for events: which permits it requires, how far in advance, what inspections it carries out and what it demands depending on the type of event and the venue. There is no answer that works for everyone, so:

  • Ask at the municipality well in advance, before opening sales, what you need for your event.
  • Ask the venue what it is licensed for and for how many people. That number is your sales ceiling.
  • Get it in writing, with the name of the person who helped you and the date.

Everything else you need to sort out before a first date is in the first event checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth selling tickets online in a small town?

It is, to know how many people are coming before the date, for the audience arriving from other towns and to avoid handling cash. It works best combined with the usual promotion: radio, shops and word of mouth, all pointing to the same link.

What permit do I need to hold an event in my town?

It depends on each municipality and the type of event, so there is no general answer. Ask at the municipality well in advance, before opening sales, and also ask what the venue is licensed for.

Can I sell at the door with ENTRÁ?
