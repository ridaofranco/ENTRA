Event re-entry: can you leave and come back in with a QR ticket

By SOMOS DER · November 14, 2026 · 6 min read

Not with the QR alone. On ENTRÁ each QR code is valid only once: the second time it goes through the scanner it is rejected, and that is exactly what stops one screenshot from letting two people in. If you want people to be able to leave and come back, re-entry is handled separately, with a wristband or a stamp, and announced before you sell.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). This guide describes how the door works with ENTRÁ tickets.

Why the QR code doesn't work for coming back in

A ticket QR code works like a key you use once. When the door scans it, the ticket is marked as used. If someone tries the same code again, the ENTRÁ scanner shows «DENEGADO · YA UTILIZADO» (denied, already used), with the buyer's name, the ticket type and how long ago they entered.

If the QR let people in twice, anyone could send a photo of their ticket to a friend, go in, and the friend would get in later with the same image. The one-use rule is what makes a ticket impossible to copy. That's why re-entry isn't solved by loosening the QR: it's solved with a different mark the person carries on them.

First decide: is there re-entry or not?

Both options are fine. What doesn't work is not deciding and letting the door improvise at two in the morning. A few questions to help you choose:

  • Do people need to step out? To smoke, go to the car, meet someone, eat at a stand outside. If so, without re-entry you'll have arguments at the door all night.
  • Do you want to stop entry at a certain time? Common at parties and clubs. If there is re-entry, decide whether someone who steps out after that time can come back.
  • Does the venue have a single doorway? If entrance and exit are the same door, re-entry adds people to the same bottleneck. Plan one more person at the door.
  • Is there an age or alcohol check? If the event is 18+, the wristband can also show that the person already showed their ID.

How to set up re-entry with wristbands or stamps

The simplest system is the classic one: the QR controls the first entry, and the wristband or stamp controls the next ones.

  • Wristband at the same step as the scan. The person shows the QR, gets the green light, you put on the wristband and they go in. If wristbands are handed out at another table, someone will skip it.
  • Wristbands that can't be taken off and put back on, the kind that lock once. One color per night or per date, so last week's wristband is useless.
  • A stamp if the budget is tight, with ink that doesn't rub off with a sweaty hand. Pick a design that is hard to copy with a pen.
  • A different color per ticket type if there are sections (for example general and VIP), so nobody switches section on the second entry.
  • Count the wristbands before opening. If at the end of the night far more are missing than people who came in, someone is giving them away.

What to announce in advance, so there are no arguments at the door

The rule has to be written where people read it before buying, and repeated at the venue:

  • In the event description, one clear line: «Re-entry with wristband until 3 am» or «No re-entry». If you're not sure where to put it, see how to write an event description that sells.
  • On a sign at the exit, not only at the entrance. The person leaving is the one who needs to know.
  • To the door team, before opening: what to do if someone arrives without a wristband saying they already went in. The scanner helps: if the QR shows «already used» and says they entered an hour ago, it's probably that person; if it says they entered two minutes ago and the person just arrived, that's a different story.

Multi-day events

At a festival or a series with several dates, a multi-day ticket is scanned once per day: the first scan of each day is accepted and a second scan that same day shows «YA INGRESÓ HOY» (already entered today). So the QR handles entry for each day, and leaving and coming back within the same day works like a single-date event, with a wristband or stamp in that day's color. If you're choosing between a pass and day tickets, see festival pass or day ticket.

If you bought the ticket

  • Before you step out, ask at the door whether there is re-entry and how it works. Being let out doesn't mean being let back in.
  • Don't send a screenshot of your ticket to anyone while you're inside. If someone uses it, the scanner will reject it when you show it again, and what counts is the wristband.
  • If you can't go and want someone else to, don't send them a photo: use the official ticket transfer, which invalidates the old QR and generates a new one for the person who receives it.

Frequently asked questions

Can I leave an event and come back in with the same QR ticket?

Not with the QR alone: on ENTRÁ each QR is valid only once and if it goes through the scanner again it is rejected. If the organizer allows re-entry, they handle it with a wristband or a stamp you get at your first entry. Before stepping out, ask at the door how it works at that event.

What does the door staff see when they scan a QR that already went in?

The ENTRÁ scanner shows «DENEGADO · YA UTILIZADO» (denied, already used) with the name, the ticket type and how long ago the person entered. With that, the door can tell whether it's someone who stepped out for a while or a copy of the ticket, and decide according to the event's rule.

Should a small event allow re-entry?

It depends on the venue and the crowd. If people need to step out to smoke, go to the parking lot or buy something outside, no re-entry means arguments at the door. If everything is inside and you want to control entry until a certain time, you can skip it, as long as you say so on the event page.

How does entry work at a multi-day event?

On ENTRÁ a multi-day ticket is scanned once per day: the first scan each day is accepted and a second one that same day shows «YA INGRESÓ HOY» (already entered today). To leave and come back within the same day, the same applies as for a one-day event: wristband or stamp.
