Ticketed pool party or country house party: how to organize and sell it

By SOMOS DER · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

A daytime party at a quinta (a country house on the outskirts of town, usually rented for parties) with a pool sells well in summer, but it adds risks a function hall doesn't have: water, heat, alcohol, a place that isn't always designed to receive the public and a trip there and back. This is what you should sort out before selling the first ticket.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First, the venue: can you do it there?

Renting a quinta for a family birthday and selling tickets to the public are two different things, and the rules for events at country houses change from one municipality to another. There is no single answer for the whole country, so:

  • Check with your municipality what permit applies to an event with an audience and paid tickets at that address, how far in advance and what paperwork they ask for.
  • Ask the owner in writing what the property is authorized for. If they are not sure, don't sell yet.
  • Ask about insurance and what it covers; if there is none, talk to an insurance broker about liability insurance for the event.

If the event is in the City of Buenos Aires (CABA), the reference is the permit to hold an event in CABA.

Capacity: the number comes from the venue, not from wishful thinking

At a quinta the number isn't set by the garden alone: it is also set by the bathrooms, the parking, the emergency exit and how many people you can look after in the water. If the municipality or the permit gives you a capacity, that is your ceiling; if nobody gives you a number in writing, check with your municipality before setting it yourself.

Once the number is defined, load it as the capacity: in ENTRÁ each ticket type has its name, price and capacity, and it closes on its own when it fills up. Comps also take up spots. The full reasoning is in how many tickets to sell based on authorized capacity.

The pool: lifeguards and clear rules

This is the most underestimated point. In the province of Buenos Aires there is a specific law, Law 14.798:

  • It covers public or private pools, for profit or not (article 1, section g), and says every aquatic setting in the province must have a lifeguard service (article 2).
  • The exception is for pools at private homes used exclusively by the family (article 5, section a). A party with tickets sold is not family use.
  • The minimum is two lifeguards per hundred people or fraction thereof at pools and aquatic settings (article 19, section c), and the law makes clear these numbers are minimums.
  • Each lifeguard's shift is six straight hours (article 16). If the party lasts longer, plan a relief or close the pool earlier.
  • The lifeguard must be certified, with their logbook and the annual proficiency test (article 6). Ask them to show it to you.

Outside the province of Buenos Aires, check with your province and municipality what is required. And beyond the law, write these rules on the page and repeat them at the venue:

  • Pool hours, shorter than the party's. When the sun goes down or the lifeguard leaves, the pool closes.
  • No glass near the water. Plastic cups all over the property.
  • No diving if the pool isn't deep enough for it, and mark where it is shallow.
  • Someone from security at the edge to stop anyone who goes in after too much alcohol: the lifeguard looks after the water.

Alcohol and age

Argentina's Law 24.788 prohibits selling alcoholic drinks to anyone under 18 throughout the country, wherever they are sold. If there is a bar, the simplest thing is to make the event 18+ only, say so before selling and check ID numbers (DNI) at the door (how, in 18+ only events). With heat and alcohol, make sure there is drinking water and shade.

Schedules and neighbors

A pool party is during the day, and that works in your favor. Even so, time and noise limits for events are set by each municipality: check with yours how late you can have music and at what volume. On the page, write the opening time, the pool closing time and the end time. If you play music, also check what to pay for music rights (in Argentina, SADAIC).

Transport: getting there and, above all, getting back

Country houses are usually far from public transport, and the trip back after an afternoon of sun and drinks is the most delicate moment. If you offer transport:

  • Sell it as a separate ticket type, for example "Ticket + transport", with a capacity equal to the seats you hired. When it fills up, it closes on its own.
  • Write the meeting point and the departure and return times in the description.
  • Hire a licensed transport company and ask for its paperwork in writing.
  • If there is no transport, explain how to get there and where to park, and recommend going home with someone who hasn't been drinking.

Plan B: rain, storms or extreme heat

Decide before selling what happens if it rains, and write it on the page:

  • If there is a covered area for the full capacity, the party goes on and the pool closes during a thunderstorm.
  • If there is no cover, set an alternative date and what time the day before you will let people know.
  • If you reschedule, in ENTRÁ the ticket stays valid for the new date and anyone who can't make it can transfer it. If you cancel, a notice email goes out to everyone who bought.

The door

At a quinta the door is usually a gate with poor signal: test the scanner on site before the day and have a backup network. Each QR is valid only once, so a forwarded screenshot only works for whoever arrives first.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need lifeguards for a ticketed pool party in the province of Buenos Aires?

The province's Law 14.798 requires a lifeguard service at public or private pools, for profit or not, and only exempts pools at private homes used exclusively by the family. A party with tickets sold is not family use, so have certified lifeguards and confirm the scope with your municipality.

How many lifeguards does the Buenos Aires province law require at a pool?

Article 19 of Law 14.798 sets a minimum of two lifeguards per hundred people or fraction thereof at pools and aquatic settings. Since each lifeguard's shift is six straight hours, a long party needs a relief.

Can I hold a ticketed party at any country house?

Not necessarily: selling tickets to the public is not the same as a family celebration, and the rules change depending on the municipality. Before selling, check with your municipality what permit applies and ask the owner in writing what the property is authorized for.

What happens with the tickets if it rains and I move the party to another date?

In ENTRÁ, if the event is rescheduled, the ticket stays valid for the new date, and anyone who can't make it can transfer it. If you cancel it, a notice email goes out to everyone who bought. The key thing is to have the rain rule written on the page before selling.
