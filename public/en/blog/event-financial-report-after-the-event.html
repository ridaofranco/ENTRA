The post-event review: what to check the next day

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

The day after an event, all you want is to sleep. But it is the only moment when everything is fresh: who came, what failed at the door, which post sold and how much was really left. If you leave it for next week, you will plan the next date from memory. This guide is the review in order, with what ENTRÁ shows you and what you have to write down yourself.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First: download both spreadsheets

The event dashboard in ENTRÁ gives you two different exports, and you need both for the review:

  • The attendee list, one row per ticket: name, email, ID number (DNI), phone, ticket type, origin (purchase or complimentary), price, ticket code, purchase date, check-in, whether it was transferred and the answers to any extra questions you added at checkout.
  • The sales spreadsheet, one row per order: buyer, details of the purchase, quantity, subtotal, fee, total, date, status, payment method, source and campaign.

Download them the same day and save them in a folder named after the event and its date. They are the record of this date and the basis for the next one. Which other numbers to ask a ticketing platform for is in what data a ticketing platform should give you.

How many bought and how many came in

It is the first number you should look at, and almost nobody does. The event dashboard shows total check-ins, the attendees who went through the door. In the attendee list, the Check-in column marks each ticket as Ingresó (entered) or Pendiente (pending). After the event, a pending ticket belongs to someone who bought and did not come.

Do the math separating complimentary tickets, which are marked in the Origen (origin) column:

  • Tickets sold and how many came in.
  • Complimentary tickets given and how many were used. If you gave away 40 and 12 came in, next time give fewer or give them to other people.
  • The difference, as a number and a percentage. That percentage is your no-show rate, and it helps for the next date: if part of your buyers always fails to show up, you know how many people you will really have inside, how much drink to buy and how many people to put behind the bar.

Also look at the Transferido (transferred) column. If lots of tickets changed hands, people bought for others, which tells you that next time you can offer group tickets. And go over the night at the door while you still remember: at what time the crowd peaked, how long the line took, whether someone could not find their ticket. What is worth reviewing about entry is in access control on the day of the event.

Where sales came from

The event dashboard has a block called De dónde vinieron las ventas (where sales came from). It groups sales by the link each buyer arrived through and shows what share of the money came from each source. If a sale has no data, it is counted as "sin dato" (no data).

What you can separate depends on what you did beforehand:

  • If you shared the plain link, the dashboard infers the network the visit came from when it can. You will see, for example, Instagram against WhatsApp, but not which Instagram story sold the most.
  • If you used utm parameters, for example ?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=story-friday, each post shows up separately. The sales spreadsheet carries that data in the Origen and Campaña (source and campaign) columns, order by order.

With the sales spreadsheet open, sort by date and see which days sold the most. There are almost always peaks: the day you opened sales, the day the tier changed, the two or three days before the event. Write down what you posted at each peak. That is what you repeat next time. If you did not use utm this time, start next time: it is explained in how to promote your event and track where sales come from.

Mercado Pago payouts

If you connected your Mercado Pago account (the Argentine payment platform) from the dashboard, each sale went straight into your account. The service charge was paid by the buyer and Mercado Pago split it off at the time of payment, so what you should find in your account is the price of each ticket.

To close the books:

  • Add up the Subtotal column of the sales spreadsheet, only for orders with status "Completado" (completed). That is the total for tickets sold.
  • Compare it with what you see in Mercado Pago. If something does not add up, look for the order by buyer email or by date on both sides.
  • Check when the money becomes available. ENTRÁ does not decide that: it depends on Mercado Pago, on your account and on how each person paid. Before promising a payment date to a band or a supplier, check it in your account.

If you did not connect your Mercado Pago, sales were collected through ENTRÁ's account: write to us to coordinate. The payment flow, step by step, is in how to sell tickets with Mercado Pago.

With ticket sales closed, put together the night's result: what came in from tickets, plus what the bar or canteen made if it was yours, minus everything you paid (venue, artists, sound, security, promotion, staff). Whatever you need to invoice is a separate topic; the basics are in how to invoice ticket sales as a small taxpayer in Argentina, and check it with your accountant.

What to write down for the next date

One page, the same day, with these points. It does not need to be neat, it needs to exist:

  • Numbers: sold, complimentary, came in, no-shows, total collected, costs, result.
  • Price and tiers: which tier sold out and when, which was left with unsold tickets, whether the price jump slowed sales.
  • Promotion: which posts sold according to the dashboard, and what you did that sold nothing.
  • Times: when most people arrived, when they left. If online sales stopped earlier than you wanted, check the end time: if the event has no end time loaded, ENTRÁ treats it as finished three hours after the start and stops online sales. Next time, load the real time.
  • Door: how many people you put there, whether it was enough, which questions kept coming up.
  • Venue and suppliers: what worked, what did not, who you will work with again and who you won't.
  • What people told you: comments, messages, complaints.

Save it with both spreadsheets. When you plan the next date, it is the first thing you open.

Thank the people who came

A thank you message the next day is the cheapest thing you can do for your next date. Some ways to do it:

  • On social media: a story or a post with photos of the night, tagging the artists and the venue. People who went see themselves and share, and people who didn't see what they missed.
  • By email or message, only to those who agreed to receive news. ENTRÁ does not send mass messages to attendees from the dashboard: you send the email yourself, with the exported list. Someone giving you their email to receive a ticket does not mean they want your news. The clean way is to have asked at checkout, with an extra question that then shows up as a column in the export.
  • Different for each group: to those who came, thanks and photos; to those who bought and did not come, a "we missed you, here is the next one".

Frequently asked questions

How do I know how many people bought and did not come to my event?

Export the attendee list from the event dashboard in ENTRÁ. The "Check-in" column marks each ticket as "Ingresó" (entered) or "Pendiente" (pending). After the event, the pending ones belong to people who bought and did not go through the door. The "Origen" column separates purchases from complimentary tickets.

Where can I see which post the sales came from?

In the event dashboard, in the "De dónde vinieron las ventas" (where sales came from) block, grouped by source. The sales export also includes the "Origen" and "Campaña" columns for each order. To tell two posts on the same channel apart, the links need to have carried different utm parameters.

When is the money from sales available?

If you connected your Mercado Pago account, sales went straight into your account, and when each one becomes available depends on Mercado Pago, on your account and on how each person paid. Check it in your Mercado Pago account before committing to payments.
