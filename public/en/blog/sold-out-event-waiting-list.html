Guide for organizers

Sold out tickets: another tier, transfers and a waiting list

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Selling out is what every organizer wants, and it is also when the most messages arrive: people left out, people who bought and can't go, and someone offering tickets at an inflated price. This guide sorts out what to check, when to open more spots, how to pass tickets from hand to hand without screenshots and how to run a waiting list even if the ticketing platform doesn't have one.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First, confirm it really is sold out

Before announcing "sold out" and before opening anything, look at the dashboard. One ticket type may be sold out and another not, or the capacity you loaded may be lower than what actually fits in the venue. Check three things:

  • Capacity against the licensed capacity. If you loaded 200 and the venue allows 250, you have room. If you loaded exactly the venue's capacity, you don't, and it stays that way.
  • The complimentary tickets you issued. If you held spots for guests who already said they are not coming, cancel those complimentary tickets from the dashboard and the spot goes back into capacity.
  • Payments not yet credited. If someone says they paid and didn't get the ticket, the dashboard has a button to look for uncredited payments, which checks with Mercado Pago (the Argentine payment platform) and issues the paid tickets. Do this before promising spots, because those purchases do count.

Unpaid tickets don't block spots

In many sales run over WhatsApp the problem is people who reserve and don't pay: the spots stay taken and nobody knows if they are released. At ENTRÁ that doesn't happen. The ticket is issued and capacity is deducted only when the payment is confirmed; a purchase left halfway does not take up a spot. So if the dashboard says sold out, those are paid tickets or issued complimentary tickets, not phantom reservations.

If you sold part of it outside the platform, with bank transfers to your account and a list in a notebook, then you do need to set a deadline: "the reservation is held until Thursday at 8 p.m.". Whoever hasn't paid by then loses the spot, and you load that spot as new capacity on the ticketing platform.

Opening another tier or adding spots

If the venue still has room, from the dashboard you can edit prices and quantities and add new ticket types at any time. You have two options:

  • Raise the capacity of the sold out type. Quick, but whoever paid more in the last tier won't understand why there are tickets again at the same price.
  • Open a new type, for example "Última tanda" (last tier), with its own price and capacity. It is tidier: it is clear these are new spots and the price reflects that you arrived late.

Don't add spots the venue doesn't have. A sell out with people outside wanting in is good news; a venue over capacity is a problem with the venue owner, with security and with the people inside.

Opening another show or a second date

If you sold out fast and there is more demand, the real answer is another show. Before announcing it, confirm the venue, the artists and the time. Then:

  • Load it as a new event, with its own date, capacity and link. It goes through the same review before being published, usually the same day.
  • Tell the people who were left out first: those on your waiting list (below we explain how to set it up) and those who messaged you.
  • Use your buyer base. The attendee list exports as a CSV with name, email and phone, and it is yours. Those who came to the first show are the most likely to buy the second.

Transfers: tickets that change hands without resale

When an event sells out, two things show up at once: people who bought and can no longer go, and people desperate for a ticket. If there is no official route, they meet in a Facebook group with QR screenshots that may or may not work.

At ENTRÁ the official route is the transfer. The person who can't go opens Mis Tickets (my tickets) in their profile, enters the other person's name, email and phone, and that person claims it by signing in with that email. At that moment the old QR is invalidated and a new one is issued in their name. The screenshot that was going around stops working. Transfers are enabled by default when you create the event and you can turn them off if you prefer tickets to be in the buyer's name only; the step by step for buyers is in how to transfer a ticket to someone else.

The transfer doesn't charge anything or set a price: whatever they agree between themselves stays between them. That is why it helps if you organize the match between the person selling and the person looking, with the waiting list.

How to curb resale

There is no way to make sure nobody ever resells, but there is a way to make reselling not worth it and to keep resale buyers from getting scammed:

  • One QR, one ticket. Each QR is valid only once at the door. If someone sells the same screenshot to three people, the first gets in and the other two stay outside. Say it in your promotion: "buy only through the official link".
  • Official transfer, not screenshots. If someone buys second hand, they should ask for it to be transferred to their email. If the seller refuses, be suspicious.
  • Spread sales across tiers. If everything goes on sale in the same minute, whoever buys ten to resell takes a big share. With tiers or a presale for your community, that gets diluted.
  • Your own waiting list. When the person who can't go has someone to pass the ticket to at the price they paid, resale loses customers.

How to tell a real ticket from a fake one, from the buyer's side, is explained in real or fake ticket in resale.

The waiting list, set up outside the app

ENTRÁ does not currently have a waiting list inside the app. You can set one up outside in ten minutes and it works well:

  • A simple form (Google Forms or whatever you use) with name, email, phone and how many tickets they want. Put it in the event description and on your social media as soon as it sells out.
  • A clear, public rule: "if a ticket frees up or we add a show, we notify people in sign up order". No promises of a spot.
  • The person who can't go messages you, and you pass them the contact of the first person on the list. They do the official transfer and settle payment at the original price. If you'd rather not handle money between them, another option is to refund the ticket from the dashboard, return the money through Mercado Pago, and the spot goes back into capacity for the next person to buy through the link.
  • If you open a show or tier, send the link to the list first, a few hours before posting it on social media.

A detail about refunds from the dashboard: the system invalidates the ticket and restores the spot, but the money is not returned automatically. First you make the refund in Mercado Pago and then you confirm it in the dashboard.

Frequently asked questions

Does ENTRÁ have a waiting list?

Not inside the app. You set it up outside with a simple form with name, email, phone and quantity, and notify people in sign up order when a ticket frees up or another show or tier opens.

Do unpaid purchases take up spots?

No. At ENTRÁ the ticket is issued and capacity is deducted only when the payment is confirmed, so an unpaid purchase does not block spots.

How does a ticket pass from one person to another without being a screenshot?

With the official transfer from Mis Tickets: the other person claims it with their email, the old QR is invalidated and a new one is issued in their name. The organizer can turn off transfers when creating the event.

Make sold out good news

With ENTRÁ you add tiers and ticket types from the dashboard, transfers invalidate the old QR and each QR is valid only once at the door. Creating the account and loading the event costs nothing.
