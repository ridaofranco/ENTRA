Guide for organizers

What data a ticketing platform should give you to know how sales are going

By SOMOS DER · July 29, 2026 · 6 min read

There is a big difference between seeing how sales are going and knowing how they are going. A big number of tickets sold on a screen tells you nothing if you don't know against what capacity, in which tier and when. These are the data points you actually use to make decisions before the event.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

1. Sold against capacity, not just sold

"You sold 340 tickets" is not information: it is a number. "340 out of 500, 68%" is, because it tells you whether you will fill the venue and how far you are from it. At ENTRÁ the event dashboard shows it that way, with the percentage next to it, and complimentary tickets issued are counted separately so invitations don't inflate the number.

Ask any platform for this. If it shows the total sold but not against what capacity, the figure is useless for deciding anything.

2. Net revenue: what you keep

This is where most budgets break. Some platforms show the gross figure in the dashboard (what the audience paid, fees included) and the organizer builds their cash flow on that number. Then the payout arrives and money is missing.

At ENTRÁ the Ingresos (revenue) figure is your net: the sum of the prices you set, without charges and without complimentary tickets, because the buyer pays the service charge separately and you receive 100% of your price. Next to it you see how much was refunded and across how many sales. Always ask which of the two numbers you are looking at.

3. Breakdown by ticket type

The total hides the problem. What you need to see is how much each tier sold: presale, general, VIP, each with its quota, its percentage and its unit price.

That is where concrete decisions come from: if the presale sold out in three days, you are too cheap and can close it earlier. If VIP didn't move, the problem is not the price but that nobody understood what it includes. If general admission stalled, it is the marketing that has to move, not the price.

4. When each ticket was sold

The sales curve is the most predictive data point in events and the one fewest people look at. Two events with the same 340 tickets sold are different things if one sold them in the first week and the other has been dragging them along ten a day.

At ENTRÁ the dashboard shows recent activity live and the full sales history with the date of each order; you build the daily curve by exporting the sales CSV and grouping by date in a spreadsheet. It is two minutes of work and it leaves you with your own history to compare with your next event, which is what it is really for.

5. Who bought: the list is yours

The asset an event leaves you is not the takings, it is the list. At ENTRÁ you export two CSV files from the dashboard at no extra cost:

  • Attendees: name, email, ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID), phone, ticket type, origin (purchase or complimentary), price, ticket code, purchase date, whether they got in or not and whether the ticket was transferred.
  • Sales: order ID, buyer, email, details, quantity, subtotal, fee, total, date, status and payment method.

If a platform doesn't let you export that list, or charges extra for it, that list is not yours. It is the question to ask before loading your first event, not after your third.

6. The door, live

On the day of the event the data changes: what matters is no longer how much you sold but how many people got in. ENTRÁ's access control shows in real time checked in, sold and remaining, with the attendance percentage and the history of the latest entries with time and result.

That percentage is business data, not operational data: the average no-show rate of your events is what tells you how much you can oversell, how much bar stock to prepare and how many staff to put on. If you have never measured it, you are guessing.

7. Whether the ticket email arrived

An unglamorous data point that prevents real problems at the door: the dashboard has an email deliveries tab where you see, buyer by buyer, whether the ticket went out or the delivery failed. When someone writes "I paid and got nothing", instead of arguing you check whether the email went out and to which address.

What is not data

To close, three things that look nice and don't change a single decision:

  • A chart without numbers next to it. The table and the CSV rule; the chart is the illustration.
  • Vanity metrics such as page visits without the conversion to sales.
  • A dashboard that only updates when you refresh. If you are at the door with your phone, the data has to come in on its own. ENTRÁ's dashboard updates live, without reloading.

Frequently asked questions

What minimum data should a ticketing platform show me during sales?

Five things: tickets sold against total capacity, the net revenue you keep, the sales breakdown by ticket type, when each ticket was sold and the list of buyers with their contact details. With that you can decide whether to move to the next tier, step up your communication or rethink the event. Everything else is decoration.

Is the revenue figure I see what I get paid or what the audience paid?

It is the question to ask any platform before signing, because it changes the whole budget. At ENTRÁ the Ingresos (revenue) figure in the dashboard is your net: the sum of the prices you set, without charges or complimentary tickets, because the organizer receives 100% of their price and the buyer pays the service charge separately. If on another platform the number is gross, you have to subtract the fee before using it for anything.

How do I know how many tickets were sold each day?

By exporting the sales CSV from the event dashboard, which includes the date of each order. With a pivot table in Excel or Google Sheets you group by date in two minutes and you have the real sales curve. It is more work than looking at a chart, but it is the same data and you keep it to compare between events.

Can I export my buyer database?

At ENTRÁ yes, at no extra cost: the event dashboard exports two CSV files, one of attendees (name, email, ID number, phone, ticket type, price, ticket code, purchase date, whether they got in and whether it was transferred) and one of sales (order, buyer, email, details, quantity, subtotal, fee, total, date, status and payment method). If a platform doesn't let you export your list or charges extra for it, that list is not yours.
