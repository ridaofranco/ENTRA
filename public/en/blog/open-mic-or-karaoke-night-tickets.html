Guide for organizers

Open mic, karaoke or jam: tickets, pay what you want and sign ups

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

An open mic night has two audiences in the same room: the people who come to watch and the people who come to get up on stage. Karaoke, the Tuesday jam and the stand up open mic share the same problem: charging little, knowing how many slots there are and having the night repeat every week without rebuilding everything. This guide goes step by step.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Three formats, the same logic

  • Open mic: musicians, poets or comedians who sign up for a short slot, with a host keeping order.
  • Karaoke: people pick a song and sing. There are usually more slots and shorter ones, and whoever sings is also audience.
  • Jam: a house band or a group of regular musicians, and others who get up to play with them. You need to know which instruments are coming.

In all three there is an audience that pays to be there and a group that also wants to get on stage. If you separate those two things from the start, sales sort themselves out.

Cheap ticket or pay what you want

These are low price nights, and there are two ways to charge for them:

  • A cheap general ticket, at a fixed price. It gives you a revenue floor and you know how much is coming in before opening. If you want to reward early buyers, use an advance ticket cheaper than the door price.
  • Pay what you want (in Argentina, "a la gorra", passing the hat), where everyone gives what they like at the end. It lowers the barrier to coming, but the takings depend on the night.

ENTRÁ does not charge open amounts: each ticket type has a fixed price. If you want to pass the hat, the tidy way is to load the event as free with reservation. The reservation costs ARS 0 for whoever signs up, with no service fee, and each person gets their QR by email. You have the capacity, the list of who is coming and control at the door, and you pass the hat at the venue, in cash or with a payment alias for bank transfers.

A middle option: a cheap general ticket and a separate hat for the house band musicians. Say so on the page so nobody feels charged twice.

If you charge for tickets, with ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set. Service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

The sign up cap: a "performer" ticket type

The classic open mic problem is the list: more people sign up than fit in the night, or they sign up by chat and then don't show. With ENTRÁ you solve it with a separate ticket type. Each type has its own name, price and capacity, and closes on its own when full.

  • "Público" (audience): people who come to watch, with the room's capacity.
  • "Participante: me anoto para subir" (performer: I'm signing up to go on): with a capacity equal to the number of slots that fit in the night.

To work out the performer capacity, start from time: if the stage is open for two hours and each slot lasts ten minutes including changeover, twelve fit. Leave one or two slots free for the host or the night's guest.

Some decisions worth making before publishing:

  • Performer price. It can be the same as the audience or lower. If the event is free with reservation, both types cost ARS 0 and the cap still does the job.
  • What it includes. Write it in the name or the description: how many minutes, how many songs, whether there is a backline or everyone brings their own instrument.
  • The slot order. ENTRÁ does not assign slots. The simplest way is to export the attendee list as a CSV from the dashboard before the night, filter by ticket type and set the order there: by sign up order, by style or by a draw at the venue. Say on the page which one you will use.
  • For a jam, you can split by instrument: "Participante: batería" (drums), "Participante: voz" (vocals), each with its own cap. That way you don't end up with six guitarists and nobody on bass.

At the door, the scanner shows each QR's ticket type. When a performer comes through, whoever is scanning already knows they are there to go on and can send them to the host to confirm their slot.

Load the regulars (the host, the house band, the closing comedian) as complimentary tickets from the dashboard, with name and reason, so they don't take performer spots and stay separate from sales.

People who sign up and don't come

It happens mostly when signing up is free. So you don't end up with a gap in the list:

  • Keep a waiting list at the venue: whoever arrives without a slot signs up with the host and goes on if someone is missing.
  • Ask performers to be on time: "performers on the list confirm their slot with the host by 9:30 p.m.".
  • A low price for performers, even a symbolic one, helps make sure whoever signs up shows up.

If the event is free, ENTRÁ sends a reminder email the day before to each person who reserved. The rest about no shows is in free event: how to stop people reserving and not coming.

The weekly series

An open mic lives on repetition: "Thursday karaoke", "Tuesday jam". For people to make it a habit:

  • One event per date. Each week has its own page, its own audience and performer capacity, and its own list. That way one week doesn't mix with the next and you see how each night sold in the dashboard.
  • Same day, same venue, same time. Every change has to be explained.
  • Open the next date the day after. The performer who went on this week is the first who wants to sign up for the next one.
  • Use your buyer base. You export it from the dashboard and it is yours: tell people who already came about the next date before posting on social media. With a discount code with a usage cap and expiry date you can give regulars priority.

Setting up a series on a fixed date is covered in monthly music or stand up series; the same applies to a weekly one, at a faster pace.

Music and the venue

Karaoke uses recorded backing tracks and at an open mic people play songs by other writers. Before starting the series, find out what has to be paid for the music rights (in Argentina, SADAIC) and who pays it, you or the bar. What the law says and what to ask is in SADAIC and music at an event. If the night is at a bar, the rest of the deal with the venue is in selling tickets to play at a bar.

The door

  • The QR is scanned from a phone. Each QR is valid only once and belongs to that date: a ticket from another week won't get through.
  • If someone can't find the email, they are looked up by last name, ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID) or email from the scanner.
  • Whoever arrives without a ticket can buy or reserve from their phone on the event page, while it stays open.
  • The performer list printed or on the host's phone, with the slot order.

Frequently asked questions

How do I set up a capped sign up list to get on stage?

With a separate ticket type, for example "Participante: me anoto para subir", with a capacity equal to the number of slots in the night. When it fills up, it closes on its own. The scanner shows the ticket type, so the door knows who is coming to play or sing.

Can I do pay what you want with ENTRÁ?

ENTRÁ does not charge open amounts. What you can do is load the event as free with reservation, to have the capacity, the list and each person's QR, and pass the hat at the venue, in cash or with a payment alias for transfers.

How do I load an open mic that happens every week?

One event per date. Each week has its own page, its own audience and performer capacity, and its own list. Open sales for the next date the day after the previous one and tell your buyer base first.
