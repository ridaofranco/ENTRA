Guide for organizers

Film or short film festival: passes, screenings and reservations

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

An independent film or short film festival works differently from a one-off screening: several days, several screenings per day, a loyal audience that wants to see everything and another that only comes for a friend's film. On top of that there are usually free screenings and a call for filmmakers that runs on a separate track.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First, what you have: a festival or a series

If what you are putting together is one screening a week with a talk afterwards, that is a series and it is explained in film and discussion nights or ticketed screenings. This guide is for several days in a row, with a schedule of screenings and an audience that moves between them. At a festival you have to decide whether you sell the whole festival, each screening or both.

The three ways to sell a festival

In ENTRÁ, when an event lasts several days, you choose the entry mode once, and that choice applies to every ticket for that event:

  • Pass: one QR for the whole event. It is paid once and the person gets in with the same code each day. It is the natural choice for whoever comes to everything.
  • Day ticket: one QR per day. The buyer chooses which days they attend, pays the one-day price for each day chosen and receives a QR for each date. The scanner rejects a QR from another day.
  • One event per screening. Each screening, or each block of shorts, is a separate event with its own link, cap and list. It is the way to sell "the 8 pm screening on Saturday" rather than "Saturday".

What ENTRÁ does not have is a time slot or session as a separate screening inside the same event: the day yes, the screening no. If your schedule has three screenings a day in the same room, and each one fills up separately, the only way to control the cap per screening is to load each one as an event. The full comparison between pass and day ticket is in festival pass or day ticket.

A pass for everything and single tickets per screening

The combination most used at small festivals is a pass for those who come to everything and a single ticket for those who pick one screening. It can't be done inside the same event, because there is only one mode. The setup that works is this:

  • A "Festival pass" event with the one-QR-for-the-whole-event mode, from the first day to the last, with its price and cap.
  • One event per screening with its date, time and cap of single tickets.
  • A cap planned as a whole. If the room has 120 seats and you sell 50 passes, each single screening has at most 70 spots, minus guests. ENTRÁ does not cross caps between different events: you do that math yourself before loading.

At the door, each phone works with the event it has selected. If someone shows a QR from another event, the scanner rejects it as "pertenece a otro evento" (belongs to another event). For a screening, that means two checks: one that validates the pass and another that validates the ticket for that screening, or a single station that switches events depending on what it is shown. With separate lines ("Passes" and "Tickets") the door moves faster.

A note about the pass: the scanner lets it through once per day. If a pass holder leaves between screenings and comes back the same day, it will show "ya ingresó hoy" (already entered today). At a festival with several screenings a day, say how re-entry works: a wristband or stamp at first entry, and after that they show that.

If you prefer one QR per day

When all the screenings of a day share the room and the audience, selling by day is simpler than one event per screening. But keep in mind how the cap is counted: it is set per ticket type for the whole event, and a three-day purchase takes one spot, the same as a one-day purchase. If Saturday is the big day, it can sell out while Thursday still has room. If you need an exact cap per day, load each day as an event.

Another detail: the automatic reminder goes out the day before the first day. If someone bought only Sunday, send them a notice yourself closer to their date.

Free screenings with reservation

Many festivals have free screenings: the opening, a showcase of film schools, a screening backed by the city. Free does not mean uncontrolled. If the room has a fixed number of seats, it is best for people to reserve:

  • A separate event marked as free, with the room's cap. In ENTRÁ free or paid is chosen per event, so the free screening can't be inside the paid event.
  • Up to 4 spots per ticket type in each reservation, the same as a purchase. Each person receives their QR by email and the door scans it like any ticket.
  • A release time written on the page: "your reservation is held until 15 minutes before the screening; after that, seats go first come, first served." More people miss free screenings than paid ones.

How much to overbook and how to run a waiting line is in free event: people reserve and don't show up.

Filmmakers, jury and press

Selected filmmakers, the jury and the press get in without paying. In ENTRÁ that is handled with complimentary tickets: invitations sent by email with their QR that don't come out of the sales cap, although they do count toward the total capacity in the dashboard. The practical move is to give each selected filmmaker a complimentary pass plus one or two spots for the screening where their work is shown. How many to give without emptying the room is in complimentary tickets and invitations: how many to give.

Film submissions go through a separate channel

The call for filmmakers is a different process, with a different audience and different deadlines. If you charge a submission fee, you can set up a separate event ("2027 Call for entries") with a ticket type called "Inscripción de obra" (work submission) and required extra questions: title, running time, screener link. Two things to keep in mind:

  • Extra questions are answered once per purchase, not per submission. If someone sends three shorts in the same purchase, they leave a single title. Ask for one purchase per work.
  • ENTRÁ does not receive files. The material goes by link (Vimeo, Drive) or through the submissions platform you use.

If submissions are free or you need a long form with technical details, use a separate form and keep ENTRÁ for the audience.

The door between screenings

There are usually fifteen minutes between one screening and the next. Open the door for the next one when the room is empty. Scanning is done from the phone's browser: green with the name and ticket type, red with the reason. ENTRÁ has no seat map or box office: seating is first come, first served, and anyone who arrives without a ticket buys from their phone with the QR of the link posted in the line.

With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket. The service and processing charges are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell a pass and tickets per screening in the same event?

No. In ENTRÁ the entry mode (one QR for the whole event or one QR per day) is chosen once per event and applies to all its tickets. If you want both, set up one event for the pass and one event per screening, each with its own link and cap.

How do I set up a free screening with limited seats?

Load it as a separate event marked as free, with the room's seat count as the cap. People reserve without paying, receive their QR by email and at the door it is scanned just like a paid ticket. It is a good idea to release the seats of anyone who has not arrived by a fixed time.

Can I charge the submission fee for shorts with ENTRÁ?

You can set it up as a separate event with a ticket type called "Inscripción de obra" (work submission) and extra questions for the title and screener link, but the questions are answered once per purchase. If each filmmaker sends a single work it works; if they send several, it is better to ask for one purchase per work or use a separate form for the material.
