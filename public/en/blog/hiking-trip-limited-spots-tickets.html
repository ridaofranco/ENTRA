Guide for organizers

Trekking, horseback ride or day trip with limited spots: meeting point, health and weather

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

A trekking outing, a horseback ride or a day trip sells with few spots, depends on the weather and involves people who need to be on time at a meeting point. Before publishing the link, it pays to have sorted out who is going, what they bring, who to call if something happens and what happens if it rains.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

The guide sets the cap, not the van

On a mountain outing the real limit is not how many seats the transport has, but how many people one guide can safely lead on that trail. On a horseback ride it is even clearer: the cap is the number of gentle horses available that day. Before loading the event:

  • Count people per guide according to difficulty. A short, marked trail allows bigger groups than a crossing with river fords or exposed stretches.
  • Load that number as the ticket type's capacity. When it runs out, sales close on their own and you don't have to keep saying no by message.
  • If there are two options, like "Con traslado desde el centro" (with transfer from downtown) and "Encuentro en la base del cerro" (meet at the base of the mountain), make a ticket type for each one, with its own price and capacity. Together they can't exceed the group's cap.
  • Count the assistant guide or the driver: complimentary tickets don't come out of the sales capacity in ENTRÁ, but they do count toward the total capacity shown in the dashboard.

One purchase can include up to four tickets of each type. That is enough for a family or a group of friends; if a group wants the whole outing, a private date for them works better.

Meeting point and time, in the first line

On a trip the most expensive mistake is someone arriving late or at the wrong place and the group having to wait. Write it at the very top of the description:

  • The exact meeting point, with a map link and a visible landmark ("in front of the tourist office, next to the sign").
  • Meeting time and departure time, separately. If the van leaves at 8:15, call people for 7:50.
  • Estimated return time, so nobody books a dinner they won't make.
  • What to bring: water, hiking boots, layered warm clothing, sunscreen, packed lunch if not included.
  • Difficulty, distance and elevation gain, in plain words. "Six hours of walking with a steady climb" is clearer than "medium difficulty".

Health form and emergency contact

When creating the event you can add extra questions for the buyer and mark them as required. For a day outing these work:

  • "Name and phone of an emergency contact", required.
  • "Any health condition, allergy or medication the guide should know about?", required, with the option to answer "none".
  • On horseback rides, "Have you ridden before?", to assign horses by experience.

Extra questions are answered once per purchase, not per ticket. If someone buys four spots for their family, they answer only once. Say so in the question: "If you are buying for several people, fill in the details for each one". If you need a signed medical form or a liability waiver, send it separately by email with the list and ask people to bring it printed or fill in a form of your own. The answers come out in the event list, which the guide can carry on their phone.

Insurance: ask your insurer

ENTRÁ sells the ticket and controls access, but does not include insurance for participants. What coverage you need depends on the activity, the province and whether you operate as a licensed provider. Ask your insurer which policy applies (personal accident, civil liability) and what details it needs from each participant, and request those details in the extra questions. If insurance is included in the price, say so in the description; if not, make that clear too.

Licensed guides, depending on the province

In Argentina each province and, in some cases, each park or protected area has its own rules about who can guide and which registrations are required. There is no single rule for the whole country. Before selling, check with your province's tourism office or the protected area's administration what license they require from you and your guides, whether there are entry caps or daily permits and whether the area charges its own separate entrance fee. If it does, mention it in the description so nobody is surprised at the gate.

Weather and rescheduling

In the mountains, the weather decides. What you can't leave for the morning of the outing is the rule:

  • Write down who decides and when: "If the forecast or conditions don't allow a safe departure, the guide calls it off by 8 p.m. the day before".
  • What happens with the ticket: change to another date or refund, at the buyer's choice. You, as the organizer, set the refund policy.
  • Notify each buyer yourself. Cancelling the event from the ENTRÁ dashboard emails every buyer that it was cancelled, but not the new date: export the list and write to them by email or WhatsApp with it.

Since each date is a separate event (ENTRÁ doesn't handle time slots as a feature inside a single event), rescheduling is simpler: you open the new date, send the link to those who want to switch and handle refunds for those who can't make it. What to write about rain and a plan B is in outdoor event: rain and plan B.

One event per outing

If you run the same trip every Saturday, load one event per date. Each one has its own link, capacity, list with health forms and check in. If Saturday fills up, Sunday stays on sale. For a two or three day trek paid all together, a single event does work, and there you choose once whether the QR is valid for the whole event or one per day; if it is one per day, the guide can take attendance each morning. The logic of capacity by time is similar to a guided tour with capacity by time slot.

Check in at the meeting point

Take attendance with the QR before boarding the van or starting to walk. It is scanned from the phone's browser: green with the name and ticket type, red with the reason. If someone brings the QR for another date or another trip, the scanner rejects it. If there is no signal at the base, entries are stored on the phone, and if someone can't find the email they are looked up by name, email or ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID). That way you know who is missing before setting out, not halfway along the trail.

Frequently asked questions

How do I ask each participant for an emergency contact?

Add a required extra question when creating the event. It is answered once per purchase, so make it clear in the text that, if they buy for several people, they should fill in the details for each one. The answers come out in the event list.

Does ENTRÁ include insurance for the trip?

No. ENTRÁ sells the ticket and controls access. Ask your insurer which policy applies to your activity and what details it needs from each participant, and request them in the extra questions.

What do I do if I call off the outing due to bad weather?

Have the rule written down before selling: who decides, by what time and whether a date change or refund is offered. Notify each buyer yourself, because the automatic email from cancelling the event only says it was cancelled, not what comes next.
