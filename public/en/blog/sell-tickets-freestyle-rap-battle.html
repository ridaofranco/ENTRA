Guide for organizers

Freestyle rap battle: tickets, MCs, minors and the door

By SOMOS DER · October 30, 2026 · 7 min read

A freestyle battle either fills up through Instagram in a week or it doesn't. The audience is young, many are under 18, they buy at the last minute from their phones and they all arrive together half an hour before the first round. On top of that, the competing MCs are also your best promotion. This guide sorts out competitor registration, the age rule, social media sales and the door.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Two audiences: those who compete and those who watch

At a battle there are people who pay to watch and people who sign up to compete. If you mix them in the same list, on the day you don't know who is in the bracket. At ENTRÁ you separate them with ticket types: each type has its own name, price and capacity, and it closes by itself when it fills up.

  • "Audience", with the room's capacity minus competitors, staff and complimentary tickets.
  • "Competitor", with a capacity equal to the spots in the bracket. If the format is 16, the capacity is 16, and whoever comes late stays out of the bracket without you having to say no by message.

The competitor price can be the same as the audience price, lower, or include something, like a ticket for a companion. Whatever it is, write it in the type's name or in the description. The same player and audience logic is developed in gaming tournament: how to sell player and audience tickets.

MC registration: stage name and rules

When you create the event you can add custom fields that the buyer fills in before paying: text, number, a list of options or long text, each required or not. For a battle these are useful:

  • Stage name, as required text. It is the one you will use in the bracket and the one the host announces.
  • Instagram handle, to tag them when you announce the bracket.
  • "I read the rules", as a list with a single "Yes" option, required. The rules go in the description: time per round, format (minutes, themes, words), who judges and what happens if someone does not show up.

Watch one detail: the questions are answered once per purchase, not once per ticket. If an MC buys their competitor spot and a friend's ticket in the same purchase, the stage name ends up on both. The tidiest way is to ask each competitor to buy their spot alone, and the companion to buy separately. The answers come out in the CSV export of the list, one column per question, and you build the bracket from there.

ENTRÁ does not build the bracket or draw the matchups. You export the list, filter by the "Competitor" type and do the draw in a spreadsheet or live, which in freestyle is usually part of the show.

Judges, host, DJ and beatbox

Lots of people work at a battle without paying: the judges, the host, the DJ or beatboxer, whoever films, whoever takes photos. Load them as complimentary tickets from the dashboard, with name, email, ID number, type and a reason ("judge", "DJ"). They get their QR by email, they do not take from the sales capacity and they do count toward the venue's capacity. That way the list does not live on a piece of paper at the door. Young audience and under 18s

Freestyle has a lot of 14, 15 and 16 year olds in the audience. That does not stop you from running the battle, but it forces you to decide the rule before publishing, not at the door. Three decisions:

  • Whether minors get in. First ask the venue what its license allows. A club or a bar may have its own age rule, and yours cannot be looser.
  • From what age and how. For example: "All ages. Under 16, with an adult". If minors come in with an adult, the adult also needs a ticket, unless you decide otherwise and write it down.
  • Which ID is checked at the door, and that it is checked for each person, not just the buyer.

The rule goes in the name of the event or ticket type and in the first line of the description, which is what people read before paying. You can also add a required single-choice question about age, which records that the person read the rule.

Keep in mind that the ID number (DNI) asked for at checkout is the buyer's, and it is a typed number. It helps find the ticket at the door, not prove the age of whoever comes in. Age is checked on the ID document.

If there is a bar, the age rule is no longer just yours. Argentina's Law 24.788 says in article 1 that the sale of any kind of alcoholic beverage to people under eighteen is prohibited throughout the national territory, and article 4 clarifies that it applies whatever kind of place is selling. At a battle with a mixed audience, the practical solution is a wristband or stamp for adults, handed out when the ID is checked, and a bar that only serves people who have it. Selling on Instagram

Almost all of a battle's sales go through Instagram: the competition's account, the stories of the announced MCs and the reels from the previous date. Some rules that keep it in order:

  • A single purchase link, the event's, in the bio and in the link sticker of every story. No "DM me" if you want people to buy through the link.
  • One link per MC. Competitors and guests promote to their own people. Give each of them the link with a campaign parameter, for example ?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=mc-name. At ENTRÁ each order stores the link the buyer arrived through, and the event dashboard shows sales grouped by source and campaign. That way you know who really brings people for the next date.
  • A code for the community. A discount code with a usage cap and an expiry date for people who came to the previous date rewards regulars without lowering the price for everyone.

Advance tickets and the door

The freestyle crowd decides late. If the advance ticket costs the same as the door, nobody buys early, and you don't know how many people are coming until the door opens. Set a cheaper advance ticket and say in every story when it ends. When it closes, you change the price from the dashboard or add a "Door" type. Whoever arrives without a ticket can buy from their phone on the event page while sales are still open. More on this in selling tickets at the door.

The door

At a battle almost the whole audience arrives together, a while before the first round. To avoid a jam on the sidewalk:

  • ID first, QR second. If the person cannot come in because of their age, don't scan: the QR would be marked as used.
  • The QR is scanned from a phone. Green with the name and ticket type, or red with the reason. Each QR is valid only once: if someone sent a screenshot to a friend, whoever arrives first gets in.
  • A line or a time slot for competitors. The scanner shows the type, so whoever comes through as "Competitor" goes straight to the organizer to confirm their spot in the bracket. Ask MCs on the event page to arrive before the audience.
  • If someone can't find the email, they can be looked up by last name, ID number or email from the scanner.
  • If an MC can't make it, they can transfer their spot to another competitor from "Mis Tickets" (my tickets), unless you disabled transfers. If you prefer the spot to go back to capacity, cancel it from the dashboard.

Frequently asked questions

How do I handle registration for the MCs who want to compete?

With a separate ticket type, for example "Competitor", with a capacity equal to the spots in the bracket, and a required question for the stage name. When the bracket fills up, the type closes by itself. The scanner shows the ticket type, so the door knows who is there to compete.

Can under 18s attend a freestyle battle?

It depends on what the venue allows and what you decide, and it has to be written down before people pay. If minors get in and there is a bar, Argentina's Law 24.788 prohibits selling them alcohol nationwide, so you need a visible mark for adults, like a wristband handed out when the ID is checked.

How do I know how many people each MC who promoted the battle brought?

Give each one their own link with a campaign parameter, for example ?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=mc-name. At ENTRÁ each order stores the link the buyer arrived through and the event dashboard shows sales grouped by source and campaign.
