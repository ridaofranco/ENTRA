Guide for organizers

Stand-up show in a bar: caps, tables, drinks and splitting the take

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

A comedy show in a bar has few seats, people who decide to go that same afternoon, a bar that needs to sell and, often, three or four comedians on the same night who afterwards want to know how much they get. All of that gets sorted before you publish the link. This guide goes step by step.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

The cap is the room as set up, not the floor plan

In a bar, the number of people who fit changes depending on how you set up the room. With tables and chairs in front of the stage fewer people fit than with the bar full of people standing, and at a comedy show people want to sit and see whoever is on the mic. Before loading the event:

  • Count chairs with the room set up for the show, not the capacity on the venue permit. If there are spots from which the stage can't be seen, take them out of the count.
  • Ask the bar how many people its permit allows and never go over it, not even with guests.
  • Save room for guests: comedians who come to watch, press, friends. In ENTRÁ complimentary tickets don't come out of the sales cap, but they do count toward the total capacity the dashboard shows. If the room has sixty chairs and you are giving out eight invitations, sell fifty-two.

Tables, bar or general admission

There are three common ways to seat people at a small show:

  • General admission, first come, first served. It is the simplest and works when the room is small and everyone can see well.
  • By zone: "Front tables", "Back tables", "Bar". Each zone is a ticket type with its own name, price and cap, and when one sells out the others stay on sale. Within each zone, people sit wherever they find a spot.
  • By table, if there are only a few: one ticket type per table with that table's chairs as the cap, for example "Table 3 · 4 seats". It works for groups who want to sit together. With more than ten or fifteen tables it becomes a long list and it is better to go back to zones.

ENTRÁ has no seat map or numbered tables on a floor plan, so the seating rule has to be written on the page: "First come, first served within each section" and what time doors open. When each option makes sense is in reserved seating or general admission.

Tickets with a drink included

In many bars the show is paid for through the bar, and a minimum spend is part of the deal with the venue. There are two ways to charge it:

  • Ticket only and drinks at the bar, with the minimum spend stated on the page ("minimum spend required at the bar"). The bar charges it and the bar controls it.
  • Ticket with a drink included, as a separate ticket type: "Ticket + drink", "Ticket + pint". The name has to say what it includes, because that is what the person sees when buying and what the door sees when scanning.

If you choose the second, keep in mind that the ENTRÁ QR works only once and is used at the door: the bar can't scan it again. What the scanner does do is show the ticket type of each QR, so the door hands over a token or wristband at that moment, to be exchanged later at the bar. Agree beforehand with the bar what share of that ticket goes to them. The full setup, with tokens and prices, is in tickets that include a drink or combo.

A night with several comedians

Shared shows are the most common thing on the small circuit: a host, three or four comedians with fifteen or twenty minutes each and, sometimes, a longer closing set. Each one brings their own audience and each one promotes. So the split doesn't end in an argument:

  • A written agreement before selling, even if it is in the WhatsApp group: which costs are deducted (what the bar charges, sound, paid promotion), how what is left is split and when people get paid.
  • Three ways to split: equal shares, according to what each one sells, or a fixed amount for the closer and the rest in shares. They can also be mixed: half in equal shares and the other half according to sales.

If the split depends on sales, you need to measure who sold what. In ENTRÁ there are two ways:

  • One link per comedian, with a different parameter, for example ?utm_campaign=comedian-1. The event dashboard shows where sales came from and the export includes the source and campaign columns.
  • One discount code per comedian, by percentage or fixed amount, with a usage cap and expiry date if you want. In the list of codes you see how many times each one was used.

Both count purchases, not tickets: if someone buys four with a comedian's link, that is one purchase. For the split, look at the quantity column in the sales export. And whatever comes in without a link, like someone buying from the event page or in the line, is not assigned to anyone: agree beforehand that it goes into a common pot. The details are in a lineup with several bands: how to split the take, which applies the same way to comedians.

With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket. The service and processing charges are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

A run of several dates

If the show repeats (every Thursday, or Thursday, Friday and Saturday), the tidy way is one event per show. Each one has its own link, cap, attendee list and door, and if Saturday sells out, Thursday stays on sale. If one night of the week sells less, a discount code for that show alone gives it a push without touching the price of the others.

Over-18 only if there is alcohol

Many comedy shows have adult content, and almost all of them happen in a place with a bar. The age rule for alcohol is not yours to set: Argentina's Law 24.788 says in its article 1 that "the sale of all types of alcoholic beverages to persons under eighteen years of age is prohibited throughout the national territory". If the bar doesn't want to check ages at the bar, the simplest thing is to make the show adults only. In that case:

  • "18+" in the name of the event or ticket type, which is what people see before paying.
  • The rule in the first line of the description, with which ID is accepted and what happens if someone is under age.
  • A required question at checkout. When creating the event you can add extra questions for the buyer and mark them as required: "Will you be 18 or older on the day of the show?". It doesn't replace the check, but it records that the person read the rule.
  • At the door, ID first and QR second. The ID number (DNI) entered at purchase belongs to whoever bought and nobody verified it: it is for finding the ticket, not for checking age.

What to do if a minor arrives with a ticket is in over-18 only event.

The door and the last-minute crowd

Comedy is bought late: many people decide on their way out of work. The event page sells until the end time you entered, and if you didn't enter one, until three hours after the start. ENTRÁ has no box office and does not record cash payments, so anyone who arrives without a ticket buys from their phone, without creating an account. To keep the line from jamming:

  • A sign with the QR for the event link in the line, away from the scanner.
  • One person dedicated to the door, not one of the comedians. Scanning is done from the phone's browser: green with the name and ticket type, red with the reason.
  • If someone can't find the email, they are looked up by name, email or ID number. If the bar's signal drops, entries are saved on the phone.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell numbered tables for a stand-up show?

ENTRÁ has no table map or numbered seats. If there are only a few tables, you can create one ticket type per table, for example "Table 3 · 4 seats", with that table's chair count as the cap. If there are many, it is better to group by zone ("Front tables", "Bar") and seat first come, first served.

How do I split the take between several comedians?

Agree on it in writing before opening sales. If the split depends on how much each one sells, give each comedian a link with their own utm_campaign or their own discount code. The dashboard shows where each purchase came from and the sales export includes the number of tickets in each purchase.

Does the show have to be over-18 only?

It depends on the content and the venue. What doesn't depend on you is the bar: Argentina's Law 24.788 prohibits selling alcoholic drinks to under-18s throughout the country. If the bar doesn't want to check ages at the bar, the simplest thing is to make the show 18+ and ask for ID at the door.
