Wine or beer tasting with tickets: how to set it up and sell it

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

A ticketed tasting is closer to a workshop than to a party: there is a route, something to learn and just the right number of people so everyone gets a proper taste. It also has its own traps: not enough glasses, a bar with a line and someone who didn't know what the ticket included. This guide goes in order: what the ticket includes, how many to sell, what to ask at checkout, the minimum age and the door.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

What the ticket includes: spell it out

The most repeated question at a tasting is "what's included?". Answer it before anyone asks, in the event description:

  • What gets tasted. How many labels or styles, from which wineries or breweries, and how much of each is poured.
  • The glass. Whether it is lent, taken home as a souvenir or collected on the way out.
  • The food. Whether there is cheese, a platter or a pairing, or whether food is bought separately.
  • The guided part. Whether someone presents and explains, how long it lasts and what time it starts.
  • The schedule. Start, end and until what time drinks are served.

If you offer two options, load them as separate ticket types. For example "Degustación" and "Degustación + maridaje" (tasting, and tasting plus food pairing), each with its own price and capacity. With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket; the service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, itemized at checkout.

Capacity: the smallest of three numbers

At a tasting, capacity is not just how many people fit in the venue. Take the smallest of these three numbers:

  • The venue's approved capacity. That ceiling is not negotiable.
  • The glasses you have. If there isn't one per person, they get washed on the fly and the tasting stalls.
  • What the team can serve. Each serving station handles a certain number of people at a time. If everyone waits in the same line, nobody enjoys it.

If the tasting is guided, think of it as a class with limited places: fewer people, everyone listens and everyone tastes at the same time. If it can run in two sessions, load each session as a separate event to have an exact cap on each one.

What to ask at checkout

In ENTRÁ you can add extra questions to the event that the buyer fills in when buying. At a tasting they are useful for:

  • Dietary restrictions, if there is food or a pairing: gluten free, vegetarian, allergies.
  • Experience level, with a multiple choice question ("first time", "I drink it often", "I work in the trade"), to adapt the guided part.
  • How they get home. An optional question about whether they are driving helps you have water and alcohol free options on hand.

The answers come as a column in the attendee export, together with the name and ticket type, so you can pass them to the kitchen or the guide before the event.

Minimum age and alcohol

A tasting with alcohol is for adults. Follow the alcohol sales rules of your jurisdiction and check them before opening sales, along with what the venue is licensed for. In practice:

  • Say it in the description and on the flyer: that it is adults only and that ID may be requested at the door.
  • Give someone at the door the job of asking for it when needed, with a clear criterion agreed before starting.
  • Water and alcohol free options available throughout the event.

Wineries, breweries and guests

If producers with their own label take part, they will want to invite people: customers, distributors, press. Agree beforehand how many invitations each one gets. In ENTRÁ each complimentary ticket is loaded with the person's details, the ticket type and a reason, and it arrives by email with its QR. Complimentary tickets don't come out of the sales allocation but they do count toward capacity, and at a tasting every guest drinks too.

The door

Each ticket has a unique QR that is good for one use only and is scanned from the phone's browser. The scanner shows the ticket type, so at the door you know who has the pairing and who doesn't, and you hand out the right glass or wristband. When it's over, export the attendee list: it is the audience for your next tasting (look after it: it is personal data).

Frequently asked questions

What should a tasting ticket include?

What each person will taste (how many labels or styles and how much of each), whether there is a glass, whether there is something to eat and whether there is a guided talk. Write it in the event description so the buyer knows exactly what they are paying for.

How do I set the capacity for a tasting?

Use the smallest of three numbers: the venue's approved capacity, the number of glasses you have and what the team can serve without lines forming. In ENTRÁ each ticket type has its own capacity.

Is there a minimum age for a tasting with alcohol?

Follow the alcohol sales rules of your jurisdiction and check them before selling. State in the event description that it is adults only and that ID may be requested at the door.
