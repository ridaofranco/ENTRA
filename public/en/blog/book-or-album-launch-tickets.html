Book or album launch with tickets: copies, capacity and door

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Launching a book or an album is a small event with one detail that complicates it: besides the ticket, there is an object that is sold, signed and handed over. If you sort it out beforehand, you know how many people are coming, how many copies to bring and who takes one home on the way in.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First: do you need to charge for tickets?

Not every launch needs it. A launch at a bookshop, with a talk and a signing, is often free and the business is in the copies sold. But there are cases where charging for tickets makes sense:

  • There is a show. An album is launched by playing it, and then there are musicians, sound and a venue that charges. A book can also have a reading with music or guests.
  • The venue is small. If 60 people fit and you have a community of 300, the ticket sorts out who comes and avoids a line on the sidewalk.
  • There are costs to cover. Room rental, sound, a welcome drink, promotion.

If none of that applies and all you want is to know how many people are coming, make it free with a reservation. In ENTRÁ a free event works with no-cost tickets, with a QR code and capacity, just like a paid one.

Ticket only or ticket with a copy

The central decision is whether the book or album comes with the ticket. There are three ways to set it up:

  • Ticket only, and the copy is sold separately at a table. It is the simplest. The problem is that you don't know how many copies you will sell and you have to bring extra.
  • Ticket with a copy only. Everyone who comes in takes one. It works when the audience is almost all sure buyers, but it leaves out whoever already has it or the companion who doesn't want one.
  • Both options, as different ticket types: "Ticket" and "Ticket + copy". This is the one we recommend most, because each person chooses and you know in advance how many copies you sold.

In ENTRÁ each ticket type has its own name, price and capacity, and it closes on its own when it fills up. Give the "Ticket + copy" type a capacity equal to the copies you will have in hand that day. That way you never sell a book you don't have. If you have vinyl and CD, or a regular and a signed edition, you can add one type per format.

Put the format in the type name, not just in the description: "Ticket + CD", "Ticket + vinyl", "Ticket + book". That name is what the door later sees on the scanner, and the clearer it is, the fewer doubts.

Price

For the type with a copy, the reasonable price is the ticket plus the copy, with a small discount as an incentive.

A few things to keep in mind:

  • The copy has a cost. If you are the author or the musician and the publisher or label sells them to you at the author's price, that is your base. If they give them to you on consignment, clarify beforehand what happens with the unsold ones.
  • The ticket alone has to cover the night. Add up room, sound and promotion and divide it by a number of tickets you really think you will sell. The full calculation is in how many tickets to sell so you don't lose money.
  • With ENTRÁ you keep 100% of the price you set for each ticket. Service and processing costs are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

Each purchase allows up to 4 tickets of each type. For a launch that is more than enough: a couple can take one "Ticket + copy" and one "Ticket" in the same purchase.

Venue capacity

Launches usually happen in bookshops, bars, cultural centers or small rooms. Before opening sales, ask how many people really fit with the stage or reading table set up, not the number on the sign.

The event's total capacity has to include everything: plain tickets, tickets with a copy and complimentary tickets. Complimentary tickets take up a spot just like a sold ticket. In ENTRÁ capacity is deducted when the payment is confirmed, so what you see in the dashboard are real sales, not abandoned carts.

There are no numbered seats or assigned tables. If there are chairs for fewer people than the capacity, say so on the page: "seating for 40, the rest standing". If there is a front row reserved for press or family, mark it with a sign and have someone from the team look after it.

Complimentary tickets for press, publisher and guests

At a launch, invitations weigh more than at other events: the press, the people from the publisher or label, guest musicians, the host, the family. They all have a reason to be there, and they all take up a spot.

  • Decide the number before opening sales, and take it from the margin you have left over capacity.
  • Issue them from the event dashboard, in the complimentary tickets tab: each person's name and email, the ticket type, the quantity and the reason. Each one comes with its own QR code and arrives by email.
  • Choose the type carefully. Give a journalist who will review the book a complimentary "Ticket + copy", so the door knows they take one. Their companion gets a plain "Ticket".

That way the door doesn't work from a paper list: the complimentary ticket is scanned just like a purchased one and is valid only once. More detail in complimentary tickets and invitations: how many to give and how to control them.

The door and handing out the copy

This is where the night is won or lost. If handing out the copy happens in the same line as the ticket check, entry jams. What works:

  • One person scans the QR codes from a phone. Each QR code is valid only once.
  • The scanner shows the ticket type. If it says "Ticket + copy", the door person hands over the book or album right then, or a token or wristband that is later exchanged at the signing table.
  • If there are many, separate the handover. With more than 50 copies, it is better for the door to hand out only the token and for someone else to deliver at a table to the side. The entry line keeps moving and the signing table organizes itself.
  • If someone can't find the email, you look them up by last name, ID number (DNI) or email from the scanner. The ID number is requested at purchase, so the search works even if the person doesn't have the QR code handy.
  • Have the copies counted and separated by format before opening. At the end of the night, copies handed out should match scanned tickets of that type.

To understand what the scanner looks like and what to do with unusual cases, see how access control works on the day of the event.

What to say on the event page

  • What is being launched and by whom: title, author or band, and who joins them.
  • What each ticket type includes, with the format of the copy if there is more than one.
  • Whether there is a signing and when: before, after or during.
  • When the copy is handed over: at the door or at the signing table.
  • Whether there are seats for everyone or part of the audience stands.
  • What time doors open, when it starts and when it ends, with the end time loaded in the event.

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth charging for tickets at a book or album launch?

If there is a show, a reading with musicians, a rented room or limited capacity, yes. The ticket sorts out how many people come and covers part of the cost. If the idea is to sell copies and the venue is free, you can do it with a free reservation to know how many are coming.

How do I sell the ticket with the book or album included?

With two ticket types: "Ticket" and "Ticket + copy", each with its own price. Give the type with a copy a capacity equal to the copies you will have that day, so it closes on its own when they run out.

How do I know at the door who gets a copy?

The scanner shows each ticket's type when validating the QR code. If it says "Ticket + copy", the door person gives them the book or album right then, or a token to pick it up at the signing table.

How do I invite the press and the people from the publisher or label?

With complimentary tickets issued from the event dashboard, in each person's name and with the right ticket type. They get the QR code by email and at the door it is scanned just like a purchased ticket.
