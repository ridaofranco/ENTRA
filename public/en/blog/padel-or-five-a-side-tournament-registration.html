Guide for organizers

Padel or 5-a-side football tournament: registration per pair or team, capacity and spectators

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

An amateur padel or 5-a-side football tournament is usually organized with a WhatsApp group, a spreadsheet and bank transfers arriving at all hours. It works until one category has two pairs too many, another is one short and nobody knows who paid. This guide puts registration in order so every spot in the draw is paid before you build the fixture list, and makes clear which part is handled outside the ticketing platform.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

What registers: the pair or the team, not the player

Padel is played in pairs and 5-a-side in teams, so what takes up a spot in the draw is the pair or the team. In ENTRÁ you set that up with ticket types: each type has its own name, price and capacity, and it closes on its own when it fills up.

  • One type per category. "Pair · Men's sixth category", "Pair · Mixed", "Team · Open", "Team · Over 35" (amateur padel in Argentina is graded in numbered skill categories, such as "sixth"). The price is the full registration for the pair or the team.
  • The capacity is the draw. If the sixth category is played with twelve pairs, that type's capacity is twelve. When it fills, it shows as sold out and nobody else signs up for a category with no room.
  • A purchase of one unit is one registration. Whoever pays is the captain or one of the two in the pair.

You can't create the event without loading each type's capacity, and that is fine: it is your limit of courts and time slots. With ENTRÁ you keep 100% of the price you set for each registration. Service and processing costs are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

Capacity comes from the courts and the clock

Before loading the numbers, work backwards from the time you have:

  • Courts and hours. How many courts there are, how many hours per day and how long each match lasts including changeover.
  • Format. Groups followed by knockout, or straight knockout. Each format works best with a number of pairs or teams that fits without gaps. Choose the format first and set that number as the capacity.
  • Guaranteed matches. If you promise "at least two matches per pair", the capacity has to fit the schedule with that in mind.

If the tournament runs over a weekend, you can load the event with several days and choose one QR code for the whole event, which works once per day. Capacity is set per type for the whole event, not per day, and for a registration that is exactly what you want: the registered pair plays on Saturday and, if they win, comes back on Sunday.

Player details, for the fixture list

To buy, ENTRÁ asks for the name, email and ID number (DNI) of whoever pays, and the phone is optional. For a tournament that is not enough: you need all the players. When creating the event you can add extra questions for the buyer, in text, number, list of options or long text format, and mark them as required. The most useful ones:

  • Name of the pair or team, as text. It is what will appear on the fixture list.
  • Players with name and ID number, as long text. For 5-a-side also ask for the substitutes.
  • Captain's phone, to send times and changes.
  • Time availability, as a list of options, if you are going to respect restrictions on the first round.
  • "I have read the rules and the liability waiver", as a list with a single "Yes" option.

Questions are answered once per purchase. That is why one registration per purchase is best: if someone registers two teams in the same payment, both end up with the same answers. Ask for that in the description and check the export before building the draw. The same reasoning, applied to runners, is in race with paid registration.

The fixture list and what is left out

ENTRÁ doesn't build fixture lists, groups or standings. What it gives you is the list: from the dashboard you export as CSV the name, email, ID number, phone, ticket type and one column for each question you added. You sort by category, build the groups in a spreadsheet or whatever tool you use and post it in the group chat. Other things handled separately:

  • The signed waiver and the medical clearance. ENTRÁ doesn't collect signatures on documents. The usual approach is a sheet each player signs at the check-in table before the first match.
  • Insurance and court rental, which are arranged with the club.
  • Prizes and referees, which go in the budget, not in the ticketing platform.

Price, tiers and discounts

A tournament fills better with a cutoff date. You can set up a cheaper first tier with its own type and capacity, "Pair · Sixth · First tier", and a general one afterwards: when the first sells out, it shows as sold out and pushes the rest. If the club has members, a discount code by percentage or fixed amount, with a usage cap and expiry date, gives them their price and tells you how many used it. How to set them up is in discount codes for an event.

Withdrawals and replacements

In amateur tournaments there is always a pair that drops out because of an injury. Write the rule into the regulations before opening: until what day the registration is refunded and whether the spot can be handed over. If it is handed over, the official transfer sends the registration to the new captain's email and generates a QR code in their name, which invalidates the old one. Keep in mind that the question answers stay those of the original purchase: update the team name in your spreadsheet. If you agreed to refund the money, from the dashboard you mark that registration as refunded: the QR code stops working and the spot goes back into the category's capacity.

If the tournament is outdoors and it rains, say beforehand what happens: it moves to another date, it is played indoors or the format is shortened. Ideas in outdoor event in the rain.

Spectators: paid or free

The finals bring family, friends and people from the club. There are three ways to handle it:

  • Free entry with no control, if the club is open and there is plenty of room in the stands.
  • Paid entry, as another type within the same event: "Spectators · Finals", with its own price and capacity. If the event has several days, that ticket works once per day.
  • Free entry with a reservation, if you want to know how many people are coming without charging. The free event option applies to the whole event, so load the spectators as a separate event: people reserve without paying and still get their QR code.

The check-in table on tournament day

The door of a tournament is the table where teams report. The captain shows the registration QR code and it is scanned from the phone's browser: green with the name and the type, which says the category, or red with the reason. At that moment they sign the waiver and, if you want, receive a player wristband. If someone can't find the email, you look them up by name, email or ID number, and if there is no signal at the club, check-ins are saved on the phone. When check-in closes, the export tells you which registrations showed up and which didn't, so you can adjust the draw before the first match.

Frequently asked questions

How do I charge registration per pair or per team rather than per player?

Create one ticket type per category with the price for the pair or team and a capacity equal to the number of spots in the draw. A purchase of one unit is one registration. The players' names and ID numbers are requested with a required long text question, which comes out as a column in the export.

Does ENTRÁ build the tournament fixture list?

No. ENTRÁ collects the registration, closes each category when it fills and gives you the list. The fixture list, groups and results are built in a spreadsheet or whatever tool you use, from the CSV export with each registration's answers.

Can I charge spectators for entry as well as the registration?

Yes. Spectators can be another ticket type within the same event, with its own price and capacity. If spectators get in free but you want to know how many are coming, load a separate free event: people reserve their spot without paying and still get their QR code.
