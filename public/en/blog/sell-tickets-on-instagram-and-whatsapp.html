Guide for organizers

Selling tickets through Instagram and WhatsApp without losing control

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

If you run small events, almost all your sales go through two places: your Instagram and your WhatsApp groups. The problem starts when sales get scattered: one person asks you by message, another sends money to your payment alias, a third buys through the link, and a week later you don't know how many tickets are left or who you owe one to. This guide sorts out how to use both networks to push sales, with a single place where people pay.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

The basic rule: a single purchase link

Instagram and WhatsApp are for people to find out and make up their minds. Payment and the ticket should always happen in the same place: the event page on the ticketing platform. That gives you three things a chat doesn't:

  • Capacity is deducted automatically. Each ticket type has its own capacity and closes when it fills up. There is no way to sell the same place twice because two people messaged you at once.
  • Each buyer gets their ticket with a QR by email, and that QR is good for one use only at the door.
  • You have the full list of who bought, which ticket type and when, exportable to CSV from the dashboard.

Instagram: the bio, the post and the stories

Link in bio

While an event is on sale, the link in your bio should be the direct link to that event. Pages that gather lots of links are for something else; here every extra step between the bio and the buy button is people dropping off along the way. When the event is over, you swap the link for the next one.

What to put in the post

The flyer alone is not enough. In the post text, right at the top, put what someone needs to decide without opening anything:

  • What it is, when and where: name, day, door opening time and neighborhood or address.
  • How much it costs and until when: the current tier price and, if it is going up, the date it goes up.
  • Where to buy: "Tickets at the link in bio". Don't write "DM me" if you want people to buy through the link.
  • Something that makes people want to go: who plays, what is included, what is different about this date.

The same goes for the event page description, which is what people read when they land. There are ideas for writing it in how to write an event description that sells.

Stories

Stories are where sales for a small event move the most, because you can repeat them without wearing out your profile. Use the link sticker with the event link on every story that talks about the event, not just the first one. Some that work:

  • The announcement, on the day you open sales.
  • The tier change notice, one or two days before the price goes up.
  • Behind the scenes: the sound check, the venue being set up, the confirmed lineup.
  • The "last tickets" story, only if it is true. If you say it and then tickets are left over, next time nobody believes you.

Direct messages

Questions will come in by DM, and it is fine to answer them. The answer always ends with the link: "Yes, there is parking nearby. Tickets are here:" and the link. If someone insists on paying you by bank transfer, explain that the ticket comes with a QR from the page and that this way there is no confusion at the door.

WhatsApp: groups, broadcast lists and status

WhatsApp is where people forward things. A good message in one group reaches ten other groups you don't control, so write it to make sense on its own, without context:

  • First line: what it is and when. Many people only read that in the notification.
  • Price and until when.
  • The event link, alone, on its own line.

When you paste an ENTRÁ event link into WhatsApp, the preview shows the event name, date and flyer. For that, the flyer has to be loaded before you share the link: if you shared it without an image, the app may keep the old version for quite a while.

For individual contacts, a broadcast list or your status works better than sending the same message one by one. And for people who have already been to one of your dates, the buyer base you export from the dashboard is the audience that responds best. Use it in moderation and say who you are.

A single link, also against copies

When an event spreads through groups, accounts show up offering tickets or copying the flyer with another link. If you have always shared the same address, from your official accounts, anyone can compare. Say it in the post and in stories: "Tickets are sold only at this link". Avoid URL shorteners and redirect pages that make your official link look like a fake one.

If your event is for a closed audience and you don't want it to appear in the ENTRÁ listings, you can mark it as a hidden event: it doesn't show in the public listings and only people with the link can get in. You hand out the link yourself, through the channels you choose.

How to track where sales come from

Knowing which channel sold for you tells you where to put the effort for the next date. At ENTRÁ each order stores the link the buyer came in through, and the event dashboard has a block called "De dónde vinieron las ventas" (where sales came from), with sales grouped by source. If someone arrives from your Instagram bio or from a link opened in WhatsApp, the sale is recorded with that source as long as the app passes the data along.

To tell apart posts on the same channel, add campaign parameters to the link. For example:

  • Bio: the link with ?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=bio
  • Friday story: ?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=story-viernes
  • Neighborhood group: ?utm_source=whatsapp&utm_campaign=grupo-barrio

Some sales will show up as "sin dato" (no data), especially when someone copies the link and pastes it by hand or opens it from another app. The dashboard shows you how many there are, so you don't mistake them for a channel.

The other way to measure, which also lets you reward whoever promotes you, is a discount code per person or per channel: each code has its own usage counter and you can give it a cap. How to set them up without giving the sale away is in discount codes for an event, and the full tracking method in how to promote your event and track where sales come from.

What to avoid

  • Selling part through the link and part by bank transfer. Capacity stops adding up and the door fills with people holding screenshots.
  • Changing the link halfway through sales. Old posts and messages keep circulating with the previous link.
  • Taking unpaid reservations by chat. A reservation that isn't paid takes a place someone else would have paid for.
  • Measuring by likes. What counts is how many tickets each post brought in, and you see that in the dashboard.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell tickets by Instagram DM or WhatsApp?

You can chat there, but it is better to close the sale through the event link. That way payment, the QR ticket and capacity stay in one place, and you don't have to match bank transfers against names noted down in a chat.

How do I know if a sale came from Instagram or WhatsApp?

At ENTRÁ each order stores the link the buyer came in through, and the event dashboard shows sales grouped by source. To tell apart two posts on the same channel, add parameters to the link such as ?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=story-viernes.

What do I put in my Instagram bio if I have several events?

If one event is selling right now, put that direct link, not an intermediate page. When it ends, swap it for the next one. Every extra click between the bio and the buy button is people lost along the way.
