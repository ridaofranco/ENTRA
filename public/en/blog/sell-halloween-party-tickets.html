Guide for organizers

Halloween party tickets: tiers, costumes and the door

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 7 min read

A Halloween or costume party sells itself if people have time to put the character together. The rest is sorting out three things: when sales open, what you do with people who come in costume and people who don't, and how a line of vampires with their phones hidden in their capes gets through the door. Here it all is, in the order you will need it.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

When to open sales

For a regular party people decide on Thursday. For a costume party they need days: finding the clothes, buying the makeup, agreeing with the group if they are going as a set. That changes the calendar.

  • Three or four weeks before, announce the date and the theme. If it is Halloween, October 31 or the nearest Saturday; if it is just a "costume party", you pick the date, and you have less competition.
  • A few days later, open the presale. People already have the date in their heads and come ready to buy.
  • The last week is when you sell the most. Save the contest prize and last year's photos for those days.

If the party is this weekend, loosen the theme ("come with something Halloween", not "full costume"). The general calendar is in when to open ticket sales.

Tiers

Tiers reward people who buy early and give you an early idea of how many people are coming. In ENTRÁ each ticket type has its name, price and capacity, and you can mark which ones are presale. Until you launch official sales, the public sees only the presale and the rest shows as the next tier. When you tap Lanzar venta oficial (launch official sales) in the dashboard, the presale is marked as finished and the others open up. Each type closes on its own when it reaches its capacity.

A setup that works for a costume party:

  • Presale, the cheapest and with a short capacity. This is the one the groups who already know their costume will buy.
  • General, from the moment you launch official sales.
  • Door, more expensive, for people who decide that night. Online sales can stay open during the party and people buy from their phones in line.

A note for groups: each purchase takes up to four tickets of each type. If a group of six is going as the Addams Family, they buy in two goes or on two phones. Say so in the description so nobody thinks something broke. How to price each tier is in how to price your tickets and set up tiers.

A discount for people in costume

It is the question every year, and it has an underlying problem: the costume is seen at the door, not at the purchase. Nobody can check from a ticketing platform whether the person paying will show up as a zombie. That is why there are three ways to handle it, from simplest to most work.

1. Same price for everyone, a reward at the door

Everyone pays the same ticket and whoever arrives in costume gets something at the door: a drink token, a different colored wristband that gives a perk at the bar, entry to the contest. It causes the fewest arguments. If in doubt, choose this one.

2. A "Costume" ticket type

You create two ticket types: "General" and "Costume", the second one cheaper. At the door, the scanner shows the type of each ticket, so the person checking knows instantly whether that ticket was bought on the promise of a costume. For it to work:

  • Write the rule before selling: what counts as a costume (does a headband with little horns count?) and what happens if someone arrives without one. The usual thing is that they pay the difference at the door.
  • That difference is collected outside ENTRÁ: in cash or by bank transfer, with someone assigned to it. Decide this beforehand, because at the door there is no time to improvise.

3. A discount code

You create a code, for example "COSTUME", from the Descuentos (discounts) tab in the dashboard. Each code belongs to that event only, can be a percentage or a fixed amount and has a usage cap. Two things to keep in mind:

  • It works on trust. Anyone who knows the code can use it, in costume or not.
  • The cap counts purchases, not tickets. If someone uses the code and takes four tickets, that is one use. A cap of 50 can end up as quite a bit more than 50 discounted tickets.

Where the code does pay off is with group costumes: a "GROUP" code with a percentage pushes the group to buy together, and since the percentage applies to the whole purchase, the more tickets, the bigger the discount. The full details are in discount codes for an event.

The costume contest

The contest is what turns a party with people in costume into a costume party. And the photos of the winners sell the next edition. What you need to define:

  • Categories: best costume, scariest, best group. Two or three are enough.
  • How the winner is chosen: a small jury (the DJ, someone from the venue, a well known guest) or by applause. Applause is more fun, a jury is fairer.
  • How people enter: at the door, with a number or a wristband, or by stopping at a corner with a photographer.
  • When: with the dance floor full but before people start leaving. Announce the time in the description and repeat it on the mic that night.
  • The prize: something you can explain in one line of the flyer. Tickets to your next party work well, because they reward and also bring people back. In ENTRÁ, comps are issued from the event dashboard and arrive with their own QR code.

What to say on the event page

  • The theme and the dress code, in the first line: whether the costume is suggested or required.
  • The discount rule, if you chose the "Costume" type: what counts as a costume and what happens if you arrive without one.
  • The contest: categories, prize and what time it happens.
  • What can't come in: prop weapons that look real, for example.
  • If it is 18+ only, in the name of the event or the ticket type, not just in the description.
  • Opening and closing time, with the end time entered in the event. If you don't enter it, ENTRÁ considers the event over three hours after the start and online sales close, which at a party that starts at midnight leaves you without door sales.

The door

At a costume party people arrive in groups, at the same time and with their phones in strange places. To keep the line moving:

  • QR code open before arriving. Ask for it in a message that day: finding a phone inside a costume takes time.
  • Each QR is valid only once. The scanner marks it as used; if someone tries to get in with someone else's screenshot, the scanner flags it.
  • If someone can't find their ticket, it can be looked up from the scanner by surname, ID number (DNI) or email. The DNI is asked for at purchase, so it is on every ticket.
  • Masks off for the check. If there is an age check, the person takes off the mask and shows their ID before the QR code is scanned.
  • The costume token or wristband is handed out at the door, not at the bar.

Frequently asked questions

How do I give a discount to people who come in costume?

There are three ways. The simplest is to charge everyone the same and give anyone who arrives in costume a perk at the door, like a token for the bar. The second is a cheaper "Costume" ticket type, which the scanner shows at the door so you can check the costume. The third is a discount code, but nobody can see the costume at the time of purchase, so it works on trust.

How far ahead should I open sales for a Halloween party?

Early enough for people to put their costume together: three or four weeks before is a good reference. First you announce the date and theme, then you open the presale, and the last week is when you sell the most.

Can I give tickets as a prize for the costume contest?

Yes. In ENTRÁ, comps are issued from the event dashboard and arrive with their own QR code. If the prize is a ticket to the next party, you issue it once that date is already loaded.
