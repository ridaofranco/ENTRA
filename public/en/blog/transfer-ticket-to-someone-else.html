Guide for buyers

How to transfer your ticket to someone else safely

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Something came up and you can't go, or you bought an extra ticket for someone who joined late. The first thing almost everyone thinks of is sending a screenshot of the QR on WhatsApp. It works until it doesn't: the day two people have that screenshot, the first one gets in and the other is left at the door. The safe way is different, and it takes less than a minute.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). This guide describes how transferring a ticket works on ENTRÁ.

Why a screenshot is not enough

Your ticket's QR is good only once. When it is scanned at the door it is marked as used, and any copy of that same code no longer gets in. That protects you against duplicate tickets, but it also means a screenshot is not a ticket: it is a photo of a code that can be on several phones at once.

If you send a screenshot to a friend, your QR stays the same and is still on your phone, in the chat and anywhere it was forwarded. If the chat leaks or someone forwards it, whoever arrives first gets in. More on how the code works in what the QR on your ticket is and what it is for.

The official transfer, step by step

At ENTRÁ the transfer is done from your profile, on the same screen where you see your tickets:

  • Choose the ticket and tap the transfer button.
  • Enter the recipient's details: name and email, and if you want, their phone and a note.
  • Confirm. The ticket is locked to that email: only that person, signing in with that email, can claim it.
  • They get the link. The system emails them the link to claim it, and sends you a confirmation. On the same screen you also have the link to send it on WhatsApp or copy it.
  • Your friend claims it. They open the link, sign in with the email you sent it to and the ticket becomes theirs.

If your friend doesn't have an account, they create one with that same email. The ticket is only delivered to a verified email, so if they haven't verified it, the screen asks them to and resends the verification email.

What happens to the old QR

This is the important part. When your friend claims the ticket, the system generates a new QR in their name, and the ticket is no longer in your profile. The code you had is no longer the one for that ticket. At the door, the one that counts belongs to whoever claimed it.

That is what makes the transfer safe: there are never two people with a valid code for the same place. And there is a record of who the ticket came from.

If you got the email wrong

Until the other person claims it, the transfer stays pending and you see it that way in your profile, with a button to cancel it. If you cancel it, the link stops working and the ticket is yours again, ready to send to the right email. Once claimed, it can no longer be undone from your side.

When you can't transfer

  • If the event has already happened. It makes no sense and the system doesn't allow it.
  • If the organizer turned it off. Each event has an option to allow transfers or not. If it is off, the button doesn't appear and there is no way to pass the ticket on through the platform (if you organize, the option is there when you load the event).
  • If there is already a pending transfer. Cancel it first and then send it again.

If the event was cancelled or rescheduled and that is why you can't go, that has its own path: we explain it in cancelled or rescheduled event.

Why you shouldn't buy resale screenshots

On the other side is the person receiving. If someone offers you a ticket and wants to pass it as a screenshot, PDF or photo on WhatsApp in exchange for a money transfer, think about this:

  • You don't know how many times that same image was sold. The first one to arrive with that code gets in; the rest don't.
  • You don't know whether it was already transferred. If the original owner passed it to someone else through the platform, the code in the screenshot is no longer the one for that ticket.
  • There is nobody to complain to. You paid outside the platform, to a private individual, with nothing backing you.

If someone really is giving you their ticket, ask them to transfer it from the platform to your email. If they refuse or make excuses, there is your answer. More signs to avoid a scam in how to buy tickets without getting scammed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I pass my ticket to a friend?

From your ENTRÁ profile, on the ticket you want to pass on, tap transfer and enter your friend's name and email. That person gets a link by email, which you can also send them on WhatsApp, and they claim it by signing in with that same email. When they claim it, a new QR is generated in their name.

What happens to the QR I had?

When your friend claims the ticket, the system generates a new QR for them and yours no longer matches that ticket. That is why an old screenshot won't get anyone in: the valid code is the one held by whoever claimed it.

Can I cancel a transfer if I got the email wrong?

Yes, as long as the other person hasn't claimed it. In your profile the ticket shows as a pending transfer, with a button to cancel it: the link stops working and the ticket is yours again, ready to transfer once more.

Why won't it let me transfer my ticket?

There are three possible reasons: the event has already happened, the organizer disabled transfers for that event, or the ticket already has a pending transfer. In the last case, cancel it first from your profile and start again.

Do you organize events?

At ENTRÁ each ticket has a unique QR that works once, and the official transfer is included: your audience passes tickets on without screenshots or duplicates, and you decide whether to enable it. Creating the account and loading the event costs nothing.
