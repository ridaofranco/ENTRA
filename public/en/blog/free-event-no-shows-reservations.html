Guide for organizers

Free event: how to stop people from reserving and not showing up

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

You opened reservations for a free event, it filled up in two days, and on the day there are empty chairs while someone outside asks whether there is any room left. It always happens: reserving something that costs nothing commits nobody. You can't avoid it completely, but you can shrink it and, above all, you can plan for it.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Why it happens

When the ticket is paid, not showing up costs money. When it is free, not showing up costs nothing, and neither does signing up. So people reserve "just in case", reserve for friends who never confirm, or sign up weeks ahead and forget. None of that is bad faith; it is what happens when there is no cost.

What you can control are four things: how much you ask for to reserve, how many you accept, how you remind them and what you do with spots that free up.

1. Ask for little data, but make it real

Every extra field on the form lowers reservations, and adding questions doesn't make people more likely to come. What works is asking for just enough so the reservation belongs to a specific person. In ENTRÁ, reserving a free event asks for name, email and ID number (DNI), and the phone number is optional. No account needed.

The email matters for a practical reason: it is where the QR code arrives and where the reminder arrives. A reservation with a mistyped email is a person you won't be able to notify. If you want to collect more information (company, job title, interests), ask after the event, to the people who came.

2. Measure your own no-show rate

You will find figures for "how many people skip free events" everywhere. None of them work for your event: it depends on the audience, the day of the week, the weather, the time and how far ahead people signed up. The only number that counts is yours, and getting it is easy:

  • In the event dashboard you see how many tickets were issued and the check-in counter, which adds up every QR code scanned at the door.
  • In the attendee export, each ticket shows its check-in status: "Ingresó" (checked in) or "Pendiente" (pending).
  • The next day you divide the people who got in by the people who reserved. That is your number.

With two or three events measured you already have a fairly solid idea. Write it down along with what changed each time (day, time, how early you opened reservations): that way you see what raises attendance and what doesn't.

3. Overbooking: only if you have the data and the venue allows it

With your rate in hand, you can accept a few more reservations than spots, like restaurants do. But with two conditions that are not negotiable:

  • You must have measured it. Without your own data, overbooking is a gamble.
  • The total can't exceed the venue's authorized capacity. Overbooking is done on the chairs or the comfortable space, not on what the venue is permitted to hold. If one day everyone shows up, they all have to fit.

Start small and go up gradually from one event to the next. In ENTRÁ each ticket type has its capacity and you can edit it from the dashboard, so you can open with the exact capacity and add spots if you see reservations coming in strong. Free reservations take up capacity just like a sale and, when it fills, that type shows as sold out.

4. The reminder, the day before

The most common cause of an empty chair is not lack of interest: it is forgetting. ENTRÁ automatically sends a reminder email the day before the event, with the subject "Es mañana" (it's tomorrow) and the event name, date, time and venue. It goes to each person with a valid ticket, one per person even if they reserved several. You don't have to schedule anything.

Two details so it goes out properly:

  • If the event is loaded with a date to be confirmed, the reminder doesn't go out. Enter the real date as soon as you have it.
  • If you also want to remind people on your social media or newsletter, do it on the same day or the day before, not a week earlier.

5. Free up the spots of people who say they can't come

Some people do let you know. Don't lose that spot: from the attendee list in the dashboard you use the option to return that ticket. It is cancelled, its QR code stops working at the door and the spot goes back to the capacity of that ticket type, so someone else can reserve it. A returned ticket doesn't get the reminder either.

For people to tell you, ask them: in the event description and in your posts, a line like "if you can't make it, message us and we'll give your spot to someone else" works better than any threat.

What to avoid

  • Opening reservations too early. The further away the date, the more people forget.
  • Making up a "standard" overbooking rate. If you didn't measure it, you don't know it.
  • Letting in people without a QR code who didn't reserve without counting them. If you are going to let in people from the door, do it once you know how many reserved guests arrived. The rest of the door setup is in how access control works on the day of the event.
  • Mixing guests with reservations. If you have named guests, give them comps separately; it is explained in complimentary tickets and guest lists.

Frequently asked questions

How many people skip a free event with reservations?

There is no number that works for every event, and be wary of anyone who gives you one. It depends on the audience, the day, the weather and how far ahead people signed up. The useful thing is to measure it at your own event: compare how many reserved with how many got in and use that figure for the next date.

Should I accept more reservations than spots?

Only if you have your own no-show data and the venue is authorized for the extra people who might come. Overbooking is calculated within the authorized capacity, never above it: if everyone comes, they all have to fit. Without those two things, the capacity equals the spots you have.

Does ENTRÁ send a reminder to people who reserved?

Yes. The day before the event an email goes out with the subject "Es mañana" (it's tomorrow) to each person with a valid ticket, one per person even if they have several tickets. You don't need to schedule it. It doesn't go out if the event is loaded with a date to be confirmed.

How do I free up the spot of someone who says they can't come?

From the attendee list in the dashboard you use the option to return that ticket. It is cancelled, its QR code stops working and the spot goes back to the capacity of that ticket type, so someone else can reserve it.
