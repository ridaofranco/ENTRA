Guide for organizers

Summer event in the heat: water, shade and timing

By SOMOS DER · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Between December and February (summer in Argentina), the heat is part of the event even if you didn't invite it. A line in the sun, a bar without water or a patio with no shade at five in the afternoon ruin the night before it starts, and in extreme heat they can become a health problem. This guide goes step by step: timing, water, shade, what to say when selling and what to do if there is an alert.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). Summer in Argentina runs from December to February, and the rules and alerts quoted here are Argentine.

Timing: the heat decides more than the calendar

In summer, the event time is chosen by the sun, not just by the venue's calendar. In practice:

  • Outdoors, better once the sun goes down. If it has to be during the day, make sure the busiest part is in the shade.
  • Think about the line, not just the event. People arrive before doors open and wait outside. If the entrance faces the sun, open with enough margin for the line to move fast, or set it up on a shaded side.
  • Stagger arrivals if you can. A wide entry window ("entry from 7 to 9 pm") spreads people out and avoids everyone waiting at once.
  • Indoors, the heat counts too. Ask the venue what ventilation or cooling it has and whether it is enough at full capacity.

Water: nobody should have to ask for it

Water is the first thing to run out and the thing people complain about most. Before selling, sort out:

  • Free drinking water, in plain sight. Signposted water points, easy to find, not inside the restrooms, with cups or a place to refill.
  • If you sell water at the bar, have plenty of stock.
  • Decide whether people can bring a bottle. If they can, say how (empty, without the cap, plastic) so the door doesn't decide case by case.

In the province of Buenos Aires there is a law. Law 15.439 requires venues licensed to sell alcohol for consumption on the premises, stadiums and mass events to offer free, sufficient and unlimited drinking water. It calls for freely accessible dispensers, signposted, clean and separate from the restrooms: it is not met if the water only comes from the bathrooms. For mass events, above the attendance set by the regulations, it adds a rest area with a hydration point and a first aid post run by the organizer. If you hold the event in another province or municipality, ask what applies there when you request the permit.

Shade and rest

  • Shade where people stay. In front of the stage, in the bar line and at the entrance. Shade cloth, gazebos or the side that doesn't get the afternoon sun.
  • A place to slow down. An area with shade, seats and water, away from the speakers, where someone who feels unwell can sit down.
  • Someone who knows what to do. Decide who helps if a person feels faint and how emergency services are called. If the venue has no medical service, hire one or confirm which one is nearest.
  • Look after the team too. Whoever works the door or the bar is on their feet for hours in the heat: water, shade and shifts.

How to say it in the description and on the ticket

Whatever is not written before you sell gets argued at the door. The event description is what the buyer accepts. It should say:

  • The door opening time and, if there is one, the latest entry time.
  • Whether there is shade and where, and if the venue is indoors, whether it has ventilation or air conditioning.
  • Where there is free water and whether people can bring a bottle.
  • What to bring: a cap, sunscreen, light clothes.
  • What happens if there is an extreme heat alert that day (see below).

The ticket that arrives by email carries the event name, date, time and venue. That is why the time you load has to be the real one. If there is an extreme heat alert

Argentina's National Weather Service (Servicio Meteorológico Nacional) runs an extreme temperature alert system for heat, with color levels according to the effect it can have on health, from yellow to red. Current alerts can be checked at smn.gob.ar. What to do:

  • Decide before selling what you will do in each case. For example: with an alert, water and shade are reinforced; at the highest level, the event moves to the evening or to another date. Write it in the description.
  • Check the forecast and alerts in the days before, not on the morning of the event, and tell your suppliers there may be changes.
  • Set a decision time and a single channel for announcements, just like with rain. The step by step is in outdoor event and rain: how to set up plan B.
  • If you change the time or date, edit it in the dashboard. The ticket stays valid and the QR is the same. Editing the event does not email the buyers: you send the notice yourself with the exported list. If someone can't go, they can pass their ticket on with the official transfer, if you have it enabled, or request a refund under the policy you announced.

Frequently asked questions

What time should doors open at a summer event?

When the sun is no longer beating down on the line, and with enough margin so nobody waits outside too long. If the event is during the day, the line needs shade or entry needs to be staggered. The door opening time goes in the event description before you sell.

Is it mandatory to provide free water at an event?

In the province of Buenos Aires, Law 15.439 requires venues that sell alcohol for consumption on the premises, stadiums and mass events to offer free and unlimited drinking water, at signposted points separate from the restrooms. In other provinces and municipalities, check the local rules when you request the permit.

What do I do if the National Weather Service issues an extreme heat alert for the day of the event?

Apply the plan you announced when selling: move the time to the evening, reinforce water and shade or move to another date. If you change the date or time in ENTRÁ, the ticket stays valid, but you send the notice to each buyer yourself using the list exported from the dashboard.

What should the event page say about the heat?

The door opening time, whether there is shade and where, where there is free water, whether people can bring a bottle and what to bring. Also what happens if there is an extreme heat alert that day, so the buyer knows before paying.
