Guide for organizers

A town festival or patron saint festival with tickets: what to charge for and what not

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

The town festival, the patron saint festival (the yearly celebration of a town's patron saint, common across Argentina) or a small provincial festival is almost never an event with a single door. There is mass and a procession, a parade, a crafts fair, a food court, and at night a stage with acts that cost money. It is organized by the municipality, a neighborhood committee or the local club, and at the end someone has to account for what came in. This guide sorts out what to charge for, how to sell several days and how to reach the final accounts with clear numbers.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First, separate what is charged from what is free

Most of these festivals have an open part, which is what makes it the town's festival, and a ticketed part, which pays for the acts. Before opening sales, sit down with the committee and make two lists:

  • Free: the mass, the procession, the parade, the crafts fair, the kids' games in the square, the midday folk gathering in the street.
  • Ticketed: the main stage at night, the dance in the club hall, the dinner show, the festival queen pageant if it is held indoors.

In ENTRÁ you load as an event only what has a ticket. The free part doesn't need to be on the ticketing platform, and it is better if it isn't: if a neighbor sees a link for the procession they will think they have to pay. What does help is for the event page to say, in the first line of the description, what the ticket includes and what is free: "The ticket is for the main stage grounds. The fair, the parade and the food court in the square are free to attend".

If a free part has limited space (a community lunch with a set number of places, a talk in a small hall), it can be set up as a separate event, free with reservation.

Sections within the grounds

Many festivals have more than one way to see the stage: general standing, chairs or seating at the front, a table area for dinner. Each one is a ticket type with its own name, price and cap, and when one sells out the others stay on sale. ENTRÁ has no numbered seat map, so within each section seating is first come, first served. Write it on the page and say what time doors open.

For neighbors with a discount (retirees, club members, kids), the simplest way is a discount code by percentage or fixed amount, with a usage cap if you want. In the list of codes you see how many times each one was used, and that helps later with the accounts.

Several days: season pass or ticket per night

A patron saint festival often runs over a long weekend. In ENTRÁ you turn on the multi-day option and load each night with its date and time. Then you choose how people get in, and that choice applies to the whole event:

  • One QR for the whole event: the same QR works every night, once per night. It is the festival pass.
  • One QR per day: the buyer chooses which nights they go to and gets a QR for each one. The price is per day.

The two can't be mixed within the same event. If you want to sell a pass and also single tickets for the night of the big act, set up two events: one with the pass and another with the tickets per night, and make it clear on both pages. Which one suits your audience is in festival pass or day ticket.

Keep in mind that each purchase takes up to 4 tickets per type. A family of six buys in two goes, or mixes types (four general and two retiree). It's not a problem, but it is worth saying on the page so nobody thinks it sold out.

The town buys at the door

In a small town, a lot of people decide to go that same night, and some aren't used to buying online. What happens in smaller provincial towns with advance sales and word of mouth is in an event in a small provincial town. For the door:

  • ENTRÁ has no box office and doesn't record cash payments. Whoever arrives without a ticket buys from their phone, without creating an account, with name, email and ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID). Put up a big sign with the QR of the link in the line, away from the scanner.
  • If the committee decides to also take cash, that goes separately: a numbered ticket book, cash counted at closing and a sheet signed by two people. Don't mix that money with what came in through the ticketing platform, because it is accounted for differently.
  • Weak signal on the grounds: if the internet drops, scanned check-ins are saved on the phone. Still, anyone wanting to buy in the line needs data, so find the spot with the best signal for the sign.

Scanning is done from the phone's browser: green with the name and ticket type, red with the reason. If someone can't find the email, they are looked up by name, email or ID number. The whole door setup is in selling tickets at the door.

Guests of the municipality and the committee

At a town festival a lot of people get in without paying: officials, musicians and their families, local press, those who helped organize, the priest. If that list lives on a piece of paper at the door, it takes up space without you noticing. Load them as complimentary tickets: name, email, ID number, ticket type, quantity and a reason ("Guest band", "Municipality"). They get the QR by email. Complimentary tickets don't come out of the sales cap, but they do count toward the total capacity the dashboard shows, so if the grounds hold a thousand people and you are giving out a hundred invitations, sell nine hundred.

If it rains or a night is called off

Festivals in provincial towns are almost always outdoors. Decide beforehand, and write it on the page, what happens if it rains: it moves to another night, it moves to the club hall or it is called off. Cancelling an event from the ENTRÁ dashboard emails every buyer that it was cancelled, but what comes next (new date or refund) you explain yourself, with the exported attendee list, and also announce it on the radio and on the municipality's social media. What to do with the tickets is in event cancelled or rescheduled.

The takings to account for

When the money belongs to a committee, a club or the municipality, the accounts matter as much as the festival. With ENTRÁ you collect 100% of the price you set for each ticket; service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying. When it's over, download the two spreadsheets from the event dashboard:

  • The attendee list, one row per ticket: type, source (purchase or complimentary), price, whether they checked in and the answers to the extra questions.
  • The sales spreadsheet, one row per order: buyer, quantity, subtotal, fee, total, status, payment method, source and campaign.

With that you put the accounts together on one sheet: tickets sold by type and by night, complimentary tickets and who got them, discounts used, and separately what was taken in cash if there was any. How to close those numbers is in the balance sheet after the event. Permits, insurance and taxes for a festival organized by a committee or a club depend on each place: check with your municipality before setting prices.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell only the show on ENTRÁ and leave the rest of the festival free?

Yes. You load as an event only what has a ticket, for example the main stage or the enclosed grounds at night, with its ticket types and cap. The crafts fair, the mass, the parade and the open food courts stay outside the event and don't need a ticket.

How do I sell three nights of festival: pass or ticket per night?

With the multi-day event you choose once how people get in: one QR for the whole event, valid once per day, or one QR per day, where the buyer chooses which nights to attend. That choice applies to the whole event. If you want to sell both, set up two events: one with the pass and another with the tickets per night.

Does ENTRÁ record what is taken in cash at the door?

No. ENTRÁ has no box office and doesn't record cash payments. A neighbor who arrives without a ticket buys from their phone in the line, without creating an account. If the committee decides to also take cash, that is accounted for separately, with its own ticket book or sheet.
