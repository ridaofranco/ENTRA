Gaming tournament: how to sell player and spectator tickets

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

A tournament or video game event has two audiences who pay differently and need different things: those who play, who take a station and have to be in the bracket, and those who come to watch, cheer or try out games. Mixing them in a single ticket is the fastest way to run out of stations or end up with people you didn't know were going to play. This guide goes in order: ticket types, capacity, sign up, teams and the door.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Player and spectator: two ticket types

At ENTRÁ each ticket type has its own name, price and quota. For a tournament, the basics are:

  • Player ticket. Includes the place in the tournament and access to the event. Its price can include the bracket entry.
  • Spectator ticket. For those who come to watch, tag along or walk around the event. Cheaper, and with a larger quota.
  • One per game or category, if needed. If there is more than one tournament at the same event and each has its own number of stations, load one player ticket per game, each with its quota.

In the event description say what each ticket includes: whether players need to bring their own peripherals, which platform is used, the player check-in time and the rules. With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket; the service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, itemized at checkout.

Capacity: stations, bracket and venue

The player quota isn't set by the venue but by the tournament. Count before publishing:

  • Gaming stations. How many computers, consoles or screens there are, and how many matches are played at once.
  • Bracket size. Many formats work better with a number of players that fits the bracket with no gaps. Choose the format and set that number as the quota.
  • Time. How many rounds fit within the event's hours. A bracket that doesn't finish before closing is a problem.
  • The venue's licensed capacity, for the total of players, spectators, staff and complimentary tickets together.

The dashboard shows sales in real time by ticket type, and when a type runs out it shows as sold out. If the tournament lasts more than one day, ENTRÁ lets you load a multi-day event; the quota is set per ticket type for the whole event, so if each day has a different tournament with its own cap, load each one as a separate event.

Sign up: nickname, game and whatever you need

A tournament needs data a regular ticket doesn't ask for. At ENTRÁ you can add extra questions to the event that the buyer fills in at checkout. They can be text, number, options or long text. The most useful:

  • Nickname or username, with a text field. It is the name they will appear under in the bracket.
  • Game or category, with an options question, if there is more than one.
  • Platform or account ID, if the game needs it to set up matches.
  • Team name, if it is played in teams.

The answers come as a column in the attendee export, along with the name, ticket type and whether the person checked in. With that you build the bracket in a spreadsheet. How to think through a sign up with limited spots step by step is in sign ups for a workshop with limited spots: the logic is the same.

Teams: one ticket per member

In team games, the tidiest approach is for each member to have their own player ticket. That way each one gets in with their own QR, and if someone doesn't show up you know at the door. To keep the team together:

  • An extra question with the team name, which every member fills in the same way.
  • One person buying for everyone, if they prefer, in a single purchase.
  • A last minute change. If a member can't go, the official transfer generates a new QR in the name of the replacement and invalidates the previous one. Agree in the rules whether changes are allowed and until when.

Streamers, casters and guests

Whoever commentates, streams and the event's guests shouldn't take player stations. Load them as complimentary tickets: at ENTRÁ each complimentary ticket is loaded with the person's details, the ticket type and a reason, and reaches them by email with its QR. They don't come out of the sales quota but they do count toward capacity.

The door and player check-in

Each ticket has a unique QR that is valid only once and is scanned from the phone's browser, and the scanner shows the ticket type. It is a good idea to separate the player line from the spectator line, with an earlier arrival time for players. If someone forgot their QR, you look them up by name, email or ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID). When check-in closes, the export tells you which players checked in and which didn't, so you can adjust the bracket before the first round.

Frequently asked questions

How do I separate player tickets from spectator tickets at a tournament?

With two different ticket types, each with its own price and quota. The player quota is the number of stations or places in the tournament bracket, and the spectator quota is what is left of the venue's capacity.

How do I ask for the nickname and game at sign up?

Add extra questions to the event: a text field for the nickname and an options question for the game or category. The answers come as a column in the attendee export, ready to build the bracket.

How do I sign up a whole team?

The simplest is for each member to have their own player ticket, so each one gets in with their own QR. Add an extra question with the team name to group them later in the export.
