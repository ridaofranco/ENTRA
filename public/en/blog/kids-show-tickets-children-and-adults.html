Kids show: child and adult tickets, babies and the door

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

A kids show is sold to children but bought by adults, and at the door whole families arrive with strollers, coats and in a hurry. This guide sorts out the decisions worth making before you sell: ticket types, babies, schedule and how to keep the door flowing.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Child and adult as ticket types

At a kids show almost no child comes alone, and almost no adult comes without a child. The clearest approach is to separate them into ticket types:

  • "Child", with the age from which you charge in the name or description ("Child, from age 2").
  • "Adult", for whoever comes along.
  • A group type, if you want to offer one: "Family, 2 adults and 2 children", with its own price. Count it as four spots in the room when working out the capacity.

In ENTRÁ each ticket type has its name, price and capacity, and each QR code is valid only once at the scanner. If a family buys four tickets, that is four QR codes, one per person.

A decision worth making before loading: does the adult pay the same as the child? At many kids shows the target audience is the child, but the one taking up a bigger seat is the adult. Both options are valid; what doesn't work is people finding out at the door.

Do babies pay?

It is the question you will get asked most, and you decide the answer. There is no single rule: it depends on the venue, the capacity and the type of show. What does have to happen is that it is written down, in the description and on the flyer:

  • From what age people pay: "Under 2s go free" or "Everyone pays from birth".
  • Whether they take a seat or sit on a lap: a baby on a lap doesn't take a seat; a three year old does.
  • Whether they still need a ticket, even if they don't pay. If you want them registered for the capacity, you can issue them a comp from the dashboard, with name, email and reason. If not, count them by hand at the door.

Watch the capacity: even if babies have no ticket, they are still in the room. If the venue has an authorized capacity, the count has to include everyone.

The schedule is part of the product

For a family, the time decides whether they go or not. What to write:

  • Doors open time and start time, separately. With kids, arriving fifteen minutes early is time for the bathroom and settling in.
  • How long it lasts. A parent wants to know whether they will make it back for nap time or the afternoon snack.
  • What ages it is for: a show for 2 to 5 year olds is not the same as one for 6 to 10 year olds.
  • Whether people can come in once the show has started, and whether there is somewhere to leave strollers.

How to organize all of that on the page is in what to write on the event page so people buy.

The door with families

A family at the door is slow: whoever has the phone with the QR codes is carrying one child, looking for another's coat and watching the line. So it doesn't jam:

  • Open early. Families arrive early and in groups.
  • Scan all of a family's QR codes in a row, from the same phone. If someone can't find the email, they can be looked up by surname, ID number (DNI) or email from the scanner.
  • Look at the ticket type when scanning. The scanner shows the type of each QR: if it says "Child" and an adult walks in, you see it right away.
  • Be clear about what happens with a baby without a ticket, so the person at the door doesn't have to decide on the spot.
  • If there are numbered seats or sections, have someone inside to seat people, so the door doesn't stall explaining where to sit.

Frequently asked questions

How do I sell child and adult tickets for the same event?

With two ticket types, "Child" and "Adult", each with its own price and capacity. Put in the name or description the age from which someone counts as a child, and each person gets their own QR code.

Do babies have to pay for a ticket?

The organizer decides, depending on the venue and the capacity. What matters is that it is written down before selling: from what age people pay, whether babies sit on a lap and whether they still need a ticket. If the venue has an authorized capacity, babies count too.

What time works best for a kids show?

One that families can fit between nap time and the afternoon snack, depending on the audience's age. Write separately the doors open time, the start time and how long it lasts, so nobody arrives late or leaves halfway through.
