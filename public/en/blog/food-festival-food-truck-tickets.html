Guide for organizers

A food festival or food truck event with tickets

By SOMOS DER · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

A food festival brings together a lot of people who arrive hungry at the same time, stay a while and leave. Food trucks on a showground, a regional food fair, a craft beer festival with food stalls: they all have the same questions. Whether to charge admission, whether the ticket includes something, how to spread the audience over the day so there isn't an hour of queues, and what happens if it rains. This guide takes it step by step.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

General admission or a ticket with food included

The first thing is to decide what people buy when they buy the ticket. There are two ways, and they can coexist:

  • General admission. It pays for entry, and inside everyone buys what they want at the stalls. It is the simplest: it covers part of your costs (venue, setup, sound, security, cleaning) and the stalls sell their own products.
  • Ticket with food or drink included. It includes something specific: "Ticket + dish", "Ticket + beer", "Ticket + 3 tastings". It gives you money up front and certainty about how much each stall has to prepare, but it requires agreeing with the stalls on what is served and how much you pay them for each voucher.

In ENTRÁ each one is a ticket type, with its own name, price and cap. If the included item has a limit (the portions a stall can make, the kegs a brewery brings), set that limit as the cap of the "included" type. When it runs out, it shows as sold out and general admission stays on sale.

The name has to say what it includes without opening the description, because that is what the person on the door will see on the scanner. "Ticket + dish" is clear; "Gourmet Pass" isn't.

How what is included gets redeemed

Let's be clear here: the ENTRÁ QR is valid only once and it is for the door. ENTRÁ doesn't keep track of what each person picks up at each stall. What the scanner does is show the ticket type of each QR, so the door knows on the spot who has food or drink included.

The included item is handled with something physical handed out right there:

  • Tokens or vouchers in a color specific to the festival, one for each included item. The stall collects and keeps them, and at the end of the day you pay them against the tokens redeemed.
  • A wristband for those with something included, which the stall marks or cuts when serving. It works if the combo includes several items.
  • A card to stamp if the included item is a tasting route: each stall adds its stamp.

Count the tokens before opening, agree in writing with each stall how much you pay per token and say in the description until what time they can be redeemed. All the detail is in tickets with a drink or combo included: how to set them up and control them.

The price

  • General admission covers your fixed costs, not the food. Add up venue, setup, sound, toilets, cleaning, security and promotion, and divide it by the tickets you really think you will sell, not by a full venue.
  • What the stalls pay you is separate income. If you charge a fee per stall or a percentage of what they sell, arrange it separately and in writing. Don't mix it with the ticket.
  • The ticket with food included has to be worth it. It should cost a bit less than buying the ticket and the dish separately on the day. If it costs the same, nobody buys it in advance.

With ENTRÁ you collect 100% of the price you set for each ticket. Service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying. If you want tiers, you can mark types as presale and launch the official sale from the dashboard whenever you want.

Time slots so it doesn't collapse

The typical problem at a food festival is the peak: at lunch and dinner time everyone wants to eat at once, and the queues at the stalls ruin the experience. One way to manage it is to sell by time slot.

ENTRÁ has no built-in feature for time slots or schedules per ticket. What it does have is ticket types with their own name and cap, and that is enough:

  • One type per slot, with the time at the start of the name: "Saturday 12 to 4 pm", "Saturday 4 to 8 pm", "Saturday 8 pm to midnight".
  • For each slot, the cap of people the venue and the stalls can serve at once. When a slot fills up, it closes on its own and the others stay on sale.
  • If you combine slots with food included, you multiply: "Saturday 12 to 4 pm · General" and "Saturday 12 to 4 pm · With dish". Each type has its own cap and they are not shared, so split the slot's total between the two.

The scanner shows the type of each QR but doesn't reject someone for arriving in another slot. That is up to the person on the door. Write the rule in the description ("your ticket is valid for the slot you chose") and decide beforehand what to do with latecomers. If the festival runs several days, you can also load it as a multi-day event and choose whether the QR is valid for every day, once per day, or whether the buyer picks which days they attend. Stalls, helpers and staff

The food truck crews, their helpers and your staff shouldn't come out of the same cap as the public without you knowing. Issue them complimentary tickets from the dashboard, with name and reason ("Stall"), so each one has their own QR, or keep a separate list at a service entrance and subtract those people from the cap by hand. If the festival is on public streets or at a showground, licensing and permits depend on the local council; for the City of Buenos Aires (CABA) there is a guide in permits to hold an event in CABA.

The door

  • The QR is scanned from a phone. Each QR is valid only once (or once per day, if it is multi-day). If someone sent a screenshot to someone else, whoever arrives first gets in.
  • Whoever scans hands out the tokens. They scan, look at the type, and if it includes food or drink they hand over what is due. They should have the tokens at hand, not in a box that has to be opened.
  • If someone can't find the email, they are looked up from the scanner by surname, ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID) or email.
  • A sign with the event link for anyone who arrives without a ticket, while sales are still open and there is room.

If it rains

A food truck festival is almost always outdoors, so the backup plan is decided before selling the first ticket. Three paths: it goes ahead with tents or a covered area, it moves to an indoor venue, or it moves to a rain date. In all three cases, check with the stalls: a food truck can work in the rain, but the public won't queue for twenty minutes in it.

Announce from day one the alternative date, the time you will confirm and where you will post it. If the date moves, you edit it from the dashboard and the ticket stays valid with the same QR; you notify buyers yourself, exporting the list from the dashboard. Frequently asked questions

Is it better to sell the ticket alone or with food included?

Both can coexist. General admission covers entry and everyone buys at the stalls. The ticket with food or drink included covers something specific, for example a dish or a drink, and is loaded as another ticket type with its own price and cap. At the door a token or voucher is handed out, which the stall exchanges for the included item.

Does ENTRÁ have time slots for a festival?

Not as a built-in feature. It is solved with one ticket type per slot, with the time in the name and its own cap, for example "Saturday 12 to 4 pm" and "Saturday 4 to 8 pm". When a slot fills up, it closes on its own. The scanner shows the type of each QR, but the time is checked by the person on the door.

What happens to tickets if the festival is postponed because of rain?

If you edit the date from the dashboard, the ticket stays valid for the new date with the same QR. You notify buyers yourself, exporting the list from the dashboard. If you cancel the event from the dashboard, ENTRÁ emails everyone who bought.
