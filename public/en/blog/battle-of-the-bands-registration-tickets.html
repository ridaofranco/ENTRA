Battle of the bands: band sign-ups, tickets and how to split the money

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

A band contest or battle of the bands is two sales in one: the bands that pay to take part and the audience that pays to see them. On top of that there is a jury, a prize and the question that always comes up at the end of the night: which band brought whom. If all of that is not written down before sign-ups open, it ends in an argument. This guide puts it in order.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Two events: sign-ups and the show

Band sign-ups and audience tickets are different things, with different audiences, prices and dates. In ENTRÁ the clearest way is to load them as two events:

  • Sign-up event: "Battle of the bands 2026 · Sign-ups". One ticket type, "Band sign-up", priced at the entry fee and with a cap equal to the number of slots in the contest. When the slots fill up, sign-ups close on their own.
  • Show event: "Battle of the bands 2026 · Final", or one date per round. This is where the audience tickets go, with their types and their cap.

Keeping them apart has concrete advantages: the show page does not display the sign-up, each event has its own list and its own revenue, and if a round sells out the sign-up is not affected. If there are several rounds (qualifiers, semifinal, final), it is worth having one event per round, with its own link and its own door.

What to ask when a band signs up

When you create the sign-up event you can add extra questions for the buyer and mark them as required. They are answered once per purchase, which here means once per band, exactly what you need:

  • Band name and city.
  • Number of members and a contact phone.
  • A link to a recorded song or a live video, if there is a pre-selection.
  • Technical needs: whether they bring their own drum kit, how many microphones they use, whether they have keyboards.

The contest rules have to be in the description, complete, before the first sign-up: how long each set lasts, how many original songs and covers, what backline the organizers provide, how bands are judged, what the prize is and what happens to the entry fee if a band doesn't show up or the date is called off. The sign-up is a sale: if the band pays and the rules change afterwards, they are right to complain.

The sign-up QR belongs to that event, not to the show: it does not get anyone in to play. For the musicians, issue complimentary tickets in the show event from the dashboard, with name, email and reason ("Band 3 · guitar"). That way each member has their own QR and the show list reflects everyone who is inside.

Audience tickets

At a small contest the audience is mostly the friends and family of each band. Some decisions to make before opening sales:

  • One price or tiers. A cheaper presale until a date and then the general price pushes people to buy early. Each tier is a ticket type with its own cap. How to set them up is in how to price your tickets: tiers and presale.
  • How many tickets per purchase. In ENTRÁ each purchase takes up to 4 tickets per type. A big group of friends needs several purchases, and it is good that they know it.
  • The venue cap, counting the musicians from every band who will be inside. Complimentary tickets don't come out of the sales cap, but they do count toward the total capacity the dashboard shows. If the bar holds two hundred people and there are six bands of five members, sell one hundred seventy at most.

Tracking who brings the audience

If the split, the running order or part of the score depends on how many people each band brings, you have to track it from day one. In ENTRÁ there are two ways:

  • One link per band, with a different parameter, for example ?utm_campaign=band-3. The event dashboard shows where sales came from and the export includes the source and campaign columns.
  • One discount code per band, by percentage or fixed amount, with a usage cap and expiry date if you want. In the list of codes you see how many times each one was used.

Both count purchases, not tickets: if someone buys four with a band's link, that is one purchase. To count people, look at the quantity column in the sales export. Whatever comes in without a link is not assigned to any band; agree beforehand whether it goes into a common pot. More on tracking channels in how to promote an event and track what sells.

Jury and audience vote

The most delicate point of a contest is who wins. Some rules that prevent trouble:

  • A jury with a written scoring sheet: what is judged (original songs, playing, stage presence, sound) and how much each item is worth. Publish it in the rules.
  • Judges with no ties to the bands. If one of them plays in, produces or is related to a band, they step out of that vote.
  • A limited audience vote. If tickets sold count as votes, the band with the most friends wins and the rest feel cheated. Better one vote per person who got in, on paper or on a form, adding a small part of the score.
  • The result, on the spot, with score sheets signed by hand. If someone complains, there is something to show.

The split and the prize

Agree in writing, before sign-ups open, what happens with the money. The usual setup:

  • Entry fees cover the fixed costs: sound, backline, venue rental, promotion. Anything left over goes to the prize.
  • Audience tickets are split between the organizers, the venue and, if agreed, the bands according to what each one sold.
  • The prize, stating what it is and when it is handed over: money, studio hours, a paid gig at the venue, an opening slot at a bigger show.

With ENTRÁ you collect 100% of the price you set for each ticket. Service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying. Ways to split between several bands, with examples, are in a show with several bands: how to split the takings.

Contest night

  • A published running order with times. If the order is decided by who sold the most, say so in the rules.
  • One fixed person at the door, who is not in any band. Scanning is done from the phone's browser: green with the name and ticket type, red with the reason. If the signal drops, check-ins are saved on the phone.
  • Whoever arrives without a ticket buys from their phone, without creating an account. ENTRÁ has no box office and does not record cash, so put up a sign with the QR of the event link in the line.
  • If the date is called off, cancelling the event from the dashboard emails every buyer that it was cancelled, but the details are yours: export the list and write to every buyer and every registered band.

Frequently asked questions

Do I charge the band entry fee in the same event as the audience tickets?

Better to keep them separate. Create one event for sign-ups, with a "Band sign-up" type and a cap equal to the contest slots, and another event for the show with the audience tickets. That way the bands don't appear on the public sales page and each one has its own list and revenue.

How do I know how many people each band brought?

Give each band a link with its own utm_campaign or its own discount code. The dashboard shows where each purchase came from and the sales export includes the number of tickets in each purchase. Whatever comes in without a link counts as a direct sale.

Can ticket sales count as the audience vote?

They can, but if you do that, the contest is won by the band with the most friends. The fairest way is for the jury to decide with a written scoring sheet and for the audience to add a limited share, for example one vote on paper or on a form per person who got in, not per ticket sold.
