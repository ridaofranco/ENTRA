Ticket service fee: what it is and when you should see it

By SOMOS DER · November 9, 2026 · 6 min read

The service fee is what gets added to a ticket price to pay for the ticketing platform and for processing the payment. It is not a tax, and it is not something the event organizer decides. Only one thing really matters: you should see it before you pay, with the final total in front of you. If it shows up afterwards, something is wrong.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). This guide describes how the service fee works when you buy a ticket on ENTRÁ.

What it is and why it exists

When you buy a ticket online, two different things are at play:

  • The ticket price. Set by the organizer: the band, the theater, the club, the production company. It is what it costs to attend the event.
  • The service fee. Charged by the platform for everything that happens between your click and walking through the door: the sales system, issuing your ticket with a QR code, the email with your ticket, the check at the door, support if something goes wrong and the payment processing.

That last point matters. Accepting cards or digital wallets has a cost for whoever receives the money, and someone pays it. Each platform decides how to split it: some deduct it from the organizer, others add it for the buyer, others mix both.

Who keeps it on ENTRÁ

On ENTRÁ the model is simple:

  • The organizer receives 100% of the price they set for the ticket. If the ticket costs ARS 10,000, they get ARS 10,000.
  • The buyer pays, separately, the service fee, which includes ENTRÁ's service and the payment processing.
  • Free events have no fee: the reservation costs nothing.

Payments are processed by Mercado Pago, with whatever payment methods you have enabled in your account at the time you pay.

When you should see it

Before you confirm. Always. Argentina's Consumer Protection Law (Law 24,240) says in its article 4 that sellers must inform the conditions of sale in a way that is accurate, clear and detailed. A fee that only appears at the last step, or worse, after you pay, goes against that idea.

On ENTRÁ the checkout reads like this, top to bottom:

  • The ticket type and quantity, with its price.
  • If you entered a valid discount code, a line with the discount.
  • A line called Cargo por servicio (service fee), with the note «Incluye el servicio de ENTRÁ y el procesamiento del pago» (includes ENTRÁ's service and the payment processing).
  • The Total. That is the amount you are charged. Nothing is added afterwards.

If the event is free, instead of the fee you will see the commission listed as free.

How to compare before buying

If you are choosing between two events, or between buying now or at the door, the number that matters is the total, not the ticket price alone. Three habits that save surprises:

  • Look at the total for the quantity you are buying. The fee is calculated on what you buy, so with four tickets it is not the same as with one.
  • Enter your discount code before looking at the total, if you have one. That way you see the real number.
  • Take a screenshot of the breakdown before paying. If there is a dispute later, you have the exact details you were shown.

Signs that something is off

  • You are asked for a «fee» after paying, by email or message, to «release» or «activate» the ticket. A paid ticket is not activated by paying again. It is one of the most common scams.
  • Someone sells you the ticket outside the platform with a «handling» surcharge. That surcharge has nothing to do with the service fee, and nobody guarantees the ticket is genuine.
  • Your card charge does not match the total. Before you panic, check whether you chose installments: the financing cost of installments is disclosed by the payment provider, not by the ticketing platform. If you paid in one go and it doesn't match, write to support.

And if the event is cancelled

What happens to what you paid, ticket and fee, depends on what happened to the event and on what the law says about distance purchases. Frequently asked questions

What is a ticket service fee?

It is what gets added to the ticket price to pay for the ticketing platform and for processing the payment. The ticket price is set by whoever organizes the event; the service fee is separate. On ENTRÁ it is shown on its own line at checkout, before you confirm the payment.

Does the organizer keep the service fee?

It depends on the platform. On ENTRÁ the organizer receives 100% of the price they set for the ticket, and the service fee covers ENTRÁ's service and the payment processing. Other platforms may work differently, so it is worth checking the breakdown before you pay.

Can a new charge appear after I pay for the ticket?

Not on ENTRÁ: the total you see at checkout is what gets charged and nothing is added afterwards. If your card statement shows a different amount, first check whether you chose installments, because the financing cost is disclosed by the payment provider. If it still doesn't add up, write to support with your order number and receipt.

Do free events have a service fee?

Not on ENTRÁ. When the event is free, the reservation has no charge at all: you confirm your details and get your QR code just like with a paid purchase.
