VIP or general admission: how to set up the ticket types for your event

By SOMOS DER · November 16, 2026 · 6 min read

For a small or medium event two or three ticket types are enough, and each one needs a difference people can see on the day: another area, another line, something included. A VIP ticket that is just more expensive won't sell. On ENTRÁ each type is set up with its own name, price and capacity, and the scanner shows the type of every ticket at the door.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). This guide explains how ticket types work with ENTRÁ.

A ticket type is not the same as a price tier

Before building the list, separate two things that often get mixed up:

  • Ticket type: the person gets something different. General, VIP, floor and seated, student, ticket with or without dinner.
  • Price tier: the person gets the same thing, but pays a different price depending on when they buy. Early bird, first tier, second tier, at the door.

If you mix both in the same list («VIP early bird», «VIP second tier», «general early bird», «general second tier») you end up with eight options and nobody knows which one is right for them. Define the types first and then, if needed, the tiers. How to step up prices is covered in how to price event tickets: tiers and early bird.

How many types to offer

The practical rule: every type has to justify itself at the door. If two types come in the same way, see the same thing and drink the same thing, they are one ticket with two prices, and that confuses people.

  • Just one (general): gigs in bars, small parties, workshops. It is the easiest to sell and to check.
  • Two (general and VIP, or general and reduced): most parties, shows and plays. You give people who want to spend more an option, without complicating things for everyone else.
  • Three: when the venue really has three different areas or experiences (for example floor, seats and a section with tables). More than three at a small event almost never helps.

What a VIP ticket must offer to sell

VIP sells when the difference is concrete and can be explained in one line. Some that work:

  • A better spot: front area, balcony, a section with seats or a view of the stage.
  • No queue: a separate line at the door. To make it true, you need one more person scanning at that line.
  • Something included: a drink, a gift, dinner. If it includes a drink, see ticket with a drink or food included for how to make sure it is used only once.
  • A separate bar or restrooms, which on a packed night are worth more than they seem.

Whatever you promise, you have to be able to deliver with the people and the space you have. A VIP ticket that promises «no queue» and ends up in the same line as everyone else is the guaranteed complaint of the night. And set the VIP capacity according to the real size of that area, not according to how many you would like to sell.

Discounted tickets: students, seniors, children

Reduced tickets help fill the venue, but only if they are checked. If anyone can buy the student ticket, everyone will.

  • Write the requirement in the ticket type name («Student with ID», «Under 12») and repeat it in the event description.
  • Announce that proof will be requested at the door and tell the person scanning what to do if someone doesn't have it (for example, pay the difference or not get in). Decide it beforehand, not on the spot.
  • If the benefit is for a closed group (followers, club members, students of a school), a discount code often works better than a separate ticket type. It is explained in discount codes for an event.

How to set it up on ENTRÁ

In the tickets section of the event, each row is a type, with Tipo de Entrada (ticket type name), Precio (price) and Cupo (Disponibles) (number available). With the Agregar otro tipo de entrada (add another ticket type) button you add as many as you need. What happens next:

  • Each type has its own capacity. If VIP sells out, the event page shows it as AGOTADO (sold out) and general stays on sale.
  • When a type has fewer than ten left, the page marks it as ÚLTIMOS ACCESOS (last spots).
  • Watch the name. If a type's name includes «preventa», «anticipada», «early», «promo» or «lote» (Spanish words for presale or batch), or you turn on Early Bird, ENTRÁ treats it as a presale: it is visible until you press Lanzar Venta Oficial (launch official sale) and then shows as finished. For a VIP ticket that must stay on sale the whole time, use a name without those words.
  • Complimentary tickets also have a type. When you create an invitation you choose its type, so a VIP guest comes in through the VIP line. How many to give is covered in how many complimentary tickets to give.

You receive 100% of the price you set for each type, and the buyer sees the service and processing fees broken down before paying.

On the day: each type to its place

  • The scanner tells you the type. When the QR is validated, the phone shows the authorized entry with the buyer's name and the ticket type.
  • One wristband color per type, put on in the same step as the scan. That way, inside, it is clear who can go into the VIP area without asking for the ticket again. If there is also re-entry, the same wristband works: see leaving and coming back in to an event.
  • A sign for each line with the exact name of the type, the same as on the event page. If the website says «Floor» and the door says «General», someone will end up in the wrong line.
  • Someone at the entrance to the area, not only at the street door. Without that check, an hour in the VIP area is full of people with general tickets.

Common mistakes

  • Unclear names («Gold Experience», «Full Pack») that don't say what is included.
  • A huge price gap between general and VIP with no real difference to explain it.
  • A VIP capacity bigger than the area, which ends with people standing where there is no room.
  • Reduced tickets with no check at the door, which eat into general ticket sales.

Frequently asked questions

How many ticket types should a small event have?

Two or three at most, for example general and VIP, or general and student. Every extra type is one more decision for the buyer and one more rule for the door. If two types don't differ in something visible on the day, merge them into one.

What should a VIP ticket include?

Something concrete that people can see and that you can deliver: an area with a better view, a separate entry line, its own bar or an included drink. Write it in the ticket name or in the event description. If the VIP ticket is only more expensive and offers nothing different, it won't sell or it will bring complaints.

Does each ticket type have its own capacity on ENTRÁ?

Yes. Each ticket type is set up with its name, its price and its number of available tickets. When a type sells out it shows as AGOTADO (sold out) while the others stay on sale, and when it has fewer than ten left the event page marks it as ÚLTIMOS ACCESOS (last spots).

How does the door know which ticket type each person has?

On ENTRÁ, when the QR is scanned the phone shows the buyer's name and the ticket type together with the authorized entry. With that, the door sends each person to their line or gives them the wristband color of their area.
