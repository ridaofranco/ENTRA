Guide for organizers

How to promote your event and track where sales come from

By SOMOS DER · July 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Promoting is the part everyone does. Measuring is the part almost nobody does, and it is the one that decides whether your next event costs you less or more. This guide covers both, with methods you can apply today without depending on tools you don't have.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

1. Before you spread the word: make sure the link looks good

Your sales link is your sales channel. It gets shared on WhatsApp, pasted in the Instagram bio, forwarded in groups. If pasting it shows a gray rectangle with a platform's logo, you lost 80% of the impact before you started.

Your event page on ENTRÁ is built for that: when WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, Telegram or X pick up the link, those bots receive your event's name, date and flyer, not the platform's generic details. The only thing you have to do is upload the flyer before sharing the link, not after. If you already shared it without an image, some apps keep that version cached for quite a while.

2. One link, and make it the official one

The temptation to set up intermediate links, URL shorteners and bridge pages is strong. Resist it. Every extra hop is a chance for someone to drop off and, more importantly, a chance for a fake copy of the link to show up in the same groups where you posted the real one.

Always share the same address, from your official accounts. When someone asks "is this link the real one?", the answer should be obvious because it is the only one that exists.

3. How to measure where sales came from (without analytics)

Here is the practical trick. The simplest, cheapest and most reliable way to know which channel sold for you is a different discount code per channel.

You create several codes in the dashboard, all with the same benefit, and give one to each front:

  • IG for the Instagram flyer.
  • GROUP for the WhatsApp group.
  • RADIO for the interview or the radio spot.
  • One for each person who promotes you: MARTINA, LEO, PABLO.

Each code has its own usage counter, and you can set a maximum cap. When sales end you open the list and you have, in numbers, how many tickets each front brought. It isn't perfect (it only counts people who actually used the code), but it is real, comparable data, instead of an argument about who moved more.

A side benefit: it gives each person who promotes you something concrete to offer. "Buy with my code" works much better than "buy".

4. The curve: not just how many, but when

Every sale is recorded with its date and time. Looking at that curve tells you things the total doesn't: whether the peak was the night you posted the flyer, whether sales died after three days, whether 40% came in during the last 48 hours.

With that you plan the next event properly: you know when you need to show up again and when spending makes no sense. And you know how many people buy at the last minute, which is the number that decides whether it makes sense to keep door sales.

5. The launch is a button: use it as news

In ENTRÁ you open official sales yourself, by hand, with the Lanzar Venta Oficial (launch official sales) button. There is no scheduled date that opens sales on its own while you sleep.

That, which looks like a technical detail, is a communication tool: you can leave everything loaded and checked, announce "we open on Thursday at 8 pm", and open exactly then. A launch at a specific time concentrates sales and gives you a clean peak to measure. An event that appears little by little gives nobody a reason to buy today.

6. People who already bought from you are your cheapest channel

At the end of each event, export the attendee list to CSV: name, email, ticket type. That list is yours, and it is the most valuable asset of everything you build.

For the next event, that list converts much better than any cold audience, because these are people who have already paid you once. Use it wisely: always identify yourself, send little, and leave a clear way to unsubscribe. Burning out your own list is the most expensive and quietest mistake you can make.

7. Things that work in your favor without you doing anything

  • The day before reminder. Everyone who bought gets an email the day before with the event details. It reduces no-shows without you lifting a finger.
  • The delivery log. You can see whether the email with the ticket went out or failed. A buyer who didn't get their ticket is a problem at the door and a bad review; catching it early is worth gold.
  • The event page indexed. While the event is published and upcoming, its page goes into the site's sitemap so search engines can find it.

What is not measuring

  • Story views. Reach is not sales. It only helps when compared against tickets sold in the same window.
  • "Loads of people messaged me". Messages don't pay for the venue.
  • Asking "how did you hear about us?" on the form without cross-checking it against anything. People answer the first thing that comes to mind.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know which social network my event's sales came from?

The simplest and most reliable way is to create a different discount code per channel: one for the Instagram flyer, another for the WhatsApp group, another for each person who promotes you. Each code has its own usage counter, so at the end you see how many tickets each one brought. It is an imperfect measurement, because it only counts people who used the code, but it is real and doesn't depend on anyone declaring anything.

When should I launch ticket sales for an event?

When you already have the date, venue, prices and flyer sorted, and you can keep up the promotion over the following days. In ENTRÁ, official sales are launched with a button, by hand: the event doesn't open on its own on a scheduled date. That lets you choose the moment and turn the launch into news, instead of a silent change nobody sees.

Why does my sales link look bad when I share it on WhatsApp?

Because the link preview is put together by a bot that reads the HTML and doesn't run JavaScript. On ENTRÁ, event pages give those bots the event's name, date and flyer, so the shared link shows your event and not the platform's generic logo. If it still looks bad, it is almost always because the flyer isn't uploaded yet or because the messaging app cached an old version of the link.

Can I use my event's buyer list to promote the next one?

Yes: from the dashboard you can export the attendee list to CSV, with name, email and ticket type. That list is yours and it is your cheapest channel for the next event, because these are people who already bought from you once. Use it wisely: say which event you are writing about, send little and always leave a clear way to unsubscribe.
