Guide for organizers

A tour across several cities: how to sell the tickets

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 7 min read

A band heading out from Buenos Aires to Rosario, Córdoba and Mendoza. A party that already works in its own city and wants to try three more. Taking something on tour multiplies everything: venues, prices, promotion and doors. What doesn't multiply is your time. This guide organizes sales so each date can be looked at on its own and the second one goes better than the first.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

One event per date

The temptation is to set up a single event called "Tour 2026" and put the cities in as ticket types. Don't. Each date has its own venue, capacity, price, schedule and door staff. Mixing them causes concrete problems:

  • Capacity: each venue has its authorized capacity. With one event per date, Rosario sales close on their own when Rosario fills up, without touching Córdoba.
  • The door: in the scanner you choose the event you are going to check. If each date is an event, a Córdoba ticket won't get in at Mendoza.
  • The numbers: each event's dashboard shows what was sold, what was collected and where each sale came from. With everything together, you don't know which city worked.

In ENTRÁ each event has its date, its venue and a "Ciudad / Zona" (city / area) field. Use a name that repeats the same structure for every date, for example "Show name · Rosario · 14/11", so it is recognizable in the listing and in the emails. Right now there is no button to duplicate an event, so each date is loaded from scratch. To make it quick, keep the base description, the ticket types and the refund policy text in a document, ready to copy and adjust.

If a city doesn't have a confirmed venue yet, you can create the event with the venue "Por definir" (to be confirmed) and fill it in later. It lets you announce the whole tour even if one room is still not closed.

Price per city

All dates don't have to cost the same. Each city has its own costs (venue, travel, accommodation, sound) and its own audience. Do the math date by date: what that night costs divided by the tickets you think you will sell there, not by a full room.

What is worth keeping is the same logic everywhere: if one date has a cheaper presale, all of them should. The audience compares, and a city that finds out another one paid less notices.

When to open each sale

There are two different moments that often get confused: announcing the tour and opening sales for each date.

  • The announcement can be a single one, with all the dates on one graphic. It builds anticipation and sets the calendar.
  • Sales for each date are best opened with a similar lead time before its show. If you open everything on the same day, the last city goes weeks without news and people forget. If you stagger them, each opening is fresh news for that city.
  • Big or distant dates can open earlier: where the audience travels or needs to get organized, the extra time helps.

For each date, mark a first ticket type as presale with a small capacity and a lower price; when it sells out, the general price remains. In ENTRÁ you can mark a ticket type as early bird and change prices from the dashboard. The timing of each stage is covered in more detail in when to open ticket sales.

Measuring which city sells and why

Knowing how many tickets each date sold is easy: it is in each event's dashboard. The hard part is knowing what made them sell. For that, there are two tools.

Measuring with utm links

When you share an event link, you can add utm parameters at the end. For example:

  • Instagram story for Rosario: the event link with ?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=rosario-story.
  • Message from the local promoter in Córdoba: ?utm_source=whatsapp&utm_campaign=cordoba-promoter.
  • Post from the Mendoza venue: ?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=mendoza-venue.

ENTRÁ reads those parameters when the person arrives and stores them in the order when they pay. In each event's dashboard, the "De dónde vinieron las ventas" (where sales came from) block groups what was sold by source and campaign, with the amount and percentage for each. If someone arrives without utm parameters, ENTRÁ tries to work out the network from the site the click came from; what can't be identified shows up as sales with no data. The order list you export also includes the source and campaign columns, so you can pull all the dates together in one spreadsheet.

One rule to make it useful: always use the same campaign name format. "city-channel" in lowercase and with no spaces, on every date. If one time you write "Rosario Story" and another "rosario-story", you will have to add them up by hand later.

Measuring with discount codes

When sales go through people, like a local promoter, a support band or a friendly bar, one code per person is clearer than a link. In ENTRÁ codes are created inside each event, with their discount and a usage cap if you want, and the dashboard shows how many times each one was used. Since codes are per event, the Rosario code doesn't work in Córdoba: if you want someone to sell for two dates, create one for them in each.

The downside is that a code means giving away some money. If you only want to measure, the utm link is enough. The two complement each other: more ideas in discount codes for an event and in how to promote an event and track sales.

Learning from one date for the next

The advantage of a tour is that each date is a rehearsal for the next one. After each show, take ten minutes and write down in one spreadsheet:

  • Sold and attended: how many tickets were sold and how many were scanned at the door.
  • Pace: how much sold in the first week, how much in the last and how much on the day of the show. If a city buys everything at the last minute, don't panic over a slow start at the next similar date.
  • Channel that worked: which source or code brought the most sales. If the local promoter sold half in Rosario, find one before opening in the next city.
  • What price held up: if the presale sold out in a day, it was cheap or the capacity was small.
  • The door: how long the line took and what was missing.

And keep the people. From each event's dashboard you export the list of buyers with name and email. If the tour comes back through that city, that list is the first one you tell. How to use it without burning it out is in your buyer list for the next date.

Small cities

In a smaller city in the provinces, word of mouth weighs more than advertising, people decide closer to the date and door sales are stronger. Don't use a big city's numbers to project a town of forty thousand people. There, a local ally with their own code and a short presale pay off more.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to have one event for the whole tour or one per date?

One per date. Each city has its own venue, capacity, price and door. With one event per date, each QR code is valid only in its city, one venue's capacity doesn't mix with another's and you see how much each city sold separately.

How do I know which post sold the most in each city?

Add utm parameters to the link you share, for example ?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=rosario-story. Each event's dashboard in ENTRÁ groups sales by source and campaign, and the order list you export includes those columns.

Should I open sales for all tour dates on the same day?

You can announce the whole tour on the same day, but it is better to open sales for each date with a similar lead time before the show, so no city goes weeks without news. Big or distant dates can open earlier.

Each date with its own event, each sale with its source

With ENTRÁ each event has its own capacity, its own codes and its own dashboard showing where each sale came from, and each QR code is valid only once at its own door. Creating the account and loading the event costs nothing.
