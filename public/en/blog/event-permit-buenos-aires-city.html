Permit for an event in Buenos Aires City: what the city requires

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

"Do I need a permit for my event?" is one of the first questions any organizer asks in the City of Buenos Aires (known locally as CABA, the autonomous city that is Argentina's capital, separate from Buenos Aires Province), and the answer depends mostly on one thing: how many people you will bring together. Here is what the city's regulations say about the mass event permit, who issues it, how far in advance and which documents it requires, and what you need to have sorted if your event is smaller. Everything comes from official sources, listed at the end.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

The key question: will you go over a thousand attendees?

The City's Law 5641 regulates mass events. It defines a mass event as any occasional act, gathering or happening with an artistic, musical or festive purpose, capable of producing a concentration of more than a thousand attendees. It can be in an open, closed or semi enclosed space, and the public can be spectators (a show) or participants (a party).

If your event fits that definition, you need the special mass event permit. If it doesn't reach that number, this procedure doesn't apply to you, but that doesn't mean there is nothing to check (more on that below).

Who processes it and how far in advance

  • Private space (grounds, a warehouse, a club): according to the official procedure page, the file is submitted to the Agencia Gubernamental de Control (AGC), the city's inspection agency, and the permit is granted by its Dirección General de Habilitaciones y Permisos (licensing and permits office).
  • Public space (a square, a street): the file is submitted to the Ministerio de Espacio Público e Higiene Urbana (the city's public space ministry).
  • Deadline: the application is filed at least 20 calendar days before the event date. That is the minimum: with the number of documents required, it is best to start much earlier.

Before the permit: the organizers' register

Law 5641 creates the Registro Público de Productores de Eventos Masivos (public register of mass event organizers), under the AGC. Every person or company that organizes mass events must be registered, and the procedure page lists the registration certificate as one of the requirements. If you have never put on an event of this size, this is the first step, before even thinking about the date.

Which documents the official page asks for

The full list is on the procedure page and other agencies may ask for more. The mandatory requirements include:

  • Permit proposal and descriptive report of the event (capacity, artists, schedule).
  • Registration certificate in the organizers' register.
  • Site plan of the grounds.
  • An approved and certified electronic access control system, to ensure the maximum authorized capacity is respected.
  • Civil liability insurance and an acoustic impact report.
  • Approval from Defensa Civil (the city's civil defense office).
  • Right to occupy the grounds and the operating license certificate.
  • Fireproofing and structural safety certificates.
  • Contracted services: sound, lighting, toilets, security, medical assistance, cleaning, fire prevention, among others.
  • Free drinking water and enough toilets for the capacity.
  • Proof of payment of the fee set by the city's tariff law.

A detail about access control: the page asks for an approved and certified system. Before submitting, ask the agency which systems they accept. Having a QR scanner at the door does not by itself mean it meets that requirement.

If your event is smaller

A party for 200 people or a show at a bar doesn't fit the definition of a mass event. What those events rest on is something else: the venue being licensed for the activity you will hold and you not exceeding its capacity. In practice:

  • Ask the venue for its license and check that the activity matches your event.
  • Ask for the licensed capacity in writing and set your ticket capacity from that number. We explain it step by step in how many tickets to sell based on licensed capacity.
  • If you are going to use an unlicensed venue or use it for a different activity, check with the AGC before selling a single ticket.

Everything else about putting it together (date, venue, sales, door) is in the checklist for organizing your first event.

How capped sales help

Frequently asked questions

When is an event a mass event in the City of Buenos Aires?

According to Law 5641 and the official procedure page, a mass event is any occasional act, gathering or happening of an artistic, musical or festive nature capable of producing a concentration of more than a thousand attendees. That is when the special permit is required.

How far in advance do I apply for the mass event permit?

The procedure page on buenosaires.gob.ar asks for the application to be filed at least 20 calendar days before the event date. Since the paperwork is long, it is best to start well before that.

Who grants the permit?

If the event is in a private space, the file is submitted to the Agencia Gubernamental de Control (AGC). If it is in public space, it goes to the Ministerio de Espacio Público e Higiene Urbana.

Does my party for 200 people need this permit?

The mass event permit is for gatherings of more than a thousand attendees. For a smaller event, the key thing is that the venue is licensed for that activity and has enough capacity: ask the venue for its license and, if in doubt, check with the AGC.
