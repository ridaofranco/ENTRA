Guide for organizers

Selling tickets in January and February, with half the city on holiday

By SOMOS DER · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

In January and February (the height of summer in Argentina) your usual audience heads off to the coast, the hills or a relative's place. If you put on events in a city that isn't a tourist destination, the most common mistake is repeating your October event and expecting the same sales. It doesn't happen. But the city doesn't empty out: other people stay, with other schedules and other budgets. This guide is about selling to those people and, if you are in a tourist spot, to the ones who arrive.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Who stays in the city

In summer, the people who stay are not a smaller audience: they are a different audience.

  • People who work all summer and take their holidays at another time. They are looking for something to do at night, after the heat.
  • People who are already back, or who went away for a week and spend the rest at home.
  • Students and young people with no classes, free time and wanting to go out.
  • Families with kids who aren't traveling and need plans for the afternoons.

Pick one or two of those groups and build the event for them.

Dates: fewer, and better chosen

In the low season it is better to concentrate sales on a few dates rather than stretch them over a weekly series. Some pointers:

  • Check your province's school calendar. The weekend before classes start and the weeks when people come back usually find the city fuller than mid January.
  • Ask your audience. A story with a poll in November ("are you in the city in January?") gives you a better idea than any assumption.

For the day and time, which change a lot in summer, see the article on which day and time to choose for your event.

Price and venue size

With fewer people in the city, the first thing to adjust is size, not just price. A small venue that is full sells the next date better than a big one half empty.

  • Reduce capacity before lowering the price. Enter in the dashboard the number of tickets you can really sell in January, not the venue's maximum capacity.
  • Use short tiers. A presale with an announced cutoff pushes early purchases. At ENTRÁ the types marked as Early Bird are sold during the presale, and you make the cutoff yourself with "Lanzar Venta Oficial" (launch official sale): announce how long it lasts and stick to it. More in how to price tickets and set up tiers.
  • Think in groups. In summer people go out in groups. A discount code with a usage limit for groups or for your regular list lets you adjust without changing the published price, and in the dashboard you see how many times it was used.

Outdoors, but with a plan

A patio, a rooftop, a square or a club with grounds changes the event in summer. But sort out two things before opening sales:

  • The heat. Evening or late afternoon timing, shade and water at hand.
  • The rain. Summer storms come out of nowhere. Decide the backup plan and say it in the description before selling, not on the day of the event. It is all in outdoor events: rain and the backup plan.

If you are in a tourist spot: sell to the people who arrive

If you put on events on the coast, in the hills or in a city that gets visitors in summer, the problem is the opposite: your audience changes every week and many don't know your event exists. The core of it is in summer events on the coast; here are two things that often get forgotten.

  • Sell before the trip. Many people plan their holidays weeks ahead and look for things to do at the destination. The event page is a link that opens from anywhere, you can buy without creating an account and the ticket arrives by email with its QR. Publish early (every event goes through a review before being published) and push the link where planners look: local accounts, rental groups, the front desk of the holiday complex or campsite.
  • Talk to the group. On holiday people buy for the family or the group of friends. If one person doesn't come, the official transfer passes that ticket to another email and the old QR stops working.

The door in high season

In tourist spots the decision to go out is made late, often the same day. That is why the door matters more. ENTRÁ has no box office: selling at the door is an online sale made from the line.

  • A sign with the QR of the event link in the line, visible and away from the scanner.
  • Two lines: one for those who already have their QR and another for those buying.
  • Check the venue's signal. Buying in the line requires mobile data, and in high season the network in some places gets overloaded.

Frequently asked questions

Is it worth holding an event in January if my city isn't a tourist destination?

It can be, if you design it for the people who stay and not for your year-round audience. That usually calls for a smaller venue, a lower price, an evening or outdoor time slot and a single date instead of a series. What doesn't work is repeating the same October event and expecting the same sales.

Which weeks in January and February are best for selling?

There is no answer that works for every city: it depends on when families, workers and students in your area go on holiday. Check your province's school calendar and ask your audience in advance. In general, the return from holidays and the last weekend before classes start are times when the city fills up again.

Can a tourist buy a ticket before arriving in the city?

Yes. The event page on ENTRÁ is a link that can be opened from anywhere, and the ticket arrives by email with its QR, with no need to create an account. That is why it pays to publish early and share the link where people planning their trip are looking.

Does ENTRÁ have a box office for selling at the door in high season?

No. ENTRÁ has no box office and doesn't record cash or card terminal payments. Selling at the door is done online from the line: you put up a sign with the QR of the event link and anyone without a ticket buys it from their phone while they wait.
