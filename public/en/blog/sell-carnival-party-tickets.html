Guide for organizers

Carnival party: how to sell tickets and run the door

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

A carnival dance brings together everything that complicates a party: lots of people deciding at the last minute, foam or water, heat, almost always outdoors and constant back and forth between the dance floor and the street. Ticket sales are sorted out with three decisions made before publishing: how you stagger the price, what you announce on the page and how you handle re-entry. This guide goes step by step.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

What kind of carnival you are putting on

A murga dance (murga is the street carnival troupe of drums and dancers typical of Argentina and Uruguay) at the neighborhood club is not the same as a party with a DJ and a foam cannon at an open venue. Before setting a price, be clear about:

  • What is on the floor: murga, comparsa (a costumed carnival parade group), band, DJ, or a mix. If there is a big act, let people know what time it goes on.
  • Whether there is foam, water or paint, and at what point in the night. This changes what people wear and what you need to announce.
  • Whether it is outdoors, indoors or both. Outside, the weather becomes part of the plan.
  • Whether children come: many carnivals are family friendly until a certain hour and then change. Decide whether children pay, from what age and until what time it is suitable for them.

Tiers

Carnival is a late purchase: many people wait to see the forecast or to know whether their friends are going. Tiers give them a reason to buy earlier. The simple way to set them up at ENTRÁ:

  • One ticket type per tier: "1st tier", "2nd tier", "3rd tier", each with its own price and quota. When a tier sells out, it closes on its own.
  • Small quotas at the start. A first tier that sells out fast gets people talking; one that lasts a month doesn't push anyone.
  • If it doesn't sell out, change the price from the dashboard on the date you announced. What you can't do is promise "last tickets" and keep selling the same tier.

Each purchase allows up to 4 tickets of each type. At a carnival that matters, because people come in groups: if there are six friends, two people buy. Say so in your promotion so nobody is surprised. How to stagger prices is covered in more depth in pricing tickets: tiers and presale.

Advance or at the door

At a carnival there will always be people who arrive without a ticket. The question is how much more they pay and how you serve them without holding up the line.

  • The door price, higher than the last tier, and announced everywhere. That is what makes buying in advance worthwhile.
  • A "Door" ticket type with its own price, so in your wrap up you can see how much you sold on the spot and how much beforehand.
  • Anyone arriving without a ticket can buy from their phone on the event page, as long as online sales are still open. Put up a sign with the page's QR next to the line and you don't need a cash desk.
  • Enter the end time in the event. If you don't, ENTRÁ closes online sales 3 hours after the start. At a carnival that starts at 6 pm and runs until dawn, without an end time sales close at 9 pm, just when the most people arrive.

If you are also going to take cash at the door, see selling tickets at the door.

Foam, water and what to announce on the page

Foam and water are part of the fun, but also the main source of complaints if nobody warned people. On the event page, somewhere visible and not at the bottom:

  • That you will get wet, and what time the foam or water starts. Some people want to watch the murga dry and join in later.
  • What to bring and what not to: clothes that can get wet, closed shoes, no glass. If there is paint, whether it washes off with water.
  • The phone: the ticket is shown on the phone, so ask people to protect it until they are through the door. A case or a zip bag solves it.
  • Whether there is a cloakroom, how much it costs and until what time it is open.
  • Whether people can bring their own foam or only the organizers' is used. The second is better, for safety and so the floor doesn't get slippery.
  • Whether it is suitable for children and until what time.

The clearer the page, the fewer messages you will get the week of the event.

Outdoors

Most carnivals are held in the street, at a club or in an open venue. That adds two risks: rain and heat.

  • Have a rain plan, decided before selling: whether it goes ahead anyway, whether there is a covered space or whether it moves to another date. Write it on the page so nobody has to ask.
  • If it moves to another date, announce it through the same channels you sold on and explain the options for those who can't make it. We go over it in cancelled or rescheduled events.
  • Drinking water and shade, if it starts in daylight. In February (high summer in Argentina), a dance that starts at 5 pm with no shade empties out.

The full plan for events without a roof is in outdoor events: what to do if it rains.

Re-entry with wristbands

At a carnival people go out to get something from the car, buy at the corner kiosk or watch the comparsa passing in the street. If you want them to be able to come back, keep in mind how the scanner works: it records entries, not exits. Each QR is valid only once, and if someone shows it again, the scanner marks it as already checked in. That stops a QR screenshot from getting in twice, but it is no use for controlling who went out and is coming back.

The solution sits outside the ticketing platform and it is the usual one:

  • Wristband on entry. The QR is scanned and the wristband goes on at the same moment. Use the kind that can't be taken off and put back on.
  • Whoever goes out with a wristband comes back with a wristband. The door doesn't scan again: it looks at the wrist.
  • One color per night, if the carnival runs several days, so nobody uses yesterday's wristband.
  • If you don't want re-entry, say so on the page and at the door: "Once you leave, you're out."

The number of wristbands has to match what you sold plus complimentary tickets, plus a margin for the ones that break.

The door

  • One person scans and another puts on the wristband. If one person does both, the line doubles.
  • The QR is scanned from a phone. If someone can't find the email, you can look them up by surname, ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID) or email from the scanner.
  • A separate line for people buying on the spot, so they don't slow down those who already have a ticket.

Frequently asked questions

Can people leave and come back in with the same ticket?

The ENTRÁ scanner records entries, not exits: each QR is valid only once and a second scan shows as already checked in. If you want to allow re-entry, put a wristband on people as they come in and have the door let back in anyone wearing one.

Is it a good idea to sell a carnival party in tiers?

Yes. Set up each tier as a ticket type with its own price and quota, for example "1st tier", "2nd tier" and "Door". When one sells out it closes on its own, and the price difference pushes people to buy earlier.

What do I need to announce if there is foam or water?

That people will get wet, what time the foam starts, that they shouldn't bring phones or documents unprotected and whether there is a cloakroom. If the ticket is shown on the phone, say so in advance so people protect it until they are through the door.
