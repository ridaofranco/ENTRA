Guide for organizers

Student Day and spring party: tickets, age and weather

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

On September 21 Argentina celebrates Student Day (Día del Estudiante) and the arrival of spring, and all over the country there are picnics, parties at clubs, daytime parties and festivals put together for high school students and young people. It is a date that sells itself, but with an audience that has its own rules: many are minors, they buy at the last minute and they depend on the weather. This guide sorts all that out before you publish the link.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Decide who it is for

The first decision is age, and the time slot, the bar and the door check all depend on it. There are three common formats:

  • A daytime party or picnic for high school students, during the day or in the afternoon, alcohol-free, with music, games and food. It is the most common format and the one that reassures families the most.
  • A party for over-18s, at night, with a bar. It is a different event, with a different audience and a different check.
  • An outdoor festival for all ages, with bands, food trucks and a picnic area, where families, teenagers and adults share the space.

What doesn't work is mixing without saying so. If the event is for teenagers, make it alcohol-free from start to finish, and say so in the first line of the description. If you want a bar with alcohol, do it for adults only and check ID; how to set up that check is in over-18 only event.

Minors: what to ask for and how to put it in writing

With an audience aged 13 to 17, the people deciding are often the parents. A clear page sells more than any flyer:

  • Age range in the name or the first line: "For kids aged 13 to 17".
  • Exact start and end times, and where to pick them up. Parents plan the ride home around that.
  • What there is and what there isn't: "Alcohol-free event. There is a snack bar with soft drinks, water and food."
  • Who is in charge: the name of the club, the school or the organizer, and a contact phone number.
  • If an adult's permission is required, say how: a signed note handed in at the door, for example.

In ENTRÁ you can add extra questions when you create the event and mark them as required. They are answered once per purchase, so they work for group details, not for each kid: "Age of the oldest minor", "School or class", "Phone number of a responsible adult". The answers show up in the attendee list and in the export, and they help if you need to call someone during the event.

Age check at the door

The QR says the ticket is valid, not who is using it. That is why, at an event with an age range, the order at the door is ID first and QR second:

  • Look at the physical ID and compare it with the range. If it is an event for minors and someone aged 25 shows up, that is also a problem.
  • Then scan. The scanner shows green with the name and ticket type, or red with the reason. If someone brings a ticket from another party, the scanner rejects it because it belongs to another event.
  • The ID number (DNI) entered at purchase belongs to whoever bought, and often a parent bought it. It is for finding the ticket, not for checking age.

If you have colored wristbands by age range, the door puts them on when validating. How many people you need so there is no line is in how many people to put on the door.

Groups and classes that buy together

Teenagers go in groups. ENTRÁ allows up to 4 tickets per type in each purchase, so a group of 10 makes three purchases. Explain it on the page so nobody thinks the system is failing. If a school or a class wants to buy as a block, the tidiest way is for each class to have a delegate who buys for their group of friends, and for the "School or class" question to show you afterwards who came with whom.

There are no group tickets with a price per quantity. If you want to reward classes that buy together, a discount code per class works: you create it with a usage cap and an expiry date, and you see how many times it was used.

Early sales: the date sells itself, but late

The spring party has the advantage that everyone knows the date. The downside is that kids decide the week before, when they see the group is going. A few rules:

  • Open sales in good time, two or three weeks ahead, with a cheaper first tier. How to stagger prices is in pricing tickets in tiers.
  • Promote where they are: Instagram, class WhatsApp groups and student unions. Use a link with a different parameter for each channel and you will know which one sold.
  • Look after those who buy from an adult's phone. The purchase is made without creating an account and the ticket arrives at the email entered. Ask in the description for them to enter the email of the kid who is going, or for the adult to forward them the ticket.

ENTRÁ has no box office and does not record cash payments. If you want to sell at the door, whoever arrives buys from their phone with the link, and a sign with the event QR in the line helps.

The weather: the plan B comes before selling

September 21 can be sunny or rainy, and an outdoor picnic depends on it. The plan B is written before publishing, not on the morning of the event:

  • An alternative covered venue, if there is one: the club's gym, a warehouse. If there is, say the event goes ahead anyway and where.
  • A rain date, if there is no roof. Pick a specific day, for example the following Saturday, and make it clear the ticket is valid for that date.
  • The decision time: "If it rains, we will let you know before 10 am on Instagram and by email."

If you have to move the date, do it from the dashboard and send the notice yourself. Changing the date does not send an automatic email to buyers, so you send the notice using the emails from the export. Everything about setting up an outdoor party is in outdoor event: rain and plan B.

Price and what you receive

For a high school audience, price matters. A "General admission" ticket type and, if there is food, a "Ticket + combo" type (which the door exchanges for a token) is enough. With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket; the service and processing charges are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell party tickets to under-18s?

Yes, ENTRÁ does not limit the buyer's age. What you have to decide is the event's age range, whether an adult's permission is required and how it is checked at the door. Write it in the description and add a required question with the age or school year.

Does the ID number (DNI) entered at purchase work for checking age?

No. It is the ID number of whoever bought, and nobody verified it. It is useful for finding the ticket if someone can't find the email. Age is checked by looking at the physical ID at the door, before scanning the QR.

What do I do if it rains on September 21?

Set the alternative date or the covered venue before opening sales and write it in the description. If you have to move the date, notify each buyer yourself: changing the date in the dashboard does not send an automatic email.

Can a group of 10 friends buy together?

ENTRÁ allows up to 4 tickets per type in each purchase, so a group of 10 needs three purchases. For schools or classes buying as a block, it is best to coordinate with one delegate per class.
