Selling tickets for milongas and dance classes: single entry or pack

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

Milongas (tango dance nights), prácticas, tango, salsa or folklore classes: the audience is loyal, the dance floor has a limit and half the people want to pay for the month while the other half pay for the night. This guide shows how to separate single entry from the pack, how to protect the floor's quota and how to handle it with ENTRÁ without promising what it doesn't do.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Single entry, pack or both

At a milonga or a práctica (an informal practice session) almost everyone pays for the night. In a dance class it is the opposite: what keeps the group going is the person who comes every week. That is why it is worth separating two products:

  • Single entry: one night or one class. For people trying it out, those passing through or those who can't commit to the month.
  • Pack: several classes or several dates together, cheaper than paying for them one by one. For regular students, and for you, since you know well in advance how many people you have.

The price difference between the two has to be clear and written on the page. If the pack costs almost the same as four single entries, nobody buys it.

How to set it up in ENTRÁ

ENTRÁ lets you load a multi-day event and choose how the QR works. That is what you need for packs:

  • One QR for the whole event. You load the dates of the series (for example, the four Tuesdays of the month) and the buyer pays once and receives a single QR valid for all of them. At the door it gets in once per day. This is the pack.
  • One QR per day. Same multi-date event, but the buyer chooses which days they will attend, pays the price times the number of days chosen and receives one QR per day. It works if you want each person to build their own schedule.

A detail you need to know: the QR mode belongs to the event, not to each ticket type. In the same event you can't have the pack with one QR for every date and, next to it, the single class with a one day QR. The tidiest approach is two events:

  • The series (multi-day event, one QR for the whole event) with the "Monthly pack" ticket type.
  • Each night or each class as a one day event, with the "Single entry" or "Single class" type.

If you prefer a single event with "one QR per day", that works too: whoever buys one day pays for a single entry, whoever buys four pays for four. What there isn't is an automatic discount for taking more days; if you want to reward people who buy the month, you can use a discount code from the dashboard.

The quota is the dance floor

An overcrowded floor means bad dancing and collisions. Before selling, define how many couples or people fit comfortably, and above all whether the venue has a licensed capacity, which is the ceiling you never exceed.

With two events you need to split the floor, because each event has its own quota and they don't subtract from each other. If 40 fit on the floor, for example, you can keep 25 places for the pack and leave 15 for single entries on each date. That way someone who paid for the month is never left out because a night filled up with visitors.

Couples, roles and price

  • If you sell by couple, the ticket type should say so ("Couple, 2 people") and count each couple as two places on the floor.
  • If role balance matters in a class, you can have two ticket types with their own quota (for example, one for each role). Each type closes on its own when it fills up.
  • A door price higher than the advance price, if you are going to sell at the door. It pushes people to buy earlier and lets you know how many are coming.

The door

At a milonga people arrive a few at a time over several hours, so the door is calm but long. What keeps it organized:

  • The QR is scanned from a phone. The pack QR gets in once per date: if someone tries to use it twice on the same night, the scanner rejects it because it already checked in that day, and on a date outside the series it marks it as not valid.
  • Anyone without a ticket buys from their phone on the event page, while sales are still open.
  • If someone can't find the QR, you look them up by surname, ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID) or email from the scanner.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell a class pack with a single QR?

Yes. You load a multi-day event with the dates of the series and choose "one QR for the whole event". The buyer pays once, receives one QR and at the door gets in once for each date.

Can I have a pack and a single class in the same event?

Not with different QR codes, because the QR mode belongs to the event and not to each ticket type. The practical approach is one event for the series with the pack and one event per date for single entries, splitting the floor's quota between them.

What happens if someone with a pack misses a class?

The ticket is valid once for each date of the series: if they didn't go, that day wasn't used and it doesn't carry over to another. If you want to offer make up classes or credit, set that rule yourself and write it in the event description.

How much do I receive per ticket if I sell with ENTRÁ?

You receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket. The service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.
