Trivia night at a bar: tickets per team or per person

By SOMOS DER · October 30, 2026 · 7 min read

A bar trivia night is played at tables, in teams, with a host, rounds of questions and a prize at the end. The bar fills a slow night and people come back every week. To sell the ticket, the underlying question is just one: do you charge per team or per person? That decision sets the capacity, the tables and how the door works. This guide goes step by step.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

What a trivia night looks like

Before loading the event, write down how the night will go, because that defines the sale:

  • Teams: minimum and maximum number of members. The most common is between 2 and 6. If a team of 8 takes up two tables, decide whether you allow it.
  • Tables: how many the bar has with room for each team to write and talk without the next table hearing. That number is your real limit, more than the venue's capacity.
  • Rounds and schedule: how many rounds, what time the first one starts and when results are given. Anyone who arrives late to a trivia night loses points, so the start time has to be firm.
  • Prize: what the winner takes home and whether the bar, a sponsor or the takings pay for it.

Option 1: tickets per team

In ENTRÁ each ticket type has its name, price and capacity, and it closes on its own when it fills up. To sell per team, you set up a type with the size in the name:

  • "Team of up to 4", with a price per team and a capacity equal to the number of tables for four.
  • "Team of up to 6", if you have bigger tables, with its own price and its own capacity.

That way the capacity does the job of the tables: if you have twelve tables for four, the capacity is twelve, and team number thirteen sees the type as sold out. ENTRÁ has no table map, so assigning which table goes to each team is up to you, at the venue or on the spreadsheet.

Two things to keep in mind with team tickets:

  • It is a single QR code for the whole team. The captain buys it and it is scanned once. It works if the team arrives together. If someone arrives later, the person at the door sorts it out with the team name.
  • The dashboard counts tickets, not people. Twelve "Team of up to 4" tickets are twelve in the dashboard, even if that is up to 48 people. You work out the bar's capacity yourself by multiplying by the number of members.

Option 2: tickets per person

If members arrive at different times, or if you want each one to pay their own way, sell per person. A "Player" type with a per person price and a capacity equal to the tables times the seats at each one. Each member buys their ticket and gets their own QR code.

To keep the team together, add a custom field. When creating the event you can add questions the buyer answers before paying (text, number, multiple choice or long text) and mark them as required. A required "Team name" field is enough. The answers come out in the CSV export of the list, with one column per question: sort by that column and you have the teams put together.

Watch out for two details:

  • Questions are answered once per purchase. If one member buys all four of the team's tickets, all four carry the same team name and that buyer's details. For trivia that's fine. If the others want their QR code in their own name, the buyer transfers it to them from "Mis Tickets" (my tickets).
  • Team names get written a thousand ways. "The Know It Alls", "the know it alls", "Know It Alls". Before the night, check the column and make them consistent.

The risk of selling per person is half empty tables: a team of three at a table for six. If tables are your limit, team tickets protect you better.

People who come alone

Almost every trivia night has people who want to play and don't have a team. They are worth having, because if they have a good time they come back with friends. Add a solo ticket type, for example "Coming alone: put me in a team", with a small capacity. Before the night you export the list, group the people who came alone and give them their own table or add them to teams that aren't full, if the teams agree.

Price, drinks and prize

Trivia lives off the bar. There are three usual ways to charge for it:

  • Ticket only, with drinks separate. It is the simplest.
  • Ticket with drinks, for example "Team of 4 + 2 pitchers". It gives the bar a guaranteed sale and gives you a higher price per table. Write in the name what it includes and how it is redeemed. Ways to control redemption at the bar are in tickets with a drink or combo included.
  • Free with reservation, if the bar prefers to earn only from drinks. The reservation costs ARS 0 for whoever signs up, each person gets their QR code by email and you keep the table capacity. For free events ENTRÁ sends a reminder email the day before to everyone who reserved, which helps avoid empty tables.

If you charge for tickets, with ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set. The service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying. If the prize comes out of the takings, say on the page what it is or how much, so there is no argument at the end.

Close the agreement with the bar (who collects what, who provides the sound and the mic, whether the bar keeps the bar sales) before publishing. There is a run through of that agreement in selling tickets to play at a bar.

The weekly series

A trivia night works when it becomes a habit: "Wednesday trivia". For people to make it part of their week:

  • One event per date. Each night has its page, its table capacity and its list, and in the dashboard you see how each one sold.
  • Same day, same bar, same time. Every change needs explaining.
  • Open the next date as soon as the previous one ends. The winning team wants to defend its title, and the runner up wants revenge.
  • Use your buyer list. You export it from the dashboard and tell teams that already played about the next date before posting on social media. A discount code with a usage cap and an expiry date for regulars also helps.

If you keep a season leaderboard, each night's CSV with the team names gives you the base. Setting up a series on a fixed date is covered in more detail in monthly music or stand-up series, and an open mic night, which is very similar, in open mic, karaoke or jam.

The door

At a trivia night people arrive in the last twenty minutes before the first round. So the start doesn't get delayed:

  • The QR code is scanned from a phone. Green with the name and ticket type, or red with the reason. Each QR is valid only once and only for that date.
  • The scanner shows the type: if it says "Team of up to 6", the person at the door already knows which table to send them to.
  • If someone can't find the email, they can be looked up by surname, ID number (DNI) or email from the scanner.
  • The list of teams with their tables, printed or on the host's phone.
  • Anyone who arrives without a ticket can buy from their phone on the event page, as long as sales are still open and there are tables left.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to sell trivia tickets per team or per person?

Per team, if teams arrive together and you want to control the tables: a "Team of 4" type with a capacity equal to the number of tables. Per person, if each member arrives at their own time: each one buys their ticket and enters the team name in a required question, and you put the tables together from the export.

How do I set a capacity per table in ENTRÁ?

ENTRÁ has no table map. Capacity is per ticket type: if you sell per team, the type's capacity is the number of tables. Keep in mind that a team ticket is a single ticket in the dashboard, so you work out the number of people yourself by multiplying by the members.

What do I do with people who want to play but have no team?

Add a solo ticket type, for example "Coming alone: put me in a team", with a small capacity. Before the night you export the list, group the people who came alone and set up a table for them.
