Guide for organizers

An event flyer that sells: what to include and in which format

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

The flyer is the first thing almost everyone who ends up buying a ticket sees. They see it in a story, in a WhatsApp group or in the preview of a link a friend sent them. If in two seconds they don't understand what it is, when, where and how much it costs, they scroll past. This guide covers what it needs to say, how to adapt it to each format and what happens to the image when you share your event link on ENTRÁ.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

The details that can't be missing

A nice flyer that makes people ask "so what time is it?" loses sales. Before the design, check that everything is there:

  • What it is: the event name and, if it isn't self explanatory, a line explaining it ("2000s party", "stand up with three comedians", "peña with dancing", a peña being an Argentine folk music gathering).
  • Day and date: "Saturday, November 15". People plan by day of the week, not by number.
  • Start and end time: "10 p.m. to 4 a.m.". The end time helps whoever goes home by bus or is comparing with another plan.
  • Venue: the venue name and neighborhood or city. The full address can go on the event page, but the neighborhood has to be on the flyer.
  • Price and tier: "Presale ARS 8,000 while it lasts". There is a section on this below.
  • Where to buy: the link, or at least "tickets at the link in bio".
  • Entry conditions, if any: over 18 only, limited capacity, whether people need to bring an ID (DNI, the Argentine national ID).

If the flyer has too much text, cut adjectives before details. "The best party of the year" sells no tickets; "Saturday 15, 10 p.m., Palermo" does. What doesn't fit in the image goes in the description, and to write it you have what to write on your event page so people buy.

The end time, on the flyer and in the event

The end time needs to be in both places: on the flyer and loaded in the ENTRÁ event. The second matters more than it seems. If the event has no end time, ENTRÁ treats it as over three hours after the start and stops online sales at that moment. At a party that starts at 11 p.m. and runs until 5 a.m., that means that at 2 a.m. someone at the door with their phone can no longer buy.

Load the real closing time when creating the event. If it is a multi day event, each day has its own start and end time.

Price and tier: how to show it without it going stale

Price is the detail that expires fastest. In ENTRÁ each tier is a ticket type with its own name, price and capacity, and when the capacity runs out that tier is no longer on sale. The flyer, on the other hand, keeps circulating with the old price. Some ways to handle it:

  • Price with the tier name next to it: "Presale ARS 8,000 while it lasts". That way whoever arrives late understands why it costs more now.
  • One flyer version per tier. When you close the presale, post the new version with the next price.
  • Use the same names as in ENTRÁ. If the flyer says "Preventa" and on the page the type is called "Early", the buyer hesitates. Make them match.
  • Mention the service fee. At ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket, and service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, visible before paying. A small "+ service fee" under the price avoids surprises at checkout.

How many tiers to set up and how much to raise between them is in how to price your tickets and set up tiers.

The link: long, short and with a source

Your ENTRÁ event link looks like entratickets.com/evento/ followed by a code. It is meant to be tapped, not typed. ENTRÁ does not generate its own short link, so depending on where the flyer goes:

  • In stories: use Instagram's link sticker with the ENTRÁ link. The link doesn't need to be readable in the image.
  • In the post: "Tickets at the link in bio", with the event link in the bio while sales last.
  • On a printed flyer (poster, handout, sign at the bar): add a QR code pointing to the event link. You can make it with any QR generator. If you'd rather have a link people can type, make one with an external URL shortener that redirects to the ENTRÁ link.

A detail worth money: if you add ?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=historia-viernes to the link, the event dashboard later shows you how many sales came from that link in the "De dónde vinieron las ventas" (where sales came from) block. Use a different utm for the story, for the WhatsApp group and for the poster QR, and you will know which piece sold. How to read those numbers is in how to promote your event and track where sales come from.

Formats: story, post and the event image

One flyer doesn't work for everything. The practical move is one base piece and three versions:

  • Story: vertical, the height of a phone screen. Leave the top and bottom strips clear, where Instagram puts the account name, the reply bar and stickers. Key details go in the center, in large type: nobody zooms into a story.
  • Post: square or slightly taller than wide. A bit more text fits here, but the rule is the same: date, time, venue and price readable in the small feed view.
  • Event image on ENTRÁ: the one you upload in "Flyer / Imagen del evento" (flyer / event image) when creating the event. This one deserves its own section.

What happens to the flyer when you share the link

We checked it in ENTRÁ's code: the image you upload as the flyer is the one used as the preview when someone shares the event link. When you paste the link into WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, Telegram or X, those apps read the event details and build a card with:

  • Your flyer as the image.
  • The event name.
  • The day, date and start time, and the venue, as you loaded them.
  • The beginning of the description, about 140 characters.

If you didn't upload an image, the card shows ENTRÁ's generic image, and a link without the event's face gets tapped far less. Three things to keep in mind:

  • The card is designed around a horizontal image, and on the event page the image is also cropped to fill the screen width. A vertical story uploaded as is may get cut off at the top and bottom. For the event image, a horizontal or square version works best, with the name and date in the center.
  • ENTRÁ shrinks the image when you upload it, to a maximum of 1,280 pixels on the longest side. Make the flyer in good resolution, but without tiny type: what can't be read large won't be read shrunk.
  • The first words of the description appear on the card. Start with what sells ("Three bands, open bar until 1 a.m."), not with "Welcome to...".

If you change the flyer, the card may take a while to update: ENTRÁ caches the preview for about 10 minutes and each app also has its own memory of links it has already seen. If you change it, wait before sharing again.

Frequently asked questions

What details can't be missing from an event flyer?

Event name, day with date, start and end time, venue with neighborhood or city, price with the tier name and where to buy the ticket. If the event is over 18 only or has another entry condition, that goes too.

Does the flyer I upload to ENTRÁ appear when I share the link?

Yes. The image you upload in "Flyer / Imagen del evento" is the one WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, Telegram and X show in the link preview, along with the event name, the start date and time, the venue and the beginning of the description. If you don't upload an image, ENTRÁ's generic image appears.

Should I put the price on the flyer if the tier will change?

Yes, but with the tier name next to it, for example "Presale ARS 8,000 while it lasts". When the tier sells out, post a new version of the flyer with the next price, so nobody reaches checkout expecting a price that no longer exists.
