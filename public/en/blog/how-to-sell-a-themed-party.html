Theme party: how to make the theme sell tickets

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

A regular party competes with every other party on Saturday night. A theme party has something extra to offer: an excuse. The 2000s party, the costume party, the party for a whole decade. That excuse gives people a reason to go and something to talk about before they go. This guide is about making the theme do that work: how to choose it, how to tell it on the flyer, how to set up the presale, what to do with the dress code and how to handle the door.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

A theme you can explain in one sentence

The theme sells if someone can tell it in a message and their friend gets it right away. Some questions to test it:

  • Is it clear without explanation? "2000s party" is clear. A theme built on a pun only the organizers understand is not.
  • Does it come with its own music? The best themes bring their own playlist. If people can already imagine what will play, half the sale is done.
  • Does it give people something to do beforehand? Finding the clothes, putting together a costume, coordinating with the group. That preparation time is what makes the party exist in people's heads before the night.
  • Is it for your audience? A decade party works with people who lived it or who have it as a reference. Think about who you are talking to before choosing.

The flyer: theme first

At a theme party the flyer sells the theme, not the venue. What has to be there, in this order:

  • The theme, big. Readable at Instagram story size without zooming.
  • Date, venue and time. With the door closing time if there is one.
  • The dress code, in one line. "Come dressed in 2000s style" or "costume suggested". If it is mandatory, say it is mandatory.
  • The presale price and until when it applies. The cutoff date pushes people to buy.
  • A single purchase link. The same one for the whole team and every social network.

After the flyer, the theme gives you material for more posts: the playlist, an outfit reference, a countdown. Each one is one more excuse to share the link again.

The presale: give the costume time

At a theme party people need days to get ready, so the presale has to open early. How to set it up:

  • Tiers with capacity. One ticket type per tier, each with its own price and capacity, and the first one cheaper.
  • Announce the theme before opening sales. First the theme and the date, then the sale. That way people reach the presale already excited.
  • Group tickets. Group costumes are part of the fun. A group discount code, created from the event dashboard with a usage limit, helps the group buy together.

The dress code: suggested, with a prize

The dress code is what makes the party look themed, and also what sparks the most arguments at the door. Two routes:

  • Suggested, with a prize. A costume contest, a perk at the bar for those who come in character, photos in a corner set up for it. Nobody is left out and almost everyone joins in.
  • Mandatory. It works if your audience already expects it, and only if it is written on the flyer and in the event description before selling. Define what counts as "dressed for the era" so each person at the door doesn't decide on their own.

Either way, make sure the photos from the night show the costumes: they are the best flyer for the next edition.

The door

A theme party usually has lots of people arriving at the same time, all in groups, and sometimes with costumes that cover their faces. To keep entry fast:

  • QR at hand. Each ticket has a unique QR that is good for one use only and is scanned from a phone. Remind people in the message before the event to have it open before reaching the line, because finding a phone inside a costume takes time.
  • One person on the scanner and another on the dress code, if it is mandatory. Splitting the two jobs keeps the line from stalling.
  • A clear rule for people who don't comply. If the dress code is mandatory, the person at the door should know what to say before it happens.

If you want to sell at the door, keep online sales open during the party and let people buy from their phones in the line.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a theme party sell?

A theme that can be explained in one sentence and gives people something to do before going: putting together a costume, finding the clothes, deciding who to go with. If the theme needs an explanation, the flyer won't sell it.

Does the dress code have to be mandatory?

It is almost always better to make it suggested, with a prize for those who join in, like a contest or a perk at the bar. If you decide to make it mandatory, say so on the flyer and in the event description before selling, so nobody finds out at the door.

How do I set up the presale for a theme party?

With ticket types per tier, each with its own price and capacity, and the first tier cheaper. Open it once the theme has been announced, so people have time to prepare their costume.
