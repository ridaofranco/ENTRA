Guide for organizers

Networking or entrepreneurs' meetup with a paid ticket

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

A networking meetup sells something different from a concert: people pay for the people they are going to meet. That is why the data you ask for at checkout is worth as much as the ticket, and what you do with that data afterwards matters too. This guide works for an entrepreneurs' meetup, a business after office in a bar or a chamber of commerce breakfast, and tells you what the ticketing platform handles and what is better handled separately.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

What you sell and to whom

Before loading the event, write in one line what the ticket includes and who it is for. "Business after office for food and drink entrepreneurs, with one drink included and a round of introductions" sells better than "Networking". The buyer wants to know three things: who else is going, what will happen at the meetup and how long it lasts.

With that defined, set up the ticket types. In ENTRÁ each type has its own name, price and capacity, and it closes on its own when it fills up:

  • General, the full price.
  • Members or community members, if the meetup is organized by a chamber, an incubator or a group with membership fees.
  • Early bird, with its own capacity, to reward those who sign up early. How to stagger prices is covered in how to price tickets and set up tiers.

For closed groups (the participants of an accelerator program, the members of a partner association) a discount code per group also works, with a usage cap and expiry date. Each purchase allows up to 4 tickets of each type, which is enough for a small team coming together.

The questions you ask at checkout

At checkout, each person enters their name, email and ID number (DNI). For a networking meetup that is not enough: you want to know what each person does so you can set up tables, introduce people or choose the speakers. When creating the event you can add extra questions for the buyer and mark them as required. There are four formats: short text, number, list of options and long text.

  • Company or project, short text.
  • Industry, as a list of options ("Food and drink", "Services", "Technology", "Retail", "Other"). A closed list lets you group people later; free text gives you forty ways of writing the same thing.
  • What you are looking for at the meetup, list of options: clients, suppliers, partners, investment, contacts.
  • LinkedIn or Instagram handle, optional.

Don't go beyond five or six questions: each extra one is a reason to abandon the purchase halfway. And ask only for what you are going to use.

One detail changes everything: the questions are answered once per purchase, not once per ticket. If someone buys three tickets for their team, all three carry the name and answers of whoever paid. If you need each person's details, ask on the page that everyone buy their own, or that the buyer transfer each ticket to their colleague from the app. The official transfer asks for the recipient's name and email, so that data is recorded for you too.

The attendee list and privacy

From the event dashboard you see each person with their ticket type and export the list as a CSV that opens in Excel, with name, email, ID number, phone, ticket type, purchase date, whether they already checked in and one column for each extra question. With that you set up the tables, prepare the introductions and, the next day, send the follow-up.

What you can't do is share that spreadsheet with all attendees because "it's a networking event". ENTRÁ's privacy policy tells the buyer that their answers are shared with the event organizer, not with the rest of the audience. And Article 11 of Argentina's Law 25.326 on Personal Data Protection (in Spanish) says personal data "may only be transferred for purposes" related to the legitimate interests of the transferor and the recipient, and "with the prior consent of the data subject".

The tidy way to handle it:

  • A consent question at checkout, as a list of options with "Yes" and "No": "Do you want to appear on the contact list we will share with attendees? (name, company, industry and LinkedIn)". It should say exactly which data is shared.
  • The shared list, only with those who said yes and only with the fields you named. ID number and phone never go on it.
  • Written on the event page, so nobody finds out afterwards.

Why the ticketing platform asks for the ID number and what it uses it for is in why the ticketing platform asks for your DNI.

Name badges, handled separately

At a networking event a badge with name and company is almost mandatory: it is what makes it OK to approach a stranger. ENTRÁ does not print badges. What it does is issue one QR code per ticket and show each ticket's name and type on the scanner. The badge is put together separately, with the exported list:

  • Printed sticker labels from the spreadsheet, with name, company and industry. Sort them by last name on a table next to the door.
  • Blank badges and markers, if the meetup is small or sales close the same day. It is the simplest option and it never fails.
  • One color per industry or per what each person is looking for (a colored dot on the label), so people find each other without asking.

If the meetup is large, with check-in and badges printed on the spot, that calls for a different tool. For a conference with paid registration, setting up check-in is covered in conference or seminar with paid registration.

Ticket with a drink included

A business after office almost always happens in a bar, and often the ticket includes a drink or something to eat. Load it as a ticket type whose name says what it includes: "Ticket + welcome drink". That way the buyer sees it when paying and the door sees it when scanning.

The ENTRÁ QR code is valid only once and is used at the door: the bar cannot scan it again. The practical approach is for the door to hand over a token or a wristband when scanning, which is then exchanged at the bar. Agree with the bar beforehand on what share of the price goes to them and what happens with someone who wants a second drink. The full setup is in ticket with a drink or combo included.

On the day of the meetup

  • Open the door before the introductions start. The first twenty minutes are the most valuable for talking.
  • One person scans and another hands out badges. The scanner runs in the phone's browser: green with the name and ticket type, red with the reason. If someone can't find the email, you look them up by name, email or ID number. If the signal drops, check-ins are saved on the phone.
  • Speakers, sponsors and hosts as complimentary tickets from the dashboard, with name, email and reason. They don't come out of the sales quota, but they do count toward the total capacity shown in the dashboard.
  • Whoever shows up without a ticket buys from their phone, without creating an account, while sales are open. ENTRÁ has no box office and does not record cash payments.

With ENTRÁ you keep 100% of the price you set for each ticket. Service and processing costs are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

Frequently asked questions

Can I ask for the industry and company when selling the ticket?

Yes. When creating the event in ENTRÁ you add extra questions for the buyer (text, number, list of options or long text) and mark which ones are required. The answers appear in the attendee list you export as CSV, with one column per question.

Can I share the attendee list with everyone who came?

Only with those who gave their permission. Argentina's Law 25.326 requires the data subject's prior consent to transfer their data. Add a question at checkout, for example "Do you want to appear on the contact list shared with attendees?", and build the list only with those who answered yes.

Does ENTRÁ print name badges?

No. ENTRÁ issues one QR code per ticket and the scanner shows each ticket's name and type. Badges are put together separately with the exported list: printed labels, handwritten badges or a check-in tool.
