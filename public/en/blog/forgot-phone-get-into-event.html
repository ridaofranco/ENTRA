You forgot your phone or it died: can you still get in?

By SOMOS DER · July 30, 2026 · 5 min read

You arrived, there is a line, and your phone is dead. Or you simply forgot it. The short answer is yes, it can almost always be sorted out, and it doesn't depend on luck. Here is why, and exactly what to say at the door.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Your ticket doesn't live on your phone

This is the idea that brings the anxiety down. The QR on your phone is a copy of a code stored in the event's system. The phone is the most convenient way to show it, not the place where it exists.

In plain terms: if the phone disappears, your ticket is still there. All you lost is the fastest way to show it.

Option 1: have them look you up by your ID

This is the one that solves 90% of cases. From the same screen they use to scan, the door staff can look up the purchase by name, email or ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID), see that ticket's status and let you in right there, without scanning anything.

That is why the most useful thing you can bring to an event, after your phone, is your ID.

How to ask for it so it goes quickly:

  • Step out of the line and let them know. Don't stay at the front while it is sorted out: the line behind you is people who do have their QR.
  • Say the sentence the door staff need to hear: "I bought a ticket, I don't have the QR on me, can you look me up by ID?".
  • Have ready the exact name and the email you bought with. If you bought with an old email, say so.

Option 2: bring it on paper

The confirmation email comes with a PDF attachment with all the tickets in the purchase. That PDF is meant to be printed: at the door it scans exactly the same, because what gets read is the code, not the screen.

If you are going to a festival, something outdoors, a long event or a place where you know there is no signal, printing is the cheapest move there is.

Option 3: a screenshot, saved in your photos

The QR is an image. If you take a screenshot before leaving home and keep it in your photos, you can show it even if your phone is in airplane mode or without data. You don't need internet on your phone to be scanned: the phone that needs a connection is the one at the door.

It also saves you from the classic "there are so many people my email won't load". Opening Gmail in the line of an event with two thousand people is a gamble.

An important note: the screenshot works for your own ticket. A screenshot of someone else's QR doesn't work, because that code is accepted only once and whoever scans it first gets in.

What really can't be fixed

  • Your ticket has already been used. If you sent the screenshot to someone and they got in first, the code is already spent. The system shows how long ago they entered, so there isn't much to argue about.
  • The ticket is in someone else's name and that person doesn't show up. If your friend bought it and didn't come, at the door the purchase shows their details, not yours. This is solved with the official transfer, before the event.
  • You bought outside the platform. If you paid a stranger by bank transfer and they sent you a photo, there is nothing to look up: that purchase doesn't exist in the system.

Before leaving home: 30 seconds

  • A screenshot of the QR saved in your photos (or the PDF downloaded).
  • Your ID in your pocket.
  • If there are several of you, each person should have their own QR, not the same one three times.
  • If your battery is low: battery saver mode and turn the brightness down, but remember to turn it up right before being scanned. A QR at low brightness takes twice as long to read.
  • If you can't find the ticket, don't leave without sorting it out: it is in your profile, under Mis Tickets (my tickets), signing in with the same email you bought with. Even if you bought as a guest, without creating an account, it still shows up.

Frequently asked questions

My phone died and I can't show the QR, can I get in?

Generally yes. Your ticket doesn't live on the phone: it lives in the event's system, and from the access control screen your purchase can be looked up by name, email or ID number and validated right there. That is why the most useful thing to bring is your ID. Ask them to look you up that way, calmly and stepping out of the line so you don't hold it up.

Can I print my ticket on paper instead of showing it on my phone?

Yes, and it is the best backup there is. The confirmation email comes with a PDF attachment with all the tickets in the purchase, ready to print. At the door it scans the same, because what gets read is the code, not the screen. If you are going to a long event, outdoors or with poor signal, bringing paper as well as your phone takes the problem off your plate.

Does a screenshot of the QR work if I have no internet?

Yes, for your own ticket. The QR is an image: if you have it saved in your photos, it shows even if the phone has no data or is in airplane mode, and it scans the same. What doesn't work is a screenshot of someone else's QR: that code is accepted only once and whoever scans it first gets in.

Someone else bought the ticket and isn't coming, can I go in?

The right way is for them to transfer it to you beforehand through the official route from their profile: that issues a new QR in your name and theirs is invalidated. If they didn't get around to it, at the door the system finds the purchase by the buyer's details, not yours, so you will depend on that person being available and on the organizer's judgment. Sort it out before leaving home, not in the line.

Find your ticket now, not in the line

Sign in to your profile with the same email you used to buy and download the QR. If you bought as a guest, without an account, your tickets still show up with that email.
