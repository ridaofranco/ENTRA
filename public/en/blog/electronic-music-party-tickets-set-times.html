Guide for organizers

Electronic music party: tickets, lineup, set times and re-entry

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 7 min read

An electronic music party is sold on the lineup and decided at the door. People buy for a DJ, want to know what time they play and all arrive together shortly before. In between come the usual questions: is there an entry cutoff, can I step out for a smoke and come back, what about minors. This guide takes it one part at a time.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

The lineup sells

At a DJ party, the name at the top of the flyer is the reason to buy. Before publishing, settle with each artist what you are going to announce:

  • Who plays and in what order. The closing set is usually the biggest name, and the warm up is where you can add local DJs who bring their own crowd.
  • Whether it can be announced now or has to wait. Some artists ask you not to announce until a certain date. If so, open the early bird with "lineup to be announced" and reveal the names one by one: each announcement is a reason to share the link again.
  • If someone doesn't show. Decide beforehand what you do if a DJ cancels: who replaces them and whether that changes anything for those who bought for them. Write it in the event description.

Set times: publish them

The set times per DJ (the timetable) are the most requested information for an electronic music party. Whoever buys for the closing set doesn't want to arrive at opening, and whoever comes for the warm up wants to know when to show up. If you publish them:

  • The line spreads out. Instead of everyone arriving at the same time, each person arrives for their DJ.
  • The warm up DJs play to a crowd, because their audience knows when to come.
  • You save yourself a hundred messages asking what time so-and-so plays.

If you don't want to commit to exact times weeks in advance, publish the order in the description and the timetable on the day. And load the end time in the event: if you don't, ENTRÁ considers the event over three hours after the start and online sales stop, which at a party that starts at midnight and ends at six leaves you without sales in the middle of the night.

Tiers

In electronic music, tiers are part of the culture: the audience already knows the first one is the cheapest and sells out fast. In ENTRÁ each ticket type has its own name, price and capacity, and you can mark which ones are presale. Until you launch official sales, the public sees only the presale and the rest show as the next tier. When you tap Lanzar venta oficial (launch official sales) in the dashboard, the presale is marked as finished and the others open up. Each type closes on its own when it reaches its capacity and shows as sold out.

  • A short, cheap first tier. Let it sell out fast and make it visible: "sold out" on the page is the best advertising for the second one.
  • Clear price jumps between tiers, announced in advance. People buy when they know it costs more tomorrow.
  • Groups: each purchase takes up to four tickets of each type. A group of six buys in two goes. Say so in the description.

How to calculate each tier's price is in how to price your tickets and set up tiers. With ENTRÁ you keep 100% of the price you set for each ticket; the service and processing cost is paid by the buyer, itemized before paying.

Entry until a certain time

Many parties set a limit: "entry until 3 a.m." or a cheaper ticket for those who arrive early. It helps fill the dance floor before the headliner and keeps the door from receiving people when the night is already at its peak. ENTRÁ doesn't put an expiry time on the QR code: a valid ticket can be scanned at any point during the event. You handle the cutoff yourself, like this:

  • A ticket type with the time in its name: "Early, entry until 1:30 a.m.". That way it shows on the page, at checkout and on each person's ticket.
  • The rule written in the description: what happens if someone arrives later with an early ticket. The most common approach is that they pay the difference at the door, outside the app, in cash or by bank transfer.
  • At the door, the scanner shows the type of each ticket. At the cutoff time, the person scanning stops accepting that type and sends them to whoever collects the difference.
  • If you want to stop online sales at a certain time, you can pause the event from the dashboard. The page stops selling and those who already have a ticket keep getting in.

Re-entry

"Can I step out for a smoke and come back?" is the question at every electronic music party. The answer is up to you and the venue, but it has to be decided beforehand. What you need to know:

  • The QR code is valid only once. The scanner records entries, not exits. If someone leaves and shows the same QR code again, the scanner marks it as already used.
  • If there is re-entry, handle it with a wristband or a stamp given at first entry. On the way back in, you check the wristband and nothing is scanned.
  • If there is no re-entry, write it in the description and put a sign at the exit. It is one of the things that causes the most arguments at four in the morning.
  • If there is a smoking area inside, say so: it takes pressure off the door.

18 and over

An electronic music party with a bar is, almost always, adults only. The notice has to be where people read it before paying: in the event or ticket type name ("General 18+") and in the first line of the description. The ID number (DNI) is requested at checkout and stays on each ticket, but it is the buyer's, who may be buying for the group, and nobody checked it against a document. Age is checked at the door, with each person's ID, before scanning the QR code: if you scan first and the person is under 18, the ticket is marked as used and you have to explain why they can't come in. All the detail, including the required question at checkout and what to do if a minor shows up with a ticket, is in 18+ only events: notice, ID and door.

The door

At an electronic music party the line forms all at once, especially before the headliner. To keep it moving:

  • Two separate checks: ID first, QR code second. If the same person does both, always in that order.
  • More than one phone scanning at peak times. How many people to put on the door depending on the size of the party is in how many people to put on the door of an event.
  • If someone can't find their ticket, you look it up from the scanner by last name, ID number or email, without them having to dig up the email in the line.
  • Each QR code is valid only once. If someone sent a screenshot to a friend, whoever arrives first gets in and the scanner flags the second attempt.
  • Wristband or stamp in the same step, if there is re-entry. It shouldn't be another line.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a ticket valid only until a certain time?

The QR code doesn't expire at a set time, so the cutoff is handled at the door. Write the time limit in the ticket type name and in the description, and at that time the door stops scanning that type. The scanner shows each ticket's type, so it can be told apart instantly.

Can people leave and come back in with the same ticket?

The QR code is valid only once: the scanner records the entry, not the exit. If you want to allow re-entry, handle it with a wristband or a stamp given at first entry, and announce it on the event page.

Do I have to publish each DJ's set times?

It is a good idea. Many people buy for a specific DJ and want to know when to arrive. If you don't want to give exact times in advance, publish the lineup order and the set times on the day.
