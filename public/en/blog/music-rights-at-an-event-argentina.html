SADAIC and music at an event: what the organizer has to pay in Argentina

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

You set up the party, got the venue, the DJ and the band, and a week before someone asks: "what about SADAIC?" (the society that collects music rights for authors and composers in Argentina). It is one of the questions that comes up most when you organize an event with music, and the one most often answered by hearsay. Here is what the law and SADAIC itself say in their official sources, what we could not confirm and what to ask before opening the doors.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). This guide is about music rights under Argentine law and the Argentine collecting societies. It is not legal advice.

Notice: this is not legal advice. It is a guide so you know what to ask. For your specific case, consult the society or a lawyer.

The basis: what Law 11.723 says

Law 11.723 is Argentina's intellectual property law. Two articles explain why the music at an event has owners:

  • Article 36, the authors. Authors of musical works "gozan del derecho exclusivo de autorizar" (enjoy the exclusive right to authorize) the public performance of their works and their public broadcast by any means. In other words: whoever wrote the song decides whether it is played in public.
  • Article 56, the performers. "El intérprete de una obra literaria o musical, tiene el derecho de exigir una retribución" (the performer of a literary or musical work has the right to demand payment) for the broadcast or retransmission of their performance, or for its recording.

They are two different rights over the same song: that of the person who wrote it and that of the person who plays or sings it. That is why two different societies are involved.

The same Article 36 includes specific exceptions, for example events at educational institutions when the show is not broadcast outside the premises. A party or a concert with paid tickets does not fall under those exceptions.

SADAIC: the authors' share

SADAIC presents itself as the society of Argentine authors and composers. On its official website it holds these positions:

  • Commercial event halls. SADAIC "reafirma que los salones comerciales deben pagar por el uso de música" (reaffirms that commercial halls must pay for the use of music).
  • Private parties and Decree 765/2024. In an interview published on its website, its director says the decree redefined the private sphere as an event with restricted entry, but did not establish that social parties don't pay. According to SADAIC, when a hall is rented for a party with music "existe una actividad económica" (there is an economic activity).
  • Who it claims from. In the same article, SADAIC says it directs its claims mainly at venues, and that the cost can be passed on to the organizer through a commercial agreement between the parties.
  • Streaming. If you stream the event online, SADAIC states that those who charge to watch it or have sponsors or brands as advertising must pay.
  • Live musicians. SADAIC asks performers to register and declare their performances in order to use the repertoire it manages.

Note one detail: the point about private parties is SADAIC's reading of the decree. We could not open the text of the decree from an official source for this article, so we are not telling you what the rule itself says.

SADAIC publishes its fees in the fees section of its website (general users, streaming of concerts and festivals, and other categories). We don't copy them here because they change and depend on the type of event: go to the source or ask them through the contact form at sadaic.org.ar.

AADI-CAPIF: the recorded music share

For this article we tried to open the official website of AADI-CAPIF (the society for performers and record producers) and it did not load. That is why we won't tell you what it charges, to whom or how it is calculated: we could not confirm it at the source.

What is in the law is Article 56: performers have the right to payment for the broadcast of their performance. If recorded music is played at your event (DJ, playlist, background music between bands), add this question to your list and ask the society directly before the event.

What to ask before the event

In practice, the issue is solved with three or four questions asked in time:

  • To the venue: does the hall, club or bar already have SADAIC payment sorted for events held there? Is it included in the rental or do you have to handle it? Get it in writing in the contract or quote.
  • To SADAIC: describe your event (party with tickets, concert, festival, online stream) and ask which category applies to you and how far in advance it must be arranged.
  • To AADI-CAPIF: if there is recorded music, ask whether your event requires a license and how to apply for it.
  • To the musicians: if they play live, ask whether they are registered with SADAIC and whether they declare their performances.

Keep the answers and receipts with the rest of the event paperwork. For the rest of the list of procedures, see what permit you need for an event in Buenos Aires City and the checklist for organizing your first event.

Put it in the budget from the start

The typical mistake is not failing to pay: it is finding out late and having the cost eat your margin. When you put together the event budget, leave a line for music rights, even if you don't know the amount yet. If the venue already covers it, the line stays at zero. If not, you already have it accounted for when setting the ticket price.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to pay SADAIC if I hold a party with tickets?

Law 11.723 says authors of musical works have the exclusive right to authorize the public performance of their works. SADAIC, the society of authors and composers, holds that when there is economic activity around an event with music, payment for that use is due. If you charge for tickets, ask SADAIC before the event what applies to you.

Does a private party in a rented hall pay SADAIC?

According to SADAIC, Decree 765/2024 did not eliminate the rights of authors and composers, and when a hall is rented for a party with music there is economic activity. The society directs its claim mainly at the venue, which can pass the cost on to the organizer by commercial agreement. Ask the hall whether it has it sorted and get it in writing.

And what does AADI-CAPIF charge?

For this article we could not open the official AADI-CAPIF website, so we don't state anything about its licenses or fees. What Article 56 of Law 11.723 does say is that the performer of a musical work has the right to demand payment for the broadcast of their performance. If you play recorded music, ask the society directly.

If I stream the concert online, do I also pay?

SADAIC states that those who charge to watch a music event streamed online, or who have sponsors or brands as a form of advertising, must pay. It has its own fees section for streaming of concerts, festivals and recitals at sadaic.org.ar.
