How many people to put on the door at an event

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

The question always comes up the week of the event: how many people will be on the door? Too few and you get a line down the sidewalk; too many and you have people standing around who still get paid. There is no magic number, but there is a way to work it out with what you already know about your audience. This guide goes step by step: scanning, how many stations, what each person does and what happens if the signal drops.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First, how scanning works

Before counting people, it helps to know what each station does. With ENTRÁ you don't need to buy scanners: the QR is scanned from the phone's browser. Each station is one phone with a camera and one person.

  • The response is immediate: green with the buyer's name and the ticket type, or red with the reason (already used, from another event, cancelled, code does not exist).
  • There is a short pause between scans, so the camera doesn't read the same QR twice while the person puts their phone away.
  • Above the scanner you see the count: how many have come in, how many tickets were sold and how many are still to arrive. It tells you how many people are still on their way.
  • Each station has a name that you enter above the scanner ("Door 1", "VIP", "Press"), and every check-in is signed with that name.

A practical detail: the scanner shows the events of the account that is signed in. Every phone on the door has to be signed in with the account of the organizer who loaded the event, with the event selected before doors open. If you don't want to give your password to the whole team, sign in yourself on each phone before the audience arrives.

How many stations: the number that matters

The total number of tickets sold does not tell you how many people to put on the door. What tells you is how many people arrive at the same time. Two hundred people arriving over three hours go through a single station with no trouble. The same two hundred arriving in the twenty minutes before the band goes on will form a line even with two.

To estimate it, ask yourself three questions:

  • Is there a time everyone wants to make? A show with a start time, a play that starts on the dot or a match concentrates arrivals. A party, a fair or an after office spreads them out.
  • How much time is there between opening doors and the main moment? If you open at 9 pm and the band goes on at 10, most people will arrive in that window, and a good share in the last half hour.
  • How prepared is your audience? An audience that buys often arrives with the QR already open. An audience buying for the first time, or that got the ticket from someone else, takes longer.

With that, the math is simple and you can do it yourself. Measure how long it takes one person to get through at your door (not in theory): run a test with your team, with the QR on the phone, and time it. Then think about how many people you expect at the busiest moment and how many minutes you have to get them in. If one station can't get that many people through in that time, add another.

An example to show the reasoning, with made-up numbers: if in your test each person takes about ten seconds, one station gets about six people through per minute. If you expect half your audience to arrive in the last half hour and that is 300 people, you need ten per minute to get through: one station won't do it, two will. Swap in your own numbers.

And always add a margin. The real line is not set by clean scans: it is set by people who arrive without the QR open, people searching for the email at the door and groups waiting for the friend who has the tickets.

What each person does

The most common mistake is putting everyone on scanning. The door works better if each person has a fixed role:

  • Scanning. One person per station, phone in hand, who only scans. If an odd case comes up, they don't investigate it: they send it to the side and move on to the next person.
  • Lookup. Anyone who can't find their QR (dead battery, deleted email, someone else bought it) is looked up from the same scanner by surname, ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID) or email, and checked in there. You can also type the code by hand if the screen is cracked or the camera won't catch it. This person works with their own phone, out of the line.
  • Problems. The ticket already used, the one from another event, the person who didn't buy and wants in. The red "already used" message shows whose name it is in and how long ago it was checked in, and that detail settles most arguments. Whoever handles this should have authority to decide and criteria agreed in advance.
  • Managing the line, if the event is big: someone who walks the line asking people to have their QR open and their ID at hand, and who separates those without a ticket.

At a small event, lookup and problems can be handled by the same person. What you want to avoid is the person scanning also being the one who sorts things out: that is where the line stalls.

Anyone who arrives without a ticket can be sent to buy from their phone on the event page, if sales are still open. How to organize that last minute sale is in selling tickets at the door.

Stations by ticket type or identical stations

If you have several ticket types (general, VIP, table), there are two ways to set up the door:

  • All stations the same: any station scans any ticket. The scanner shows the type, so whoever scans knows where to send each person. It is the most flexible option.
  • One station per type: "General" and "VIP", each with its own line. It keeps things tidier when the areas are separate, and it has an advantage if the signal is bad, as you will see below.

What happens if the internet drops

The ENTRÁ scanner keeps validating offline with the tickets the phone downloaded when the event was selected. It marks each ticket as used on the spot, saves check-ins on the phone and syncs them by itself when the connection comes back. Meanwhile, a yellow banner warns that it is working offline.

The limit has to do precisely with the number of stations: with no connection, each phone only knows the check-ins it scanned itself. With one station nothing changes. With two or more offline, the same ticket (a shared screenshot, for example) could get through once at each station. When the connection returns, the dashboard flags which tickets got through twice and at which stations, but it can't be stopped in the moment.

So, if the venue has poor signal:

  • Select the event on each phone while connected, before opening, and test a QR on each one.
  • Keep at least one station with mobile data, if necessary with a line from another carrier.
  • Split lines by ticket type, so each type goes through a single phone.
  • Bring the attendee list exported as CSV from the dashboard, in case a phone fails.

The full detail is in access control without internet, and the rest of the door preparation in access control on event day.

What to bring to the door

  • One phone per station, charged, signed in and with the event selected.
  • A spare phone with the scanner open.
  • One power bank per station.
  • Each station's name entered in the scanner.
  • The attendee CSV downloaded.
  • A message to the audience before the event: bring your ID and arrive with the QR open. It is what shortens the line the most.

Frequently asked questions

How many people do I need on the door at a small event?

For a small event with staggered arrivals, two are enough: one who scans with the phone and another on the side who looks up anyone without a QR by surname or ID number and handles problems. If the whole audience arrives at once, add a second scanning station.

Do I need to buy scanners to control the entrance?

No. With ENTRÁ the QR is scanned from the phone's browser. Each station needs a phone with a camera, battery and a signed in session with the event selected before doors open.

What happens with several stations if the internet drops?

Each phone keeps validating with what it already downloaded, but with no connection it only knows the check-ins it scanned itself. With two stations offline, the same ticket could get through once at each one. When the connection returns, the dashboard flags which tickets got through twice and at which station. If the signal is bad, make sure at least one station has mobile data.
