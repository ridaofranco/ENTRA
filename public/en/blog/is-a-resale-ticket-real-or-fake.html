Real or fake ticket: how to tell before buying a resale

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

The event is sold out, someone in a group or a post says they have a ticket and sends you a photo of the QR so you can see it is real. The photo tells you nothing. This guide is for that moment: what proves nothing, what you can actually check and the point where you should stop.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). The advice here works for any resale; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

You can't tell by looking at the ticket

This is the first thing to accept, and the one that saves the most money. A QR ticket is not valuable because of how it looks but because its code has not been used yet and still belongs to the person offering it. Neither of those shows up in an image.

  • The QR screenshot looks identical whether the ticket is untouched, already used or sent to four other people.
  • The PDF or the purchase email proves the ticket was bought at some point. Not that it is still valid.
  • The buyer's name on the ticket doesn't protect you: the seller may be that person and still sell it twice.
  • A video opening the app shows the same thing as the screenshot, with more effort.

The code works only once: the first one to reach the door gets in and the rest stay outside. What that looks like from the door side is explained in how duplicate tickets are stopped at the door.

The only thing that gives certainty: the ticket moving into your name

If you can't verify the ticket by looking at it, the only way to be sure is for the ticketing platform to change its owner. At ENTRÁ that is the official transfer, and it works like this:

  • The ticket holder transfers it from their profile and enters your name, email and phone.
  • You get an email with a claim link pointing to www.entratickets.com/claim/ followed by a code. If the link goes to another domain, it is not from ENTRÁ.
  • To claim it you have to sign in with the same email it was sent to, with that email verified. Even if the link gets passed around, nobody else can keep it.
  • When you claim it, a new QR is issued in your name and the previous code stops working. Now it's settled: the other person's screenshot no longer gets in.

The step by step, including what the email you receive says, is in how to buy tickets without getting scammed. Here only one idea matters: the ticket is yours once you have claimed it, not when the email arrived or when the other person said "I already sent it".

While it is pending, it still belongs to the other person

This is where people get confused the most. A transfer that was started and not claimed has not changed anything yet: the old QR is still valid and the transfer can be cancelled. If you open the link and it says the ticket was already claimed by someone else or that the transfer was cancelled, that ticket is not yours, even if you have the email.

There are two cases where ENTRÁ simply does not allow a transfer:

  • The event has already happened. A transfer for a past date cannot be started or claimed.
  • The organizer disabled transfers for that event. If they tell you "it can't be transferred but don't worry, I'll send you the QR", what they are offering you is a photo.

Before looking for a resale, check the official channel

It sounds obvious and almost nobody does it. Many "sold out" events have a new release, another ticket type or places freed up by refunds. Go to the event from the link the organizer posted on their social media and see what is available. If there is nothing, write to them: they are the person who knows best whether more places will open.

And keep this in mind: ENTRÁ's terms prohibit reselling outside the platform. The transfer exists to pass your ticket to someone when you can't go, not to build a parallel market. A ticket that changed hands outside cannot be validated or replaced, and there is nobody to complain to.

Signs that a resale is a scam

The general ones (payment to a personal alias, pressure, a brand new profile) are already listed in the safe buying guide. These are specific to resales between individuals:

  • "I'll transfer it once you pay." The seller sets the order, always in their favor. There is no way the ticket is yours before you claim it.
  • They forward you the transfer email or send a screenshot of it. The real email reaches you, in your own inbox, because the person transferring entered your address.
  • The claim link is not on entratickets.com or asks for card details to "release" the ticket. Claiming a transfer costs nothing.
  • They offer you several single tickets for the same event "from friends who can't go", all as screenshots.
  • They ask for your password to "make the transfer faster". With it they get into your account and keep whatever is there.
  • They won't let you talk to the organizer or tell you not to ask anything because "they get annoyed".

If you are the one who can't go

On the other side, the rule is the same. If you are passing it to a friend, do it through the official transfer and don't send screenshots: a photo that gets around can be used by anyone before they get there. And if the event was cancelled or rescheduled, what happens to your ticket is in cancelled or rescheduled event.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if a resale ticket is real?

You can't tell by looking at it: a screenshot, a PDF or a forwarded email of a real ticket look the same as those of a ticket already used or sold to someone else. The only thing that gives you certainty is the ticket moving into your name through the platform's official transfer. At ENTRÁ, when you claim it a new QR is issued for you and the previous one stops working.

Does it help if the seller shows me the purchase email?

It proves the ticket was bought at some point, nothing more. It doesn't say whether it was used, passed to someone else or shown to five buyers at once. It is the same information as the QR screenshot.

When is a ticket transferred to me on ENTRÁ mine?

When you have claimed it, not when the email arrived. The claim link only works when you sign in with the email it was sent to, and only then is the new QR issued in your name. While the transfer is pending, the ticket still belongs to the person passing it to you.

Can I resell an ENTRÁ ticket to a stranger?

No. ENTRÁ's terms prohibit reselling outside the platform, and a ticket that changed hands that way cannot be validated or replaced. The official transfer exists to pass your ticket to someone when you can't go.
