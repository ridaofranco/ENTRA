New Year's Eve party: how to sell the tickets

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

A New Year's Eve party has everything a regular party has plus three problems of its own: the date is confusing, people compare what each ticket includes and at the door everyone arrives at once after the toast. This guide goes over how to solve each one before you sell.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

When to open sales

New Year's Eve is the only party of the year with a fixed date everyone knows, and many people make their plans early: who they have dinner with, where, how they get home. That argues for opening earlier than for a regular party. But there are also many people who decide in the last few days, when another plan falls through. In practice:

  • Open with a short, clear early bird, with a date or quantity limit, for those who already know they are going.
  • Raise prices in tiers until the last week, and keep a final tier for those who decide late.
  • Have something to announce every week: venue, DJ, what the ticket includes. Open sales with no news go cold.

How to think about opening time in general is in when to open ticket sales. In ENTRÁ each event goes through a review before being published, usually the same day: if you announce an opening time, load it with some margin.

The date: "night of the 31st into the 1st"

The party almost always starts after midnight, which means it is technically January 1. That is confusing: some people read "January 1" and think it is the following night. To avoid it:

  • Load the real date and time the doors open, and set the end time.
  • In the title or description, write "night of December 31 into January 1", and repeat it on the flyer.
  • State the entry hours in full: "entry from 12:30 a.m. until 3 a.m.". If there is a cutoff time for getting in, it should be written down before you sell, not announced at the door.

What the ticket includes

On New Year's Eve people compare: a party with a toast included versus one without. What your ticket includes has to be on the page, and in the ticket type name when something changes:

  • Toast: if there is one, at what time (if the party opens after midnight, the toast is not the midnight one, and it is worth making that clear).
  • Bar: if the ticket includes drinks, how many and which ones, or if there is an open bar with set hours. If it includes nothing, say the bar is separate.
  • Different ticket types, if there are any: "General", "With open bar", "Table for 6". In ENTRÁ you set up each type with its own name, price and capacity, and each type closes on its own when it fills up.

If you are going to offer tables or groups, the name should say how many people it is for.

Price and tiers

  • An early bird cheaper than the following tiers, with the difference stated everywhere.
  • If you are going to sell at the door, make it the most expensive tier, with "only if available" made clear.
  • Discount codes per promoter, if you work with promoters: in the dashboard you see how many times each code was used.

With ENTRÁ you keep 100% of the price you set for each ticket. Service and processing costs are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

The door with everyone at once

The peculiarity of New Year's Eve is that a lot of people arrive in a short time, right after the family toast. What helps:

  • More people at the door than at a regular party, with several phones scanning at once.
  • Separate lines: one for those who already have the QR code and another for those buying on the spot from their phone, if sales are still open.
  • Each QR code is valid only once. If someone sent a screenshot to a friend, whoever arrives first gets in. To avoid that problem, enable the official transfer: the old QR code is invalidated and the recipient gets a new one.
  • If someone can't find the email, you look them up by last name, ID number (DNI) or email from the scanner.
  • Check the venue's signal. With the venue full the connection can get worse; test the scanner before opening and have a backup network.

Frequently asked questions

What date do I set for a New Year's Eve party that starts after midnight?

Load the real date and time the doors open, which is usually January 1. So nobody gets confused, write "night of December 31 into January 1" in the title or description and repeat it on the flyer.

What should the ticket say about the toast and the bar?

If there is a toast, at what time; if the bar is included, how many drinks or until what time; and if nothing is included, that the bar is separate. If what is included changes, use different ticket types with that detail in the name.

Can I set a cutoff time for getting in?

Yes, but it has to be written on the page before you sell, not announced at the door. Buyers need to know until what time they can arrive.

Can people keep buying on the night itself?

Yes, while sales are still open. In ENTRÁ the page sells until the end time you loaded, and whoever buys from the line gets their QR code on screen and by email.
