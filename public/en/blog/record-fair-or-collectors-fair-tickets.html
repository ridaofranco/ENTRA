Guide for organizers

Vinyl record or collectors fair with tickets: timing and multi-day setup

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 6 min read

A record and vinyl fair, a comics meetup, an antique toy expo or a trading card swap have their own logic: collectors want to arrive first, because the good stuff sells in the first hour, and the rest of the public drops by to browse later. Stalls take up space, exhibitors charge in their own way and sometimes the fair lasts a whole weekend. This guide sorts out how to sell the ticket so the door is not the problem.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Who charges for what

A fair has two separate charges, and it is best not to mix them up from the start:

  • The public's ticket, sold and checked with ENTRÁ.
  • Each exhibitor's stall, charged separately. ENTRÁ does not collect stall rentals or take commissions on what each stall sells. That arrangement is made with each exhibitor, with its own payment and receipt.

The same goes for sales inside the fair: each exhibitor charges for their records or magazines however they like. Say on the page whether there are stalls that take cards or whether it is better to bring cash, because collectors ask that before anything else.

General entry, free with reservation or early entry

Most of these fairs work with a cheap general ticket. It covers part of the venue and setup, and filters out people who were just walking by. Three setups work well:

  • General entry, a single type, with the venue's capacity.
  • Free with reservation, if you prefer not to charge but want to know how many people are coming. The public reserves without paying and still gets their QR.
  • Early entry, for those who want to dig through the crates before anyone else. It is set up as a separate, more expensive ticket type, with the time slot in the name: "Ingreso temprano 10 a 12" (early entry, 10 to 12). Give it a small capacity, which is exactly what makes it valuable.

An important clarification: ENTRÁ does not have separate time slots. The scanner shows the ticket type of each QR, but it does not reject someone with a general ticket who arrives at 10. The time slot is controlled by the person at the door, looking at the ticket type name that appears when scanning. If someone with a general ticket arrives early, they wait outside until general opening; write that in the description. If you want kids to get in free, say from what age they pay in the first line of the description. If you sell a reduced ticket, it goes as another type with its own price.

How many people fit

At a fair people come in, walk around and leave, so the number that matters is how many people can be inside at the same time. It comes from the venue's approved capacity minus what the stalls, crates, exhibitors and team take up. Ask the venue for that number in writing.

The scanner records who comes in, not who leaves: it is not a counter of people inside. If the first hour can fill up, have someone count exits by hand or limit early entry with its capacity.

Exhibitors

Exhibitors and their helpers don't buy tickets, but they take up space and arrive early to set up. Two ways to organize them:

  • Complimentary tickets from the dashboard: you enter each person's name, email, phone and ID number (DNI), the ticket type, the quantity and a reason, for example the stall name. They get their QR by email and go through the scanner without mixing with sales. Complimentary tickets don't come out of the sales allocation, but they do count toward the total capacity shown in the dashboard.
  • A separate list at the loading door, if you prefer they don't go through ENTRÁ. In that case, subtract those people from capacity by hand.

Either way, give them a setup time that ends before early entry opens. Nothing annoys a collector more than walking in with boxes still in the aisle.

Several days

If the fair runs on a Saturday and a Sunday, you can load it as a multi-day event, with each day and its schedule. Then you choose the entry mode, which is one for the whole event:

  • One QR for the whole event: the person gets in on both days with the same code, once per day. It is the weekend pass.
  • One QR per day: the buyer chooses which days they attend and receives one code for each.

If someone shows the ticket on a day it doesn't cover, the scanner rejects it. Keep in mind that capacity is per ticket type and applies to all days: there is no separate cap for Saturday and Sunday. If Saturday fills up much more and you need an exact limit per day, load each day as a separate event. What to say on the page

  • Times: early entry, general opening and closing, for each day.
  • What there is: approximate number of stalls and categories (vinyl, CDs, comics, magazines, toys). If you can, name some exhibitors.
  • How to pay inside: whether stalls take cards or it is better to bring cash.
  • Whether there is buying or swapping: many fairs have stalls that buy collections. If so, say whether people can come with records to sell.
  • Whether people can leave and come back, and how.

If you want to know something about each buyer, for example which category they are looking for, you can add a question to the event. It is answered once per purchase. The buyer list comes out as a CSV and helps you measure the fair and announce the next edition. The day before, each buyer gets a reminder by email.

With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket. The service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

The door

  • Two lines at opening: one for early entry and another for general, so the person at the door doesn't have to check each type in a single line.
  • The QR is scanned from a phone, without buying readers. If the line is long, several phones scan at once.
  • If someone can't find the email, they are looked up by last name, ID number or email from the scanner.
  • Re-entry: the QR is good once per day. If people can step out to eat and come back, use a stamp or a wristband at entry.
  • Whoever arrives without a ticket buys from their phone, using a sign with the QR of the event link, as long as sales are open and there is capacity. ENTRÁ has no box office and does not take cash.

Frequently asked questions

Can I charge exhibitors for their stall with ENTRÁ?

No. ENTRÁ sells and checks the public's ticket. Each stall rental is arranged with the exhibitor separately, with its own payment and receipt. To let exhibitors in, you can issue them complimentary tickets from the dashboard, each with its own QR.

How do I sell an early entry ticket for collectors?

With a separate ticket type, with the time slot in the name, for example "Ingreso temprano 10 a 12", its own price and capacity. The scanner shows the type of each QR, but it does not reject by time: the time slot is controlled by the person at the door.

Is the same ticket valid for both days of the fair?

It depends on the mode you choose when loading the multi-day event. With one QR for the whole event, the ticket is good once per day. With one QR per day, the buyer chooses which days they attend and receives one code for each.
