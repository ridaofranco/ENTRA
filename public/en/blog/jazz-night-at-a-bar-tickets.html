Jazz night or music series at a bar: tables, sets and season passes

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 6 min read

A jazz night at a bar, a singer songwriter series at a club or a jam with a guest have something in common: a small crowd, tables that fill up first, a bar that works all night and an audience that comes back if they had a good time. Selling the ticket is easy. What you need to decide beforehand is what it includes, how the space is divided and whether the series is sold date by date or with a pass.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First, what the night looks like

Before loading anything, write in one line how the night works, because that is what you will put on the page:

  • Sets: how many and what time each one starts. In jazz it is common to play two sets with a break in the middle. If the second has a guest, say so.
  • Who plays: the house trio, the guest of the month, the open jam at the end. A series audience comes back for who is playing that night.
  • Real schedule: what time the bar opens, what time the music starts and what time it ends. Enter the end time in the event.
  • Kitchen and bar: whether people can have dinner, until what time the kitchen serves and whether the bar takes cards.

Ticket with a table or without

At a bar with music, where you sit matters: a table in front of the stage is not the same as standing at the bar. ENTRÁ has no seat or table map, so the specific table isn't chosen at purchase. What you can do is divide the room into ticket types, each with its name, price and quota:

  • "With table", with a quota equal to the chairs you have. When they fill up, that type shows as sold out and the rest stays on sale.
  • "Bar or standing", cheaper, with the quota of the space left over.

The bar assigns the actual table on arrival, first come first served or by looking at the buyer list. Say so on the page so nobody arrives at 10:30 pm expecting the front table. If you want to sell a whole table, you can set up a "Table for 4" type with its price, but keep in mind that it is a single ticket with a single QR: the group gets in together. If you prefer everyone to have their own, sell individual "With table" tickets; one purchase can include up to 4 of each type.

To know how many tables and chairs really fit, start from the bar's licensed capacity, not from what you would like. The details are in selling tickets to play at a bar or cultural center, along with the door deal with the owner.

Drink included

Many bars ask for the ticket to include a drink, or for a minimum spend per table. If so, the type name should say it: "With table + glass of wine", "Bar + drink". The buyer has to know what they are paying for without opening the description.

The ENTRÁ QR is valid once and it is for the door: the scanner shows the ticket type, but it doesn't keep track of what is collected at the bar. The drink is handled with something physical the door hands over when scanning: a token, a voucher or a wristband. How to set it up so it isn't collected twice is in tickets with a drink or combo included.

If the bar prefers "pass the hat" instead of a fixed ticket, that doesn't go through ENTRÁ: there is no pay what you want amount. You can sell a base ticket and pass the hat separately, stated on the page.

One date per event, or a pass

If the night is part of a series ("Jazz on Thursdays", "Tuesdays with guest musicians"), it is best to load each date as its own event. Each one has its page with the lineup, its quota and its sales, and in the dashboard you see how each night went. If one is called off, you change it without touching the others. A ticket for another date won't get in: the scanner rejects tickets for another event. How to sustain a series on a fixed date is in monthly music or stand-up series.

If you also want a pass for regulars, it is set up as a multi-day event, with one day per date and the "one QR for the whole event" mode: the same code is valid once on each date. Two things to keep in mind:

  • The quota is per type and applies to every day. Forty passes are forty places taken on every date of the series.
  • Pass and single ticket go in separate events. If you load the pass in one event and each single date in another, split the bar's capacity between them before publishing, so you don't sell more places than chairs.

Price

Add up what the night costs (musicians, sound, the bar's share under your agreement, promotion) and divide it by the tickets you really think you will sell. Some guidelines:

  • A table is worth more than the bar, and the difference should be noticeable.
  • Advance cheaper than the door. At a bar with few tables, buying in advance also secures the spot, and that sells itself.
  • A code for regulars, with a usage cap and expiry date, sent before announcing on social media.

With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket. The service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying. Whatever the bar is owed you settle with the venue yourself.

What to say on the page

  • Time of each set and what time the bar opens.
  • What each type includes: table or bar, with or without a drink.
  • How tables are assigned: first come first served or reserved by the bar.
  • Whether people can have dinner and until what time the kitchen serves.

If you need some information from each purchase, for example whether they are coming for dinner or a dietary restriction, you can add a question to the event. It is answered once per purchase, not per person. The answers appear in the buyer list, which you export as CSV and pass to the kitchen.

The day before, ENTRÁ sends each buyer a reminder email. If you have to cancel a date, careful: cancelling from the event edit screen doesn't notify anyone by email. Write to the buyers yourself using the exported list.

The door and the break between sets

  • One person stationed at the door, who is neither a musician nor a waiter.
  • The QR is scanned from a phone. The scanner shows in green the name and ticket type: table or bar, with or without a drink. If it is a table ticket, whoever greets them walks them over or points out the table.
  • If someone can't find the email, you look them up by surname, ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID) or email from the scanner.
  • The break between sets. People step out to smoke or get some air. The QR is valid only once per date, so re-entry is controlled with a stamp or wristband put on at entry.
  • Anyone arriving without a ticket can buy from their phone on the event page while sales are still open. ENTRÁ has no box office and doesn't take cash.

If there is live music, also check what you owe for music rights (in Argentina, SADAIC) in SADAIC: what to pay if you play music at an event.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell numbered tables for a jazz night?

ENTRÁ has no seat or table map. What you can do is separate ticket types, for example "With table" and "Bar or standing", each with its price and quota. The bar assigns the specific table on arrival, first come first served or according to the buyer list.

Do I load the series as one event or one per date?

For a series that lives on single date sales, one event per date is best, with its lineup and quota. If you also want to sell a pass, it is set up as a multi-day event with one QR for the whole event, valid once on each date.

Can the audience leave between sets and come back in?

The QR is valid only once per date, so re-entry isn't controlled with the scanner. The practical approach is a stamp or wristband put on at entry, and the door lets back in anyone who has it.
