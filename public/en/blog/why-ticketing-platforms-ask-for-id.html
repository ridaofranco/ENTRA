Guide for buyers

Why the ticketing platform asks for your ID, email and phone, and what is done with that data

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

You are about to buy a ticket and the checkout asks for your name, email, ID number and phone. It is fair to wonder why, especially if a club's box office never asked you for them. Here is what a ticketing platform does with each piece of data, what ENTRÁ asks for and what it uses it for, who else sees it and what Argentine law says about your personal data.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). The law quoted here is Argentina's personal data law.

What data ENTRÁ asks for at purchase

In the "Tus datos" (your details) step of the ENTRÁ checkout there are four fields:

  • Full name. Required.
  • Email. Required, and the checkout won't let you continue if it is missing the @ or is misspelled, because the ticket travels that way.
  • ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID). Required.
  • Phone. Optional.

You don't need to create an account to buy. If you have an account, your ID number and phone are saved in your profile so you don't have to type them again next time. What ENTRÁ does not ask for is your home address or postal code, and it does not store card details: the payment is processed by the provider (for example, Mercado Pago, the Argentine payment platform).

What each piece of data is for

None of them is there for decoration. Each one solves a concrete problem that comes up before, during or after the event:

  • The email is the delivery. That is where the ticket with its QR arrives. If you type it wrong, the ticket won't reach you, and ENTRÁ's terms say that entering the email and details correctly is the buyer's responsibility. If it happened to you, see what to do if your ticket did not arrive by email.
  • The ID number identifies you at the door. If you arrive with a dead battery or your QR got deleted, whoever is checking entry looks you up by last name, ID number or email in the scanner and validates your ticket from there. It is the piece of data that doesn't change and that you carry with you. The ID number is also printed on the downloadable ticket, next to the holder's name. More on this in I forgot my phone: how do I get into the event.
  • The ID number and phone help keep the payment from being declined. ENTRÁ passes your name, email, ID number and, if you entered it, your phone to Mercado Pago as payer details. They are signals the payment provider's anti-fraud system uses to assess the transaction. With less data, a legitimate payment is more likely to bounce.
  • The phone is a backup channel. If the event changes time or there is a problem with the purchase, the organizer has another way to reach you besides email.

Who sees your data

This is the most useful thing to know before buying on any ticketing platform. In ENTRÁ's case, according to its privacy policy:

  • The data controller is SOMOS DER, the company behind ENTRÁ.
  • The event organizer receives the details of the people who bought for their event, to manage entry and attendee control. From their dashboard they can export the attendee list, which includes name, email, ID number and phone, among other ticket details.
  • The providers that make the service work: the payment processor and the email delivery service, only to the extent necessary.
  • Authorities, when there is a legal obligation.

The same policy says ENTRÁ does not sell your data to third parties and does not use cookies for third-party advertising purposes.

What Argentine law says

In Argentina personal data is regulated by Law 25.326 on Personal Data Protection. Without getting into the fine print, there are four points that help explain why a ticketing platform asks for what it asks for and no more:

  • Only what is needed. Article 4 says the data collected must be "accurate, adequate, relevant" and not excessive in relation to the purpose for which it is obtained.
  • It is not used for anything else. The same article states that it cannot be used for purposes different from or incompatible with those that justified collecting it.
  • They have to tell you. Article 6 requires that you be informed "expressly and clearly", before your data is requested, of things like what it will be used for. That is what the privacy policy is for.
  • You can ask to see it, correct it or delete it. Article 14 grants a right of access, and whoever holds the data must respond within ten calendar days of the formal request. Article 16 grants the right to correct, update or delete it, within a maximum of five business days from the claim.

At ENTRÁ, that request is made by writing to contacto@somosder.com.ar, as stated in the privacy policy, which also names the Agencia de Acceso a la Información Pública (AAIP, Argentina's Agency for Access to Public Information) as the authority that enforces the law.

How to protect yourself when buying on any ticketing platform

  • Read what it asks for and why. If a ticket site asks for data that has nothing to do with the event (bank passwords, photos of your ID, codes sent to you by SMS), be suspicious.
  • Check your email twice before paying. It is the most common mistake and the one that causes the most trouble later.
  • Buy through the event's official link. If you get a link by private message, check it with the organizer. More warning signs in how to buy tickets without getting scammed.
  • Look for the privacy policy and check who the data controller is and which email to write to.

For organizers: handle the list with care

If you export the attendee list, you are holding the names, emails, ID numbers and phone numbers of your audience. Use it for what it was collected for: controlling entry and communicating about the event. Keep it somewhere safe, don't share it in open groups and don't send it to third parties. The law that governs that data is the same for you as for the ticketing platform.

Frequently asked questions

Why does a ticketing platform ask for my ID number to buy a ticket?

To identify you at the door if you don't have the QR and so the payment provider can assess the transaction. At ENTRÁ, whoever checks entry can look you up by last name, ID number (DNI) or email and validate your ticket, and the ID number is sent to Mercado Pago as payer data.

Is giving my phone number required at ENTRÁ?

No. At the ENTRÁ checkout, name, email and ID number are required. Phone is optional and serves as a backup channel and as one more piece of data for the payment.

Who sees my data when I buy a ticket on ENTRÁ?

According to the privacy policy, SOMOS DER as data controller, the event organizer to manage entry, the payment and email delivery providers as needed, and authorities if there is a legal obligation. ENTRÁ does not sell your data to third parties.

Can I ask for my data to be deleted?

Yes. Argentina's Law 25.326 grants the right to access, correct, update or delete your personal data. At ENTRÁ you request it by writing to contacto@somosder.com.ar.

We ask for just enough to get you in

At ENTRÁ you buy without creating an account, the ticket arrives by email with a unique QR and, if you don't have it at hand, the door finds you by your ID number.
