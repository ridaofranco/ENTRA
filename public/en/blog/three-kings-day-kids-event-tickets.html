Three Kings' Day event for kids: tickets and gifting

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 6 min read

A show, a play or an afternoon of games for Three Kings' Day (Día de Reyes, January 6, when children in Argentina traditionally get presents) has two audiences: the child who goes and the adult who gives it as a gift. Sales start in December, between Christmas and New Year, and the event falls in the middle of a heat wave with half the city on vacation. This guide sorts out what to decide before opening sales.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

The date: January 6, or close to it

January 6 is the date everyone has in mind, but it is not always the best one for the event. In 2027 it falls on a Wednesday, and many parents work. Three ways to handle it:

  • The day itself, with an afternoon or evening show, when parents get off work. It has the symbolic value of the day.
  • The closest weekend, before or after. It is the most convenient for families and lets you do a morning show and an afternoon show.
  • Several shows between January 2 and 10, if you have the venue and the cast. Each show is loaded as a separate event, with its own quota and link, and the scanner rejects tickets for another show.

What cannot be missing is a fixed date before people start buying. When you load the event you can mark the date as Próximamente (coming soon), but nobody gives as a gift something they don't know the date of.

Selling as a gift

Most tickets for a Three Kings' Day event are bought by someone who is not the parent: grandparents, aunts and uncles, godparents. That changes when and how you sell.

  • Open sales before Christmas. People giving a Kings' Day present usually sort it out in December, along with the rest of their shopping. If you publish on January 2, you are late. How to think about when to open is in when to open ticket sales.
  • Allow for the review. Every event goes through a review before it is published, usually the same day. In December, load it with time to spare.
  • Explain how the ticket is passed on. The person giving the gift buys with their email. For the ticket to end up on the parents' phone, they transfer it from Mis Tickets (my tickets) in their profile, entering the name and email of the recipient. That person claims it by signing in with that email and gets a new QR; the old one stops working. Tickets are transferred one at a time.
  • Leave transfers turned on. It comes on by default when you load the event; if you turn it off, the button doesn't appear and nobody can pass on their ticket.

There is a detail worth telling the gift giver: the email with the claim link goes out as soon as they confirm the transfer. If they want it to be a surprise, they should do it on the day they hand over the present. And another that helps you: ENTRÁ sends a reminder email the day before the event to whoever holds the ticket. If grandma bought and never transferred, the reminder reaches her, not the parents. All the details are in tickets as holiday gifts.

A line for the description you can adapt: "Is it a gift? Buy with your email and transfer the tickets from your ENTRÁ profile to the family's email. Don't send QR screenshots: each code is valid only once at the door."

Child and adult tickets

Children don't go to a Kings' Day show alone, and the gift giver needs to know how many tickets to buy. Set up ticket types with name, price and quota:

  • "Child, from age 2", with the age from which they pay.
  • "Accompanying adult", at a different price or the same one, but spelled out.
  • Babies: say whether they pay and whether they sit on a lap. If you want to count them without charging, issue them a complimentary ticket from the dashboard.

State in plain words that every child and every adult needs a ticket. The grandfather giving "the Kings' Day show" to two grandchildren thinks of two tickets, and at the door two children and a mother show up. Each purchase allows up to 4 tickets of each type; a big family fits in one purchase, a group of cousins might not. If you want to know something about the children, like their ages or names for a shout-out on stage, add an extra question to the form: it is answered once per purchase, so ask for all the names in the same one. Capacity

With gift sales concentrated in December, capacity can fill up sooner than you think. What to define:

  • The real capacity of the venue or space, counting adults and children. If the place has a licensed capacity, babies and complimentary tickets count toward it too.
  • A quota per ticket type. If the venue has 150 seats and you expect one adult for every two children, split it that way and adjust in the dashboard as you watch sales.
  • What you do if it sells out. Adding a second show is easier if you planned it in advance: same place, another time, another event.

In the dashboard you see sales in real time and can export the buyer list as CSV, which helps you announce changes: editing the event does not send an email to those who bought.

The January heat

A kids' event in January (midsummer in Argentina) carries a risk that a July one doesn't: heat, with small children and adults loaded with bags.

  • Outdoors, early in the morning or when the sun goes down. Never in the early afternoon.
  • In a venue, ask about the cooling with the room full, not empty.
  • The line counts too: with shade, and open with enough margin so it moves fast.
  • Water at hand, and written on the page whether bottles are allowed in.
  • Say what happens if there is a heat or storm alert before selling, not on the day of the event.

The details are in summer events in the heat: water, shade and timing. And if your city empties out in January, selling tickets in January and February covers who to sell to.

The door

Families arrive together and with only one person's phone. Scan all the QR codes in a row; if someone can't find the email, you can look them up by surname, ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID) or email from the scanner, and the scanner shows the type of each ticket. ENTRÁ has no box office and does not record cash: anyone who arrives without a ticket buys from their phone while there is capacity left. Frequently asked questions

Is it better to hold the event on January 6 itself?

It depends on the day of the week it falls on and on your audience. January 6, 2027 is a Wednesday, and many people work. Other options are the closest weekend or several shows between January 2 and 10. What matters is that the date is published before Christmas, so people can give it as a gift.

Can a ticket for the Kings' Day show be given as a gift?

Yes. The gift giver buys with their email and then transfers the tickets from Mis Tickets (my tickets) in their profile to the email of the child's father or mother. That person claims it by signing in with that email and gets a new QR; the previous one stops working. Tickets are transferred one at a time.

What time works best for a kids' event in January?

If it is outdoors, early in the morning or when the sun goes down. If it is in a venue, it should have enough cooling with the room full. Write on the page the opening time, the start time, how long it lasts and what to bring.
