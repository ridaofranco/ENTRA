Selling tickets in December: the holidays, the aguinaldo and presales

By SOMOS DER · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

December looks like the best month to hold an event: people get the second half of their aguinaldo (Argentina's mandatory year-end bonus for employees), they are in a good mood and they want to go out. But it is also the month with the busiest calendar of the year. Work year-end dinners, school events, farewells, Christmas and New Year compete for the same nights and the same money. If you are going to sell an event in December, what decides the result is when you open sales, which date you choose and how you remind people who bought.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

What you are competing against in December

In other months your competition is other events. In December you add everything that is not an event but takes up the same night:

  • Year-end dinners with work, the club, the group of friends.
  • Year-end shows and recitals at schools, workshops and academies.
  • Holiday spending. Gifts, food, trips. The aguinaldo helps, but it has many places to go.
  • Summer holidays. From the second half of the month, part of your audience starts leaving or saving to leave.

Dates worth avoiding

There are three nights that, unless your event is designed for them, are best left alone:

  • December 24. Christmas Eve: a family dinner almost everywhere in the country.
  • December 25. A public holiday and a day of lingering at the table. Many people sleep or travel.
  • December 31. New Year's Eve. Only a party that starts after the midnight toast works, and that is a different product. If that is your case, see how to sell tickets for a New Year's party.

The nights before those dates aren't neutral either. If you can choose, the cleanest dates are usually the weekends in the first half of December, before holidays and trips begin. How to weigh day and time in general is in what day and time to choose for your event; what changes here is that other people's calendars weigh more than yours.

The aguinaldo: use it without depending on it

The December half of the aguinaldo is paid to salaried employees, not to your whole audience. People under the simplified tax regime for the self-employed (monotributo), freelancers and students don't get it. That is why it works as a push, not as a plan.

  • Think about the deadline, not the exact day. The law sets the second installment of the aguinaldo to be paid by December 18, but each employer pays it when they can within that deadline, and some earlier. If you want to ride that moment, have your general sale open during the week of the 18th, not starting that day.
  • Offer two moments to buy. An early presale for people who already follow you and want to secure their spot, and a general sale that coincides with the time of the month when people have the aguinaldo in hand.
  • Don't raise the price because of the aguinaldo. Raise it by tier, as always, and announce the change with a date. More on setting up tiers in how to price tickets: tiers and presale.

A sales calendar for December

The general rule from when to open ticket sales still applies, but in December it pays to move everything a bit earlier:

  • Early November: publish the event. Every event goes through a review before it is published, usually the same day, so load it with some margin before announcing it.
  • November: presale. In ENTRÁ the ticket types you mark as Early Bird are the ones sold during the presale. Announce how long it lasts and send it to your list first.
  • Late November or early December: official sale. When you press "Lanzar Venta Oficial" (launch official sale), the presale closes and the other types remain. You make the cut yourself; there is no automatic switch by date.
  • The last two weeks before the event: push. Reminders on social media, a discount code with a usage limit for a specific group, and checking the dashboard to see whether sales are keeping pace.

Reminders: December's problem is that people forget

In December people buy in advance and then their calendar fills up. The risk is not only that they don't buy: it is that they buy and don't come, or arrive late because they were coming from another dinner.

  • The email the day before. ENTRÁ sends a reminder email the day before the event to each buyer with a valid ticket, one per person even if they bought several. If the event lasts several days, it goes out the day before each day to whoever has a ticket for that day.
  • Your own notices. A week before and on the day, on social media and WhatsApp, with doors time, address and what to bring.
  • Transfer as a way out. If someone bought and then the work dinner came up, they can pass their ticket on with the official transfer instead of not coming. Keep it enabled on the event and say so in the description.

What to watch while you sell

In the ENTRÁ dashboard you see sales in real time, the caps for each ticket type and who bought. In December watch two things: whether the presale moved in the first week (if not, the problem is the date or the announcement, not the price) and how much sells in the days before. If sales stall mid-month, try a one-off push before lowering the price. Ideas for that in why your tickets aren't selling.

Frequently asked questions

Is it a good idea to hold an event on December 24, 25 or 31?

For an ordinary event, no: they are family nights and people have had plans for weeks. The exception is a party designed for after dinner, like a New Year's party, which sells on a different logic. If your event is not that, find another date.

When do I open sales if the event is in mid-December?

Well in advance, because December's calendar fills up fast with year-end dinners and school events. Open a presale in November for people who already follow you and leave the general sale for the weeks before. That way you reach December with a base already sold.

Does ENTRÁ send a reminder to people who bought?

Yes. The day before the event, ENTRÁ emails each buyer with a valid ticket for that day, one per person even if they bought several. If the event lasts several days, the notice goes out the day before each day to whoever has a ticket for that day.

Can I change the price when the presale ends?

Yes. In ENTRÁ the ticket types marked as Early Bird are the ones sold in the presale, and when you press "Lanzar Venta Oficial" (launch official sale) the presale closes and the other types remain. You make the cut yourself, so announce the date and stick to it.
