Guide for organizers

Charity match or a player's farewell game: tickets, stands and club members

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

A charity match, a friendly between club legends or the farewell game of a retiring player fills a neighborhood ground like few things can. People who never go show up, the usual club members come, whole families come. It is organized by the club, a group of friends or the player himself, almost never someone who sells tickets every weekend. This guide sorts out how to set up the stands, what to do with members and minors, and what to check before opening sales.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Before selling: permit, league and capacity

A match with spectators at a club ground is not a barbecue among friends. Before setting a price, sort out three things:

  • The permit. What is needed depends on the city, the league and whether the players are registered. Check with your municipality and the league, and also ask about insurance and how many security staff they require for the number of people you will receive.
  • Capacity. Ask the club how many people the ground can hold under its license, stand by stand, and never exceed it, not even with guests.
  • Where the money goes. If it is for charity, say on the page who or what it is for, and who receives the money. That sells more than any flyer. How to tell it without it sounding like begging is in charity or fundraising events.

The stands of the ground are ticket types

Each stand is a ticket type with its name, price and quota: "Platea" (seated stand), "Popular local" (home terrace), "Popular visitante" (away terrace), "Palco" (box). When one sells out it shows as sold out and the rest stay on sale. You can't create the event without entering how many tickets go on sale in each type, and the dashboard shows total capacity adding up what was sold and complimentary tickets.

ENTRÁ has no numbered seat map, so within each stand seating is first come first served. Write it on the page: "Unnumbered seated stand, first come first served. Gates open at 3 pm." If both teams bring their own supporters, give the away terrace a low quota and sell it separately, with a link you pass to the other club.

Each purchase can include up to 4 tickets per type. A family of five buys in two goes or mixes types, for example three general and two child tickets. Say so on the page so nobody thinks it sold out.

Members, minors and retirees

At a club match there is almost always a price for club members. There are two ways to set it up:

  • A "Socio" (member) ticket type, with its price and quota. It is the clearest for the buyer. On the page and in the name put "showing your membership card at the gate".
  • A discount code the club hands out only through its member list, by percentage or fixed amount, with a usage cap and expiry if you want. In the list of codes you see how many times it was used.

In both cases, checking that the person is a member happens at the gate: the scanner shows the ticket type of each QR, and if it says "Socio", the card is requested. You can add a required extra question at checkout, such as "Membership number", but keep in mind that extra questions are answered once per purchase, not per ticket.

The same goes for minors and retirees: their own ticket type at the price you decide, or no ticket if children don't pay. If they don't pay, they still have to count toward capacity. Write the rule on the page: "Under 12s free, accompanied by an adult with a ticket."

Guests: players, legends, press

At a farewell game many people get in without paying: the players of both teams and their families, invited legends, referees, local press, the club's board. Load them as complimentary tickets from the dashboard: name, email, ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID), ticket type, quantity and a reason ("Squad", "Press"). Each one gets their QR by email. Complimentary tickets don't come out of the sales quota, but they do count toward total capacity, so if the seated stand has six hundred places and you will give out a hundred invitations, sell five hundred. How many to give and how to control them is in complimentary tickets and invitations.

Price, donations and what reaches the cause

With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket. The service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying. So if the ticket is ARS 10,000, ARS 10,000 per ticket reaches the cause.

ENTRÁ has no pay what you want amount: there is no field for each person to enter whatever they like. If you want to offer the option of giving more, set up a "Ticket + donation" type at a higher price with a clear name, and put it next to the general one. For people who can't go but want to help, the channel is different: a payment alias or a club account, accounted for separately.

On match day, at the gate

A club ground has one or two gates, lots of people arriving in the half hour before kickoff and sometimes poor signal. To avoid chaos:

  • One scanner per gate, with a dedicated person who isn't part of organizing the match. Scanning is done from the phone's browser: green with the name and ticket type, red with the reason. If someone can't find the email, you look them up by name, email or DNI.
  • If the signal drops, scanned entries are saved on the phone.
  • Anyone arriving without a ticket buys from their phone, without creating an account. ENTRÁ has no box office and doesn't record cash payments, so put up a sign with the link's QR in the line, away from the scanner. If the club decides to take cash as well, that goes separately, with its own ticket book and cash box. The setup is in selling tickets at the door.
  • Members and minors in the next line, where the card or age is checked before scanning.

If it rains or the match is called off

Football is played in the rain, but a heavy storm or a waterlogged pitch still calls it off. Write on the page what happens in that case: a new date, a refund or the ticket stays as a donation. Cancelling the event from the ENTRÁ dashboard emails every buyer that it was cancelled, but what comes next you explain yourself using the exported attendee list, and post it on the club's social media.

Afterwards: reporting what was raised

If the match was for charity, people want to know how much was raised. When it is over, download from the dashboard the attendee list, one row per ticket with type, origin (purchase or complimentary) and check-in, and the sales spreadsheet, one row per order with quantity, subtotal, total and status. With that you build the final figure: tickets sold per stand, complimentary tickets, member discounts and, separately, what came in as cash or by alias. Publish it. How to close those numbers is in the wrap up after the event.

Frequently asked questions

Can I have numbered seats in the club's seated stand?

ENTRÁ has no numbered seat map. Each stand of the ground (seated stand, home terrace, away terrace) is a ticket type with its price and quota, and within each stand seating is first come first served. If the club needs assigned seats, it is worth knowing before choosing a ticketing platform.

How do I set a price for club members?

There are two ways: a "Socio" (member) ticket type with its price and quota, stating that the membership card is checked at the gate, or a discount code the club hands out only to members, with a usage cap. In both cases, the real check that the person is a member happens at the gate.

What permit do I need for a charity match with spectators?

It depends on the city, the league and whether affiliated teams are playing. Check with your municipality and the league before opening sales, and also ask about insurance and the number of security staff they require for the capacity you will sell.
