Guide for organizers

Graduation party: tickets, places per class and family

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

A graduation party (in Argentina, the fiesta de egresados that celebrates the end of secondary school or a degree) brings very different audiences together on the same night: the graduates, their families, friends and sometimes teachers. Each group pays differently, arrives at a different time and has different rules. If you sort it out before opening sales, the night runs itself. If not, you end up with one class left without places and another that brought twice as many guests.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First, who organizes and who gets paid

Before loading anything, put three things in writing with the parents' committee, the student council or whoever hires you:

  • Who the organizer is in front of the public. That is whoever loads the event, gets paid and answers if something changes.
  • Whether the graduate's ticket is already paid as part of a package (dinner, trip, venue) or whether each graduate buys their own.
  • How many people the venue is approved for. That number overrides everything else. How to calculate it without going over is in approved capacity: how many tickets to sell.

Ticket types: graduates, family and guests

In ENTRÁ each ticket type has its own name, price and capacity, and it closes automatically when it fills up. For a graduation party, a setup that works:

  • Graduate: its own price, with a capacity equal to the number of graduates. If the ticket is already paid in the package, don't sell it: issue it as a complimentary ticket from the dashboard, with each graduate's name and email. It arrives by email with its QR.
  • Family: for parents, siblings and grandparents who attend the dinner or the graduates' entrance.
  • Late guest: if the party opens to friends later on, a separate type with another price and the entry time in the name, for example "Invitado desde las 2" (guest from 2 am).
  • Kids: if younger siblings come with the family, decide from what age they pay and write it on the page.

The door scanner shows each ticket's type, so the person at the entrance knows on the spot whether a graduate, a family member or a late guest is coming in. How to hand out complimentary tickets without losing capacity is in complimentary tickets and invitations: how many to give.

Places per class

When the party brings together several classes (5° A, 5° B, the other school), the usual question is how to stop one class from buying everything before the others. There are two ways to solve it with what you have in the dashboard:

  • One ticket type per class: "Familia 5° A", "Familia 5° B", each with its own capacity. It is the clearest option: you see in the dashboard how many each class sold and each one closes separately. It works best with few classes, because with many the list of types gets long for the buyer.
  • One code per graduate: you give each graduate their own discount code with a usage cap equal to the guests they are entitled to. Each code has its own usage counter, so you know who has already filled their places. The discount can be small; what matters is the cap.

An honest clarification: the ticketing platform does not tie a guest ticket to a specific graduate. If you need a list of "who is whose guest", the code per graduate is the closest thing, because the use of the code is recorded on each purchase. If the committee wants something more detailed, a shared spreadsheet where each family writes down names handles the rest outside the platform.

Minors, alcohol and ID

At a secondary school graduation party there are almost certainly minors, among the graduates or their siblings. That changes things you need to sort out before selling:

  • What the venue allows. Ask the hall or club whether its license admits minors and at what times. That is a condition of the venue, not the ticketing platform.
  • Alcohol. Argentina's Law 24.788 prohibits selling alcoholic drinks to anyone under eighteen nationwide. If there is a bar, it has to be able to tell who is a minor: a colored wristband handed out at entry is the most practical way.
  • Age is checked at the door, with the ID in hand. ENTRÁ does not verify ages when selling.

At checkout, ENTRÁ asks for the buyer's name, email and ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID). It is one per purchase: if a parent buys four tickets for the family, their ID number is recorded, not each person's. That detail helps at the door to find the purchase when someone can't find the email, because the scanner searches by last name, ID number or email. What that data is used for is explained in why the ticketing platform asks for your ID, email and phone.

Price tiers

Families tend to buy early and late guests buy at the last minute. Price tiers help you organize both:

  • First tier for families, cheaper, with a cutoff date announced from the start. It gives you an early idea of how many people are coming to dinner.
  • Second tier, from that date on. You change the price from the dashboard when the first one closes.
  • Late guests with their own price and capacity, which you can open closer to the date.

To set price jumps without scaring anyone off, see how to price your tickets and set up tiers. With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket; the service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

If someone can't go

At a graduation party it is common for an aunt who can't make it to pass her place to a cousin. The official transfer handles that: the buyer enters the other person's details, that person gets a link to claim the ticket and, once they claim it, the old QR is invalidated and a new one is issued in their name. If you prefer tickets not to change hands, you can keep transfers disabled for your event. Decide before selling and write it on the page.

What to say on the page

  • What each ticket type includes: whether there is dinner, from what time each group gets in.
  • Whether minors are admitted and that entry requires an ID.
  • How places are split per class or per graduate, and until when.
  • Whether there is a dress code, parking or transport.
  • What time it ends, with the end time loaded in the event, so families can plan the trip home.

If the party is only for the school community, mark the event as hidden: it does not appear in the public listings and only people with the link can get in.

Frequently asked questions

How do I limit how many guests each graduate can bring?

The simplest way is to give each graduate their own discount code with a usage cap equal to the guests they are entitled to. Another option is to set up a "Familia" ticket type per class with a total capacity. The ticketing platform does not tie a guest ticket to a specific graduate, so you define the split with those caps.

Can minors attend a graduation party?

It depends on the venue and what its license allows. If there are minors, selling alcohol to anyone under 18 is prohibited by Argentina's Law 24.788, so you need to be able to tell them apart at the door, for example with a wristband. Age is checked with the physical ID at entry.

Is the ID number requested at checkout each guest's?

No. The ID number entered at checkout belongs to the person making the purchase. If a parent buys four tickets for the family, their ID number is recorded. At the door it helps find the purchase if someone doesn't have the email at hand, but it does not replace the age check.
