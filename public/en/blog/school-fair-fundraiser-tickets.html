Guide for organizers

School fair or fundraiser with a contribution ticket: tickets, raffles and accounting

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

The spring kermés (a school fair with games and food stalls), the cooperadora's peña (a folk music night run by the school parents' association), the end of year party to raise money for the class trip or to fix the playground. They are organized by a group of mothers and fathers with little time, the audience is whole families and, at the end, everyone wants to know how much was raised. This guide sorts out what is charged as the ticket, what goes separately and how to report the accounts without leaving any doubt.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First, separate what is the ticket from what isn't

A school party mixes several things people pay for: the ticket or contribution, game tokens, the food stand, the raffle, sometimes a prize draw or a cake stall. If it all goes into the same cash box, afterwards nobody knows where each peso came from. Before loading the event, write down on a sheet:

  • The ticket or contribution: what each person pays to be at the party. This is what is sold with ENTRÁ.
  • Tokens, food stand and stalls: paid for inside, with their own cash box and their own person in charge.
  • The raffle: completely separate, with its own authorization, numbers and count. Further down is why.

Whatever you decide goes in the event description and in the message that goes around each class's group chat: "The contribution is the ticket to the fair. Tokens and food are paid for inside."

Contribution or ticket: same thing, different name

Many schools in Argentina call it a "bono contribución" or "bono colaboración" (contribution voucher), which at any other event would be the ticket. For selling it the name doesn't matter: at ENTRÁ you load it as a ticket type, with the name the school uses, its price and its quota. Some ways to set it up:

  • One contribution per person, at a single price. The simplest to explain in a WhatsApp group.
  • "Adult contribution" and "Child contribution", at different prices. Each type has its name, price and quota, and the dashboard shows how many of each were sold.
  • A higher solidarity contribution, for the family that wants to give more, next to the regular one. It is the tidiest way to receive extra donations without passing the hat.

Each person gets their own QR by email, without creating an account. One purchase can include up to four tickets of each type, so a family of two adults and three children can do it in a single purchase if you separated adult and child. If you set up a single contribution type, a family of six makes two purchases: say so in the description so nobody thinks it sold out.

With ENTRÁ the parents' association receives 100% of the price it sets for each contribution. The service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, visible before paying. To get paid, the organizer connects a Mercado Pago account (the Argentine payment platform) from the dashboard and sales go there: if the association has its own account, use that one and not the account of a person in the group.

Children and the ticket

The question that will come up most in the group is "do kids pay?". The association decides the answer, but it has to be written before selling:

  • From what age people pay: "Under 4s go free." Put it in the type name ("Child, from age 4") and in the description.
  • Whether the school's students pay or go in free as the hosts. If they go in free and you want them counted, you can issue them complimentary tickets from the dashboard, with name, email and reason. If not, count them by hand at the door.
  • That children who don't pay still take up space. If the playground or hall has a capacity limit, the count includes everyone, paying or not.

Who sees the sale: open or community only

A parents' association peña can be open to the neighborhood or meant only for the school's families. Both are possible:

  • Open: the event is published and anyone with the link can buy.
  • Community only: you mark the event as hidden, so it doesn't appear in the listings and only people with the link get in, which is sent through each class's group chat.

If the school wants the party to be free and raise money only through the food stand and tokens, that works too: the event is marked as free and each family reserves its place with a QR, without paying anything and without going through Mercado Pago. That way you know how many people are coming and how many portions to prepare.

Raffles go separately from the ticket

Many school parties have a raffle or a contribution with a prize draw. That is not a ticket and it isn't sold as a ticket: a raffle has its own authorization, its numbers and its own control. If the contribution you sell includes a draw, it is no longer just a ticket.

An example of how the rules treat it: in the province of Buenos Aires, Decree-Law 9403/79 (in Spanish), in its article 1 (as worded by Law 11.349), says that raffles may only be promoted, sold or circulated with express municipal authorization. And it defines a raffle broadly: any number, ticket or slip with draws of goods, registrable or not, or other incentives as a prize. Each province and each municipality has its own rules, so before printing a single number ask your municipality what procedure applies and who can apply for it.

What this means in practice for the party:

  • The ticket doesn't include raffle numbers. If you want to give something away, make it an authorized draw managed separately, not a prize tied to the contribution.
  • The raffle has its own person in charge, its ticket book and its count, and its result goes on its own line in the final accounts.
  • ENTRÁ doesn't sell raffles or draw numbers. It sells tickets with a QR to get into an event.

On the day of the party

A school door with families is slow: strollers, grandparents, kids running off to the playground. To keep it moving:

  • Two people at the door, not the same ones running the food stand. Scanning is done from the phone's browser: green with the name and ticket type, red with the reason.
  • All of a family's QR codes in a row, from the same phone. If someone can't find the email, you look them up by surname, ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID) or email from the scanner.
  • A sign with the QR of the sales link for anyone arriving without a ticket, while sales are open. ENTRÁ has no box office and doesn't record cash payments: whoever wants to buy at the door buys from their phone.
  • If the association accepts cash at the door, it should be with a single person in charge, a fixed price and a numbered ticket book or a list. That money doesn't appear in ENTRÁ's reports and is counted separately at closing.
  • If the playground has poor signal, the scanner keeps working: entries are saved on the phone and synced when the connection comes back.

Tokens, food stand and raffle, each with its own cash box and sheet. At the end of the night, each person in charge counts their part with someone else beside them and writes it down on the spot.

Reporting the accounts

This is what matters most for the next party: if families see where the money went, they buy again. A good report has these lines:

  • Contributions sold, by type and total amount. It comes from the ENTRÁ dashboard, where you also export the buyer spreadsheet.
  • Complimentary tickets: how many and for whom (students, teachers, performers at the peña).
  • What was raised separately: tokens, food stand, stalls, cash at the door and raffle, each with its figure.
  • Expenses: sound, supplies, prizes, rentals, each with its receipt.
  • What is left and what for: "This much was left. It goes toward the playground awning."

Announce from the start when you will publish the accounts ("the following Monday, in each class's group chat") and stick to it. Putting those accounts together is covered in the wrap up after the event, and how to tell people where the money goes from the moment you start selling is in charity or fundraising events.

The tax and administrative side (whether the association has to issue invoices, how it records what it collects, what authorization the school requires to hold the party) depends on each association and each jurisdiction. This guide doesn't give advice on that: confirm it with the school management and with whoever keeps the association's books before opening sales.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell the fair's contribution ticket with ENTRÁ?

Yes, if the contribution is the ticket to the party: you load it as a ticket type, for example "Adult contribution", with its price and quota, and each person gets their QR. If the contribution includes a prize draw, that is a raffle: it needs its own authorization and is sold outside ENTRÁ.

How do I make the youngest children go free?

Write from what age people pay and load only the types that are charged, for example "Adult" and "Child from age 4". Count the youngest by hand at the door or, if you want them included in the capacity count, issue them a complimentary ticket from the dashboard.

How does the parents' association show how much was raised?

With a report that separates each source: tickets or contributions sold by type (they come from the ENTRÁ dashboard), tokens and food stand, raffle if there was one, expenses with receipts and what is left. Publish it on the date you promised, through the same channel where the party was promoted.
