Guided tour with a cap per time slot: how to sell each departure

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

A tour of a historic building, a winery visit, a night tour of a neighborhood, a guided walk through a nature reserve. They all share one thing: small groups leaving at fixed times. If you sell a single ticket for the whole day, at eleven you have forty people and at four, three. The way to avoid it is to sell each departure with its own cap. This guide explains how to do it in ENTRÁ and what to tell buyers.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First, what ENTRÁ does and doesn't do

So there are no surprises: ENTRÁ has no time slot feature or calendar of sessions. What it does have is ticket types, each with its own name, price and cap, and when a type sells out nobody else can choose it. That is enough to set up departures: one ticket type per time slot.

At the door, the scanner checks that the QR belongs to that event and has not been used, and shows the name of the ticket type. It doesn't reject anyone for arriving at another time. That is decided by the person receiving the group, looking at what the screen says. How to organize it is further down.

How many departures and how many people

Before loading anything, sketch the schedule on paper:

  • How long the tour lasts and how much time you need between one group and the next. If the visit lasts an hour and ten minutes, don't schedule departures every hour: they will bump into each other in the first room.
  • How many people can hear the guide well. In a narrow corridor or a small room, fifteen is already a lot. Outdoors or with a microphone, more.
  • How many guides you have. With just one, departures go one after another. With two, they can be every half hour.
  • Whether the venue has a total limit on people inside at the same time: two overlapping groups shouldn't go over it.

The result is something like this: Saturday, departures at 10:00, 11:30, 14:00 and 15:30, with 20 people each.

How to load it in ENTRÁ

You create one event for that day, with the date and time from the first departure to the end of the last one. The description covers the whole tour. And in ticket types, one per departure:

  • Departure 10:00, price, cap 20.
  • Departure 11:30, price, cap 20.
  • Departure 14:00, price, cap 20.
  • Departure 15:30, price, cap 20.

Some details that help:

  • The time at the start of the name. It is the first thing buyers read and the first thing the door sees on the scanner. "Departure 10:00" is clear; "Slot A" makes people look it up.
  • If there are different prices (general, retirees, children), you multiply: "10:00 · General" and "10:00 · Retirees". Careful, because each type has its own cap and they are not shared. If the group total matters to you, split the cap between the two (for example, 15 and 5) or use a single price per departure.
  • A tour in another language is set up the same way, as its own departure: "12:00 · English tour".
  • If you want a presale, each type can be marked as presale with its own price and cap.

If you run tours on several different days, the clearest way is one event per day, each with its departures. That way the reminder the day before reaches each person on the right date and each day's list stays separate.

If the tour is free, you turn on the free event option when creating it: the types show as "Gratis" (free) and people reserve their time slot without paying, still with their QR. To keep the cap from filling up with people who reserve and don't come, see free event: how to stop people reserving and not showing up.

What to tell buyers

Since the system doesn't stop anyone because of the time, the rule has to be clear before buying. In the event description, put it in these words or similar:

  • "Your ticket is valid for the time you chose." Said just like that.
  • How early to arrive, for example ten minutes before, and what happens if you arrive late: whether the group waits, whether you can join along the way or whether you miss the departure.
  • The exact meeting point, which is not always the venue's address. If it is long, write it out in full, for example Avenida de Mayo 575, entrance hall, next to the marble staircase.
  • How long it lasts, whether there is a lot of walking, whether there are stairs and whether it is accessible.
  • Whether photos are allowed, whether you can bring a backpack, whether there is a cloakroom.
  • What happens if it rains, if part of the tour is outdoors. Say whether it goes ahead anyway, is shortened or is rescheduled.

The email with the QR shows the ticket type, so the time is written there too. And the day before, ENTRÁ automatically sends a reminder with the event name, date, time and venue, as long as the date is entered and the event is still active. That email carries the event's general schedule, not each departure's: that is why it matters that the type name states the time.

At the door, departure by departure

With several groups during the day, the door needs a simple routine:

  • One person dedicated to receiving people, not the guide who is about to leave.
  • The QR is scanned from a phone, and each QR works only once. If someone sent the screenshot to another person, whoever arrives first gets in.
  • Look at the type the scanner shows. If it says "Departure 11:30" and it is 10:00, the person waits for their group. If there are free spots in the departure that is leaving, you can add them: it is your call, and it is best to have it decided beforehand so you don't decide differently with each person.
  • If someone can't find the email, look them up by last name, ID number (DNI) or email from the scanner.
  • To know how many are missing from each group, export the attendee list from the dashboard before you start and filter it by ticket type. That way the guide knows whether to wait two more minutes or leave.

If someone can't make their time slot, ENTRÁ has no button to move them to another departure. The practical way is to handle it at the door: if another departure has spots left, you take them there and make a note. If they can't make any time, they can transfer the ticket to someone else; that is explained in transferring a ticket to someone else.

When a departure sells out, nobody else can buy it and the others stay on sale. If they all fill up, the guide sold-out tickets: waiting list has ideas so you don't lose those people.

Frequently asked questions

Does ENTRÁ have time slots for a guided tour?

Not as a separate feature. It is solved with one ticket type per time, for example "Departure 10:00" and "Departure 11:30", each with the group's cap. When a time slot fills up, nobody else can choose it and the others stay on sale.

Does the scanner reject someone who arrives at another time?

No. The scanner checks that the QR belongs to that event and has not been used, and shows the ticket type. Whoever is on the door looks at that name and decides: if the person came to the wrong departure, they add them if there is room or ask them to wait for theirs.

How do I sell tours on several different days?

The clearest way is to load one event per day, each with its ticket types per time slot. That way the reminder the day before reaches each person on the date they chose and each day's list stays separate.
