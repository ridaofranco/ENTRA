Guide for organizers

Kids theater during school holidays: shows, tickets and groups

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

Argentina's winter school holidays are the high season for children's theater and puppet shows: two weeks with performances almost every day, families who decide in the morning what to do in the afternoon, grandparents with three grandkids and day camps that arrive by bus. All of that gets sorted before you open sales. This guide goes step by step.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

One show, one event

If the play has performances from Tuesday to Sunday for two weeks, the temptation is to load one long event. It is not a good idea. The tidy way in ENTRÁ is one event per show:

  • Each show has its own cap, which is the room's. If Saturday at 3 pm sells out, Tuesday is still on sale.
  • Each show has its own link, which you can send on its own: "There are still spots for Thursday at 5 pm."
  • Each show has its own list and its own door. When scanning, someone with a ticket for Wednesday doesn't get in on Tuesday.

If there are two shows on the same day, at 3 and at 5 pm, those are two events. ENTRÁ has no time slots or sessions as an option inside an event, and in theater each show is its own full or empty room.

ENTRÁ does have multi-day events, with an entry mode chosen once per event: one QR for the whole event or one QR per day. They work for a festival or a fair, not for theater performances. In "one QR per day" mode, the family chooses the days and pays for each one, but the cap for each ticket type is a single one for all days, not one per day. With a 120-seat room you need 120 spots per show, and only one event per show gives you that. How to set up a run with several dates is in selling tickets for independent theater.

Child ticket, adult ticket and free entry for little ones

At a children's show almost nobody comes alone. The clear way is to split people into ticket types, each with its own name, price and cap:

  • "Child, from age 3", with the age from which they pay in the name.
  • "Adult", for whoever comes along. It can cost the same or less; what doesn't work is the family finding out at the door.
  • "Under 3, on a lap", a ticket type at ARS 0 with its own cap. The family adds it when buying, the child receives their QR and is counted in the room.

The free type has an advantage over not giving a ticket at all: you know how many babies are at each show. A baby on a lap doesn't take a seat, but is in the room, and if the venue has an approved capacity, the count has to include everyone. Give the free type a small cap, for example fifteen, so it doesn't eat up the room. If you want it to be taken only together with a paid ticket, write it in the description and check it at the door: the ticketing platform doesn't prevent it on its own. Each purchase allows up to 4 tickets of each type. A family of two adults, three kids and a baby fits in a single purchase; a birthday party with twelve kids doesn't. More on child and adult tickets in children's show: child and adult tickets.

Matinee: the time slot is part of the show

For a family on holiday, the time decides whether they go or not. The 3 or 4 pm matinee is the most popular, because it falls after lunch and before the afternoon snack (merienda, a fixture of Argentine afternoons). Morning shows work for day camps and for very young kids, who nap in the afternoon. What to write in each event:

  • Doors open and start time, separately. With kids, arriving fifteen minutes early is time for the bathroom and parking the stroller.
  • How long it lasts. Fifty minutes and an hour and a half are different audiences.
  • What ages it is for: a play for kids aged 2 to 5 is not the same as one for kids aged 6 to 10.
  • Whether people can come in once the show has started, and where strollers are left.

Enter the end time of each show. The event page sells until that time; if you don't enter it, until three hours after the start. During the holidays many people buy on the way, so keep sales open until the show starts.

School groups and day camps

Winter day camps and some schools with activities during the break arrive with thirty or forty kids and four adults. Since each purchase allows up to 4 tickets of each type, a group like that can't be bought in one go, and it makes no sense to ask a teacher to make ten purchases. Two ways to handle it:

  • An agreement outside the platform and complimentary tickets. You agree on a price per child with the institution, collect it by bank transfer and issue the tickets as complimentary tickets from the dashboard, with name, email and reason ("Club Norte day camp"). Complimentary tickets don't come out of the sales cap, but they do count toward the total capacity the dashboard shows: if the room has 120 seats and 40 come from the camp, lower the sales cap to 80.
  • A show just for groups. A whole morning reserved for day camps, with no public sales, with each institution's list at the door. If you still want it to go through the scanner, load it as a hidden event and issue complimentary tickets.

Complimentary tickets are issued with the details you enter, so the institution's name and the email of the person in charge are enough; there is no need for each kid's ID number. Collecting money outside the ticketing platform has its pros and cons.

The door with families

A family at the door is slow: whoever has the phone with the QR codes is carrying one kid and looking for another one's hat. To keep the line from jamming:

  • Open early. During the holidays families arrive early and in groups.
  • Scan all of the family's QR codes in a row, from the same phone. If someone can't find the email, look them up by last name, email or ID number (DNI).
  • Look at the ticket type. The scanner shows the type of each QR: if it says "Under 3" and a seven-year-old walks in, you see it right away.
  • If the signal drops, entries are saved on the phone.

ENTRÁ has no box office and does not record cash payments: a family arriving without a ticket buys from their phone, without creating an account. A sign with the QR for the show's link, away from the scanner, helps. With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket; the service and processing charges are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

Frequently asked questions

How do I load several shows of the same play?

One event per show. That way each show has its own link, cap, attendee list and door check, and if Saturday at 3 pm sells out, the others stay on sale. If there are two shows on the same day, they are two events.

How do I make kids under a certain age free?

With a ticket type at ARS 0, for example "Under 3, on a lap", with its own cap. The family adds it when buying, the child receives their QR and is counted. If you want it to be taken only together with a paid ticket, write it on the page and check it at the door.

How do I sell to a school or a day camp coming with thirty kids?

Each purchase allows up to 4 tickets of each type, so a large group can't be bought in one go. The practical way is to agree with the institution outside the platform, collect by bank transfer and issue the tickets as complimentary tickets from the dashboard, or reserve a whole show for them and check it with a list.
