How to choose a ticketing platform: what to check in the fees

By SOMOS DER · July 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Choosing a ticketing platform is not about picking the lowest percentage. A percentage on its own says nothing: what matters is who pays it, what it is calculated on, what it includes and when the money reaches you. Two platforms that both say "8%" can leave you with completely different numbers.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to any ticketing platform; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

This guide is what we would ask any ticketing platform before loading an event on it.

1. Does the fee come out of your price, or does the buyer pay it?

These are two different models and they change everything:

  • Fee deducted from the organizer: you price the ticket at 10,000, the buyer pays 10,000 and you receive less.
  • Service charge paid by the buyer: you price the ticket at 10,000, you receive 10,000 and the buyer sees the charge itemized before paying.

Neither model is a trick in itself; the trick is not knowing which one you are being offered. At ENTRÁ the model is the second one: the service charge is 8% plus VAT (IVA, the Argentine value added tax) on the ticket price, and the buyer pays it, itemized at checkout. The organizer sets a price and receives that price.

2. What is it calculated on, and is tax included?

An "8%" can be calculated on the ticket price or on the total with charges already added. And the tax may be included in the number you are given or added afterwards. Ask for it in writing with a worked example. If they cannot put it in one line, that tells you something.

3. When do you get paid?

This is the question that breaks the most budgets, and almost nobody asks it in time:

  • Does the money go into your account, or does it stay with the platform until after the event?
  • If it stays, how long until it is paid out?
  • Can you use ticket revenue before the event to pay suppliers?

An organizer who has to pay the sound company two weeks before the show cannot work with a payout 30 days after the event. It is not an admin detail: it decides whether the event is viable.

4. What is included and what costs extra?

This is where the "cheap" percentage gets expensive. Ask explicitly about:

  • Issuing and reissuing tickets.
  • Access control at the door (included, or a different provider?).
  • Complimentary tickets, guest lists and discount codes.
  • Exporting your buyers' data.
  • Support on the day of the event.
  • Monthly minimums or fixed fees per event.

At ENTRÁ, publishing events, QR issuing, built-in access control, live stock, discounts and complimentary tickets, official ticket transfer, buyer data export and WhatsApp support come at no extra cost. Whoever you work with, ask for that list in writing.

5. What happens with refunds?

Ask two concrete things: if a ticket is refunded, is the fee refunded too? And if the event is cancelled or rescheduled, who handles the refunds and how fast? Better to have it settled before you sell, not on the day it happens.

6. Can your audience buy without friction?

The best fee in the world is useless if you lose sales at checkout. Go through the whole purchase yourself, on a phone: how many steps, whether it forces you to create an account, whether the final price is clear before paying, whether the ticket arrives by email right away. At ENTRÁ people can buy as a guest, without creating an account, because every extra step is people dropping out.

How to really compare: two numbers

Forget percentages and bring everything down to this:

  1. How much you keep per ticket sold, net.
  2. How much your audience pays, final price with every charge and tax.

Ask each platform for those two numbers on the same example price. That settles it.

Red flags

  • They cannot explain the fee with a concrete worked example.
  • They dodge the question of when you get paid.
  • Door control is "something you sort out with another provider".
  • Your buyers' data is not yours, or it costs money to export.
  • The final price only appears at the last step of checkout.

Frequently asked questions

Who pays a ticketing platform fee, the organizer or the buyer?

It depends on the platform, and it is the first thing to ask. There are two models: the fee is deducted from what the organizer receives, or it is added to the price as a service charge paid by the buyer. At ENTRÁ, an Argentine platform, the service charge is 8% plus VAT on the ticket price and the buyer pays it, itemized at checkout: the organizer sets a price and receives that price.

How do I compare fees between platforms if each one explains it differently?

Bring everything down to two numbers: how much you keep per ticket and how much your audience ends up paying. Pick an example price, ask each platform for the organizer net and the buyer final price with every charge and tax included, and compare those two numbers. Loose percentages cannot be compared because they are not all calculated on the same base.

What extra costs usually show up on top of the fee?

The most common ones are charges for issuing or reprinting tickets, for door access control, for exporting buyer data, for complimentary tickets and discounts, or a monthly minimum. Ask in writing what the fee includes and what is billed separately before you load your first event.

When does the organizer get the ticket money?

It is the question that breaks the most budgets and you should always ask it: whether the payout happens after the event, how long it takes, and whether you can use the revenue earlier to pay suppliers. Get it in writing, together with what happens to the fee if a ticket is refunded.
