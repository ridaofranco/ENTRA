Guide for organizers

What day and time to choose for your event

By SOMOS DER · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

A good event on a bad day sells half. The date is chosen before the flyer, before the price and often before the venue, and it is chosen by looking at three things: the public holiday calendar, what else is happening that same night in your city and how people get back home. This guide goes step by step.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Start with your audience, not the calendar

There is no good day for every event. There are good days for your audience. Before looking at dates, answer this:

  • Do they work the next day? A party for 25-year-olds on a Wednesday night sells little; an after-work event on a Thursday at 7 pm, well. A workshop for people who work Monday to Friday fills up on a Saturday morning.
  • Do they come with kids? A children's show goes during the day and on weekends, with an end time before dinner.
  • Where do they come from? If half of them travel from another town, the schedule is set by transport, not by you.
  • When was your last date and how did it go? If you have previous sales, look at which days and at what time tickets were bought. It is the best data you have.

Public holidays and long weekends

A public holiday can work for you or against you, and the only way to know is to open the official calendar. In Argentina public holidays are set by Law 27.399, and each year's calendar, with the days already moved, is published on argentina.gob.ar (in Spanish). Don't build it from memory: movable holidays shift, and new days appear every year.

Two rules from the law worth keeping in mind. Movable holidays that fall on a Tuesday or Wednesday move to the previous Monday, and those that fall on a Thursday or Friday move to the following Monday (article 6). And the national government can set up to three public holidays or non-working days for tourism purposes per year, which fall on a Monday or Friday (article 7). In practice, almost every long weekend ends on a Monday.

What is left of 2026, according to the official calendar published as of today:

  • Monday, November 9: national holiday for the visit of Pope Leo XIV. In addition, Tuesday the 10th is a holiday in Córdoba and in the City of Buenos Aires, and Wednesday the 11th in the Province of Buenos Aires.
  • Monday, November 23: National Sovereignty Day, moved from the 20th.
  • Monday, December 7: non-working day for tourism purposes, right next to Tuesday the 8th, the Immaculate Conception, which does not move.
  • Friday, December 25: Christmas.

If your event falls near any of these dates, check the calendar again before publishing, in case there were changes.

When a holiday helps you and when it empties the room

  • The night before a holiday is the best night of the week for parties and shows in the city: the Sunday before a Monday holiday behaves like a Saturday, because nobody gets up early.
  • A long weekend empties the city if your audience goes away. A theater run or a small band's gig on a four-day long weekend can sell much less than the weekend before.
  • A long weekend fills you up if you are somewhere people travel to: the coast, the hills, a provincial city that gets tourists or people coming back to see family. For those cases, see an event in a small provincial town.
  • A non-working day is not the same as a public holiday. They are listed separately in the calendar. Expect part of your audience to work that day anyway.
  • If it is a special or local holiday, like the ones for the Pope's visit, check where it applies. A holiday in one province and not in the next one changes who can travel to your event.

What competes for your audience that night

Your event doesn't compete with every event; it competes with the ones your own people care about. Before locking in the date:

  • Check your niche's calendar. Dates for bands of the same genre, parties aimed at the same audience, festivals in your city. If a big band your audience likes plays that same Saturday, move.
  • Check the football. An important match at the same time can empty the first hour of your event, or fill your bar if you show it on a screen. Decide beforehand.
  • Check paydays and the end of the month. Your audience has more money to spend in some weeks of the month than in others. If you sell advance tickets, when you open sales matters too; that is in when to open ticket sales.
  • Check the school and exam calendar if your audience is students or families.
  • Ask the venue. The owner of the bar or hall knows which nights fill up on their own and which don't, and what other events they have that week.

The time: how people get there and, above all, how they get home

The start time is chosen by your audience based on how they get there. The end time is chosen by transport. Some guidelines:

  • Find out the last service of the train, subway or bus line your audience uses, on the operator's official site or your city's transport app. Make sure the main act ends in time to catch that last service, or say clearly that it won't.
  • Holidays and Sundays have a different schedule. Service is usually different from a weekday. Check it for the exact date of your event, not for any old day.
  • If the venue is far away, say on the page how to get there: which buses go by, whether there is parking, whether carpooling is a good idea.
  • Doors and the main act are two different things. If doors open at 9 pm and the band plays at 11:30, say so. Many people decide whether to go based on what time the act they came to see goes on.

If you don't have the date locked in yet

It happens often: the venue confirms late, the band has to close another date first. In ENTRÁ you can create the event with the date set to "Próximamente" (coming soon) and enter it later. It helps you have the page ready and start building interest, but not to sell: people don't buy something they can't put in their calendar. Open sales when you have the day, time and venue.

If the date you chose isn't selling, before moving it check everything else: the price, the flyer, the description and where you are promoting it. That is in why your tickets aren't selling. And if in the end you have to change the date, you edit it from the dashboard and the ticket remains valid, but you notify buyers yourself: export the buyer list from the dashboard and write to them.

Frequently asked questions

Is it a good idea to hold an event on a long weekend?

It depends on your audience. If they are city people and many go away, the long weekend empties the room. If your event is in a tourist spot or your audience stays, the night before the holiday is usually a good night because nobody works the next day. Check the official public holiday calendar on argentina.gob.ar before locking in the date.

What public holidays are left in November and December 2026?

According to the official calendar published on argentina.gob.ar: Monday, November 9, national holiday for the visit of Pope Leo XIV (Tuesday the 10th is a holiday in Córdoba and the City of Buenos Aires, and Wednesday the 11th in the Province of Buenos Aires); Monday, November 23, National Sovereignty Day, moved; Monday, December 7, non-working day for tourism purposes; Tuesday, December 8, Immaculate Conception, and Friday, December 25, Christmas.

Can I publish the event without a confirmed date?

Yes. When creating the event in ENTRÁ you can set the date as "Próximamente" (coming soon) and enter it later. What is not a good idea is opening sales without a date: people don't buy something they can't put in their calendar.
