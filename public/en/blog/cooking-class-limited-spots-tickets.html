Guide for organizers

Cooking class or food workshop with limited spots: stations, ingredients and allergies

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

A cooking class, a fresh pasta workshop or a cocktail night have something in common: few spots, ingredients bought according to how many people come and someone who tells you about their allergy when they already have their apron on. All of that gets sorted before you publish the link.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Capacity is set by the counter, not the room

In a cooking class the number that matters is not how many people fit standing in the space, but how many work stations there are. Each person needs their board, their knife, their share of the counter and, depending on the class, a burner or a shared oven slot. Before loading the event:

  • Count stations that are fully set up, with complete utensils. If you have twelve boards and eight burners, the capacity for a pasta class can be twelve, but for a stir fry class it is eight.
  • Decide whether people cook in pairs. If two people share a station, say so in the ticket type name: "Station for two", with its own price and capacity, separate from "Individual station".
  • Keep a place for whoever helps: an assistant who washes up, restocks and chops what is missing. At ENTRÁ complimentary tickets do not take from the sales capacity, but they do count toward the total capacity shown in the dashboard, so if you give a place away, take one off sale.

Each ticket type has its own name, price and capacity. When one sells out, the others stay on sale. Up to four tickets of each type can be bought per purchase, which is enough for a couple or a group of friends; if a larger group wants the whole class, set up a private date for them.

What the ticket includes, written before payment

In food classes the most repeated question is "do I need to bring anything?". Answer it in the first line of the description so you don't answer it twenty times on WhatsApp:

  • Ingredients included: what the class provides and whether there is enough to take something home ("you take home 500 g of fresh pasta").
  • What each person has to bring: apron, food container, a dish towel. If you lend them, say so.
  • Whether there is a tasting or drinks, and which. In cocktail classes this is central: if alcohol is served, the class is 18+ only and ID is checked at the door before the QR.
  • Real duration, with the arrival time. A three hour class that starts late ends with people leaving before the final dish.

If the price goes up as the date approaches because ingredients are bought at the last minute, an early price ticket type with small capacity is better than changing the price mid-sale. How to set it up is in workshop registration with limited spots.

Allergies and restrictions as your own question

Don't wait for someone to tell you at the door that they are celiac when you have already bought the flour. When creating the event you can add extra questions for the buyer and mark them as required. Two that always help:

  • "Any allergy, intolerance or dietary restriction?", required, with the option to answer "none".
  • "Is this your first cooking class?", to know how to start.

Watch one detail: extra questions are answered once per purchase, not once per ticket. If someone buys two places, they answer for both. Spell it out in the question text: "If you are buying for more than one person, list each person's restriction". The answers come out in the event list, so you can review them the day before and buy alternative ingredients in time.

If a restriction makes the recipe impossible (a pasta class for someone who cannot eat gluten), write to them before the date and offer a change or a refund. It is cheaper than having them watch for three hours.

One event per date

If the same class repeats every Saturday, or there is a pasta class on Tuesday and a cocktail class on Thursday, load one event per date. ENTRÁ does not handle time slots as separate sessions within one event: each date is an event with its own link, capacity, answers and attendee list. That has advantages in a kitchen:

  • Ingredient shopping comes from that date's list, not from an overall total.
  • If one Saturday fills up, the next one stays on sale and you can send people there.
  • If one date sells poorly, a discount code just for that date gives it a push without touching the others' price.

For a series of four sessions paid together (a monthly bread course, for example) a single event does make sense, and there you choose once whether the QR is valid for the whole event or one per day. The logic of capacity per time slot is similar to a guided tour with capacity per time slot.

If you don't reach the minimum

A class with two sign-ups doesn't cover the ingredients or the kitchen rental. The clean way is to put the minimum in writing from the start:

  • In the description: "The class is confirmed with 6 sign-ups. If we don't reach it, we will let you know 48 hours before and offer another date or a refund".
  • Check sales in good time, not the morning of the class. The dashboard shows how many tickets sold for each type.
  • If you call it off, notify each buyer yourself. Cancelling the event from the ENTRÁ dashboard emails every buyer that it was cancelled, but the details are yours to give: export the list and write to them with the new date and how to request a refund. You, as the organizer, set the refund policy, so have it in writing before you sell.

The same goes for cancellations on the student's side: say until when a date change can be requested. With perishable ingredients, 72 hours is reasonable and easy to explain.

The day of the class

The door at a cooking class is calm, but it is still worth checking. Scanning is done from the phone's browser: green with the name and ticket type, red with the reason. If someone brings the QR for another date or another of your events, the scanner rejects it, which avoids the classic mix-up of someone who came on the wrong Saturday. If someone can't find the email, they are looked up by name, email or ID number (DNI), and if the kitchen has no signal, check-ins are saved on the phone.

With the list in hand, go over the allergies before they start cooking and place people with restrictions near you. With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket; the service and processing charges are paid by the buyer, itemized before they pay.

Frequently asked questions

Can I charge differently if people come as a couple to the cooking class?

Yes. Create two ticket types, for example "Individual station" and "Station for two", each with its own price and capacity. Up to four tickets of each type can be bought per purchase.

How do I find out each student's allergies?

Add a required extra question when creating the event. It is answered once per purchase, so ask in the text that, if they are buying for several people, they list each person's restriction. The answers come out in the event list.

What happens if I don't reach the minimum number of sign-ups?

Put the minimum and the refund policy in the description before selling. If you call it off, cancelling the event from the dashboard emails every buyer, but the new date or how the refund works you tell them yourself.
