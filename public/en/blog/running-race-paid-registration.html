Guide for organizers

Running race or walk with paid registration: capacity, kit and QR

By SOMOS DER · October 30, 2026 · 7 min read

A road race, a charity walk or a small trail run have something a concert doesn't: whoever pays is not coming to watch, they are coming to run. Registration includes a shirt, a race bib, sometimes a medal, and a kit pickup day where the longest line of the whole event forms. This guide sorts out how to charge for it, what data to ask for, how to use the QR and what stays outside the ticketing platform.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

What you sell when you sell a race

Before loading anything, write down on a sheet what registration includes. It is what you will put in the description and what runners will complain about if it is missing:

  • Distances: how many there are (for example 10K, 5K and a 3K walk) and how many runners fit in each. The limit is set by the start area, the course and the permit, not by enthusiasm.
  • The kit: shirt, bib, timing chip if there is timing, bag, water along the course, medal at the finish.
  • Kit pickup: where, which day and at what times. Whether it is the day before or the same morning changes the door a lot, as we will see below.

Distances and capacity: one registration type per distance

At ENTRÁ each ticket type has its own name, price and capacity, and it closes by itself when it fills up. For a race that means one type per distance: "10K registration", "5K registration", "3K walk". You cannot create the event without loading each type's capacity, and that's fine: it is your start line limit.

Each distance's capacity comes from what you can really handle, not from how many fit on the street. Count shirts, bibs, medals and water stations. If you have 400 medals, the combined capacity cannot exceed 400 minus those for guests.

Load invited runners, walk guides, volunteers and sponsors who run as complimentary tickets from the dashboard, with name, email, ID number (DNI), type and a reason. They do not take from the sales capacity and they do count toward the total.

Price and tiers

Races usually sell in tiers: a cheaper first one for those who sign up as soon as it opens, and a more expensive one close to the date. You can set it up two ways: change the price from the dashboard when the tier ends, or load separate types ("10K · First tier", "10K · General") with their own capacity. The second approach has an advantage: when the first tier sells out, it shows as sold out, and that creates urgency.

For running groups, which usually sign up together, a discount code per group with a usage cap and expiry date lets you reward them and also know how many each one brought.

With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each registration. The service and processing charges are paid by the buyer, itemized before they pay.

The shirt: size with capacity or size as a question

Size is the question that complicates a small race the most. There are two ways to ask for it, depending on when you order the shirts:

  • If the shirts are already made, sizes have stock. Set up one type per size within each distance, for example "10K · Shirt S", "10K · Shirt M", with a capacity equal to the shirts of that size. When the M shirts run out, that type closes by itself and nobody signs up for a shirt that doesn't exist.
  • If you order them after registration closes, a question is enough. When you create the event you can add custom fields (text, number, list of options or long text) and mark them as required. A "Shirt size" field with the list S, M, L, XL won't let anyone pay without choosing.

The answers come out in the list export, one column per question. That CSV is what you send to the print shop with the count per size.

Runner data: one registration per purchase

To buy, ENTRÁ asks for name, email and ID number, and phone is optional. Custom fields are answered once per purchase, not once per registration. If one person buys three registrations for their family, all three get the size and details that person entered, and their ID number.

That is why, for a race, it is worth asking in the description and in your promotion that each runner signs up separately, with their own details. That way each registration has its own name, ID number, size and QR, and at the kit table you look them up by last name. If someone bought several anyway, the official transfer solves the rest: from "Mis Tickets" (my tickets) they send it to the other runner's email and a new QR is generated in their name. We explain it in how to transfer a ticket to someone else.

Other fields that are useful for a race, always keeping in mind that they are one per purchase:

  • Emergency contact: name and phone, as required text.
  • Date of birth or category, if you will award prizes by age.
  • "I read the rules and the liability waiver", as a list with a single "Yes" option, required.

What is handled outside the ticketing platform

There are parts of a race that ENTRÁ does not do, and it is worth knowing before you publish:

  • The liability waiver. ENTRÁ does not collect signatures. The usual approach is a printed form the runner signs at kit pickup, or one they send in already completed. The required checkout question records that they read it, but it does not replace the signature.
  • Medical clearance and parental authorization for minors. If you require them, they are presented at the kit table. Say in the description from what age each distance can be run and what the responsible adult has to sign.
  • Insurance and permits. Street closures, the ambulance and insurance depend on the municipality and the organizer.
  • Bib numbers and timing. ENTRÁ does not assign numbers or take times. You export the list as CSV, sort by distance and assign bibs in the spreadsheet; if there is a timing company, that file is what you hand them.

Kit pickup with the QR

Kit pickup is a race's door. That is where registration is validated, the bag handed out and the signed form collected. There are two ways to set it up with the QR:

  • Pickup the day before. Turn on the multi-day event, with one day for kit pickup and another for the race, and choose one QR for the whole event. That QR is valid once per day: it is scanned at the kit table on Saturday and again in the start corral on Sunday.
  • Pickup the same morning. The QR is scanned only once, at the kit table. From there, the bib is what controls the start.

The scanner works from the phone's browser: green with the name and registration type, or red with the reason. The type tells the person at the table which distance and which size to hand over, if you loaded them as types. If someone can't find the email, they are looked up by last name, ID number or email. And if pickup is in a park or club with no signal, check-ins are saved on the phone; it is explained in access control without internet.

So the line doesn't eat up the morning: one table per distance and kits packed in advance with the size visible.

When it fills up

If one distance sells out, the page shows it as sold out and the others stay on sale. If someone can't run, they can transfer their registration, unless you disabled transfers for the event. For a race it is worth leaving them on: the injured runner passes their spot to someone else and you don't have to refund anything or reopen capacity. And if you cancel a registration from the dashboard, the QR stops working and the spot goes back to that distance's capacity.

Frequently asked questions

How do I control how many shirts of each size are ordered?

If the shirts are already made, set up one registration type per size, for example "10K · Shirt M", with a capacity equal to the shirts of that size. When they run out, that type closes by itself. If you order them after registration closes, a required list question with the sizes is enough.

Can I use the QR for kit pickup and for the start?

Yes, if you load the event with two days, one for kit pickup and one for the race, and choose one QR for the whole event. That QR is valid once per day. If the kit is picked up on race day, the QR is scanned only once, at the kit table, and the start is controlled with the bib.

Does ENTRÁ collect signatures for the liability waiver?

No. The waiver, the medical clearance and authorization for minors are handled by the organizer separately, for example with a signed form handed in at kit pickup. What you can do is add a required checkout question confirming they read the rules, and publish the rules in the description.
