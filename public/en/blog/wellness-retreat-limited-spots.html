Wellness retreat or day with limited spots: how to sell it

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

A yoga day at a quinta (a country house with a garden on the outskirts of the city), a Saturday meditation retreat, a morning of breathwork and walking: these are small events, with fifteen or twenty places, where every person counts. Here selling has to do more than take payment. It has to tell you how many are having lunch, who is coming for the first time and who doesn't eat gluten, before the day. This guide goes step by step.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Before loading anything: the real capacity

At a wellness day the number isn't set by the room, it is set by other things:

  • Mats or floor space. If the room fits twenty people standing but only fourteen lying down with room to stretch their arms, capacity is fourteen.
  • What the person leading can handle. A teacher who corrects postures doesn't attend to twelve the same way as to thirty. If there is an assistant, add places; if not, don't.
  • The food. If lunch is catered, the meals are a fixed number ordered days in advance.
  • Transport, if there is any: the seats in the van are another cap.

The event's capacity is the smallest of those numbers. Better to fall short and open two more places than to have someone without a mat.

Ticket with or without lunch

The clearest way to sell a day with food is to split the option into ticket types. At ENTRÁ each type has its own name, price and quota, and when a type sells out nobody else can choose it. For example:

  • Full day with lunch, with a quota equal to the meals you can order.
  • Full day without lunch, for people who bring their own or leave at midday.
  • Morning only or afternoon only, if the day has two blocks and you want to sell half days.

Put what it includes in the name, without abbreviations: the scanner shows that name at the door, and it is what the person greeting people will see. Add up the quotas of the types and make sure they don't exceed the real capacity of the place.

If you want to reward the first to sign up, each type can be marked as presale, with its own price and quota. For a retreat with limited places, a short presale is usually enough to know within a few days whether the date is working. How to stagger prices is in pricing tickets: tiers and presale.

Your own questions: level, food and whatever you need

This is the difference from a regular ticket. When you create the event at ENTRÁ you can add your own questions for the buyer. Each one can be short text, long text, a number or a list of options, and you can mark which are required. The questions appear at checkout, below the usual details (name, email, ID number, which in Argentina is the DNI, and optional phone), and each answer comes out in its own column of the attendee spreadsheet you export from the dashboard.

For a wellness day, the most useful are:

  • Level or experience, as a list of options: "Never done it", "Done it a few times", "Practice regularly". It tells you how to plan the class and who to keep a closer eye on.
  • Food restrictions, if there is lunch: a list of options with "None", "Vegetarian", "Vegan", "Gluten free" and "Other", and a separate text question to explain.
  • Something the leader should know, as long text and not required: an injury, a pregnancy, recent surgery. Ask respectfully and say what you use it for.
  • Whether they bring their own mat, if you lend some: "I'll bring mine" or "I need one".

Two rules to keep checkout simple:

  • If the answer changes the price or the quota, it is a ticket type, not a question. Lunch changes the price and has a cap: it goes as a type. Diet doesn't change the price: it goes as a question.
  • Ask only what you will use. If you can't offer a gluten free meal, don't offer it in the list; say in the description which options really exist. And three or four questions at most: every extra field is a reason to abandon the purchase halfway.

There are more ideas on what to ask and what not to in sign ups for a workshop with limited spots.

The spreadsheet, before the day

From the dashboard's attendees tab you see each person with their ticket type, you search, filter and export the list. With that spreadsheet open, a few days before:

  • Count the lunches by ticket type and order meals for the real number.
  • Count the diets in the restrictions column and pass them to whoever is cooking, with names if needed.
  • Look at the levels and pass them to the leader, along with the health notes.
  • Count the mats you need to bring.

Those answers are personal data and some are sensitive. Share the spreadsheet only with whoever needs it and don't forward it to group chats.

What to say on the event page

The description is what hesitant people read, and at these days people hesitate a lot. Include:

  • Who leads and what happens, in the order of the day, with approximate times.
  • Who it is for: whether it works for beginners or experience is needed.
  • What each ticket type includes: lunch, afternoon snack, materials.
  • What to bring: comfortable clothes, mat, a jacket, water, sunscreen if it is outdoors.
  • How to get there, especially if it is a quinta or a place without a sign. If the address is long, write it in full, for example Camino de los Granaderos 1450, entrance through the green gate.
  • What happens if it rains, if the activity is outdoors. Say whether there is a covered space or it moves to another date.

The reminder the day before

You don't have to schedule it. ENTRÁ automatically sends an email the day before the event, based on the date in Argentina, with the name of the day, the date, the time and the place. Each person gets a single email even if they bought several places, and it tells them how many tickets they have. In the dashboard's deliveries tab you see which emails went out and which failed.

For it to go out, the date has to be set (not "to be confirmed") and the event has to still be active. If you filled capacity and paused sales, it still goes out.

That email doesn't include your what to bring list. That is why it should be in the description and, on top of that, you should send a short message to the list two or three days before with the practical details: what to bring, how to get there, what time to arrive to settle in without rushing.

Arrival

At a small day the door doesn't get overwhelmed, but it is still worth organizing:

  • The QR is scanned from a phone and each QR is valid only once.
  • The scanner shows the ticket type, so you know on the spot who is having lunch and can hand them the token or mark that applies.
  • If someone can't find the email, you look them up by surname, DNI or email from the scanner.
  • If a person can't go, they can transfer their ticket to someone else instead of asking for a refund. How it works is in transferring a ticket to someone else. Careful: the answers to the questions belong to the buyer, so ask the new attendee to tell you their level and diet.

Frequently asked questions

How do I sell a day with and without lunch?

With two ticket types, for example "Full day with lunch" and "Full day without lunch", each with its own price and quota. Give the lunch type a quota equal to the meals you can order; when it sells out, nobody else can choose it.

Can I ask for someone's yoga level or whether they are celiac when selling the ticket?

Yes. When you create the event at ENTRÁ you add your own questions: text, long text, number or list of options, and you mark which are required. They appear at checkout and each answer comes out in its own column of the attendee spreadsheet you export from the dashboard.

Do I have to send the reminder the day before myself?

No. ENTRÁ automatically sends an email the day before with the name of the day, the date, the time and the place. For it to go out, the date has to be set and not to be confirmed. Whatever people need to bring is best written in the description and repeated in your own email to the list.
