Guide for organizers

Choir or classical music concert: tickets, contribution passes and program

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

A choir, orchestra or chamber music concert sells differently from a gig. The venue is often a church, an auditorium or a cultural center with pews or seats, the audience is older and the ticket is often a contribution that supports the ensemble. This guide goes step by step.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

The venue defines how people are seated

Before loading anything, look at the venue with the concert set up:

  • Church or chapel: long unnumbered pews, and spots with a poor view (behind columns, in the side aisles). Count how many people really fit seated on each pew and subtract the space for the choir or orchestra, which sometimes takes the first rows.
  • Auditorium or room with fixed seats: the number of seats is the ceiling. Ask whether the venue requires you to reserve places for the institution or for officials.
  • Cultural center or hall: loose chairs, arranged according to how many people come. Numbering makes no sense here.

With that number you load the capacity. At ENTRÁ each ticket type has its own name, price and capacity, and when it runs out it shows as sold out. The dashboard shows total capacity adding up sales and complimentary tickets, so you know how much room you have left.

Numbered or general seating

For most choir and chamber concerts general admission is enough: the audience arrives early, sits wherever they want and the door only validates the QR. If you want different prices by proximity, set up areas: "Stalls · rows 1 to 10" and "Stalls · rows 11 to 20", each with its own price and capacity, and first come seating within each area.

To be clear: ENTRÁ has no seat map, so nobody picks "row F, seat 12" on a floor plan. If the venue requires you to sell with assigned seats, you need a ticketing platform with a seating map. For everything else, areas give you different prices and a fast door. How to think it through is in reserved seating or general admission.

With an older audience, open seating makes people arrive an hour early. Write what time doors open and, if you can, open early so they wait seated inside.

Ticket, contribution pass or free entry

Independent choirs and orchestras often fund themselves through the ticket, which is why a contribution ticket ("bono contribución") is common. There are three ways to set it up:

  • A fixed price ticket, like any event: "General", and if you want, a cheaper "Students and retirees". ENTRÁ does not verify credentials: the scanner shows the ticket type and whoever is at the door asks for the card.
  • A contribution pass, which is a ticket type with that name and its price. If you want to let those who can contribute more, add a second type with a higher price, for example "Friend of the choir". At ENTRÁ the buyer does not choose the amount: each level is a type with its fixed price. Two or three levels are enough; more is confusing.
  • Free entry with reservation, when the concert is free but the venue has limited capacity. When creating the event you turn on free event: prices are set to zero, the page says "reserve your place", each reservation gets its QR by email and ENTRÁ sends a reminder the day before. Free event mode applies to the whole event, so it doesn't mix with paid types.

If the concert is a fundraiser (the choir's trip to a gathering, restoring the organ), say so on the page and share afterwards how much was raised. The accountability side is in charity event: how to sell tickets for a cause.

With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket or contribution pass. The service and processing charges are paid by the buyer, itemized before they pay. If the venue is a church or an institution, agree beforehand on what share goes to them.

An audience that buys differently

The classical music and choir audience is older than a party crowd, and part of it doesn't buy online or is wary of doing so. It is not worth fighting that; it is worth making it easier:

  • You buy without creating an account. Name, email and ID number (DNI), and pay. When finished, the ticket with the QR appears on the screen and also arrives by email. Say it that way in your promotion: "no need to sign up".
  • A relative can buy for someone else. The grandson buys, receives the email and forwards it to his grandmother, or prints it.
  • Choir members sell. In a choir, a good part of the audience is family and friends of the singers. Give each choir member the event link to send on WhatsApp. If you want to know which section brought the most people, give each one a link with its own parameter, for example ?utm_campaign=sopranos, and the dashboard shows where sales came from.
  • Open sales early. This audience decides earlier than a gig crowd and organizes in groups.
  • Whoever can't go, transfers. The official transfer invalidates the old QR and generates a new one in the recipient's name.

What ENTRÁ doesn't have is a box office: there is no screen to take cash at the door. Whoever arrives without a ticket can buy from their phone while sales are open, which is until the end time you loaded. If part of your audience only pays cash, that money stays outside the system and you control it yourself. The options are in selling tickets at the door.

The program and the event page

This audience wants to know what they will hear. The event description is the place for the program:

  • The works and composers, in the order they are played, and whether there is an intermission.
  • Who performs: the choir or orchestra, the conductor, the soloists.
  • Approximate length and end time, loaded in the event.
  • Accessibility: whether there are steps, whether it is wheelchair accessible, whether there is a restroom.
  • Whether latecomers can enter, or whether the door closes when it starts.

If you want to know something about the audience for the next concert (whether it is their first time, how they heard about it), when creating the event you can add extra questions for the buyer. Then you export the attendee list as CSV and have the base to let the same audience know about the next date.

Copyright

"Classical" music is not necessarily free of rights. Argentina's copyright law, Law 11.723, says in article 36 that authors of musical works have the exclusive right to authorize the public performance of their works, and in article 5 that ownership passes to the heirs for seventy years counted from January 1 of the year following the author's death. A Bach program is not the same as one with twentieth century Argentine composers, and choral arrangements of popular songs have authors and arrangers who may be alive. If you charge for tickets, check the program work by work and ask beforehand. What the law says and what to ask is in music rights at an event in Argentina.

The door

  • Two people if the audience is large: one scans and the other guides.
  • Scanning is done from the phone's browser: green with the name and ticket type, red with the reason. Each QR is valid only once.
  • If someone can't find the email or didn't bring the printout, they are looked up by last name, ID number or email from the scanner. In a church with poor signal, check-ins are saved on the phone.
  • Complimentary tickets for the conductor's family, press or the parish: load them from the dashboard with name and reason, so they have their QR and don't suddenly appear taking the first row.

Frequently asked questions

How do I sell a contribution pass for a concert?

As a ticket type with that name and its price, for example "Contribution pass". If you want those who can to contribute more, add a second type, like "Friend of the choir", with a higher price. Each one has its own capacity and the scanner shows which one each person bought.

Does ENTRÁ sell numbered seats for an auditorium?

No. ENTRÁ has no seat map. You can set up areas with their own price and capacity, like "Stalls · rows 1 to 10" and "Stalls · rows 11 to 20", with first come seating within each. If the venue requires an assigned seat for each person, you need a ticketing platform with a seating map.

What do I do about the audience that doesn't buy online?

You buy without creating an account, with name, email and ID number, and the ticket arrives by email, so a relative can buy it and forward or print it. At the door, if someone doesn't bring the QR, they are looked up by last name, ID number or email. ENTRÁ has no box office and does not record cash payments.
