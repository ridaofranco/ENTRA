Summer events on the coast: how to sell tickets in high season

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

A high season event on the coast or in a tourist town sells differently from one in your own city. A good part of the audience is passing through, decides what to do on the day and doesn't know you. The weather rules more than at any other time of year. And the season is long enough for several dates, not just one. This guide is for setting up sales by those rules: who you are selling to, when, what to do about the weather and how to organize the dates.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Two audiences: the one who lives there and the one passing through

In high season two audiences coexist, and they buy in different ways:

  • Locals and those who come back every summer. They know you, plan ahead and buy early. They are the ones who fill the first tier.
  • The holidaymakers. They arrived a few days ago, don't know what is on and decide in the afternoon what to do that night. They didn't see your flyer from a month ago and probably don't follow you on social media.

Sales have to work for both: an early bird for those who plan and sales open until the last minute for those who find out on the day.

A short sales window, close to the date

With an audience passing through, most of the decision is made a few days before, or on the same day. That changes the promotion calendar:

  • Open sales early, but don't spend everything at the start. The first tier is for your regular audience. The bulk of the promotion goes in the days before, when people are already in town.
  • Be where people are. Beach bars, bars, shops on the pedestrian street, accommodation, the seasonal radio station. A poster with a QR code that leads to the purchase link works all day long.
  • A link that is easy to share. Many people buy through the WhatsApp group of the family or friends they are traveling with. Buyers don't need to create an account and the ticket reaches them by email with its QR code.
  • Keep sales open until event time. ENTRÁ has no box office and does not record cash, but you can keep online sales open so people buy from their phones in the line. Options and pitfalls of selling on the day are in selling tickets at the door.

Follow sales in the dashboard, which shows them in real time: in high season, what happens in the last two or three days defines the night, and you will want to know whether to step up promotion or staff.

The weather: plan B is decided before selling

In summer, an outdoor event depends on the sky. Rain, wind or an afternoon storm can change everything. What to settle before publishing:

  • Is there a covered space? If there is, say in the description that the event goes ahead rain or shine.
  • Does it move to another date? If the answer is yes, decide which one, and when and how people will be told.
  • What happens to the tickets? Buyers should know before paying, not on the afternoon of the event.
  • Heat counts too. Shade, water and a schedule that doesn't put people in the midday sun.

How to build the plan B step by step is in outdoor event and rain: how to set up a plan B. If the date has to move, what happens to the ticket is in cancelled or rescheduled event.

Several dates in the season

A season allows for a series: every Saturday in January, a week of shows or a long weekend festival. There are two ways to load it in ENTRÁ:

  • A multi-day event. When creating the event you turn on the multi-day option and load each day with its date and time. You choose between one QR code for the whole event, which the person uses once per day like a pass, or one QR code per day, where the buyer picks the days and the price is multiplied by the days chosen. If someone shows the ticket on a day it doesn't cover, the scanner marks it as not valid today.
  • One event per date. The capacity of a multi-day event is set per ticket type for the whole event, not per day. If each date has its own exact cap, or the shows are different from each other, load each date as a separate event.

The door in high season

With lots of people arriving together and not knowing the place, the door has to be fast. Each ticket has a unique QR code that is valid only once and is scanned from the phone's browser, so you can add several team members scanning without buying readers. At beach venues the signal can be weak: test it beforehand. If it drops, check-ins are saved on the phone and sync when the internet comes back.

When the season ends, export the attendee list from the dashboard. It is the audience you will want to notify next summer (look after it: it is personal data).

Frequently asked questions

How far in advance should I sell a summer event on the coast?

Less than in your own city, because a good part of the audience decides once they are already on holiday. Open sales early for locals and those who plan, but concentrate promotion in the days before and keep sales open until the last minute.

Can I sell several dates of the season in a single event?

Yes, if the dates are part of the same series. In ENTRÁ you can load a multi-day event and choose between one QR code for the whole event, like a pass, or one QR code per day. Capacity is per ticket type for the whole event; if you need an exact cap per date, load each one as a separate event.

What do I do if it rains at an outdoor summer event?

Decide the plan B before selling and write it in the event description: whether there is a covered space, whether it moves to another day or what happens to the tickets. That way buyers know what to expect before paying.
