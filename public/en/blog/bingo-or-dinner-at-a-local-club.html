Bingo or dinner at a neighborhood club: how to sell the tickets

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 7 min read

The Saturday bingo, the anniversary dinner, the party to raise money for the gym roof. At a neighborhood club (in Argentina, the "club de barrio", a community sports and social club) these events are put together by the board, the parents of the youth teams and the canteen volunteers, and almost always with the same question: how many people are coming to eat. This guide sorts out the decisions before opening sales: what each ticket includes, how to treat members, when to close advance sales and what to do with whoever shows up with cash in hand.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

First, what you are selling

A club fundraiser usually mixes several things: entry to the hall, dinner, bingo cards, the raffle, the bar. Before you load anything, separate what goes inside the ticket and what is paid separately:

  • The ticket: entry to the hall and, if there is one, the show or the dance.
  • Dinner: included in a ticket type or sold separately at the canteen.
  • Bingo: cards are usually sold in the hall, round by round. If the ticket includes one, say so in the type's name ("Entry + 1 card").
  • Raffle and bar: outside the ticket, with their own person in charge and their own count at closing.

Plain entry or entry with dinner

The clearest way is to have two ticket types with the information in the name: for example, "Bingo" and "Bingo + dinner". At ENTRÁ each type has its own name, price and capacity, and it closes by itself when it fills up. That solves the kitchen's main problem:

  • The capacity of the type with dinner is the number of meals the kitchen can serve. If it is 120 plates, the capacity is 120, even if the hall fits 200.
  • The type without dinner takes the rest of the hall's capacity.
  • If there is a kids' menu, set up a third type ("Child + dinner") with its own price.

A practical detail: up to four tickets of each type can be bought per purchase. A family of six with dinner has to make two purchases, unless they split it across different types (four adult and two child, for example). Say it in the description so nobody thinks it sold out.

ENTRÁ has no numbered tables. If tables are organized by family or by team, the board arranges the seating with the buyer spreadsheet and leaves it printed at the door; if not, it is first come, first served, and you say so. How to think about a night with food and a show is developed further in dinner show: tickets with or without dinner.

Members and non-members

At a club the request almost always comes up: "charge members less". There are two ways to do it, and each has its cost.

Two ticket types

You load "Member + dinner" and "Non-member + dinner", with different prices. It is the easiest for buyers to understand, and the dashboard shows you how many members are coming. The problem: any ticket type on sale is visible on the event page to everyone, and nothing stops a non-member from buying the member ticket. To keep it in order:

  • Add a question at checkout, for example "If you are buying a member ticket, your membership number". ENTRÁ lets you add your own questions when you create the event, and the answers come out in the spreadsheet. The question is answered once per purchase and appears for every type, so do not make it required.
  • Check at the door. The scanner shows the ticket type; if it is a member ticket, the person at the door asks for the membership card.

A discount code

You leave a single price visible and the board sends members a code through the club's group chat. Each code belongs to that event only, can be a percentage or a fixed amount, and can have a usage cap and an expiry date. Three things to keep in mind:

  • The discount applies to the purchase total, not per ticket. With a fixed amount, a member buying four dinners gets the same discount as one buying a single dinner. If you want the discount to grow with the family, use a percentage.
  • The cap counts purchases, not tickets. A cap of 50 uses can end up as quite a bit more than 50 discounted tickets.
  • Codes leak. If it shows up in a group it was not meant for, you pause it from the dashboard.

The safeguards that keep a code from giving the sale away are in discount codes for an event.

Advance sales to know how many meals to cook

The kitchen needs the number days ahead, not on the night:

  • Set a closing date for dinner and say it everywhere: "dinner is sold until Thursday". On Friday the groceries get bought.
  • To close it, in the dashboard you lower that type's total to what has already been sold and it shows as sold out. The ticket without dinner can stay on sale.
  • Count the plates with the spreadsheet. From the dashboard you export the buyer spreadsheet: it includes name, email, ID number (DNI), phone if they entered it, ticket type and the answers to your questions. Filtering by type gives you the exact number of meals.
  • Ask about special menus in the same purchase: a choice question ("Any special menu?" with "None", "Vegetarian", "Gluten free") gives the kitchen the details without chasing anyone on WhatsApp.

The price

Add up what the night costs (groceries, gas, dish rental if needed, sound, bingo prizes) and divide it by the tickets you really think you will sell. If it is a fundraiser, put a number on the goal and count how many tickets it takes to get there; the math is in how many tickets to sell so you don't lose money.

With ENTRÁ the club receives 100% of the price it sets for each ticket. The service and processing charges are paid by the buyer, itemized before they pay.

Cash at the door

At a club it will happen: the regular neighbor shows up with money in hand. So as not to promise what isn't there: ENTRÁ has no box office, does not take cash and does not record payments that do not go through its checkout. You can handle it like this:

  • Keep online sales open during the event for the ticket without dinner, with a sign showing the link's QR in the line. Whoever has a phone buys right there, without creating an account, and goes to the scanner.
  • If the club decides to accept cash, make it only for the ticket without dinner (dinner is already closed), at a fixed price, with a single person responsible for the cash box and a numbered ticket book or a paper list. That money does not appear in any ENTRÁ report: it is counted separately at closing.

More on how to set up the door is in selling tickets at the door.

The night of the event

  • The QR is scanned from a phone. Each QR is valid only once, and the scanner shows the type: if it says "with dinner", the token or mark for the kitchen is handed over; if it says "member", the card is checked.
  • Whoever can't find the email is looked up by last name, ID number or email from the scanner.
  • At closing, what was sold per type comes from the dashboard; the cash box, the raffle and the bar, from their own count.

Frequently asked questions

How do I sell plain entry and entry with dinner for the same club event?

With two ticket types, for example "Bingo" and "Bingo + dinner", each with its own price and capacity. Give the type with dinner a capacity equal to the number of meals the kitchen can serve: when it fills up, it closes by itself.

How do I charge club members less?

There are two ways: a "Member" ticket type with a lower price, or a discount code that the club board sends to members. The ticket type is visible on the page to everyone, so it is worth asking for the membership number with a checkout question and checking at the door. The code only reaches whoever you send it to, but it needs a usage cap.

Can I take cash at the door with ENTRÁ?

ENTRÁ has no box office and does not record cash payments. Whatever comes in by hand is controlled by the club separately, with its own cash box and its own count. What you can do is keep online sales open during the event so people can buy from their phones in the line.

How many tickets can a family buy in a single purchase?

Up to four tickets of each type per purchase. A family of six that wants dinner for everyone makes two purchases, or combines types (for example, four adults with dinner and two children with dinner) if you set them up separately.
