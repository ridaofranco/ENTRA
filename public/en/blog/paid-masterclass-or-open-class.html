Guide for organizers

Paid masterclass or open class: capacity, price and registration

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

A masterclass by a musician on tour, an open class at a yoga studio, an intensive one afternoon photography seminar. They are short events, with a capacity that matters and an audience that wants to know beforehand what they will learn. Here is how to set it up so it fills without chaos: capacity, prices, what to ask, the reminder and the certificate.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Masterclass, open class or workshop

Even if they look alike, it is worth knowing which one you are running, because it changes capacity and price:

  • Masterclass: someone with a track record shows how they work. Many people watch and a few take part. Capacity is set by the room more than by the practice.
  • Open class: a regular class at your space, open to non students. It lets people see how you teach. Capacity is set by the practice: no more people than you can attend to.
  • Workshop: people make something with their hands, sometimes with materials included. It is the smallest. If that is your case, it is all in registration for a workshop with limited places.

Capacity

Set the number before publishing and don't stretch it later. Think about three limits and keep the smallest:

  • The room: how many people fit seated, or with a mat, or with an instrument.
  • The practice: how many you can correct or attend to during the class.
  • What you hand out: if there are printed notes, a sample or materials, how many you have.

In ENTRÁ each ticket type has its own capacity and closes automatically when it fills up, so overselling doesn't happen. If you want to separate places that participate from places that only watch, set up two types: "Participante" (participant) with a small capacity and "Oyente" (listener) with the rest of the room. If it fills up and people still want in, sold out tickets and waiting list explains how to handle those left out.

General and discounted tickets

Almost every open class has a general price and a lower one: students at your space, university students, members of an association, people who already took another class. There are two ways to set it up:

  • A discounted ticket type, for example "Estudiante" (student) or "Alumno del estudio" (studio student), with its own price and capacity. Everyone can see it, so write the condition in the name or description and say that proof will be requested at the door.
  • A discount code for the event, known only to the people you send it to. In ENTRÁ codes are per event and have a usage cap. Watch out: the cap counts purchases, not tickets. If you set a cap of 20 uses and each purchase can include several tickets, more than 20 discounted places can go out.

Each purchase can include up to 4 tickets of each type. For a class that is fine: it covers someone coming with a friend, and stops anyone from taking half the room with a single purchase. Discount variations are covered in more depth in discount codes for an event.

With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket. The service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

What to ask registrants

Checkout already asks for name, email and ID number (DNI). For a class, you almost always need to know something more, and that is what the event's own questions are for. You load them when creating the event, they appear at checkout, they can be required or optional, and each answer comes out in a column of the attendee CSV spreadsheet.

Questions that are actually useful:

  • Level: "How long have you been playing / practicing?" with short options. It lets you plan the class for who will be there.
  • What they bring: "Are you bringing your instrument / camera / mat?". If someone isn't, you know how many you need to get.
  • What they want from the class: an open, optional question. You read it the night before and adjust the example you will use.
  • Details for the certificate, if you are giving one: the name exactly as they want it to appear. The one on the purchase sometimes belongs to someone else who paid.
  • How they heard about it, optional, if you want to know beyond what the dashboard shows in "De dónde vinieron las ventas" (where sales came from).

What to avoid: asking too much. Every required field is a reason to abandon the purchase. If a piece of data doesn't change anything you will do, don't ask for it.

If the answer changes the price or capacity (with or without materials, participant or listener), it doesn't go as a question: it goes as a ticket type.

What to put on the page

  • Who teaches the class and why they are worth hearing, in two lines.
  • What will be covered, as a list. Three or four concrete topics convince more than a paragraph.
  • Who it is for: the level needed and the level it won't help.
  • What to bring and what is provided on site.
  • Real duration, with start and end times loaded in the event.
  • Whether there is a certificate and how it is delivered.

The reminder

People forget a class more than a concert: they bought it three weeks ago and something else comes up that day. ENTRÁ sends a reminder email the day before the event, based on the date in Argentina, and each person gets only one even if they bought several places. For it to go out, load the real date: if the event was left with the date to be confirmed, it is not sent.

If you want to remind people of something specific (bring the instrument, arrive ten minutes early, the exact classroom address), put it in the event description from the start and repeat it through your usual channel, in the group or by message.

The door

A class starts on time, so the door has to be fast. The QR is scanned from a phone, each QR is good for one use only and the scanner shows the ticket type: if it says "Estudiante", that is when you ask for proof. If someone can't find the email, they are looked up by last name, ID number or email. That record of who came in helps you later with the certificate.

The certificate, handled separately

ENTRÁ does not issue certificates. It is easy to handle separately:

  • Make a template with your logo, the class name, the date, the duration and the signature of whoever taught it.
  • Export the attendee spreadsheet. It has each person's email and the answer to the certificate name question.
  • Cross check it with who came in, so you only send it to people who attended.
  • Send it within the week, by email, as a PDF. With a mail merge, it takes minutes.

Don't promise a certificate with official validity if it doesn't have it. A certificate of attendance from your space is just that, and saying so avoids complaints.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a cheaper ticket for students?

You have two routes. A separate ticket type, for example "Estudiante", with its own price and capacity, and the condition written in the name or description. Or a discount code for the event, which you send only to that group and give a usage cap. The cap counts purchases, not tickets.

Does ENTRÁ issue the certificate of attendance?

No. You make the certificate separately, with your own template. What ENTRÁ does give you is the spreadsheet with each registrant's details and the answers to your questions, and the record of who came in with their QR, which is what you need to know who to send it to.

Is a reminder sent before the class?

Yes. The day before, based on the date in Argentina, one email goes out per person even if they bought more than one place. For it to go out, the event date has to be loaded; if it was left to be confirmed, it is not sent.
