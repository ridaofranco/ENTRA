An 18+ only event: the notice, the ID and the door

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 7 min read

A party with a bar, a stand-up night with adult content, a wine tasting: some events can only be for people over 18. The problem is almost never the rule, it is how it is communicated. If the notice comes too late, a 17 year old shows up at the door with a paid ticket and an argument nobody wanted. This guide sorts out the before, the purchase, the door and the bar.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Decide the rule before publishing

The first thing is to be clear about why the event is 18+ and how strict the rule is. A party where the bar is the center of it is not the same as a show where alcohol is sold but the audience could be mixed. Define three things in writing:

  • The minimum age: 18 on the day of the event, not on the day of purchase. It sounds like a detail, but it is the most common question from people whose birthday falls that week.
  • Which ID is accepted: physical national ID card (DNI), the digital DNI in the Mi Argentina app (the Argentine government app), passport. If you are not going to accept a photo of the ID on a phone, say so.
  • What happens if someone doesn't meet it: whether the ticket is refunded or not. This is the part that prevents the most conflict and we go into it below.

If the venue is a nightclub or a licensed bar, also ask what its license requires. The venue often already has its own age rule and yours can't be looser.

How to say it on the event page

The notice has to be where people read it before paying, not in small print at the end. In ENTRÁ you have three places to put it:

  • The name of the event or the ticket type: "Halloween Party (18+)" or a ticket type "General 18+". It is what shows in the event listing and at checkout, before paying.
  • The first line of the description: "This event is 18+ only. ID will be checked at the door." At the very top, before the schedule or the lineup.
  • A required question at purchase: when you create the event you can add extra questions for the buyer and mark them as required. A single choice question such as "Will you be 18 or older on the day of the event?" with the options "Yes" and "No" leaves a record that the person read the rule. It doesn't replace the check at the door, but it covers you in an argument.

Repeat it in your promotion: in the post, in the story and in the WhatsApp message.

The ID number at purchase

When buying on ENTRÁ, checkout asks for name, email and ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID), and without that data it won't let you pay. That ID number is saved on each ticket and you can see it in the event dashboard and in the list you can export.

Be careful about what that data proves and what it doesn't. The ID number at purchase is the buyer's, and they may be buying for a group of friends. And it is a typed number; nobody checked it against a document. It is useful to identify the purchase and to find it at the door. It is no use for age: age is checked on the ID of each person coming in. If you want to understand why it is asked for, we explain it in why the ticketing platform asks for your ID number.

The check at the door

What works is to separate two checks, even if the same person does both:

  • ID first, then the QR. If the person is not 18, there is no point scanning the ticket. If you scan first, the QR is marked as used and then you have to explain why they can't come in.
  • Everyone's ID, not just the buyer's. With groups, ask everyone to have their ID in hand before reaching the door. Put it on a sign in the line.
  • Each QR is valid only once. The ENTRÁ scanner marks the ticket as used and, if someone tries to get in with a repeated screenshot, it flags it.
  • If someone can't find their ticket, it can be looked up from the scanner by surname, ID number or email.

Put someone on the door with the judgment to say no without escalating the situation, and with the backing of whoever runs the venue. For the full setup of the entrance, see access control on event day.

If a minor arrives with a ticket

It will happen, especially at parties. What you do depends on what you said beforehand:

  • If the notice was clear (in the name, in the description and with the required question), you can apply the policy you published. The recommendation is to write it like this: "18+ event. If you are under age, you will not be able to get in and the ticket will not be refunded." No ambiguity.
  • If the notice was not clear or appeared after the person bought, refund the ticket. Arguing at the door with a weak notice is a waste of time, money and reputation.

Keep in mind that ENTRÁ's Terms refer to Argentina's Law 24.240 on Consumer Protection: your refund policy can't override the buyer's rights. That is why the rule should be public, clear and in place before payment.

If you decide to refund, you cancel that ticket from the event dashboard: the QR stops being valid at the scanner and the spot goes back into the cap so someone else can buy it. You refund the money yourself from your Mercado Pago account (the Argentine payment platform), which is where the payment came in, and the dashboard drafts the email to let the buyer know. More detail in how to refund a ticket bought online.

An in-between case: the minor who came with a group of adults. The ticket is theirs or the group's, but the rule is per person. They don't get in and the rest do.

Selling alcohol

If there is a bar at the event, the age rule is no longer just yours. Argentina's Law 24.788 on Fighting Alcoholism (in Spanish) says in its article 1 that the sale of any kind of alcoholic beverage to people under eighteen is prohibited throughout the national territory. Three points of the law worth keeping in mind:

  • Any drink with alcohol counts: article 3 considers an alcoholic beverage any drink that contains alcohol, whatever its strength. A beer or a light cocktail counts just the same.
  • It doesn't matter who sells: article 4 says the ban applies whatever the nature of the place, whether it is wholly or partly devoted to selling drinks. The bar at a party, a cultural center or a club is covered.
  • There are penalties: article 14 provides for a fine or closure of the premises for anyone who sells to minors, with higher amounts and longer terms for repeat offenses. The closure hits the venue, and the venue will make you pay for it.

The same article 4 also prohibits drinking alcohol on public streets and inside places hosting mass sporting, cultural or artistic activities, except in the areas and times authorized by the authorities. If your event is big or outdoors, check what the permit you obtained allows. Provinces and municipalities can add their own rules, so confirm the local ones too.

In practice: if ID was already checked at the door, the bar has it easier. If the event is mixed (adults and minors), you need a visible mark for adults, such as a wristband or a stamp, handed out when ID is checked, and the bar only serves those who have it. If the ticket includes an alcoholic drink, make that clear in the ticket type; there are ideas for setting it up in tickets with a drink or combo included.

Quick checklist

  • "18+" in the name of the event or the ticket type.
  • First line of the description with the rule, the accepted ID and the refund policy.
  • A required age question at purchase.
  • The rule repeated in every piece of promotion.
  • At the door, ID first and QR second.
  • A bar that doesn't serve without a checked ID or a wristband.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if someone under 18 arrives with a ticket they bought?

They don't get in. If the event page clearly said it was 18+ only and that there was no refund for being under age, you apply that policy. If the notice was not clear, the sensible thing is to refund the ticket: you cancel it from the dashboard and refund the money through Mercado Pago.

Does the ID number entered at purchase work to check age?

It works to identify the buyer and to look up the ticket at the door, but it doesn't prove age. Age is checked by looking at each person's physical ID at the entrance.

What does the law say about selling alcohol to minors?

Argentina's Law 24.788 prohibits throughout the country the sale of alcoholic beverages to people under eighteen, regardless of the kind of place that sells them. Article 14 provides for a fine or closure of the premises for anyone who doesn't comply.
