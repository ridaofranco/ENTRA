Ticket with a drink or combo included: how to set it up and control it at the bar

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

"Ticket with a drink included" is one of the oldest formulas of the night and it still works: people feel they are paying for more than the door, and you lock in part of the bar before opening. Combos go one step further: ticket + drink, ticket + food, ticket + two beers. This guide covers three things: how to load it as ticket types, how to stop the same drink being collected twice and how to price it without giving the bar away.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Each combo is a ticket type

At ENTRÁ each ticket type has its name, its price and its quota, and when it runs out it shows as sold out. That is enough to set up combos without inventing anything:

  • Ticket only. The base price, for people who prefer to buy at the bar.
  • Ticket + drink. A separate type, priced at the ticket plus the value of the drink.
  • Ticket + food or whatever combo makes sense for your event: ticket + pint, ticket + choripán (the Argentine chorizo sandwich), ticket + two fernets (fernet and cola, a classic Argentine drink).

The name is what the bar staff will look at, so it has to say what it includes without opening the description. "Ticket + drink" is clear; "Gold Combo" is not. In the event description spell out the details: which drinks are included, whether the food is a portion or a full plate, until what time it can be collected.

If the combo depends on something limited (the portions you cook, the beers from a tap a brand sponsors), set that limit as the ticket type's quota. When it runs out, the combo shows as sold out and the plain ticket stays on sale.

Combos and tiers

Combos live alongside tiers. You can mark ticket types as presale: until you press Lanzar venta oficial (launch official sale) in the dashboard, the public only sees those. A scheme that works:

  • Presale with a drink. The incentive to buy early is the drink included at the same price the plain ticket will cost later.
  • Official sale with the plain ticket and ticket + drink separately.

How to stagger prices is in how to price tickets and set up tiers. If you want the combo to be a promo for a specific group (the band's followers, members of a club), you can also create a discount code by percentage or fixed amount on that ticket type.

The real problem: making sure the drink is used only once

Let's be clear here. The ENTRÁ QR is valid once and it is for the door: if the same code comes through again, the scanner rejects it. What ENTRÁ does not do is keep track of what each person collected at the bar. If you scan the QR at the door, it is already used, and the bar has no way to validate it again.

What the scanner does give you is the ticket type of each QR: when it scans, it shows green with the name and the type, or red with the reason. With that, the door knows on the spot who has a combo. The drink is handled with something physical handed over right there:

  • Token or voucher. A plastic token, a stamped card or a numbered ticket the door hands to each "Ticket + drink" QR. The bar takes it and keeps it. It is the simplest thing to count at the end of the night: tokens handed out against tokens redeemed.
  • Wristband. A wristband in one color for people with a combo. The bar serves and cuts the wristband or marks it. It works if the combo includes more than one drink: two marks, two drinks.
  • Hand stamp. The cheapest option, but the easiest to copy with a ballpoint pen. It works at small events where the bar knows the people.

A couple of precautions that save arguments:

  • Tokens that can't be found elsewhere. A color or a stamp unique to the night. Generic tokens can be bought anywhere.
  • Count the tokens before opening and give the door an exact number. At closing, what is left plus what was handed out has to add up to the total.
  • Say in the description until what time the token can be exchanged. If the bar closes at 4, nobody should show up at 4:30 with a voucher.

For food the logic is the same: the token is exchanged at the food stand instead of the bar. If you have two different combos, use two colors.

How to set the price

The combo has to work for both sides: good value for the buyer and enough to cover what comes out of the bar.

  • Start from the cost of what you serve, not the bar price. What a drink costs you is what you have to cover no matter what; the bar price is your ceiling.
  • The combo should be a bit cheaper than buying the ticket and the drink separately that night. If it costs the same, nobody buys it in advance.
  • Count the drinks that are never collected. There are always people who forget or leave early. That improves the numbers, but don't use it to inflate the price: if someone complains, they get their drink.
  • If someone else runs the bar (the venue where you hold the event, a concession), agree in writing how much they charge you per token exchanged, and pay against tokens, not against tickets sold.

At ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket; the buyer pays the service charge and sees it itemized at checkout. That means the number you load on the combo is the one that reaches your account. To do the full math for the event, bar included, see how many tickets to sell so you don't lose money.

How much to order for the bar

The dashboard shows in real time how many tickets of each type were sold. That figure is the number of drinks already paid for, so it is the floor of what the bar needs ready. If the combo includes something specific (a beer brand, a signature cocktail), order based on that number and add what you expect to sell separately. Sales and the attendee list export as CSV with each person's ticket type, in case you need to pass the number to the supplier.

The door, step by step

  • One scanner and someone with the tokens next to it. Scan, check the type, hand over the token if it is a combo. If the door is one person, they should keep the tokens in a pocket, not in a box that has to be opened.
  • Groups. If one person bought several tickets, each has its own QR. Scan each QR and hand over one token for each.
  • No signal. If the connection drops, the scanner shows that it is validating offline and keeps working. The tokens don't depend on anything.

The rest of the door setup is in how access control works on the day of the event.

Frequently asked questions

How do I sell a ticket with a drink included?

As its own ticket type, with a name that says what it includes (for example "Ticket + drink"), its price and its quota. If you also sell the plain ticket, it goes as another ticket type with its own price.

Does ENTRÁ check that the drink is used only once?

No. The ENTRÁ QR is valid once at the door and the scanner shows the ticket type, but it does not keep track of what is collected at the bar. The practical approach is to hand a token, a wristband or a stamp at the door to people with a drink included, and have the bar exchange that for the drink.

How much should a ticket with a drink included cost?

The difference from the plain ticket has to cover the cost of what is served, plus some margin. A small discount against the bar price is enough to make buying it in advance worthwhile.
