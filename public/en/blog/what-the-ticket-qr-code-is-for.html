Guide for buyers

What the QR code on your ticket is and what it is for

By SOMOS DER · July 30, 2026 · 5 min read

You bought, you got a little square full of dots and it is not entirely clear what it is, whether you need to print it, whether it works without internet or whether you can pass it to someone. Here is the full explanation, in plain words.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). This guide describes how the QR code on an ENTRÁ ticket works.

It is a number, not a drawing

The QR code on your ticket is the visual version of a unique code. That code identifies that ticket and no other: if you bought three tickets, you have three different codes, one per person.

What the QR code does not contain: your card details, your password or any sensitive information. It is, literally, the number that opens your spot. That is why the look of the little square doesn't matter: what counts is the code inside it.

Where you have it saved

It is in three places, and it is worth knowing because on the day of the event one of the three always fails:

  • In the confirmation email. The subject line is "Tu entrada para [event name]" (Spanish for "Your ticket for") followed by ENTRÁ. The QR code is in the message and there is also an attached PDF with all the tickets from the purchase.
  • On the confirmation screen, right after you finish buying. There is a button there to download the tickets.
  • In your profile, in the Mis Tickets (my tickets) section. This is the original: the email is a copy.

One fact that resolves most scares: even if you bought as a guest, without creating an account, your tickets are still in your profile. They are looked up by email address, not by account. You log in with exactly the same email you entered when buying and they appear on their own.

What happens when it is scanned

You show the QR code, the door person points the camera at it and in less than a second three things happen:

  1. The system looks up that code.
  2. It checks that it belongs to that event and that it has not been cancelled or refunded.
  3. It checks that it has not been used.

If everything checks out, a green sign appears with your name and ticket type, and you go in. At that same moment your ticket is marked as used: it is single use.

If something doesn't check out, a red sign comes up with the reason. The most frequent case is "already used", and it comes with a key detail: how long ago it was used and in whose name. That detail is what settles arguments at the door.

Why you shouldn't share it

The QR code is accepted only once: whoever scans it first gets in. If you sent a screenshot to a group "so they can see the ticket" and someone gets there before you, you are the one left outside, even though you paid.

The same goes the other way: if someone offers you a ticket by sending you a screenshot of a QR code, that is not a ticket. There is no way to know whether they sent that same image to five other people. This is covered in the guide to buying without getting scammed.

If you pass it to someone else, the code changes

If you can't go, there is the official transfer from Mis Tickets: you enter the person's name, email and phone, and the system generates a claim link that reaches them by email (and that you can also send them yourself on WhatsApp).

When that person claims it by logging in with that email, a new QR code is generated in their name and yours is invalidated on the spot. That is the whole point: the ticket can't be in two hands at the same time. If you saved a screenshot of the old code, delete it.

Two limits to keep in mind: only the email you entered can claim it, and the organizer may have transfers disabled for their event.

What you DON'T need

  • Internet on your phone. The QR code is an image: it just has to be visible. The phone that needs a connection is the one scanning, not yours.
  • To print it. It can be shown from the screen with no problem. Still, if you have a printer, printing it is an excellent backup.
  • An app. Everything works from the browser.

How to arrive prepared

  • Download the PDF or take a screenshot of the QR code before leaving home, and save it in your photos.
  • Turn up your screen brightness in the line. A dark QR code takes twice as long to read.
  • If your phone has a scratched screen protector, clean it or bring the printed PDF.
  • Bring your ID (DNI). If your phone dies, the door can look up your purchase by name, email or ID number.
  • If you bought for several people, send each one their own QR code, not the same one three times.

Frequently asked questions

What is the QR code on a ticket for?

It is your proof of access. It contains a unique code that identifies that ticket and only that one. When it is scanned at the door, the system looks up that code, checks that it belongs to that event and that it has not been used, and only then allows entry. It doesn't store your payment details or your card: it is simply the number that opens your spot.

Can I send a friend a screenshot of my ticket's QR code?

You can, but it is a terrible idea. The QR code is accepted only once: whoever scans it first gets in and the second person is left outside, no matter who bought it. Also, the rejection records whose name the ticket was in and how long ago it was used. If you want to pass your ticket to someone else, use the official transfer: it generates a new code in their name and invalidates yours.

Do I need internet on my phone to get my QR code scanned?

No. The QR code is an image: it just has to be visible on the screen, even if your phone is in airplane mode or has no signal. The phone that needs a connection is the one scanning, not yours. That is why it is best to have the QR code downloaded or screenshotted before you arrive, instead of depending on opening an email in the line with the network overloaded.

I transferred my ticket. Does my old QR code still work?

No. When the person you transferred it to claims the ticket by logging in with the email you entered, a new code is generated in their name and yours is invalidated at that same moment. It is intentional: it is what stops the same ticket from circulating in two hands. If you saved a screenshot of the old QR code, delete it: at the door it will be rejected.

Can't find your QR code?

Log in to your profile with the same email you used to buy: your tickets are there even if you bought as a guest. If they still don't show up, write to soporte@entratickets.com or message us on WhatsApp.
