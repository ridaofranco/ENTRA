Guide for organizers

Company year end party: guest list or paid tickets

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

The year end party of a company, a club or a large group has a different problem from an open party: you don't need to sell, you need to know who is coming. And the usual question is whether the company pays, whether each person chips in or whether it is a mix. This guide goes in order: the three ways to set it up, what to do with plus ones, how to keep it private and how to handle the door.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Three ways to set it up

Before loading anything, decide who pays. Everything else follows from that:

  • The company pays for everything. Nobody pays a peso. What you need is a list with a cap and one QR per person.
  • Each person chips in. Common in large groups, graduating classes, clubs, or companies that cover part of it. Each guest pays for their ticket.
  • A mix. Staff get in free and plus ones pay, or the company covers the dinner and whoever wants to stay for the party pays something.

If the company pays: free list with reservation

In ENTRÁ the event is marked as free when you create it. Prices stay at zero and the event page says "reservá tu lugar" (reserve your spot) instead of buy. For a company party, that solves almost everything:

  • Zero cost for everyone. In a free event the reservation has no price or service fee and does not go through Mercado Pago (the Argentine payment platform).
  • Data for each guest. When reserving, people are asked for name, email and ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID), with phone as optional. Nobody has to create an account.
  • Your own questions. You can add fields to the event, as text, number or options: department or branch, vegetarian or gluten free menu (in Argentina labeled "sin TACC"), whether they are staying for the after party. The answers come as a column in the export.
  • The cap closes on its own. When it fills up, it shows as sold out.
  • Reminder. ENTRÁ sends a reminder email the day before to everyone who reserved. At a free party, where people sign up fast and forget fast, it helps.

If people chip in: paid ticket

If each person pays their share, the event is paid and the contribution is the ticket price. You receive 100% of that price; the service fee is paid by the buyer, who sees it itemized at checkout before confirming. Some ideas for a large group:

  • Ticket types that make sense. "Cena + fiesta" (dinner and party) and "Solo fiesta" (party only) if there are two parts; "Socio" (member) and "No socio" (non member) at a club.
  • Presale with a cutoff date. If the venue or the caterer needs the final number, mark the ticket as presale and say until when it runs. How to think about prices is in how to price your tickets and set up tiers.
  • Discount codes by percentage or fixed amount, if the company covers part of it for some people (for example, staff from one branch).

The mix: staff free, plus ones paid

An ENTRÁ event is either free or paid: you can't have, in the same event, one type with a price and another at zero by reservation. For the mix, the way to do it is a paid event with staff invitations as complimentary tickets:

  • Complimentary tickets for staff. In the Cortesías (complimentary tickets) tab you enter name, email, phone and ID number, the ticket type and the quantity. Each guest gets their QR by email. Complimentary tickets do not come out of the sales capacity, but they do count toward total capacity, so you don't go over the venue's limit.
  • An "Acompañante" (plus one) ticket as a paid type, with its own price and capacity. Whoever wants to bring someone buys it through the link.

Complimentary tickets are loaded one at a time, so for a staff of hundreds it takes a while. Split it among two or three people from HR with the staff spreadsheet at hand, and do it well in advance. Plus ones: decide before sending the link

It is the question that keeps coming back over WhatsApp, so answer it in the event description:

  • Can people bring a plus one? Yes, no, or only one.
  • Do they pay or not? If it is free, in a free list each person can reserve more than one ticket in the same reservation (up to four per ticket type in each purchase). If you want to limit it to one, create an "Acompañante" type with a small capacity and ask each guest to reserve only one.
  • Plus one details. If you will need each plus one's name on the list, add a text question to the reservation.

ENTRÁ does not on its own limit how many reservations the same person makes in separate purchases. If that matters, review the exported list before the party: it includes name, email and ID number for each reservation, and duplicates stand out quickly.

Keeping the party private

  • Hidden event. In the dashboard you can mark the event as hidden: it doesn't appear in the listings and only people with the direct link can get in.
  • The link, through internal channels. Company email, the team WhatsApp group, the intranet. Anyone with the link can sign up, so don't post it on social media.
  • Transfers turned off. When creating the event you decide whether tickets can be transferred. It comes enabled; at a party with named invitations it is better to turn it off, so each QR stays in the name of the person invited.
  • Review the list. In the attendees tab you can search and filter. If someone shows up who isn't part of the group, you see it before the night.

The door

Each ticket has a unique QR valid only once. It is scanned from the phone's browser, without buying scanners: green with the name and ticket type, or red with the reason.

  • Two phones if there are a lot of people. At a company party everyone arrives together, at the same time. Two people scanning cut the line in half.
  • Whoever can't find the email. It always happens. Anyone who arrives without the QR is looked up by name, email or ID number.
  • Someone from the company at the door. They recognize faces and sort out on the spot the person who didn't sign up.
  • No signal. If the connection drops, the scanner shows it is validating offline and keeps working.

The next day, the CSV export includes a column showing who checked in. For the company that is the figure of how many people really came, which helps when negotiating the venue and catering for next year. The scanner step by step is in how access control works on the day of the event.

Frequently asked questions

Is a free guest list or a paid ticket better for a company party?

If the company pays for everything, a free list with reservation: each guest gets their QR without paying and you know how many are coming. If a contribution is asked, it is a paid ticket. If staff get in free and plus ones pay, you combine complimentary tickets for staff and paid tickets for plus ones.

How do I keep the party from being visible to anyone?

In ENTRÁ you can mark the event as hidden: it doesn't appear in the listings and only people with the direct link can get in. Still, anyone with the link can sign up, so it is best to share it only through internal channels.

How is the door handled at a company party?

Each guest has a unique QR valid only once, scanned from a phone. Anyone who arrives without the QR is looked up by name, email or ID number. Afterwards you export the list with the check in column to know who came.
