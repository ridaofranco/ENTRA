Outdoor event and rain: how to put together your plan B

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

A party on a rooftop, a festival on open grounds, a fair in a square: anything held outdoors depends on something you don't control. The plan B is not put together the day the forecast says storm; it is put together before you sell the first ticket. This guide goes step by step: what to decide beforehand, what to announce when selling and what to tell people who already bought.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

The three possible ways out

When it rains, an outdoor event has three paths. Choose yours before publishing, because each one changes what you need to hire and what you need to say:

  • It goes ahead anyway, with cover. A tent, a roof, a covered area at the same venue. It works if the rain is light and the event can take it. Watch out for sound and electricity: ask the supplier what they need to work in the rain and how far they can keep going.
  • It moves to an indoor venue. A hall, a warehouse, an alternative space booked as a backup. Check that the backup venue's capacity covers the tickets you plan to sell; if fewer people fit, your cap has to be the smaller venue's.
  • It moves to another date. The rain date. It is the most common option for parties and small events, and the one that keeps communication tidiest, as long as it is announced from the start.

If your plan B is another date, lock it in before selling: confirm with the venue that the date is free for you, and ask each supplier (sound, bar, security, staff) whether they can take it and what they charge if the event moves. A plan B nobody confirmed is not a plan B.

Announce the rain date when you start selling

This is what prevents the most problems. If the event page says from day one what happens if it rains, buyers accept it when they buy. If you make it up later, every buyer experiences it as a change you imposed. In the event description, include:

  • The alternative date, with day and time: "If it rains, it moves to the following Saturday, same time."
  • The decision time: "We confirm on the day of the event before noon on our social media." A fixed time gives the audience somewhere to look and gives you a deadline to decide.
  • Where the update will be posted: the Instagram account, the WhatsApp channel, whatever you use. Always the same place.
  • What happens to the ticket: that it works for the new date, and what option there is for anyone who can't make it (transfer it, ask for a refund, depending on your policy).

Repeat it on the flyers and in the stories during event week. Many people don't read the full description, but they do read the post from the day before.

The day of the bad forecast

When the forecast starts to look bad, the order of things matters:

  • Check the official forecast in the days before and let suppliers know there is a chance of moving. Don't wait for the decision time to pick up the phone.
  • Decide at the time you announced, not earlier out of nerves or later out of hope. If there is no certainty at that time, what rules is the safety of the audience and the team, not sales.
  • Tell your team and suppliers first, then the audience. Nobody should find out on social media that they don't need to go set up.
  • One clear message: "Because of rain, the event moves to Saturday the 15th at 8 pm. Your ticket works for that date, you don't need to do anything. If you can't make it, message us here."

What to do in ENTRÁ if the date moves

To reschedule, you edit the event information from the dashboard (date, time and, if it changes, the venue) and update the description with the notice. What is worth knowing:

  • The ticket is still valid for the new date. The buyer doesn't have to do anything: the same QR goes through the scanner on the day it happens.
  • You send the notice about the new date. Editing the date does not email each buyer. Export the buyer list from the dashboard and write to them by email or through the channels you use. The automatic email to everyone who bought goes out when the event is cancelled from the dashboard, not when it is rescheduled.
  • Anyone who can't go has a way out. If you have the official transfer enabled, they can pass their ticket to someone else: the old QR is invalidated and the person who receives it gets a new one. If they ask for a refund, apply the policy you communicated.
  • Sales can continue. If the event stays published with the new date, the page sells for that date.

If there is no plan B

Some events have no alternative date: an artist who only comes that day, grounds you can't get again. If that is your case, say so when selling too ("the event goes ahead rain or shine" or "if it can't go ahead, it is cancelled and the ticket price is refunded under this policy") and write the refund policy before selling. If in the end you have to cancel, do it from the dashboard: ENTRÁ emails everyone who has a ticket, and you back it up with the message on social media.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to announce the rain date before selling?

It is highly advisable. If the alternative date is in the event description from day one, buyers already know what happens if it rains, and the day you activate it there are no surprises. If you make it up later, every buyer experiences it as a one-sided change.

If the date moves because of rain, is the ticket still valid?

At ENTRÁ, if the event is rescheduled, the ticket is still valid for the new date and the QR is the same. Anyone who can't make the new date can transfer it, if the organizer has transfers enabled, or ask for a refund under the policy the organizer communicated.

Does ENTRÁ notify buyers if I change the date?

If you cancel the event from the dashboard, a notice email goes out to everyone who bought. If instead you edit the date to reschedule it, you have to send the notice yourself: export the buyer list from the dashboard and write to them through the same channels where you promoted the event.

When should I decide whether it goes ahead?

At a fixed decision time announced in advance, for example noon on the day of the event. That way the audience knows when to check your social media, suppliers know how long they can wait, and the decision does not depend on how the sky looks in the afternoon.

If the date moves, the ticket still works

In ENTRÁ you edit the date from the dashboard, export your buyers to let them know and each QR works only once at the scanner. Creating the account and loading the event costs nothing.
