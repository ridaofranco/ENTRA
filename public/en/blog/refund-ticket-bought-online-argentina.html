Can you return a ticket you bought online?

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

You bought a ticket online and now you can't go, or you changed your mind two days later. Here is what the rules people usually quote actually say, word for word, what changes if the event is cancelled and how a refund works at ENTRÁ. Before going on: this is not legal advice. It is a reading of the official texts so you know what to ask and who to ask.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). The laws quoted here are Argentine; this guide is not legal advice.

What Argentina's Consumer Protection Law says

Argentina's Law 24.240, in its article 34, regulates the revocation of acceptance (the right to back out of a purchase) for two kinds of sale: door-to-door sales (article 32, those made outside the seller's premises) and what the law calls sales "by mail and others" (article 33), where the offer is made by post, telecommunications, electronic or similar means and the answer comes back the same way. An online purchase fits that description.

For those cases, article 34 says the consumer can revoke their acceptance:

  • Within ten calendar days, counted from when the goods are delivered or the contract is made, "whichever happens last".
  • Without any liability, and it is a right that "cannot be waived or renounced".
  • The seller has to state it in writing, clearly and visibly, on every sales document.

What the Civil and Commercial Code says

Argentina's Civil and Commercial Code comes back to the subject. Article 1105 defines distance contracts as those concluded between a seller and a consumer using only remote means of communication. And article 1110 says: "In contracts made outside commercial premises and at a distance, the consumer has the non-waivable right to revoke their acceptance within ten days counted from when the contract was made." It adds that if the period ends on a non-business day it moves to the next business day, and that any clause that prevents exercising this right is treated as not written.

What about article 1116? The list of exceptions

This is where the real question lies. Article 1116 is titled "Exceptions to the right to revoke" and begins: "Unless otherwise agreed, the right to revoke does not apply to the following contracts." The list has three items:

  • a) products made to the consumer's specifications or clearly personalized, or that by their nature cannot be returned or can deteriorate quickly;
  • b) sound or video recordings, discs and software unsealed by the consumer, and files that can be downloaded or played immediately for permanent use;
  • c) daily newspapers, periodicals and magazines.

Read as written, article 1116 does not mention shows, events or leisure services on a set date. What that means for your particular ticket (whether the period applies, when it starts counting, what happens if the event is before the ten days are up) is a matter of interpretation that is not ours to settle. If you are in that situation, ask Argentina's consumer protection office (Defensa del Consumidor) or a lawyer.

If the event is cancelled or rescheduled

That is a different situation, and usually the most common one. At ENTRÁ, when an event is cancelled on the platform, a notice email goes out to everyone who bought. The refund policy is set by the organizer, who is responsible for the event: if you paid for your ticket, message us on WhatsApp and we handle the refund with them.

If instead the event is rescheduled, your ticket is still valid for the new date. If you can't make it, you can transfer it to someone else. We explain it step by step in what to do if an event is cancelled or rescheduled.

How a refund works at ENTRÁ

On the organizer's side, the refund is marked from the event dashboard. It can be done by the organizer who owns the event or by an administrator. When the refund of a ticket is confirmed:

  • The ticket is recorded as refunded, with the date and who marked it.
  • The QR stops working: if someone shows it at the door, the scanner rejects it with the message "Ticket cancelado / devuelto" (ticket cancelled / refunded).
  • The spot goes back on sale: one is added to the available cap for that ticket type.
  • If it was a paid ticket, it is subtracted from sales in the dashboard and a pre-written email opens to let the buyer know the refund is in progress.

For an event that falls through entirely, the dashboard also has a refund of all tickets at once, which asks you to type the exact name of the event to confirm and avoid accidents.

One detail so you don't get confused: that record in the dashboard is what invalidates the ticket and keeps the accounts straight, but the money is returned through the payment method. How long it takes to show up depends on the bank or card.

If you are an organizer: make your policy clear before selling

  • Write it in the event description: what happens if it is cancelled, if it is rescheduled, if someone can't go.
  • Offer the transfer as the first option for anyone who can't go: the ticket is not lost and you don't refund money.
  • When you refund, mark it in the dashboard right away. If you return the money and don't mark it, the QR stays live and that person (or whoever has the screenshot) can still get in.

Frequently asked questions

How many days do I have to change my mind about an online purchase?

Article 1110 of Argentina's Civil and Commercial Code and article 34 of Law 24.240 talk about ten days to revoke acceptance in distance contracts. The law counts ten calendar days from delivery of the goods or the making of the contract, whichever happens last. Whether it applies to your particular ticket is something to check with Defensa del Consumidor (the consumer protection office) or a lawyer.

Does the Civil Code exclude event tickets from the right to revoke?

Article 1116 lists three exceptions: made-to-measure products or ones that cannot be returned or deteriorate quickly, unsealed or downloadable recordings and software, and daily newspapers, periodicals and magazines. That list does not mention shows or leisure services on a set date. How that is interpreted for a specific ticket is not something we can answer.

What happens to my ticket if the event is cancelled?

At ENTRÁ, when an event is cancelled on the platform, a notice email goes out to everyone who bought. The refund policy is set by the organizer, who is responsible for the event; if you paid, message us on WhatsApp and we handle the refund with them. If the event is rescheduled, the ticket is still valid for the new date.

If I get my money back, does my QR still work?

No. When the organizer marks the refund in their dashboard, the ticket is recorded as refunded and the door scanner rejects it with the cancelled or refunded ticket message. The spot becomes available for sale again.
