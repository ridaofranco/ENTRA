Guide for organizers

Tickets as holiday gifts: how to sell yours

By SOMOS DER · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

In December a lot of people are looking for gifts, and a ticket for a show, a play or a summer party (December is the start of summer in Argentina) takes care of Christmas, Three Kings Day (Reyes, on January 6, when kids traditionally get presents) or the Secret Santa without taking up space. If your event falls in the following months, that is an extra audience. But someone giving a gift doesn't buy the same way as someone who is going: they need a date they can write on the card, to know the ticket can be passed on and to understand what it includes. This guide covers how to get ready before December.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

What the gift giver needs to be able to do

Think about it from the side of the person buying for someone else. Before paying they ask themselves three things:

  • When is it? They will say "I'm giving you the show on Saturday, January 17", not "I'm giving you something for the summer".
  • Can I pass it on? They buy with their email, but someone else will use the ticket.
  • What am I giving? Whether it includes more than entry, whether it is 18+, whether there are different seating areas.

If your page doesn't answer those three questions, the gift giver hesitates, and in December they have plenty of other options at hand.

A presale with a clear date

Gift sales work when the event is already published with date, time and venue confirmed. When loading an event in ENTRÁ you can mark the date as "Próximamente" (coming soon), but for this audience it doesn't work: nobody gives something without knowing when it is.

  • Publish before mid December if you want to reach Christmas, and keep sales open until Three Kings Day. How to think about when to open sales in general is in when to open ticket sales.
  • Use the presale as a hook. At ENTRÁ the ticket types you mark as Early Bird are the ones sold during the presale; when you press "Lanzar Venta Oficial" (launch official sale), the presale closes and the other types remain. You make the cutoff yourself, there is no automatic change by date, so announce how long it lasts and stick to it.
  • Allow for the review. Every event goes through a review before being published, normally the same day. If you are going to announce the opening on social media, load it with some margin.

If you want to give gift buyers a special price without changing the published price, set up a discount code with a usage limit. In the dashboard you see how many times it was used. More on that in discount codes for an event.

How the ticket goes from the giver to the recipient

This is what needs the most explaining, because the first thing anyone thinks of is sending a screenshot of the QR. At ENTRÁ the way is the official transfer, and it works like this:

  • The giver buys with their email. You can buy without creating an account; the ticket arrives by email with its QR.
  • To transfer it, they sign in to their profile with that same email. Tickets are looked up by email, so they appear automatically in "Mis Tickets" (my tickets).
  • They tap transfer and enter the recipient's details: name, email and phone, and a note if they want. Tickets are transferred one at a time: if they bought two, they make two transfers.
  • The recipient gets an email with the link to claim it, and the giver can also send it over WhatsApp from the same screen.
  • They claim it by signing in with that email, verified. At that moment a new QR is generated in their name and the old one stops working.

Two details the giver should know. First, the email with the link goes out as soon as they confirm the transfer: if they want it to be a surprise, they should do it on the day they hand over the gift. Second, while nobody has claimed it, the transfer stays pending and can be cancelled from the profile; if they got the email wrong, they cancel it and send it again. The full step by step for your audience is in how to transfer a ticket to someone else.

What you need to leave enabled

  • Transfers. Each event has an option to allow transfers or not, and it comes enabled when you load it. If you turn it off, the button doesn't appear and nobody can pass on their ticket through the platform. To sell as a gift, leave it on.
  • The real event date. A transfer can only be made before the event has passed. With a show in February, someone giving it at Christmas has plenty of time.

What to say in the description

Add a short paragraph aimed at gift givers, at the end of the description. An example you can adapt:

"Is it a gift? Buy with your email and, when you want to give it, transfer it from your ENTRÁ profile to the recipient's email. They get a link to claim it and end up with a new QR in their name. Don't send screenshots of the QR: each code is valid only once at the door."

And check that the rest of the page has what the giver needs to tell:

  • Date, door time and venue, written the same way as on the flyer.
  • What each ticket type includes. If there is "General" and "With drink", the name should say so.
  • Restrictions: minimum age, whether minors must be accompanied, whether there is numbered seating.
  • What happens if the date changes. If the event is rescheduled, the ticket stays valid for the new date, and anyone who can't go can transfer it.

More on how to write that text in the event description that sells.

How to communicate it in December

  • Say it plainly: "Give the January show as a gift". Many people don't think of a ticket as a gift until someone suggests it.
  • Use the dates everyone knows: Christmas, the office Secret Santa, Three Kings Day. One post per date is enough.
  • Show the transfer step in a story or a short video: it is the question that holds back purchases most.
  • Measure whether it worked. In the dashboard you see sales in real time, who bought and when; if you set up a code for the campaign, you see how many times it was used.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy a ticket on ENTRÁ to give to someone else?

Yes. The giver buys with their email and then transfers the ticket from "Mis Tickets" in their profile, entering the recipient's name and email. That person claims it by signing in with that same email and ends up with a new QR in their name.

Does ENTRÁ sell gift cards or undated tickets?

No. At ENTRÁ each ticket belongs to a specific event, with its type and price. What you give is a ticket for a published date, which is then passed to the other person with the official transfer.

When should I make the transfer if I want it to be a surprise?

On the day you hand over the gift. As soon as you confirm the transfer, the recipient gets an email with the link to claim it, so if you do it earlier they find out earlier. Until then the ticket stays in your profile.

What happens if the organizer doesn't allow transfers?

Each event has an option to allow transfers or not, and it comes enabled when you load it. If the organizer turns it off, the transfer button doesn't appear and the ticket can't be passed on through the platform. If you want to sell for gifts, leave it on and say so in the description.

Tickets you can give without screenshots

At ENTRÁ each ticket has a unique QR valid once, and the official transfer is included: the giver passes it to the recipient's email and the old QR stops working. Creating the account and loading the event costs nothing.
