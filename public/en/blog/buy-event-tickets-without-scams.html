Guide for buyers

How to buy event tickets without getting scammed

By SOMOS DER · July 29, 2026 · 6 min read

Almost no ticket scam is sophisticated. There are no hackers or tickets forged on fancy printers: there is a screenshot, a payment alias and a rush. And it works because the buyer is excited and afraid of missing out, which is exactly the state of mind the scammer needs.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). The advice here works for buying tickets anywhere; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

This guide is short on purpose: four things to check before paying and the signs that show up in almost every scam.

1. Go through the organizer's link, not the one you were sent

Most scams start with a link that arrived through an Instagram story, a group or a private message. Before entering a single detail, look up the organizer or the event on their official channels and go in from there. A one minute detour.

And look at the address bar: fake sites imitate the name with one letter changed, an extra hyphen or an odd domain tacked on at the end. If the link came through a URL shortener and you can't see where it goes, don't use it.

2. The final price has to be visible before you pay

A legitimate purchase shows you the full bill before you confirm: the subtotal, the service charge if there is one and the total. At ENTRÁ that is visible both on the event page and at checkout, and the service charge is itemized, not hidden.

If the final price only appears after you enter your card details, or if the number changes from one screen to the next, close it and walk away. It is not a design detail: it is the clearest sign that something is badly put together.

3. Pay with a payment method, not a transfer to a stranger

This is the rule that saves the most money. A real purchase is paid through a payment processor (at ENTRÁ, Mercado Pago, the Argentine payment platform), which gives you a receipt, protection and a way to file a claim. On your card statement the charge appears identified, not as some random first and last name.

If they ask you to transfer to a personal alias, send money through a person-to-person app or pay cash on a street corner, there is no purchase: there is a donation. And if they rush you ("I have two left and someone else is asking"), the urgency is part of the script.

One more detail: the platform never needs your card's security code over WhatsApp or a photo of your card. Payment data is handled by the processor; ENTRÁ does not store card data.

4. They should only ask for what is necessary

To issue a ticket at ENTRÁ three pieces of data are needed: name, email and ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID; phone is optional). And you don't need to create an account: you can buy as a guest and the ticket arrives at the email you entered.

That is why you should be suspicious if they ask for more than that: a password, a photo of your ID by private message, your full bank details or that you sign up on a site that is not the one selling the tickets.

A screenshot of a QR is not a ticket

This deserves its own section because it is the most common scam of all. Someone can't go, offers you their ticket, sends you a photo of the QR or the PDF, you transfer the money and that's it. The problem is that other people can have that same image, and the code is single use: the first one to reach the door gets in and the rest don't.

Even worse: whoever sold it to you can officially transfer that ticket to someone else after you pay, and at that moment the code in your screenshot is invalidated and a new one is issued in the other person's name. You find out at the door.

What a legitimate transfer looks like

If someone really wants to pass you their ticket, at ENTRÁ the way to do it is the official transfer, and from your side it looks like this:

  • You receive an email with the subject "X te transfirió una entrada para [event name]" (X transferred you a ticket for...).
  • That email has a link to claim it. It is not an image or an attachment: it is a link.
  • To claim it you have to sign in with the same email it was sent to. Even if the link is shared, nobody else can keep it.
  • When you claim it, the sender's QR is invalidated and a new one is issued in your name, which stays in your profile.

If what you are offered doesn't look like that, it is not a transfer: it is a photo.

Signs of a fake resale

  • They ask you to pay outside the platform, to a personal alias.
  • They send you a screenshot or a PDF instead of an official transfer.
  • They rush you with an invented deadline.
  • The profile is new, with no history, and the only proof they give you is screenshots.
  • The price is far below the official one for a sold out event.
  • They refuse to do the official transfer "because this way is faster".
  • They ask for data nobody needs to give you a ticket.

If it happened anyway

Keep everything: screenshots of the conversation, the payment receipt, the alias and the profile. With that you can report it to the police and file a claim with your bank or payment app, depending on the method you used. And keep something important in mind: reselling outside the platform is prohibited under ENTRÁ's terms, so a ticket bought that way cannot be validated or replaced. If you have a problem with a purchase made on ENTRÁ, write to us and we will look into it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if a ticket someone wants to sell me is real?

A screenshot of a QR proves nothing: the same code can be on five phones and the first one to reach the door is the only one that gets in. The only safe way to receive a ticket from someone else is the official transfer inside the platform, which invalidates the previous code and issues a new one in your name. If they offer to send you a photo, a PDF or a file over WhatsApp in exchange for a transfer, you are not buying a ticket: you are buying an image.

What should I check before paying for a ticket online?

Four things: that the link is the one the organizer published on their official channels, that the total price with every charge is visible before you confirm, that payment goes through a recognized payment method and not a transfer to a personal alias, and that they only ask for the data needed to issue the ticket, which is your name, email and ID number. If any of that is missing, stop.

Is it safer to buy on the platform than from a private seller?

Yes, for a concrete reason: if you buy outside it there is nobody to complain to. Reselling outside the platform is prohibited under ENTRÁ's terms, and a ticket obtained that way may have been used or transferred before you reach the door. Buying through the official channel, the ticket is in your name, with a unique QR issued by the system.

Do I need to create an account to buy a ticket?

At ENTRÁ, no: you can buy as a guest, without creating an account, and the ticket arrives at the email you entered. That is why it is a red flag if a supposed seller asks you for an odd account, a password, a photo of your card or the security code. Name, email and ID number are enough to issue a ticket, and your card details are handled by the payment processor, never by the seller.
