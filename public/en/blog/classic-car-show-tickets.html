Guide for organizers

Classic car show or exhibition: visitors, exhibitors, parking and rain

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

A classic car meet, a motorcycle or vintage vehicle exhibition at a showground, an outdoor show put on by a club. They have two audiences arriving down the same street: those who come to look and those who bring their car to exhibit. And they have a problem a show in a venue doesn't have: where people park and what happens if it rains. This guide sorts all of that out before sales open.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Two audiences, two ticket types

The visitor and the exhibitor don't pay the same, don't arrive at the same time and don't come in through the same gate. The clearest way is to load them as different ticket types. In ENTRÁ each type has its own name, price and cap, and when one fills up it closes on its own while the others stay on sale:

  • "General admission", with the number of people the showground can hold.
  • "Child", if kids pay a different price, with the age in the name ("Child 6 to 12").
  • "Exhibitor with vehicle", with the number of display spots. If there are 80 spots for cars, the cap is 80, even if more people are interested.

Decide beforehand what the exhibitor registration includes and put it in the name: "Exhibitor car + 1 companion" or "Exhibitor motorcycle, rider only". If it includes a companion, remember that person is also on the grounds and counts toward capacity.

If the meet is organized by a club and some cars come as guests (the founding members, a car brought by a brand, the one on the flyer), don't sell them a registration: issue them a complimentary ticket from the dashboard, with name, email and reason ("Guest exhibitor"). They get their QR and go through the scanner like everyone else. Complimentary tickets don't come out of the sales cap, but they do count toward the total capacity the dashboard shows.

Vehicle details: a separate event if they are required

To lay out the display area you need to know which car each person is bringing: make, model, year, sometimes the category. When you create the event you can add your own questions for the buyer, and the answers come out in the spreadsheet you export from the dashboard. What you need to know is that those questions are answered once per purchase and appear for every ticket type in the event. There are two ways to handle it:

  • Everything in one event, with an optional question such as "If you are an exhibitor, make, model and year". Visitors leave it blank. It works when exhibitors are few and you know them.
  • Exhibitor registration as a separate event, with the questions marked as required. You can mark it as hidden, so it doesn't appear in the listings and only those who get the link through the club group sign up. It has its own cap, its own spreadsheet and its own access control.

If you go with two events, the exhibitor gate opens the access control of the registration event, and the visitor gate opens the one for the general event. Each gate validates only its own.

Parking has a cap too

At a car meet, half the audience arrives by car, and the grounds have the spots they have. If you don't organize it, visitor parking ends up mixed in with the display. Three options:

  • Parking as a ticket type: "Car parking", with a price (or whatever minimum you decide) and a cap equal to the spots available. It has its own QR, which is scanned at the gate when the car comes in. Each person in that car also needs their own visitor ticket.
  • Free parking outside, on the street or on a nearby lot. Write it in the description with the exact address, and if you can, coordinate with the local council on closing or organizing the street.
  • Parking run by the venue, which charges for it. In that case don't sell it yourself: make it clear on the page so nobody thinks their ticket includes it.

Always keep visitor parking separate from the entrance for exhibiting vehicles, with signs and a person directing traffic. A display car stuck behind a line of visitors' cars is the classic morning delay.

How many people fit

At an outdoor exhibition people don't all arrive together: they come in, walk around, stay for lunch, leave. The number that matters is how many people can be there at the same time, which is the venue's capacity minus the space taken by the vehicles, the food stalls and the staff. Ask the venue for that number in writing before selling. How to work it out is in permitted capacity: how many tickets to sell.

The ENTRÁ scanner records who comes in, not who leaves, so it is not a counter of people inside. If the meet can fill up at a peak (Sunday morning, the time of the car parade), you can spread out visitors with one ticket type per time slot, each with its own cap, as explained in a fair or market with tickets. ENTRÁ has no built-in time slot booking: the slot is checked by the person on the gate, looking at the type that shows up on the scan.

If the meet runs over a weekend, you can load it as a multi-day event and choose whether the QR is valid for every day (once per day) or whether the buyer picks which days they attend.

Two gates and poor signal

Large grounds often have poor signal and more than one entrance. What you should know:

  • Each gate is one phone. Scanning is done from the browser: green with the name and ticket type, red with the reason. Seeing the type lets the vehicle gate turn away someone showing a visitor ticket.
  • Without internet, the scanner keeps working and check-ins are saved on the phone. The limit: offline, each phone only knows what it scanned itself. If the connection drops at both gates at once, the same ticket could get through at both; when the connection returns, the dashboard shows which tickets got through twice and at which station. More detail in access control without internet.
  • If someone can't find the email, they are looked up by surname, ID number (DNI, the Argentine national ID) or email from the scanner.
  • There is no box office. ENTRÁ doesn't take cash or record sales outside its checkout. If you want to sell on site, leave online sales open and put up a sign with the QR of the link, away from the vehicle gate so the line of cars doesn't stall.

The rain plan, from the start of sales

A classic car is not shown in the rain: owners don't bring it and visitors don't come. That is why the backup plan has to be written on the event page from day one:

  • The alternative date, with day and time, confirmed beforehand with the venue and suppliers.
  • The decision time ("we'll confirm on Saturday at 6 pm on our social media") and where the announcement will be made. For an early Sunday meet, deciding the afternoon before gives exhibitors who travel time to plan.
  • What happens to the ticket and the registration: that they are valid for the new date, and what option someone who can't attend has (transferring it, if you enabled that, or a refund according to your policy).

If the date moves, you edit it from the dashboard and tickets stay valid with the same QR. Editing the date does not email each buyer: export the list from the dashboard and let people know yourself, exhibitors first and then visitors. If instead you cancel the event from the dashboard, ENTRÁ emails everyone who has a ticket. The full setup is in outdoor events and rain: the backup plan.

Price and payment

Add up what the meet costs (venue, security, sound for the announcer, portable toilets, insurance if the venue asks for it, prizes if there is a contest) and divide it between what you expect from registrations and from tickets. Many meets charge more for exhibitor registration and keep visitor tickets cheap, because the cars are the attraction. With ENTRÁ you collect 100% of the price you set for each type; the service and processing fees are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

If you'd rather let visitors in free and only charge exhibitors, the visitor event can be free with reservation: each person gets their QR without paying and you know how many people to expect.

Frequently asked questions

How do I separate visitor tickets from exhibitor registration?

With different ticket types, each with its own name, price and cap: "General admission" on one side and "Exhibitor with vehicle" on the other. The exhibitor cap is the number of spots for cars or motorcycles the grounds have. If you need required vehicle details, it is better to make registration a separate event, because purchase questions appear for every type.

Can I sell parking together with the ticket?

Yes, as one more ticket type, for example "Car parking", with the number of spots the grounds have as its cap. It has its own QR, which is scanned when the car comes in, and each person in the car also needs their own visitor ticket.

What happens to tickets if the meet is postponed because of rain?

If you reschedule the event from the dashboard, tickets stay valid for the new date with the same QR. You notify buyers yourself, using the list exported from the dashboard. Ideally, announce the rain date and the decision time from the first day of sales.
