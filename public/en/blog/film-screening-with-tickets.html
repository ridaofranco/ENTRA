Guide for organizers

Film screening or film and discussion night: seats, first come seating and passes

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

A film series at a cultural center, a film and discussion night ("cine debate") at a neighborhood club or a documentary screening with its director: these are events with cheap tickets, a small room and a loyal audience. What needs solving is how many seats to sell, how to seat people without numbering and whether a pass for the whole series makes sense. This guide goes step by step.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

What kind of screening it is

Before loading anything, decide which of these formats is yours, because it changes how you sell:

  • Single screening. One film, one night. For example, the premiere of an independent documentary with a talk by the crew. It sells like any event: one event, one date, one capacity.
  • Film and discussion. A film followed by a talk with a guest. It is usually part of a series, but each screening has its own topic and guest, and people choose which one to attend.
  • Series. Several screenings with a common thread: a director, a country, a theme. Some people go to one and some want to go to all of them. This is where the pass comes in.

Price: cheap, but it has to work

An independent film audience expects an affordable ticket. That doesn't mean giving it away. Add up what each screening costs:

  • Room or space, if it is rented or if revenue is shared with the venue.
  • Projector, screen and sound, if the room doesn't have them.
  • Screening rights, if the film requires them. Check with the distributor or whoever holds the rights before announcing the screening.
  • The guest for the discussion, if they charge or if their travel needs to be paid.
  • Promotion, even if it is small.

Divide that by the seats you really think you will fill, not by the whole room. If the number comes out high, cut costs or look for a venue that works on a percentage. The full math is in how many tickets to sell so you don't lose money.

If you want a price for students or retirees, set up a separate ticket type with that information in the name and make it clear on the page that ID may be requested at the door. The scanner shows the type, so the person at the door knows whom to ask.

With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket. The service and processing charges are paid by the buyer, itemized before they pay.

Seat capacity

At a screening, capacity is easy to count: it is the seats or chairs. Count them yourself, on the day you visit the room, and subtract the ones that don't work (behind a column, too far to the side of the screen).

That number is the event's capacity. It has to include tickets sold, reduced price tickets and complimentary tickets for the guest and their people. At ENTRÁ capacity is deducted when payment is confirmed, so the dashboard shows real sales and sales stop by themselves when it fills up.

If the venue's licensed capacity is lower than the chairs that fit, the license rules. More on that in how many tickets to sell based on the venue's licensed capacity.

No numbered seats: first come, first served

At ENTRÁ there are no numbered seats or assigned tables. For a small screening that is not a problem if you say it clearly:

  • Write it on the page: "First come seating. The room opens 20 minutes before the screening".
  • Open the room early. If people know they can arrive and sit where they want, they arrive earlier and the screening starts on time.
  • If there are guests, reserve a row with a sign and have someone from the team keep it until the film starts. If it is still empty at start time, it is released.
  • Don't sell more than the seats. Without numbering, the only guarantee that everyone gets a seat is a capacity equal to the chairs.

For complimentary tickets for the guest, the host or whoever helps promote, issue them from the event dashboard in each person's name. They get the QR by email and at the door it is scanned just like a purchased ticket.

A pass for the series or one event per screening

This is the decision that raises the most doubts. There are two paths, and both are built with what ENTRÁ already has:

  • One event per screening. Each film has its own page, date, capacity and sales. It is the clearest when each screening is promoted separately or has a different guest. If one is called off, you don't touch the others.
  • A multi-day event, with one day per screening. When creating the event you choose the entry mode: "Un QR para todo el evento" (one QR for the whole event), where the same QR gets into every screening, once per day, or "Un QR por día" (one QR per day), where people choose which screenings to attend and get a QR for each, priced per screening.

The pass, then, is a multi-day event with "one QR for the whole event". If you also want to sell single screenings, you have two options: use the "one QR per day" mode and let everyone build their own combination, or have the pass as one event and each single screening as another.

If you choose the second, watch capacity: pass holders take a seat at every screening. Subtract the passes sold from each single screening's capacity, or reserve a fixed number of seats for pass holders from the start. ENTRÁ does not cross-check capacity between two different events, so you keep that count yourself.

The pass price has to be lower than the sum of the screenings, so it is worth buying, but not so low that it leaves you without margin if a pass holder misses half of them. The same logic, applied to a festival, is in festival pass or day ticket. And if the series is monthly and has no pass, see monthly music or stand-up series: how to sell each date.

The door

  • One person scans the QR codes from a phone. Each QR is valid only once per screening.
  • The scanner shows the ticket type: if it is a reduced price ticket, ask for ID; if it is a pass, they go straight in.
  • If someone can't find the email, they are looked up by last name, ID number (DNI) or email from the scanner. The ID number is requested at checkout.
  • Whoever arrives without a ticket can buy from their phone on the event page, as long as there are seats left and sales are open.
  • Close the door when the film starts, or say on the page how long after the start people can still come in. In a dark room, latecomers are a disturbance.

What to say on the event page

  • Film, running time and age rating.
  • Who is in the discussion and how long the talk after the film lasts.
  • That seating is first come, first served and what time the room opens.
  • What the pass includes, if there is one, with the dates of every screening.
  • What time it ends, with the end time loaded in the event.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell numbered seats for a screening?

ENTRÁ has no numbered seats. What you do is set the capacity to the number of seats and seat people on a first come basis. If you want to reserve a row for guests, mark it with a sign and issue those tickets as complimentary.

How do I set up a pass for a film series?

With a multi-day event, one day per screening, and the "one QR for the whole event" mode: the same QR gets into every screening, once per day. If you prefer people to choose single screenings, use the "one QR per day" mode, where the price is per screening.

Is it better to have one event per screening or one for the whole series?

If each screening has its own film, guest and promotion, one event per screening is the clearest. If the series is sold as a whole, a multi-day event works better. The important thing is not to sell the same seats twice from two different events.

What price should I set for a film and discussion night?

An affordable one that covers the room, the projection equipment and screening rights if there are any. Add up those costs and divide them by the seats you think you will fill, not by the whole room.
