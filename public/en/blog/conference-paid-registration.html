Guide for organizers

Conference or seminar with paid registration: how to set it up

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 6 min read

A seminar, a small conference or a series of talks with paid registration has little in common with a concert. People all arrive together at the same time, many pay a different price depending on whether they are students or professionals, there are rooms with limited seats and, at the end, everyone wants their certificate. This guide puts each part in order and tells you what the ticketing platform handles and what is better handled separately.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

Before opening registration

Three decisions shape everything else:

  • What registration includes. Whether it gives access to the whole day or to a specific activity. Whether there is lunch, a coffee break or printed material, and whether that is included or paid separately.
  • Who pays what. General admission, students, members of an association, speakers. Each group with a different price is a different ticket type.
  • How many people fit. The venue's total capacity and, if there are smaller rooms, each one's capacity.

If you have never organized an event with online sales, the checklist for your first event works as a general review.

General, student and other categories

In ENTRÁ each ticket type has its own name, price and capacity, and it closes on its own when it fills up. For a seminar, the usual setup is:

  • General, the full price.
  • Student, cheaper, with the requirement written in the name: "Student (with student ID)". Give it its own capacity if you don't want half the room getting in at the reduced price.
  • Members or registered professionals, if the seminar is organized or backed by an institution, such as a professional association.
  • Early and last week, if you want to reward those who register early. This is explained better in how to price tickets and set up tiers.

One point to be clear on: the ticketing platform doesn't verify whether someone is a student. What it does is show each ticket's type on the scanner, so at check-in the door person sees "Student" and asks for proof. Write it on the page: "Student registration is validated with a student ID or certificate of enrollment at check-in". Whoever doesn't have it pays the difference there or doesn't get in; decide that beforehand and say it.

For discounts for closed groups (the students of a university course, the members of a chamber of commerce) discount codes also work: one code per group, with a usage cap and expiry date. The details are in discount codes for an event.

At checkout, each person enters their name, email and ID number (DNI). That is what you will later use for check-in and for the certificates, so ask on the page that they enter it exactly as they want it to appear.

Capacity per room

Here you have to choose between two setups, depending on how your program works:

  • One registration for the whole day. You set the main hall's capacity and the smaller rooms fill on a first come, first served basis. It is the simplest. Say so on the page so nobody is surprised at the workshop door.
  • Activities with their own registration. If a workshop has twenty spots and you want people to choose it in advance, load it as a separate event, with its own capacity and price (or free with a reservation). That way each activity has its own list and its own access control. For activities with limited spots, see registration for a workshop with limited spots.

What you shouldn't do is put the rooms in as ticket types within the same event if people need to attend several: each type is a ticket, and you end up forcing them to buy several for the same day.

QR check-in at the door

The critical moment of a seminar is the first twenty minutes: everyone arrives for the opening. To keep the line moving:

  • More than one check-in desk if you expect a lot of people at the same time. The scanner runs on a phone, so you can open two or three desks for the same event.
  • Each QR code is valid only once. If someone forwarded their ticket, whoever shows it first gets in.
  • Whoever can't find the email is looked up by last name, ID number or email from the scanner, and checked in anyway.
  • If you want to hand out a badge or materials, do it at the same desk: the scanner shows you the name and ticket type, and with that you prepare the badge or give them the right bag.
  • If the seminar lasts all day, the QR code is validated on first entry. So people can go out for lunch and come back, use a badge or wristband from check-in.

If the venue has poor signal, read access control without internet before the day.

Certificates of attendance

ENTRÁ does not issue certificates. What it gives you is the basis for doing them right: from the dashboard you export the attendee list in a file that opens in Excel or Google Sheets, with name, email, ID number, ticket type and a check-in column that says "Ingresó" (checked in) or "Pendiente" (pending).

With that list, the shortest route is:

  1. Export the list after the event and filter for those marked "Ingresó".
  2. Create a certificate template in a document, with space for name and ID number.
  3. Generate one per person with a mail merge (Word's, or a Google Docs add-on) and send it by email.

That way the certificate goes only to those who checked in, not to those who paid and didn't come. If the seminar runs over several days and the certificate requires attending all of them, load each day as a separate event and cross-check the lists.

What to say on the page

  • The program, with times and who speaks in each slot.
  • What each category includes and what proof is required for the student one.
  • How the rooms work: first come, first served or with their own registration.
  • Whether there is a certificate, how and when it arrives, and that it is issued with the name and ID number entered at purchase.
  • What time check-in opens, and the advice to arrive before the opening.

With ENTRÁ you keep 100% of the price you set for each registration. Service and processing costs are paid by the buyer, with the breakdown visible before paying.

Frequently asked questions

How do I sell a student registration without just anyone using it?

Set up a "Student" ticket type with its own price and capacity, and write in the name and on the page that a student ID or certificate of enrollment is required at check-in. The scanner shows each ticket's type, so the door person knows who to ask for proof.

Does ENTRÁ issue certificates of attendance?

No. What it does give you is an exportable attendee list with name, email, ID number (DNI), ticket type and a column that says whether each person checked in or is still pending. With that list you filter those who checked in and generate the certificates separately, for example with a mail merge.

How do I handle each room's capacity?

If the rooms have different capacities and people choose in advance, the tidiest way is one event per activity, each with its own capacity. If the general ticket gives access to everything, set the venue's total capacity and handle the rooms first come, first served, saying so on the page.
