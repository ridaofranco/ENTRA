Circus or variety show in a tent: shows, kids and rain

By SOMOS DER · October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

A circus or a variety show in a tent lives on many back-to-back shows: Friday night, Saturday and Sunday with two or three times, and the following weekend another town. The audience is families who decide late, compare times and look at the sky before going out. This guide organizes sales so every show fills up and rain doesn't turn into a mess of complaints.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

One event per show

The temptation is to load "Circus in Tandil, weekend" as a single event. It doesn't work well. In a tent each show fills separately, with its own time, capacity and door, and people buy a specific time. At ENTRÁ, the tidy way is to create one event per show:

  • The name says the day and time: "Circus in Tandil · Saturday 3 pm", "Circus in Tandil · Saturday 6 pm". It is what the family sees in the email and what the door sees when scanning.
  • Each show has its own link and capacity. If the 6 pm show sells out, the 3 pm one stays on sale.
  • Each show has its own attendee list. If one has to be called off, you know whom to notify, without mixing them with another time slot.

The entry mode (one QR for the whole event or one per day) is chosen once per event. For a circus with one show per event it changes nothing: each ticket is for that show and is valid only once at the scanner.

Several towns, same structure

When the tent moves, repeat the structure and change venue and date. It helps to have a description template with what never changes (show length, age from which children pay, what people can bring) and fill in what does change: the site address, parking, times. To know which town performs, look at sales per event in the dashboard and run each local promotion with its own tracked link. The full setup for a tour is in touring several cities: how to sell tickets.

Kids and adults as ticket types

At a circus almost the whole audience comes as families. The clearest way is to separate prices into ticket types:

  • "Child, 3 to 12 years" and "Adult", with the age written in the name. Each type has its own name, price and capacity.
  • From what age children pay, written in the description and on the flyer: "Under 3s on a lap go free". If you want them registered, you can issue complimentary tickets from the dashboard with name, email and reason. If not, count them at the door: they still take up space in the tent.
  • A limit worth knowing: at ENTRÁ each purchase includes up to 4 tickets per type. A family of two adults and five children has to make two purchases, or you can create a "Family, 2 adults and 2 children" type with its own price and count it as four places when calculating capacity.

If the family buys seven tickets, that is seven QR codes, one per person. The scanner shows the type of each QR, so if it says "Child" and an adult walks in, you see it right away. More detail on babies, times and a door with strollers in kids' show: tickets for children and adults.

General and premium seating, and the tent's capacity

Tents usually have bleachers or loose chairs around the ring, unnumbered. ENTRÁ has no seat map, so seating is organized by area:

  • "Premium seating", the first rows around the ring, with a higher price and small capacity.
  • "General seating", the rest of the bleachers.
  • The rule written on the page: "First come seating within each area. Doors open 30 minutes before".

Total capacity comes from counting the places with the tent set up, not from a catalog figure. Remove the places behind the poles or with a blocked view, and never exceed what the municipality authorized for that site. ENTRÁ complimentary tickets do not take from the sales capacity, but they do count toward the total capacity shown in the dashboard: if the tent has four hundred places and you will give twenty invitations to the school that lent the grounds, sell three hundred eighty. When numbering makes sense and when it doesn't is in reserved seating or general admission.

Rain and wind: the rule before selling

In a tent, the weather decides more than at any other event. What has to be written on the page before the first sale:

  • Whether the show goes ahead in the rain. If the tent is closed and access is good, the show goes ahead. Say so.
  • Who decides to call it off and until what time. For example: "If it is called off because of wind, we will let you know by email and Instagram up to two hours before".
  • What happens to the ticket. It moves to another show, the money is refunded, or the person chooses.

If a show is called off, keep in mind how ENTRÁ works:

  • Cancelling the event from the dashboard emails every buyer that it was cancelled, but the next step (another show or a refund) is yours to explain: export that show's list and write to each buyer. Post it on the tour's social media too.
  • If you refund the money, first you refund it through Mercado Pago and then you mark the ticket as refunded in the dashboard: the QR is invalidated and the place goes back to capacity. The dashboard offers to open your email with a refund message ready to send. There is also a bulk refund for the whole event, which asks you to type the event name to confirm.
  • If you move people to another show, the cleanest way is to issue them complimentary tickets for the new show from the dashboard, with their name and email, and refund or cancel the ticket for the called-off show. That way the same person doesn't end up with two valid QR codes.

Questions at checkout

When creating the event you can add extra questions for the buyer. They are answered once per purchase, not per ticket, so they work for family information, not for each child. Useful examples: "How did you hear about the show?" or "Do you need an accessible place?". If you need information for each child, like age, it is better to put it in the ticket type name than in a question.

The tent's door

Between shows there is little time: one audience leaves and the next comes in. To keep the door moving:

  • Open the line early and separate the exit from the entrance.
  • Scan from a phone, in the browser: green with the name and ticket type, red with the reason. The scanner belongs to the event, so a ticket for the 3 pm show won't work at the 6 pm one.
  • If someone can't find the email, they are looked up by name, email or ID number (DNI). If there is no signal at the site, check-ins are saved on the phone.
  • Last minute sales happen on each person's phone, with a sign showing the QR for the show's link. ENTRÁ has no box office and does not record cash. The page sells until the end time you loaded or, if you didn't load one, until three hours after the start.

With ENTRÁ you receive 100% of the price you set for each ticket. The service and processing charges are paid by the buyer, itemized before they pay.

Frequently asked questions

Do I load all the weekend's shows as a single event?

No. At ENTRÁ one event per show works better: each one has its own link, capacity, list and door. If the Saturday afternoon show sells out, Sunday's stays on sale, and if one is called off because of wind, you know exactly whom to notify.

Can I sell numbered seats in the tent?

ENTRÁ has no seat map. The usual approach in a tent is to sell by area, for example "Premium seating" and "General seating", each as a ticket type with its own price and capacity, with first come seating within each area.

If I call off a show because of rain or wind, does ENTRÁ notify the buyers?

Cancelling the event from the dashboard sends every buyer an email saying it was cancelled. The new date or how the refund works you have to tell them yourself: export that show's list, write to each buyer and post it on your social media. If you refund money, first you refund it through Mercado Pago and then you mark the ticket as refunded in the dashboard.
