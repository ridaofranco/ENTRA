Folk music peña with tickets: price, food and the door

By SOMOS DER · October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

A peña (an Argentine folk music night with live music, dancing and food) has its own rhythm: several artists, people who dance and go back to their table, empanadas and wine at the canteen. Selling the ticket is simple if you first decide what it includes, how many people fit with the tables set up and how the door is organized. This guide goes step by step.

A note before you start: ENTRÁ is an Argentine ticketing platform. It sells tickets for events held in Argentina, priced in Argentine pesos (ARS). What this guide says applies to most events; when it describes how ENTRÁ works, it means in Argentina.

What a ticketed peña is

A peña mixes three things: live music, dancing and food. The ticket usually covers the music, and food and drink go through the canteen. Before setting a price, sort out who provides what:

  • Artists: how many groups or solo acts play, in what order and how long each one plays. If there is a headliner, make sure people know what time they go on.
  • Dancing: if there is a dance floor, how much space is left free with the tables set up. At a peña people get up to dance a chacarera (a traditional folk dance) and go back to the table; you need room for both.
  • Food: whether the canteen is yours, the venue's or a third party's, and whether it is included in the ticket or paid separately.

Ticket with or without food

There are two ways to sell it, and it is best to pick one before publishing:

  • Ticket only, with the canteen separate. It is the simplest and the most common: someone who comes to dance doesn't pay for a dinner they don't want.
  • Ticket with food, for example "Entrada + empanadas" or "Entrada + locro" (locro is a traditional stew). It tells you exactly how much food to prepare, but you have to say what it includes and what time it is served.

If you want to offer both, use two ticket types with that detail in the name. In ENTRÁ each type has its own name, price and capacity, and each type closes automatically when it fills up. If the food has a limit (the portions you can prepare), give the "with food" type that capacity. The decisions for a night with dinner are covered in more depth in dinner show: ticket with or without dinner.

Price

Add up what the night costs you (venue, artists, sound, promotion) and divide it by a number of tickets you truly believe you will sell, not by a full house. If the canteen is yours, what it brings in is separate income, but don't count on it to make the ticket numbers work. Some guidelines:

  • Advance tickets cheaper than at the door, and say so everywhere. At peñas many people decide at the last minute; the price difference pushes them to buy earlier.
  • Tables or groups, if the venue has tables: "Mesa para 6" (table for 6) as a ticket type, with its own price, counted as six places of capacity.
  • If there are kids, decide whether they pay and from what age, and write it down.

What to say on the page

  • Who plays and at what time, even if approximate.
  • Whether there is a dance floor and whether there is a dance class before the music starts.
  • What the canteen offers and whether it takes cards or cash only.
  • Whether there are tables: first come, first served or reserved with the ticket.
  • What time doors open and what time it ends, with the end time loaded in the event.

The door

At a peña people arrive in groups, say hello and stay chatting at the entrance. To avoid a bottleneck:

  • One person fixed at the door, not someone from the kitchen or the artists.
  • The QR is scanned from a phone. Each QR is good for one use only: if someone sent a screenshot to someone else, whoever arrives first gets in.
  • If someone can't find the email, they are looked up by last name, ID number (DNI) or email from the scanner.
  • The scanner shows the ticket type: if it is "with food", the door can hand out the token or mark that is later exchanged at the canteen.
  • Whoever arrives without a ticket can buy from their phone on the event page, as long as sales are open.

Frequently asked questions

Does a peña ticket have to include food?

Not necessarily. Most commonly the ticket covers the music and the canteen is paid separately. If you want to offer food included, set up a separate ticket type with that detail in the name and a capacity equal to the portions you can prepare.

How do I sell tables at a peña?

With one ticket type per table, for example "Mesa para 6", with its own price. Count it as six places of the venue's capacity and say on the page whether tables are reserved with the ticket or first come, first served.

Can I charge more at the door than in advance?

Yes, and you should. You change the price from the dashboard when advance sales close, or add a "Puerta" (door) ticket type with another price. Say it in your promotion so people have a reason to buy earlier.
